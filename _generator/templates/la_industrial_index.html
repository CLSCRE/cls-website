Why LA Industrial Splits by Submarket, Not by City

Los Angeles County's industrial base breaks into infill and big-box halves that trade on fundamentally different economics. Infill submarkets like the South Bay and the rail-served Vernon-Commerce corridor sit on land that is effectively built out, so scarcity alone keeps vacancy structurally tight even on decades-old buildings; big-box submarkets like the San Gabriel Valley and Mid-Counties compete on newer, larger-footprint product built on land that was still available when it went up. Neither half is "better," but they underwrite differently, and a lender pricing an infill South Bay building against a big-box comp set in the San Gabriel Valley is pricing the wrong risk.

The SCAQMD's WAIRE Program is the regulatory line that cuts across every submarket on this list: warehouses at or above roughly 100,000 square feet must invest in zero-emission trucks, solar, or EV charging, or pay a mitigation fee, and that threshold catches a much larger share of buildings in the big-box submarkets (San Gabriel Valley, Mid-Counties, Gateway Cities) than in the smaller-bay infill markets (South Bay, Sun Valley-Pacoima, Inglewood-Hawthorne). Rail access along the Alameda Corridor and eligibility for Foreign Trade Zone 202 are the other two variables worth checking before assuming a given site's underwriting profile from its submarket label alone.
