Why life companies win

Who life company lenders are — and why their pricing beats the market.

Life insurance companies lend from their general account — the pooled premium reserve backing their policyholder obligations. That funding base is long-duration, stable, and does not mark to market, which is why life companies can commit to fixed rates for 10, 20, even 25 years when banks reprice every 3 to 5 and CMBS carries refinancing and defeasance risk at the balloon.

The trade-off is discipline. Life companies underwrite to hold the loan to maturity, not to sell it, so they concentrate on institutional-quality assets, proven sponsorship, and conservative leverage — typically 55–65% LTV and 1.25x+ DSCR. When a deal fits that box, the pricing is consistently the tightest available in the private market.

What we place with them: stabilized multifamily, Class A industrial and logistics, anchored retail, net lease with credit tenancy, and medical or creative office with durable income. Loan sizes from $5M, with the most competitive execution at $10M+.

Structures we negotiate

Where the real value hides.

Beyond the headline rate, life company term sheets differ materially on:

  • Prepayment: lockout + defeasance vs. yield maintenance vs. declining penalty — we model the hold-period economics, not just the coupon
  • Recourse carve-outs: non-recourse is standard, but the carve-out definitions (and guaranty scope) vary desk to desk
  • Fixed-rate periods: 3+3, 5+3, and 5+2 reset structures vs. full-term fixed — the reset spread is negotiable
  • Amortization: 25- and 30-year schedules materially beat bank 20- or 25-year balloons on cash flow
  • Proceeds: some desks stretch leverage for strong sponsors; we know which ones are stretching today
