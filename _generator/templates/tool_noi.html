What Is Net Operating Income?

Net operating income is all the income a property actually collects minus the expenses required to operate it, measured before loan payments, income taxes, depreciation, and capital expenditures. It is the single number the entire commercial real estate capital stack is built on: values are quoted as multiples of it, loans are sized against it, and business plans succeed or fail by growing it. Full definition in the NOI glossary entry.

What Goes Into NOI, and What Stays Out

Income: scheduled rent, less a vacancy and credit loss allowance, plus ancillary income that is actually collectable: parking, laundry, storage, utility reimbursements (RUBS), late fees, expense recoveries under NNN leases.

Operating expenses included: property taxes, insurance, management fees, repairs and maintenance, owner-paid utilities, payroll, landscaping, trash, administrative costs, and a replacement reserve if the lender underwrites one.

Excluded, always: mortgage payments, capital expenditures, depreciation, income taxes, and leasing commissions. Debt service is a financing decision, not a property characteristic; that separation is the entire point of NOI.

How to Calculate NOI

  1. Gross potential rent: every unit or suite at market or contract rent, fully occupied.
  2. Subtract vacancy and credit loss: use actuals or the submarket norm, typically 5% to 10%.
  3. Add other income to get effective gross income (EGI).
  4. Subtract operating expenses to get NOI.

Worked example: a 24-unit building with $32,000/month gross potential rent is $384,000/year. Less 6% vacancy ($23,000) plus $14,000 of RUBS and laundry income gives $375,000 EGI. Operating expenses of $158,000 (42% of EGI, normal for multifamily) leave NOI of $217,000. At a 5.75% market cap rate that NOI supports a value around $3,770,000; run the inversion in the cap rate calculator.

NOI vs Cash Flow vs EBITDA

NOI stops before the loan payment. Subtract annual debt service and you have pre-tax cash flow, the number your cash-on-cash return is built on. EBITDA is the corporate cousin of NOI; in hotels and operating-intensive assets like senior housing, underwriters often quote EBITDA because the business and the building are inseparable.

How Lenders Use Your NOI

Every commercial loan sizing starts with NOI. Lenders divide it by the proposed annual debt service to get DSCR, with 1.20x to 1.25x the usual minimum, and divide it by the loan amount to get debt yield, where 9% to 10% is the common floor. Whichever constraint binds first sets your maximum loan. This is why a $10,000 NOI improvement is worth far more than $10,000: at a 6% cap rate it adds roughly $167,000 of value and supports roughly $100,000 of additional loan proceeds.

Growing NOI

On the income side: push rents to market on rollover, cut vacancy with better management, bill back utilities, add fee income. On the expense side: appeal the tax assessment after any purchase, re-shop insurance annually in the current market, and audit contracts nobody has re-bid in years. Because value is NOI divided by the cap rate, every dollar of durable NOI you add is worth 15 to 20 dollars of value at today's cap rates.
