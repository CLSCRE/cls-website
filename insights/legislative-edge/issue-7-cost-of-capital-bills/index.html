The final sprint is about whether housing can get financed

The 2025-26 California legislative session adjourns August 31. The final sprint is not about zoning anymore. It is about whether housing can get financed.

August 14, 2026

A Commercial Lending Solutions Publication Issue No. 7 | August 2026
The Legislative Edge
California housing bills, decoded into deals.

This week's theme

The final sprint is about whether housing can get financed

California's 2025-26 legislative session adjourns August 31. In the final weeks, the housing fight has moved past zoning and CEQA. The bills racing the floor deadline are finance bills in disguise.

They answer the question the industry actually asks: not whether homes can be approved, but whether the capital stack will support them.

The four financing levers these bills touch

1Unknown infrastructure costs that blow up contingencies.
2Construction and defect risk that raises reserves and lender caution.
3Presale credibility that affects buyer deposits and takeout financing.
4Fees and building-code costs that quietly change feasibility.

SB 1014: force the offsite cost conversation earlier

SB 1014 is the bill lenders should watch most closely. It pushes cities and counties toward earlier disclosure of onsite and offsite improvement requirements, and creates a 30-business-day itemized list once a postentitlement permit application is deemed complete.

For lenders, that means fewer late-stage surprises after entitlement, cleaner contingency assumptions, and more defensible construction-loan sizing. It passed the Senate 30-9 in May, cleared Assembly Appropriations on August 5, and now sits on the Assembly floor.

Why it matters: a project can be entitled today and still run into surprise street, sewer, utility, or site-improvement costs that change the bankability of the deal.

AB 1903: construction-defect reform is really a financing bill

AB 1903 was materially amended August 4 and revises claimant requirements, builder repair rights, recoverable investigative costs, and the path to litigation. It passed the Assembly 70-0 in May, was withdrawn from its Senate committee on August 5, and is now in Senate third reading.

This is not an abstract legal debate. It affects warranty reserves, insurance pricing, presale viability, and lender appetite for for-sale product. The condo shortage in California is partly a cost-of-capital problem.

Warranty and reserve assumptionsChange how lenders look at for-sale risk
Insurance and deductible exposureAffect project carry and pricing
Presale and takeout certaintyDetermine how much equity and debt a condo project can support

Two more bills already on the Assembly floor

A SB 1117 applies ADU and JADU impact fees only above 750 sq ft, which quietly improves larger-ADU economics in high-fee cities.
B SB 1116 is the starter-home cleanup and SB 1123 continuation, tightening height, setbacks, density, and ministerial mechanics. Continuity from last issue, not a repeat deep dive.

The one actually on today's suspense: AB 2074

If you are tracking today's Senate suspense file, AB 2074 is the financing-adjacent bill on it. It concerns regional transit hub districts and downtown housing developments, and was referred to the suspense file August 3.

Capital-markets note: separate what a bill actually authorizes from the marketing around it. A bank cannot lend against a talking point. Weigh the machine language, not the press release.

Golden nugget: read the stalls, not just the wins

AB 1406, the condo-deposit bill, had its Senate Judiciary hearing cancelled on June 29 and has not moved since. It still gets cited as an advancing pro-housing financing bill. Verify before you build a presale strategy on it.

The pattern across the final sprint: a bill that passed one house is not law, a bill on a suspense file is not decided, and a marketing line is not a machine. Reading the actual status is the edge.

Closing thought

The housing debate has moved beyond zoning. The next phase is whether California can reduce the actual cost of capital, and the final sprint to August 31 is where part of that gets decided.
