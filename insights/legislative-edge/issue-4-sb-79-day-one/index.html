SB 79 Day One: Underwrite the Ordinance, Not the Statute

Los Angeles delayed full SB 79 implementation and activated local low-rise rules. Map the parcel in ZIMAS before underwriting density or land value.

July 14, 2026

A Commercial Lending Solutions Publication Issue No. 4 | Week of July 14, 2026
The Legislative Edge

This Week's Featured Bill

SB 79 Day One: Don't Underwrite the Statute, Underwrite the Ordinance

July 1, 2026 was supposed to be a clean story: denser housing near transit, statewide. That is the statewide story. It is not the City of Los Angeles story. Two days earlier, Council put a Phased Implementation Ordinance and a Low-Rise Ordinance into force, delaying full SB 79 floors toward ~2030 while expanding corridor-style density near stations. If you priced land on pure statute optimism, pull ZIMAS before you reprice the basis.

What the Bill Does

Signed 10/10/2025 (Sen. Wiener) | Statewide operative 7/1/2026 | Gov. Code §§65912.155-65912.162

SB 79 makes qualifying transit-oriented housing developments an allowed use near specified TOD stops in urban transit counties. Local zoning that would block qualifying heights and densities cannot stand in the way, unless the city has a compliant local ordinance, phased implementation strategy, or HCD-approved TOD alternative plan.

Illustrative statute floors (verify against HCD / MPO maps): Tier 1 heavy rail up to ~95 ft and the highest density band; Tier 2 light rail / Metrolink / BRT up to ~75 ft; Tier 3 lower-tier transit up to ~55 ft. Density often cited in the ~80-160 du/ac and ~2.5-4.5 FAR range depending on tier and distance to the stop.

The line that matters for capital: an optional ministerial path exists when projects add affordability and labor standards. Everything else still starts with one question, does the local map actually put this parcel under the floors you just underwrote?

What Los Angeles Actually Did

This is the day-one correction to pre-implementation coverage. LA did not hand the market a citywide midrise blank check.

Date Event
Oct 10, 2025 SB 79 signed; July 1, 2026 operative date set
June 23, 2026 Council adopts Low-Rise Ord. 188967 + Phased Implementation Ord. 188968
June 30, 2026 Both ordinances effective
July 1, 2026 Statewide SB 79 operative; LA runs on local path

Plain English: LA used the statute's alternative compliance tools to delay full SB 79 floors toward ~2030 while expanding low-rise / corridor incentives near stations. The operational layer is ZIMAS, not a press release about transit upzoning.

Three Capital Scenarios

A. Full SB 79 floors confirmed. Construction or bridge to bank / agency takeout. Unit count jumps, commercial construction underwriting, not SFR hard money.

B. LA Low-Rise / corridor only. Smaller ground-up or value-add multifamily. Faster path, smaller debt. Still stack AB 2097 parking + density bonus tools.

C. Delayed / excluded. Status quo: SB 9, ARO, AB 2011, conventional MF. Do not pay an SB 79 land premium.

Land value rule: no automatic 50-150% uplift until the ordinance and the map confirm it. Pre-implementation "what if" math is a ceiling case. Day-one underwriting uses the actual case.

Stacking Matrix

Layer Role on a real package
SB 79 / Low-Rise map Density and use floor, or not
AB 130 CEQA exemption candidate for qualifying infill
AB 2097 Parking minimums off within ½ mile of transit
State DBL / MIIP Bonus above greater of base zoning or SB 79 floor
SB 9 Primary tool for SF parcels off the TOD map

The pitch to a lender is never "SB 79 alone." It is map + stack + exit.

Underwriting Checklist

1.Confirm jurisdiction (City of LA vs. other urban transit city)
2.Pull ZIMAS / local SB 79 + Low-Rise layers
3.Confirm TOD tier and station type
4.Check existing multifamily / RSO / demolition constraints
5.Labor / PW path if claiming ministerial SB 79
6.CEQA path (AB 130 vs. discretionary)
7.Parking plan under AB 2097
8.Exit: agency vs. bank vs. CMBS given affordability
9.Timeline under local rules, not pure statute optimism
10.Stress land basis without SB 79 premium if map is unclear

Three Other July 1 Files

LA RSO formula is live. Annual increases now use 90% of CPI, with a 1% floor and 4% ceiling. Utility adders are gone. Pre-1978 multifamily NOI growth is tighter, model it on every City RSO refi and acquisition.

Measure ULA thresholds moved. Closings after June 30, 2026: 4% on full consideration for sales >$5.4M and <$10.9M; 5.5% for ≥$10.9M. Cliff still applies from dollar one. Jarvis ballot threat remains for November.

November bond teaser. The Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026 (~$11.25B) is on the November ballot. If it passes, it is a construction and permanent-debt demand story for the affordable pipeline. Full coverage as election week approaches.

Closing Thought

SB 79 is real. Day one is not uniform. Statewide, the statute is live and will reprice transit-adjacent land where cities did not phase. In Los Angeles, the city traded full midrise floors for time, and put low-rise corridor tools on the map instead. That is not a failure of the bill. It is how the bill was written to be used.

The capital mistake is treating a press release like a title report. The capital edge is the same one we bring to every construction and bridge package: verify the entitlement path, size the stack to the unit count you can actually build, and finance the exit that matches the rent restrictions you just accepted.

If you have a transit-adjacent parcel and want a straight read on map status, stacking, and the construction or bridge structure, talk to us. We will pull the ordinance layer before we talk about basis.
