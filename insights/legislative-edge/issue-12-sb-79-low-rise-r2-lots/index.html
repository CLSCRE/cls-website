The Legislative Edge

The quiet upzone nobody marketed: 16 units on an ordinary R2 lot, and it has been live since June 30.

Since June 30 an LA ordinance has let an ordinary R2 lot near a rail station carry up to 16 units with no parking, no hearing, no bonus paperwork.

September 18, 2026

A Commercial Lending Solutions Publication Issue No. 12 | September 2026
The Legislative Edge
California housing bills, decoded into deals.

This week's theme

The quiet upzone nobody marketed: 16 units on an ordinary R2 lot, and it has been live since June 30.

While the industry watched the Governor's desk, LA Ordinance 188967 quietly put the Low-Rise tier of SB 79 into the zoning code. On an ordinary R2 lot near a rail station, the same footprint that held one duplex can now hold up to sixteen units with no parking required, no density bonus paperwork, no elected-official vote. It is ministerial. If your parcel clears the test, the counter has to approve it.

This is not a tower story. It is the single-family and small-lot story, and it is the hottest incentive in the state right now for one dated reason: the desk package becomes law September 30 whether or not the Governor signs, AB 2576's new historic exclusion just took effect September 15, and every week that passes, sellers near the E and B Lines start to catch on.

The four-step eligibility test

Run this on any parcel before you pay an upzoned price for it.

1Base zone. Low-Rise applies to parcels zoned R1, R2, RU, RW1, R3 and comparable residential zones. Commercial and industrial parcels follow the taller SB 79 tier tables instead.
2Subarea. Ordinance 188968's maps split Low-Rise areas in two. LR-2, the strong band, is within 250 feet of the station in urbanized areas or within a quarter mile elsewhere, and allows 12 to 16 units. LR-1 is the next band out and allows 5 to 11. A designated historic resource cannot use the higher LR-2 range.
3Exclusions. Four things disqualify a parcel, and this is where most wrong deals die: prime farmland, wetlands, and conservation easements. Sites that already permit at least half of the SB 79 capacity under local zoning. City affordability programs that meet the state's substitute standards. And the historic exclusions, just widened: AB 2576, signed September 15 (Chapter 214, Statutes of 2026), lets cities exclude contributing sites inside historic districts and individually listed parcels on the State Historic Resources Inventory designated before January 1, 2025. Check ZIMAS and the Inventory before you close.
4Pick your unit count. The Low-Rise ladder trades units against floor-area. You choose the row. Parking ratio: zero. No discretionary hearing. Height, setbacks, and floor plates follow the ordinance's objective standards.

Stack the state density bonus on top. The Low-Rise envelope is your base, and Government Code 65915 stacks on it. An 80 percent bonus takes a 12-unit base to about 21 or 22 units and a 16-unit base to about 28 or 29, plus the bonus parking reduction and your choice of incentives and concessions. The bonus moves the affordability set-aside you carry, so the correct product mix is a math problem, not a guess.

The math on a real parcel shape

A 7,500 square foot R2 lot, 600 feet from an E Line station, LR-2, clean title, no historic designation, not in an exclusion area:

Base path: 12 units at FAR 2.30 = 17,250 square feet of floor area. All units at market rate, no bonus paperwork, no parking required.

Bonus path: an 80 percent bonus takes 12 units to about 21 to 22 units. The affordability set-aside, roughly 4 to 5 units depending on the menu you choose, is the cost. Per-unit floor area comes down to roughly 820 square feet, which is townhome-scale product.

Exit math (illustrative): at $650,000 to $700,000 achievable for entry-level townhomes near the E Line, 12 units is a $7.8M to $8.4M gross exit with no affordability drag. About 21 units with the affordable set-aside is roughly $13.6M to $14.7M gross before the affordable-unit discount. Land sellers are still pricing many of these blocks as duplex lots.

The financing read. A ministerial 12-unit base with zero parking is underwritable on less land carry and no entitlement contingency. Banks that still treat small-lot product as special-purpose money are pricing the old envelope. The bonus path adds units but adds the affordability layer, and the construction loan has to be sized on the blended income, not the headline count. We underwrite both paths side by side before you go hard on the land.

What changed this week (verified September 17, 2026)

AB 2576 (historic exclusions) SIGNED. Chapter 214, Statutes of 2026, approved September 15. It narrows SB 79 eligibility on listed historic parcels. Screen before you price.

Six bills still on the Governor's desk: AB 2074 (downtown high-rise districts, enrolled September 3, still unchaptered as of today), SB 1116 (starter homes), AB 956 (two ADUs), SB 1117 (ADU fees), SB 1014 (infrastructure cost transparency), SB 677 (faster map approvals). Under Article IV, Section 10(b)(2), silence is a signature on September 30. Re-verify chapter numbers before relying on any of them.

AB 1903 (condo defect reform) died in session. The for-sale defect premium stays.

The property type that pencils

R2 and R3 parcels within 750 feet of an E Line or B Line station, outside HPOZs and the industrial-hub exclusions, held by owners who bought before 2021. Eastside stations from Boyle Heights through El Sereno, the K Line south of the 10, and the San Fernando Valley B Line blocks all fit. The seller often does not know the lot is worth twelve units.
