The Legislative Edge

The ballot is the capital event

California's November 3 ballot is fully set at 14 propositions. Three of them are competing for the same capital: a $11.25B housing bond, a $25B homebuyer program, and an $8.4B research bond.

August 21, 2026

A Commercial Lending Solutions Publication Issue No. 8 | August 2026
The Legislative Edge
California housing bills, decoded into deals.

This week's theme

The ballot is the capital event

California's Legislature adjourns August 31. Last week we walked through the cost-of-capital bills racing that deadline. But the single biggest decision about California housing capital is not being made in Sacramento.

The November 3 ballot is now fully set at 14 statewide propositions (qualified as of August 19), and three of them are competing for the same voter dollars and the same state borrowing capacity. For a 2027 deal, the bills decide what is legal. The ballot decides what is fundable.

Three measures, one capital pool

1Prop. 1: $11.25B housing G.O. bond (Veterans & Affordable Housing Bond Act, SB 417). Refills exhausted affordable-housing capital.
2Prop. 37: $25B homebuyer program (CalHFA revenue bond, second-mortgage assistance). Loosens the buyer down-payment constraint.
3Prop. 38: $8.4B research G.O. bond (immunology/immunotherapy). Draws on the same G.O. capacity as Prop. 1.

Prop. 1 and Prop. 38 are both General Obligation bonds drawing from one capacity pool. If both pass, the room for the next state housing ask tightens. Read the ballot as a portfolio, not a series of wins.

Prop. 1: refilling the subsidy gap

Prop. 1 puts $11.25 billion behind affordable rental housing, preservation, and homeownership. The mix is heavy on the gap-fillers: $5.1B for the Multifamily Housing Program (incl. $1.15B supportive housing), $1.25B CalVet home loans, $600M CalHome, $750M Portfolio Reinvestment, and $500M Infill Infrastructure.

Why this is the financing story: the prior 2018 housing G.O. bond is fully exhausted, the 2026 budget left only $500M LIHTC and $200M MHP, and MHP is oversubscribed about nine to one, with more than 46,000 affordable homes across 491 developments waiting on state funding to break ground.

Why it matters: pass → MHP and supportive starts accelerate and construction-debt demand follows. Fail → affordable starts stall into 2027-28 and the gap equity gets harder to assemble.

Prop. 37: the takeout and presale play

Prop. 37 establishes a CalHFA second-mortgage homebuyer program backed by up to $25 billion in revenue bonds. Qualified buyers earn at or below 200% AMI and put at least 3% down.

Because it is a revenue bond (self-funded from repayments), it does not consume the G.O. capacity Prop. 1 uses. But it does something bigger: it loosens the buyer down-payment constraint, a direct presale and takeout signal for the for-sale product (condos and townhomes) we flagged last issue as the hardest to finance.

Pass → the for-sale pipeline gains a demand floor lenders can underwrite against.

The ULA durability story: Prop. 43

For anyone underwriting Los Angeles, the critical piece is quiet: the direct repeal-style threat to Measure ULA is off the ballot. The Howard Jarvis initiative that would have required two-thirds votes for local special taxes and prohibited charter-city transfer taxes above the statutory rate was withdrawn after a legislative compromise.

On the ballot instead is Prop. 43, a constitutional amendment requiring a two-thirds vote to enact, extend, or increase local special-tax initiatives, and prohibiting property taxes via the initiative process.

Existing ULA is durableNo repeal in front of voters. Underwrite the cliff, not the repeal.
Live LA transfer-tax cliff$5.4M / $10.9M thresholds on gross consideration, closings after Jun 30, 2026.
Prop. 43 effectMakes future local transfer/special taxes harder to create → mild tail-risk reducer.

What to watch between now and November 3

Polling on Prop. 1 and Prop. 37. These two most change whether a 2027 deal pencils. Watch undecideds after ballots mail in early October.

The final voter guide. Current status is "qualified as of Aug 19, 2026." Re-verify dollar figures and mechanics against the final guide text before you hard-code pro forma assumptions.

And keep the Aug 31 legislative outcome from last issue paired with this ballot. Together they are the full setup for the 2027 cycle.

Closing thought

The Legislature wrote the laws. The ballot funds the build. The next capital cycle starts when the votes are counted on November 3, not when a bill is signed.
