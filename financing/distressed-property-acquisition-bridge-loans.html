Bridge Loans for Distressed Commercial Property Acquisitions

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated November 2026

A bridge loan for a distressed acquisition is short-term, interest-only debt that closes fast enough to buy a property from a lender, a receiver, an auction or a seller whose sale or refinance fell apart. The lender sizes it to the property's as-is value and the price you pay, and it is repaid by a refinance or a sale once the problem is fixed.

Quick AnswerBuy first, fix second. A distressed property usually cannot qualify for permanent debt, so a bridge lender finances the purchase on as-is value, holds back money for repairs and interest, and expects you to repay it by refinancing or selling once the property is stable. What decides the loan is the price you pay, your plan and budget, your experience and a clear exit. The discount you get is your equity cushion, not extra loan proceeds.

What counts as a distressed acquisition

"Distressed" describes the seller's situation or the property's condition, and each situation changes how fast you have to move and what the lender will want to see. These are the ones we finance most often.

Lender owned (REO)Purchase from the lender after foreclosure, with a normal contract and escrowThe simplest case. Title comes from the lender, pricing is usually below market, and the loan is a standard bridge acquisition on as-is value.

How lenders underwrite a distressed acquisition

A distressed property usually cannot pass the income tests a permanent lender applies, so bridge underwriting leans on four other things.

As-is value and the price you pay

The loan is sized on the lower of the purchase price and the appraised as-is value. A big discount to value is your equity cushion. It does not add loan proceeds, so plan the equity check from the price, not from what the property will be worth after repairs.

The business plan and the budget behind it

Lenders want the scope of work, contractor bids, the lease-up timeline and a stabilized NOI that supports the takeout. Where renovation money is part of the loan, it is held back and released as work is completed.

Carry during the fix

If the property produces little or no income, the loan includes an interest reserve funded at closing, which reduces your net proceeds. Without enough carry, either the loan shrinks or the equity requirement grows.

Why the property is distressed

A lender asks what went wrong: weak management, deferred maintenance, a tenant loss, a capital structure that could not be refinanced or a market problem. A cause you can fix is financeable. A cause you cannot control is priced or declined.

Sponsor experience and liquidity

Distressed deals concentrate execution risk, so lenders weigh the sponsor's record on comparable projects and the cash left after closing. See how to qualify for a commercial bridge loan.

The exit is part of the approval, not an afterthought. Lenders want to know how the loan gets repaid before they fund it.

  • Refinance into permanent debt once the property is stabilized: a life company, agency multifamily, bank or CMBS loan, depending on the asset. A letter of interest from the takeout lender strengthens the file.
  • Sale of the repositioned asset, which suits shorter plans and smaller business plans.
  • Extension inside the loan documents when the plan is on track but running late.
  • Recapitalization with mezzanine debt or preferred equity when the bridge needs to be paid down. See bridge loan vs preferred equity.

Compare the paths in bridge loan vs permanent refinance and hard money vs bridge loan.

Bridge loan or hard money for a distressed deal

Hard money lenders can fund in days and ignore most of the file, and they charge for it with higher rates, shorter terms and heavier fees. An institutional bridge lender takes longer and offers better pricing and longer terms. The right choice depends on the clock. If the seller needs a close in under two weeks and the plan is short, hard money can be the tool. If you have 30 days or more and a 12 to 36 month business plan, a bridge loan is usually cheaper over the life of the deal. Our guide to hard money loans covers that side.

When a sale or a refinance fails

The same bridge structure solves the situation from the other side. If a buyer's financing falls apart, a permanent loan cannot close because the appraisal came in short, or an existing loan is about to mature, a bridge loan can pay off the debt and give the property time to be repaired, re-leased or re-priced. Lenders ask the same question every time: what caused the failure, and is the cure credible? Our comparisons of refinance vs sell and acquisition vs cash out refinance show how that choice is usually made.

What to have ready

  • Purchase contract, notice of sale, court order or payoff statement
  • Default or demand notices, if any
  • Title report with a lien and judgment search
  • Rent roll and operating statements, even if partial
  • Appraisal, broker opinion of value or comparable sales
  • Scope of work with contractor bids
  • Business plan with lease-up timeline and stabilized NOI
  • Exit plan, ideally with a takeout lender's letter of interest
  • Property condition and Phase I environmental reports, or the plan to order them
  • Sponsor financial statement and schedule of real estate owned
  • Entity documents for the buyer
  • Proof of equity and post-closing liquidity

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a bridge loan to buy a distressed commercial property?

Yes. Bridge lenders routinely finance acquisitions of properties that are vacant, behind on maintenance, in default or being sold by a lender, because the loan is sized on as-is value, the sponsor's plan and a credible exit rather than on the income a permanent lender needs. Expect interest only payments, a reserve for interest and repairs when the property does not carry itself, and a lender that wants to see how the loan will be repaid.

Does the lender lend on my discounted price or on the property's value?

Lenders generally size to the lower of the price you pay and the as-is value, and then apply their leverage limit to that lower figure. A discount to value therefore works as your equity cushion, not as extra loan proceeds. If you buy a property worth $10 million for $7 million, the loan is sized against the $7 million price, so the discount protects you but does not raise the loan.

How fast can a distressed acquisition close?

A clean bridge file can close in as little as 2 to 4 weeks, and the pace is set by the appraisal, the environmental report and title. The seller's side often sets the real clock: a lender approving a short sale or discounted payoff, a court confirming a receiver's sale, or an auction that requires funds almost immediately. Tell us the deadline on day one so the lender can commit to it.

Will a lender finance a note purchase?

Fewer lenders will, and the terms are tighter. When you buy a loan you do not yet own the real estate, so the lender is relying on your ability to modify the loan or take title through foreclosure or a deed in lieu. Expect lower leverage than on a property purchase, more of your own equity, and questions about the timeline and cost of taking title. Many note buyers fund the note with equity and arrange the bridge loan when the property is acquired.

Can I use a bridge loan to buy at a foreclosure auction?

Usually only if the loan is approved before the sale. Trustee sales are generally paid in certified funds at or immediately after the sale, with no financing contingency and little or no chance to inspect. A bridge lender can commit in advance to funding a defined property up to a stated bid, but the approval, appraisal and title work all have to be finished before the auction date.

How is a distressed bridge loan repaid?

By a refinance or a sale once the property is cured. The usual refinance is a bank, agency multifamily, life company or CMBS loan after the property is leased and stabilized. If the plan runs late, most bridge loans carry extension options tied to milestones such as occupancy or completed work, and a recapitalization with mezzanine debt or preferred equity is another route.
