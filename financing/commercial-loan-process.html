How we place debt

Our Commercial Loan Process, Step by Step

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated November 2026

Every CLS CRE financing follows the same eight steps: intake, underwriting, a review call, a signed engagement agreement, a lender package, a comparison of proposals, application with third-party reports, and closing. We stay on the file from your first document to funding. Most permanent loans close in 45 to 90 days and life company loans in 60 to 90 days from application to funding; bridge and construction timing depends on the lender and the deal. The eight steps in detail

  1. 1

    Step 1: Intake and document list

    What we doWe listen to the scenario: the property, whether it is a purchase or refinance, the amount, the timeline and the sponsor. We confirm the request fits our $2 million engagement minimum and send a document list tailored to the loan type, with a secure folder for uploads. We respond within 24 hours.

    What we need from youThe basics of the deal, then the documents on the list. For an income property that usually means the rent roll, trailing twelve month operating statement, leases, the purchase contract or current loan statement, a sponsor personal financial statement and schedule of real estate owned, and entity documents.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Underwriting

    What we doWe build our own underwriting of the property income, debt service coverage, leverage and sponsor strength. A lender will do this in its first week anyway; doing it first means we know what the asset supports and can present it in its strongest honest frame, and weak points are fixed or priced before they become a lender's reason to re-trade.

    What we need from youAnswers to follow-up questions as the numbers take shape, and any new documents as they arrive. We refresh the document list whenever something comes in.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Review call

    What we doWe walk you through the underwriting, the structures that fit, which capital sources are likely to compete, and an indicative view of terms. Terms are presented on the call so you can ask questions as we go.

    What we need from youYour priorities: leverage, recourse, speed, prepayment flexibility and the business plan behind the asset. These decide which structure we recommend.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Engagement agreement

    What we doWe send a written engagement agreement for electronic signature. It sets out our fee and the term of the engagement. On a new engagement, we take your deal to lenders only after it is signed.

    What we need from youReview and sign. Ask us anything about it before you do.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Lender package and targeting

    What we doWe assemble a complete loan package: a deal overview, the loan request, an underwriting summary with our Excel analysis, a borrower overview, photos, maps and the supporting documents. We then present it to the lenders whose current appetite fits the property, the market and the structure, rather than to every lender, and we follow up on any lender that goes quiet.

    What we need from youYour sign-off on the request and the sponsor information that goes with it.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Proposals and comparison

    What we doWe do not hand you the first term sheet. We compare every proposal side by side and negotiate, then recommend a lender.

    Comparison pointWhat we pressure-test
    Rate and spreadThe spread over the matching index, not only the all-in number quoted on the day
    ProceedsThe amount offered against the amount requested, and any future funding
    RecourseGuaranty scope and carve-out definitions, which vary lender to lender
    ReservesEscrows and reserve requirements that tie up cash after closing
    PrepaymentLockout, defeasance, yield maintenance or step-down, modeled over your actual hold period
    Closing certaintyThe lender's approval path and its recent funding record

    What we need from youA decision. We give you the comparison and our recommendation; the choice is yours.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Application, third-party reports and title

    What we doOnce you select a lender we move into application: the signed application or term sheet and any deposit, then title and escrow. We confirm which third-party reports the lender requires and who orders each: the appraisal, environmental report and property condition report are standard, and a survey or zoning report may be added. We keep an open items list for the lender and for you and chase every item.

    What we need from youPrompt answers on the lender's application list, access for the report vendors, and the deposit if one is required.

  8. 8

    Step 8: Closing and funding

    What we doWe work from the lender's closing checklist: loan documents, title requirements, insurance, final conditions and closing date, and we coordinate with the lender, its counsel and escrow until the loan funds. After funding we close out the file and stay available for your next financing.

    What we need from youSigned loan documents, the remaining closing items and funds to close.

Updates and what moves the timeline

Every placement in process gets a standing weekly update: where the loan is, what the lender asked for last, what is due this week, and any movement in pricing or structure. You should never have to call us to find out where your deal stands.

  • Complete documents early. Most delays trace back to a missing rent roll, operating statement or sponsor financial statement. The sooner the list is complete, the sooner underwriting and lender outreach can start.
  • Lender type. Life company, agency, CMBS and bank executions each have their own approval path and third-party report schedule. See the life company placement process for a worked example of one path.
  • Third-party reports. Appraisal, environmental and property condition reports are the longest fixed items in most schedules. We confirm early which ones the lender needs and who orders them.
  • Responsiveness. Lender requests answered the same week keep a loan moving; requests that sit are the usual reason a closing date slips.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the commercial loan process take?

It depends on the loan type, the property and how quickly documents arrive. Most permanent executions close in 45 to 90 days, and life company placements typically run 60 to 90 days from application to funding. Bridge and construction timing depends on the lender and the scope of the deal. A complete document package at the start is the biggest single way to shorten the schedule.

What do I need to send first?

The list depends on the loan type, and we send you a tailored list in step one. For an income property it usually starts with the rent roll, the trailing twelve month operating statement, leases or lease abstracts, the purchase contract or current loan statement, a sponsor personal financial statement and schedule of real estate owned, and entity documents.

When do I sign an engagement agreement, and what does it cover?

After our review call and before we take your deal to lenders. It is a written agreement, signed electronically, that sets out our fee and the term of the engagement. You see it before any lender work begins on a new engagement.

Who orders the appraisal and other third-party reports?

It depends on the lender. Some lenders order the appraisal and reports themselves and others have the borrower engage an approved vendor. As soon as a lender is selected we confirm which reports it requires and who orders each one.

Will I know where my deal stands?

Yes. Every placement in process gets a standing weekly update covering where the loan is, what the lender asked for last, what is due this week and any movement in pricing or structure.

What if no lender wants the deal as submitted?

We say so early. We tell you which capital source we would run it to instead, or what would need to change in the structure, leverage or sponsorship to make it financeable.
