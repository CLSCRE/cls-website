Quick AnswerPermanent loan ranges (life company, CMBS, agency, HUD and FHA, bank, DSCR) are rebuilt from Treasury data plus a spread for each program. Bridge and construction ranges are set by our lender desk. All of them live in one table that the rate pages read, and a page's Updated date moves only when its range changes. A few program pages are not on the table yet and show the date their figures were last reviewed.

Where each range comes from

Every headline range on this site is stored once, in a single rate table. Pages read from that table, so one range cannot appear on one page and a different range on another.

ProgramHow the range is built
Life companyThe 10-year Treasury yield plus a published spread, rounded. The published range only moves when an end of it moves by a set amount, so it does not churn on small daily changes.
CMBS, agency, HUD and FHA, bank and credit unionThe 10-year Treasury yield plus a spread band for that program.
DSCR rental loansThe 5-year Treasury yield plus a spread band.
Bridge and constructionSet by our lender desk from term sheets and quotes, dated with the as-of date shown, and carried forward until the desk changes them. They are not computed from SOFR.

The Treasury, SOFR and Prime values come from Federal Reserve data. The benchmark table on Commercial Mortgage Rates shows them with their dates.

What the dates mean

Updated

On a rate page, the update date is the day the range shown actually last changed. If the job runs ten times and the range does not move, the date does not move.

As of

The day the market data behind the range was read. It can be later than the update date, because a range can stay the same while the data is re-checked.

Reviewed

On the program pages that are not on the table yet, the date is when our desk last reviewed its figures. Those pages say so in a note under the rate table.

Dated articles

Weekly rate posts and market commentary describe the week or month they were written for. They are snapshots, not current rates, and carry the date they were published.

Which pages follow the table

  • Commercial Mortgage Rates, the master page for every program
  • Life company loan rates
  • Bridge loan rates
  • Construction loan rates
  • Agency loan rates, CMBS loan rates and HUD and FHA loan rates
  • Rates and programs, the homepage market table and the Los Angeles permanent loans page

The hard money, net lease, portfolio and bridge to perm rate pages are not on the table yet. Each shows the date its desk figures were last reviewed.

What a range tells you, and what it does not

A range is where we are seeing deals price for that program on the date shown. It is not an offer. The rate you receive depends on the property type and market, leverage, debt service coverage, the sponsor and the lender that wins the deal, and loans start at $2 million. If you cite a range from this site, cite the page and its update date.

To see pricing for a specific deal, send us the scenario or compare programs on Commercial Mortgage Rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the rates on clscre.com updated daily?

The rate table is checked on a schedule through the day, but a range only changes, and its date only moves, when the underlying benchmark moves enough to change the range. A page showing an update date of a few days ago can still be current.

Why do two of your pages show different ranges for the same product?

Because a few program pages are not on the automated rate table yet. Each of them shows the date its desk figures were last reviewed, and the table pages show the date their figures last changed. When two pages disagree, use the table page and the later date.

How is the bridge loan range set if bridge loans float over SOFR?

The bridge and construction ranges are set by our lender desk from the term sheets and quotes lenders are sending, and they are dated with the as-of date shown. They are not computed mechanically from SOFR. The benchmark table on the Commercial Mortgage Rates page shows where SOFR and the Treasury yields stood on the as-of date.

Is a range on your site a quote?

No. Every range is indicative. A quote depends on the property, the leverage, the sponsor and the lender, and it is not a commitment to lend. Send us the deal and we will return pricing from the lenders that fit.
