When to Use a Debt Fund vs. a Bank Bridge

Many borrowers default to their existing bank relationship for bridge and transitional financing, often because they do not realize that a debt fund can deliver a materially better outcome. Here is a head-to-head comparison of the two approaches.

Debt Fund Advantages

  • Speed: 2-4 weeks to close vs. 60-90 days for a bank
  • Leverage: Up to 80% LTV / 85% LTC vs. 65-70% for banks
  • Certainty: Single decision-maker vs. bank credit committee
  • Flexibility: Interest reserves, future funding, earn-outs
  • Property types: Hotels, retail, office, types banks avoid
  • Non-recourse: Available for experienced sponsors at $10M+
  • No deposits required: No deposit relationship needed

Bank Bridge Advantages

  • Rate: SOFR+200-350 vs. SOFR+275-500 for debt funds
  • Origination fees: 0.25-0.50% vs. 1.00-2.00% for debt funds
  • Relationship: Ongoing banking relationship, cross-sell value
  • Extensions: Often more flexible on maturity extensions
  • Reporting: Less onerous ongoing reporting requirements
  • Rate caps: Not typically required (debt funds often require)
  • Recourse: Standard (but also available non-recourse for large deals)

Choose a debt fund when: Speed is critical (acquisition deadlines, competitive situations), you need higher leverage than a bank will provide, your deal involves a property type banks are avoiding (hotels, office, retail), you need structural flexibility (future funding, interest reserves, earn-outs), or you do not have an existing bank relationship with bridge capacity.

Choose a bank bridge when: You have an existing deposit relationship and the bank can offer a competitive rate, speed is not the primary driver, the deal fits comfortably within bank parameters (65-70% LTV, strong property type, strong market), and the lower rate and fees produce a meaningfully better total cost of capital over the expected hold period.

Debt Fund Rate Structures & Fee Economics

How Debt Fund Pricing Works

Debt fund loans are floating-rate, priced as a spread over SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate), which replaced LIBOR as the benchmark rate in 2023. The all-in rate equals the current SOFR rate plus the fund's spread. With SOFR at approximately 4.35% in Q1 2026:

Senior Bridge
SOFR + 275-375
Stretch Senior
SOFR + 325-450
Heavy Value-Add
SOFR + 375-500
Construction
SOFR + 350-500
Mezzanine
10% - 15%
Preferred Equity
8% - 14%

SOFR Floors

Most debt fund loans include a SOFR floor, a minimum base rate regardless of where SOFR actually trades. Typical floors are 2.00%-3.50%. If SOFR drops to 3.00% but your floor is 3.50%, you pay 3.50% + spread. In the current rate environment where SOFR is above most floors, this is academic. But if rates decline significantly, the floor becomes a real cost. Negotiating the lowest possible SOFR floor is a meaningful lever in the term sheet negotiation.

Origination Fees

Debt funds charge origination fees (points) at closing: typically 1.00-2.00% of the loan amount for senior bridge, 1.50-2.50% for stretch senior and construction, and 1.00-2.00% for mezzanine. On a $20M bridge loan, 1.50 points equals $300,000, a significant cost that must be factored into the total return analysis. Some funds charge fees on the full committed amount (including unfunded future advances); others charge only on the funded amount at closing with additional fees as future funding is drawn.

Exit Fees & Extension Fees

Exit fees: Some debt funds charge an exit fee (0.25%-1.00% of the loan balance) payable at repayment. This is separate from any prepayment penalty and is simply a cost of exiting the loan. Exit fees are negotiable, an experienced broker can often eliminate them or reduce them in exchange for a slightly higher spread.

Extension fees: Most bridge loans have a 2-3 year initial term with one or two extension options of 6-12 months each. Extensions are subject to performance tests (minimum DSCR, maximum LTV based on an updated appraisal, no event of default) and an extension fee of 0.25%-0.50% per extension period. Extensions are not automatic, if you do not meet the performance tests, the loan matures and you must refinance or sell.

Rate Cap Requirements

Because debt fund loans are floating-rate, many funds require borrowers to purchase an interest rate cap, a derivative contract that limits the maximum SOFR rate the borrower pays. For example, a SOFR cap at 5.00% means that even if SOFR rises to 7.00%, the borrower's effective SOFR is capped at 5.00%. Rate cap costs vary based on the strike rate, term, and notional amount, and have fluctuated significantly in recent years. As of Q1 2026, a 2-year SOFR cap at 5.00% on a $20M loan costs approximately $100K-$200K. This cost is paid upfront at closing and is a non-trivial addition to the total cost of the loan.

Case Study 1: $16M Multifamily Value-Add Bridge

  • Property: 160-unit Class B garden-style apartment community in a major Sun Belt MSA, built 1998, 88% occupied, unrenovated interiors
  • Acquisition Price: $18.8M ($117,500/unit)
  • Renovation Budget: $3.2M ($20,000/unit for kitchen, bath, flooring, fixtures, common areas)
  • Total Cost Basis: $22.8M ($142,500/unit)
  • Projected Stabilized Value: $29.0M ($181,250/unit at 5.50% cap rate)
  • Capital Source: A national institutional debt fund
  • Loan Amount: $16.0M initial funding + $3.2M future funding for renovations = $19.2M total commitment (84% LTC)
  • Rate: SOFR+310 bps (7.45% all-in at closing), SOFR floor of 2.50%
  • Origination: 1.00% on initial funding, 0.50% on future funding as drawn
  • Term: 3 years initial + two 12-month extensions
  • Structure: Interest-only, 9-month interest reserve, future funding drawn via monthly requisitions approved by third-party inspector
  • Recourse: Non-recourse with standard carve-outs (sponsor had 12 similar completed projects)
  • Timeline: 19 days from term sheet to closing
  • Outcome: Borrower completed renovations on 120 of 160 units within 14 months, achieving average rent premiums of $275/month on renovated units. Projected permanent refinance via Fannie Mae DUS at $23M (80% of stabilized value) in month 20, returning equity to investors and generating a 22% levered IRR on the value-add execution.

Case Study 2: $6.5M Mezzanine Behind Agency Senior

  • Property: 280-unit Class A multifamily property in a major West Coast MSA, recently completed lease-up to 94% occupancy
  • Appraised Value: $62.0M
  • In-Place NOI: $3.72M (T-12, trending up)
  • Senior Loan: Freddie Mac Optigo, $40.3M (65% LTV), 5.85% fixed, 10-year term
  • Capital Source (Mezz): A private credit platform specializing in multifamily mezzanine
  • Mezz Loan Amount: $6.5M (filling to 75.5% LTV total leverage)
  • Mezz Rate: 11.5% current-pay coupon
  • Mezz Term: 5 years, co-terminus with senior loan initial fixed period
  • Mezz Origination: 1.50 points ($97,500)
  • Intercreditor: Freddie Mac-approved mezzanine structure with standard intercreditor provisions
  • Timeline: Mezz commitment issued in 7 days; closed simultaneously with senior loan
  • Outcome: The mezzanine loan allowed the borrower to extract $6.5M in equity from the recently completed development project, reducing their total equity investment by 42% while maintaining cash-flow-positive operations (combined DSCR of 1.12x on total debt). The extracted capital was redeployed into a new acquisition, effectively doubling the borrower's deployment capacity.

Case Study 3: $12M Construction Loan, Industrial Build-to-Suit

  • Property: 85,000 SF single-tenant industrial distribution facility in a major Midwest logistics market, build-to-suit for a regional distribution company on a 10-year NNN lease
  • Land Cost: $2.1M (owned, contributed as equity)
  • Hard & Soft Construction Costs: $11.3M ($133/SF)
  • Total Development Cost: $13.4M ($158/SF including land, soft costs, and carry)
  • Projected Stabilized Value: $17.8M ($209/SF, 6.75% cap rate on $1.2M NNN rent)
  • Capital Source: A debt fund with an active construction lending program
  • Loan Amount: $12.0M (89.5% LTC including land contribution)
  • Rate: SOFR+400 bps (8.35% all-in), SOFR floor of 3.00%
  • Origination: 2.00 points ($240,000)
  • Term: 30 months (18-month construction + 12-month stabilization/lease commencement)
  • Structure: Interest-only, funded via monthly draw requests, 12-month interest reserve, completion guarantee from guarantor
  • Recourse: Completion guarantee (full recourse until certificate of occupancy and tenant occupancy), then burn-off to non-recourse
  • Timeline: 28 days from term sheet to closing
  • Outcome: Construction completed on schedule and within budget. Tenant took occupancy in month 17. Permanent refinance via a life insurance company at $12.5M (70% of stabilized value), 5.55% fixed, 10-year term. Borrower invested $1.4M in net equity and created $4.4M in value ($17.8M value - $13.4M cost), a 3.1x equity multiple before financing costs. The build-to-suit lease eliminated lease-up risk, which was critical for securing the debt fund construction loan at 89.5% LTC.

How Commercial Lending Solutions Sources from 200+ Debt Funds

The debt fund market is fragmented and heterogeneous. Each of the 200+ funds we work with has a distinct lending mandate defined by multiple parameters: geographic focus (some lend nationwide, others prefer specific regions), property type preference (multifamily-only funds, industrial specialists, all-property-type generalists), deal size sweet spot ($5M-$20M, $15M-$50M, $25M+), leverage tolerance (conservative 65% LTV vs. aggressive 80%+ LTC), and risk appetite (light bridge only vs. heavy value-add and construction).

When a deal comes in, we match it against our database of active fund mandates to identify the 5-15 funds most likely to offer competitive terms for that specific deal. This matching process is relationship-based: we know which funds are actively deploying (some funds are fully invested and not quoting new deals), which have capacity constraints (a fund nearing the end of its investment period may offer more aggressive terms to deploy remaining capital), and which are adjusting their appetites (a fund that just had a construction loan default may temporarily pause construction lending).

This depth of relationships produces materially better outcomes than borrowers can achieve on their own. Most borrowers know 2-3 debt funds. We know 200+. The competitive tension between multiple funds bidding on the same deal consistently produces tighter spreads, lower fees, higher leverage, and more favorable structural terms than any single-source approach can achieve.

Debt Fund Loans FAQ

A debt fund (private credit) loan is a commercial real estate loan originated by a private investment fund rather than a bank, insurance company, or CMBS conduit. Debt funds raise capital from institutional investors and deploy it into short-term, higher-yielding CRE loans. They provide bridge, transitional, mezzanine, and construction financing at higher leverage and faster speed than traditional lenders, with rates reflecting the higher risk and shorter duration.
The institutional debt fund market starts at $10 million. Below this threshold, borrowers enter the hard money space with significantly higher rates (10-14%+) and fees (2-4+ points). At $10M+, borrowers access institutional debt funds charging SOFR+275-500 bps with 1-2 points, meaningfully cheaper. Commercial Lending Solutions focuses on debt fund transactions of $10M and above.
Institutional debt funds can close in 2-4 weeks for experienced sponsors with clean assets. The fastest closings (14-18 days) occur when the borrower has a recent appraisal, clean title, current environmental report, and a track record with the specific fund. More complex deals (construction, hospitality, portfolio) may take 3-5 weeks. This speed advantage versus banks (60-90 days) is a primary reason borrowers use debt funds.
Debt funds are institutional platforms managing $500M-$10B+ from sophisticated investors, with professional underwriting and standardized documents, pricing at SOFR+275-500 bps with 1-2 points. Hard money lenders are smaller, privately funded operators charging 10-14%+ with 2-4+ points. Debt funds are cheaper, larger ($5M+ minimum), more professional, and offer structured products (mezzanine, preferred equity, construction) that hard money cannot.
Debt funds dominate transitional and value-add deals: multifamily renovation and lease-up, hotel repositioning, retail remerchandising, office repositioning, ground-up construction (select funds), land with entitlements, and note-on-note financing. The common thread is a business plan that involves changing the property's income profile. Debt funds underwrite to the business plan's feasibility and exit strategy, not just current cash flow.
SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) is the benchmark interest rate. SOFR+350 means the loan rate equals the current SOFR plus 3.50% (350 basis points). With SOFR at approximately 4.35% in Q1 2026, SOFR+350 produces an all-in rate of approximately 7.85%. Most debt fund loans include a SOFR floor (2.00%-3.50%) setting a minimum base rate regardless of where SOFR trades.
A typical 85% LTC structure includes: senior bridge debt at 65-75% LTC (first-lien mortgage), mezzanine debt filling to 80-85% LTC (secured by ownership pledge, intercreditor agreement with senior), and borrower equity at 15% of total cost. Some structures replace mezzanine with preferred equity, which sits in the equity layer rather than as subordinate debt. Intercreditor agreements govern payment priorities, cure rights, and foreclosure procedures.
