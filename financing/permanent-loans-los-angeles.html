Permanent Loans in Los Angeles, CA

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated November 2026
Quick answer: A permanent loan is the long-term, fixed-rate mortgage on a stabilized commercial property: 5- to 25-year terms, 25- to 30-year amortization, 55% to 75% loan-to-value. In Los Angeles, Commercial Lending Solutions places permanent loans from $2MM through life insurance companies, CMBS conduits, banks, credit unions and the agencies. We have 20 documented permanent closings in Los Angeles County totaling $197M, from $725K to $31.4M, with the rates and terms published on each case study.

Permanent loans national overview  ·  How to qualify

Most of what we close in Los Angeles is permanent debt: refinancing a maturing loan, taking out a construction or bridge loan once a property is leased, or financing an acquisition the buyer intends to hold. Los Angeles is a market where the headline rate is rarely the hard part. The hard part is leverage, because compressed cap rates leave less debt service coverage than lenders want; prepayment, because owners here hold for decades and then recapitalize in a single move; and the local rules that lenders underwrite around, from the Rent Stabilization Ordinance to Measure ULA to seismic reports on buildings built before 1978. This page covers who is lending on stabilized Los Angeles property in November 2026, what they are pricing, how they size loans here, and what our closed deals looked like.

How we read the market right now

Life insurance companies are the best execution for Los Angeles industrial, grocery-anchored retail and well-located net lease, and for newer multifamily at moderate leverage. They want low leverage and clean collateral and in return give the lowest fixed rates and the longest terms. They will not stretch, and most want $5MM or more.

CMBS conduits are the deepest source of non-recourse money for cash-flowing retail, industrial and office in Los Angeles at 65% to 70% leverage, often with full-term interest-only. Our three largest LA permanent closings, $31.4M in Little Tokyo and $27.5M and $28M in Vernon, were conduit or conduit-style executions. The cost is structure: lockboxes, cash management, reserves and defeasance.

Banks and credit unions do the most volume in Los Angeles between $1MM and $15MM. They are the only option for owner-occupied buildings and the most flexible on prepayment. Several of our LA credit union closings came with hybrid-rate structures (fixed for 5 or 7 years, then a capped reset) and pricing tied to deposits. Expect personal guarantees.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are usually the most competitive source for stabilized Los Angeles apartment buildings with five or more units, including buildings under the Rent Stabilization Ordinance, at up to 75% to 80% leverage and non-recourse. HUD 223(f) gives the lowest rate and a 35-year fully amortizing loan for owners who will hold and can wait six months or more to close.

Los Angeles Specifics

How Permanent Lenders Underwrite Los Angeles Deals

Leverage is set by cash flow, not by the LTV in the term sheet

Los Angeles cap rates sit below most of the country. When a property trades at a 5% cap and debt costs 6.5%, a 1.25x debt service coverage test caps the loan well below 70% of value, often at 55% to 62%. Lenders also test debt yield (net operating income divided by loan amount), which CMBS desks want at 8% to 9% or better. We size every request on all three tests and tell the borrower which one controls, because it changes what to negotiate: a longer amortization or interest-only period helps a DSCR-bound deal, while nothing but more equity helps a debt-yield-bound one.

Measure ULA pushed owners from selling to refinancing

Since April 2023 the City of Los Angeles has charged a 4% transfer tax on sales above roughly $5 million and 5.5% above roughly $10 million, on the full price, with thresholds indexed each year. The tax does not apply to a refinance. The result is a steady stream of cash-out permanent loans from owners who would otherwise have sold, and lenders in Los Angeles now underwrite cash-out requests as routine as long as the property supports the debt. The Sherman Oaks creative office refinance below was a cash-out at 55% LTV; the four-property Beverly Hills and Los Angeles portfolio was a hold-and-recapitalize rather than a sale.

Rent-stabilized apartments

Most Los Angeles apartment buildings built before October 1978 fall under the city’s Rent Stabilization Ordinance, which limits annual increases on occupied units. Lenders underwrite these buildings to in-place rents with little growth, which lowers proceeds versus market-rent underwriting but also makes the income predictable. Agency lenders treat regulated rent rolls as stable and are often the best source. We cover the financing side in detail on financing rent-controlled apartments in Los Angeles.

Seismic, retrofit and insurance

Life companies and CMBS lenders order a seismic probable maximum loss study on Los Angeles collateral. A PML above about 20% means earthquake insurance or a reserve, which can move pricing more than the rate itself. Soft-story wood-frame buildings should have their retrofit completed and certified before the loan application; banks will ask for the compliance letter. Older concrete tilt-up industrial buildings in Vernon, Commerce and the South Bay are routinely financed, but the engineer’s report should be in hand early.

Property taxes on acquisitions

Under Proposition 13 a sale resets the assessed value to the purchase price. On an acquisition loan every lender underwrites the reassessed tax bill, not the seller’s. On a refinance there is no reassessment, which is one reason refinancing a long-held Los Angeles property often supports more debt than the same property would after a sale.

What a Los Angeles permanent loan needs to close

Three years of operating statements and a current rent roll; leases and estoppels for commercial tenants; the title report with any recorded restrictions; the property condition, environmental and seismic reports (ordered by the lender); and a sponsor package with a schedule of real estate, liquidity and credit. Bank and credit union loans close in 45 to 60 days, CMBS and life company loans in 60 to 75, agency in 60 to 90. The most common Los Angeles delays are slow tenant estoppels, retrofit documentation and clearing title on properties held in family trusts for decades.
