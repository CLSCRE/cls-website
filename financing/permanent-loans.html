Permanent Commercial Real Estate Loans

Permanent loans provide long-term, fixed-rate financing for stabilized commercial real estate assets. These loans typically feature 5 to 25-year terms with competitive rates from banks, life insurance companies, CMBS conduits, and agency lenders. Ideal for cash-flowing properties with stable occupancy, permanent financing locks in predictable debt service and maximizes long-term returns.

Long-Term Permanent Commercial Mortgage Financing

As the most common form of commercial real estate financing, permanent loans serve as the backbone of investment strategy for owners seeking to hold and operate income-producing properties. Permanent CRE Loans Nationwide: What Investors Need to Know

Across every major U.S. market, permanent financing represents the most consequential capital decision a commercial real estate investor makes. This is the loan that replaces expensive short-term debt, locks in your cost of capital for a decade or more, and determines whether a stabilized asset generates durable, predictable cash flow or bleeds yield through high-interest carry. At Commercial Lending Solutions, we have closed more than one billion dollars in permanent CRE transactions nationwide, and the fundamentals driving permanent loan demand in 2025 and 2026 are as compelling as any period in recent memory. Borrowers who built their capital stacks on floating-rate bridge debt or construction financing from 2020 through 2023 are actively seeking the certainty that only a fixed-rate permanent loan delivers.

Why Investors Nationwide Choose Permanent CRE Loans

The core motivation is straightforward: replace expensive, short-term, recourse debt with long-term, fixed-rate, often non-recourse financing that aligns with the hold horizon of a stabilized income-producing asset. When a multifamily project in Atlanta or a last-mile industrial facility in the Inland Empire reaches stable occupancy, the bridge loan that funded lease-up has fulfilled its purpose. Carrying it another day costs money. A permanent loan converts that liability into a defined, manageable obligation with a known payment for 10, 15, or 20 years. Nationwide, we are seeing this refinance wave in markets coast to coast as sponsors seek to preserve equity, reduce risk, and position assets for long-term performance or eventual sale.

Who Qualifies for Permanent Financing

Lenders underwrite permanent loans against the property first and the borrower second, but both matter. Qualifying deals typically share the following characteristics:

  • Stabilized occupancy of 90% or greater, sustained for a minimum of 90 days prior to loan closing
  • Demonstrated net operating income supporting a debt service coverage ratio of at least 1.20x to 1.30x at the proposed note rate
  • Loan amounts generally starting at $2 million, with no practical upper ceiling for institutional-quality assets
  • Property types including multifamily (conventional and affordable), industrial, anchored retail, NNN single-tenant, self-storage, and medical office
  • Sponsors with verifiable CRE ownership experience, clean credit, and sufficient liquidity post-closing
  • Assets located in primary, secondary, and select tertiary markets with demonstrable rent comparables and absorption data

Office assets present the most significant qualification challenge in the current environment. Life insurance companies, historically the most aggressive permanent lenders on high-quality office, have largely exited the non-trophy office category nationwide. CMBS conduits remain selectively active on well-leased suburban office, but underwriting is conservative and leverage is capped well below multifamily or industrial standards.

How Permanent Loans Are Structured Nationwide

The permanent lending market draws from four primary capital sources, each with distinct structure and pricing. Life insurance companies offer the most competitive rates, typically in the 5.40% to 6.25% range, at conservative leverage of 55% to 65% LTV, with fixed terms of 10 to 20 years, full amortization schedules of 25 to 30 years, and prepayment protection via defeasance or yield maintenance. CMBS conduits reach up to 75% LTV, carry rates in the 5.90% to 6.75% range, are almost universally structured as 10-year fixed with defeasance, and are fully non-recourse. Agency lenders, specifically Fannie Mae DUS and Freddie Mac program lenders, serve the multifamily sector exclusively with highly competitive pricing, flexible prepayment options, and leverage up to 80% LTV on certain programs. Regional and national banks remain active on smaller loan sizes and mixed-use assets where life company appetite is limited.

Nationwide Market Context That Shapes Underwriting

The breadth of the U.S. market means that permanent loan underwriting is never one-size-fits-all. A industrial distribution facility in the Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex benefits from one of the most liquid permanent lending environments in the country, with multiple life company programs and CMBS conduits competing aggressively on rate and terms due to strong rent growth fundamentals and institutional occupancy. A garden-style multifamily community in a Sunbelt secondary market like Boise or Raleigh commands strong life company interest given population in-migration data, but lenders are now stress-testing rental assumptions against recent new supply deliveries. Nationally, industrial and multifamily remain the two property types commanding the deepest permanent lender interest, followed by NNN retail leased to investment-grade tenants. Across all markets, lenders are scrutinizing insurance costs, property tax reassessment risk, and operating expense growth in their DSCR calculations with greater rigor than at any point in the last decade.

When to Choose Permanent Financing Over Alternatives

Permanent financing is the correct tool when your asset is fully stabilized, you have a defined hold period, and your priority is locking in cash flow certainty and reducing cost of capital. If occupancy is still climbing or you anticipate a major lease event within 12 to 24 months, a short-term bridge extension may be the wiser path. If you plan to sell within three years, the prepayment penalties embedded in life company and CMBS permanent loans can erode exit proceeds significantly. However, for the investor who is holding a stabilized multifamily, industrial, or NNN retail asset with a five-to-ten-year horizon, there is no more efficient capital tool available in the current market than a well-structured permanent loan at today's fixed rates.

Permanent Loans FAQ

A permanent commercial loan is long-term financing (5-25 years) for stabilized, income-producing commercial properties. Unlike bridge or construction loans, permanent loans feature fixed or floating rates with full amortization schedules, providing predictable debt service for property owners.
Permanent commercial loan rates typically range from 5.34% to 8.25%, depending on the lender type, property quality, borrower strength, and market conditions. Life insurance companies and agency lenders (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) generally offer the most competitive rates for qualifying properties.
Most permanent commercial loans offer up to 75% loan-to-value (LTV), though agency multifamily programs can go up to 80% LTV. LTV limits depend on the lender type, property type, and borrower qualifications.
Yes. Non-recourse permanent loans are available from life insurance companies, CMBS conduits, agency lenders, and select banks. Non-recourse means the borrower is not personally liable beyond standard carve-out guarantees for fraud, environmental issues, and other bad acts.
Virtually all stabilized commercial property types qualify for permanent financing, including multifamily apartments, industrial warehouses, retail shopping centers, office buildings, hospitality, self-storage, and mixed-use assets. The key requirement is stable occupancy and cash flow.
Permanent commercial loans typically close in 45-90 days from application, depending on the lender and property complexity. Bank loans may close faster (45-60 days), while life insurance company and CMBS loans typically require 60-90 days for underwriting, appraisal, and legal review.
