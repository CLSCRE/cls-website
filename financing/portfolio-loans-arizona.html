Portfolio Loans in Arizona

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated August 2026

Balance sheet financing from the banks and credit unions actually lending in Phoenix, Tucson, and across Arizona. Flexible underwriting from $1M, matched from 1,000+ lender relationships.

Quick answer: a portfolio loan is a commercial mortgage the lender keeps on its own balance sheet, so it can underwrite to judgment instead of a securitization checklist. In Arizona that means community banks, regional banks, and credit unions funding deals from $1M at 65% to 75% LTV, priced roughly 125 to 300 basis points over Treasuries, with simple prepayment instead of defeasance.

What Is a Portfolio Loan?

Most commercial mortgages are originated to be sold: to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, into a CMBS trust, or to another investor. A portfolio lender does the opposite. It originates the loan with its own deposits and holds it to maturity, which changes everything about how the deal gets underwritten. There is no securitization rulebook to satisfy, so the lender can weigh a strong sponsor against a soft trailing twelve months, lend on a property type the agencies will not touch, or close in weeks because the decision maker sits at the same institution.

The trade is usually a modestly higher rate and recourse. For stabilized, vanilla assets at size, agency and CMBS execution wins on price. For everything else, and for borrowers who value speed and a relationship over the last 25 basis points, portfolio lending is often the right execution. Why Portfolio Lending Fits Arizona Right Now

Arizona has spent a decade as one of the fastest growing states in the country, and its lending market reflects it. Maricopa County consistently ranks among the national leaders in population growth, the semiconductor buildout in north Phoenix has anchored one of the largest industrial investments in United States history, and the West Valley logistics corridor along Loop 303 keeps absorbing new distribution product. Tucson adds aerospace, defense, and University of Arizona demand drivers with a lower cost basis.

That growth attracts exactly the lender set portfolio borrowers want: community and regional banks competing for Arizona commercial relationships, and credit unions that have become aggressive commercial real estate lenders in the Southwest. Growth markets also produce the deals securitized programs handle poorly, lease-up multifamily, build-to-rent communities, owner-user industrial, and small mixed-use in transitioning submarkets, which is precisely where balance sheet flexibility earns its keep.

Typical Arizona Portfolio Loan Terms

  • Loan amount: $1M to $25M+ is the sweet spot for bank and credit union balance sheets.
  • Rate: generally 125 to 300 basis points over comparable Treasuries, depending on asset, leverage, and deposit relationship.
  • Structure: 3, 5, 7, or 10 year fixed periods with 20 to 30 year amortization; interest-only available case by case.
  • Leverage: 65% to 75% LTV, sized against a 1.20x to 1.25x DSCR. Run yours in the DSCR calculator.
  • Recourse: standard, often with burn-off as the asset seasons.
  • Prepayment: step-down schedules (for example 3-2-1) rather than defeasance or yield maintenance.

Equity requirements follow LTV: plan on 25% to 35% down for investment property. The commercial property down payment guide covers the full picture by loan type.

Where Portfolio Beats Agency and CMBS

  • Story deals: partial vacancy, lease-up, recent renovation, or a sponsor credit event that needs a human read.
  • Property types securitized programs decline: small mixed-use, owner-user industrial, specialty assets, older vintage product.
  • Speed: local credit committees close in 30 to 45 days without securitization timelines.
  • Flexibility at exit: selling or refinancing early without defeasance math ruining the trade.
  • Smaller balances: the $1M to $5M loans that agency and CMBS desks price poorly.

Portfolio Loans in Arizona FAQ

What is a portfolio loan in commercial real estate?

A commercial mortgage the lender originates and keeps on its own balance sheet instead of selling. Because the lender holds the risk, it underwrites to its own judgment: mixed-use, partial vacancy, credit stories, and property types agency and CMBS programs decline.

Who makes portfolio loans in Arizona?

Community banks, regional banks, and credit unions active in Phoenix and Tucson, plus national balance sheet lenders following Arizona's growth. We canvass 1,000+ lender relationships to find the ones actually lending on your asset type this quarter.

What are typical terms?

From $1M, fixed 3 to 10 years with 20 to 30 year amortization, 65% to 75% LTV, roughly 125 to 300 basis points over Treasuries, recourse standard, step-down prepayment instead of defeasance.

When is portfolio better than agency or CMBS?

When the deal needs judgment or speed: lease-up, specialty assets, smaller balances, sponsors with a story, or a 30 to 45 day close. Agency and CMBS win on rate for large stabilized assets; portfolio wins on flexibility.
