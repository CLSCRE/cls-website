Student Housing financing

Financing for Purpose-Built Student Housing Near Universities

Student housing loans finance the acquisition, construction, and refinancing of purpose-built student housing communities, off-campus apartment complexes, and mixed-use student housing near major universities and colleges. These assets offer strong occupancy, predictable demand, and parental guarantees that make them attractive to lenders. Capital sources include Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, banks, CMBS, and debt funds with dedicated student housing programs. Minimum loan size is $2 million.

When it fits

When Student Housing financing fits.

Purpose-built student housing (PBSH) has matured into a distinct institutional asset class with dedicated lender programs and investor pools. Proximity to a tier-1 research university with 15,000 or more enrolled students is the primary underwriting criterion, followed by distance from campus, unit configuration (bed-per-bedroom pricing), and amenity package. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac both have student housing loan programs with competitive permanent financing. CMBS conduits are active in PBSH deals near large universities. The asset class benefits from parental guarantors reducing credit risk, annual lease turnover creating mark-to-market rent opportunities, and university enrollment trends that have proven resilient even during economic downturns.

Structure before pricing: leverage, recourse, reserves, prepayment, and closing certainty can matter as much as the headline coupon. We test those trade-offs before recommending a path.

Lender selection

How We Source Student Housing Capital

We do not treat the lender list as a directory. We target the desks whose current appetite fits the asset, sponsorship, market, and business plan, then compare the full execution, not only the rate.

Capital sourceWhere it may fit
Fannie Mae / Freddie MacProgrammatic multifamily or housing capital with defined underwriting, documentation, and eligibility requirements.
CMBS ConduitsNon-recourse execution with broad property appetite, balanced against more standardized servicing and prepayment provisions.
BanksRelationship-oriented capital that can offer local market knowledge and structural flexibility, with recourse often considered.
Debt FundsFlexible capital for transitional, complex, leveraged, or time-sensitive executions where conventional credit boxes may not fit.
Life Insurance CompaniesLong-duration fixed-rate capital for high-quality assets and experienced sponsors, generally with conservative leverage.
