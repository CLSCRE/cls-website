Los Angeles Commercial Real Estate Market Data, Q3 2026

Quick answer: As of Q3 2026, Kidder Mathews (CoStar data) puts Los Angeles direct industrial vacancy at 6.0 percent, direct office vacancy at 16.4 percent, and multifamily vacancy at 5.4 percent with an average cap rate of 5.9 percent. City of Los Angeles rent stabilization allows a 3 percent annual increase through June 30, 2027. Different publishers report different numbers, so every figure below is cited.
By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker (Los Angeles) · Updated November 2026

Lenders underwrite Los Angeles deals off a short list of market numbers. This page collects the ones that matter most for industrial, office and apartment loans, with the source for each, and explains how a lender uses them.

Industrial

Industrial

The direct industrial vacancy rate in Los Angeles was 6.0 percent in the third quarter of 2026, up 30 basis points from 5.7 percent a year earlier. Total vacancy, which adds sublease space, was 6.7 percent and total availability was 8.4 percent (Kidder Mathews, CoStar data).

Submarkets diverge sharply. La Mirada in the Mid-Cities had the highest direct vacancy at 15.0 percent, followed by Whittier at 14.4 percent, while La Puente in the San Gabriel Valley had the lowest at 0.6 percent. That spread is why lenders ask for submarket and building-level leasing data rather than a citywide average. Our LA industrial submarket pages cover the main pools, and industrial and cold storage construction covers ground-up.

Office

Office

The direct office vacancy rate was 16.4 percent in the third quarter of 2026, down slightly from 16.5 percent in the second quarter and up from 15.9 percent a year earlier. Total vacancy, including sublease, was 17.7 percent (Kidder Mathews, CoStar data).

Vacancy at that level, with flight to quality inside the market, keeps most conventional lenders to low leverage and strong sponsors. Capital is most available for conversion and repositioning, see adaptive reuse financing and office conversion loans in Los Angeles.

Multifamily

Multifamily

Multifamily vacancy was 5.4 percent in the third quarter of 2026, down from 5.5 percent in the second quarter and up from 5.2 percent a year earlier. Average asking rent was $2,323 per unit per month, up 0.6 percent year over year. Net absorption was 4,528 units in the quarter and 24,325 units were under construction, down 11.0 percent from a year earlier (Kidder Mathews, CoStar data).

On pricing, the average sales price was $281,576 per unit, down 7.2 percent from a year earlier, and the average cap rate was 5.9 percent, up 40 basis points from 5.5 percent. Higher cap rates and lower per-unit values tighten debt service coverage and debt yield tests, which is why sponsors are leaning on rent-controlled apartment financing structures and neighborhood-level underwriting.

Regulation

Regulation Numbers Lenders Price In

  • Rent Stabilization Ordinance: the annual allowable increase is 3 percent from July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2027. For increases effective on or after February 2, 2026 the formula is 90 percent of average CPI, with a 1 percent floor and 4 percent cap, and no extra percentage for utilities (Los Angeles Housing Department).
  • Measure ULA: a City of Los Angeles transfer tax that adds 4 percent on the middle tier and 5.5 percent on the top tier of sales, with thresholds indexed each year. Full tiers and exit math are in our Measure ULA guide.
  • Development programs: ED1, TOC and adaptive reuse change which construction lenders will quote a site.
Underwriting

What Lenders Do With These Numbers

Data pointWhat a lender typically does with it
Industrial vacancy near 6% with wide submarket spreadUnderwrites rollover and market rent building by building, haircuts near-term expirations, and prices vacant big-box space more cautiously.
Office vacancy in the mid-teens and higher downtownHolds leverage low, requires stronger sponsorship and reserves, and favors conversion or repositioning plans.
Multifamily cap rate near 5.9%Tests debt yield and coverage against current value, which caps proceeds on loans sized to prior-cycle pricing.
Rent Stabilization Ordinance capSizes on the capped rent roll, not market rent, and checks turnover assumptions against vacancy decontrol.

This is how we read the data when we place loans, not a lender's published policy. Every lender differs.

Method

Sources and Method

Figures were retrieved on November 26, 2026 from the Kidder Mathews Los Angeles industrial, office and multifamily market reports (all citing CoStar data) and the Los Angeles Housing Department renter protections page. Other brokerages and data providers publish different numbers for the same quarter because they define the market, direct versus total vacancy, and building classes differently, so we use one source family consistently. Check the original report before relying on a figure in a lender package. Data updates each quarter.
