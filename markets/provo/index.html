Commercial Real Estate Financing in Provo, UT

Provo anchors the Utah Valley and the Silicon Slopes technology corridor extending north to Salt Lake City, home to the largest concentration of software companies between Austin and the Pacific Northwest. Major employers including Adobe, Qualtrics, Ancestry, Vivint, Domo, and Podium have made the metro one of the highest-velocity commercial office markets in the country, while Brigham Young University's 35,000 students anchor retail, multifamily, and student housing submarkets. Population growth consistently ranks among the top three metros nationwide, and Utah's favorable tax and business climate continues to attract relocating technology, venture capital, and private equity firms that drive premium Class A office and amenitized multifamily absorption.

Commercial Real Estate Lending in Provo — FAQ

In Provo and the broader Utah Valley market, we finance a wide range of commercial property types including Class A and B office buildings along the Silicon Slopes corridor, multifamily and student housing assets near BYU, retail centers serving the rapidly growing residential base, industrial and flex properties tied to tech sector logistics needs, and mixed-use developments in downtown Provo and surrounding submarkets. Hospitality, self-storage, and medical office are also active here given the metro's population growth dynamics and healthcare expansion. Whether you own a single-tenant net lease asset or a large apartment complex catering to BYU's 35,000-student population, we have lender relationships built specifically around Utah Valley's property fundamentals.
Commercial loan sizes in Provo typically start around $1 million, though some community banks and credit unions active in Utah County will consider smaller deals in the $500,000 to $750,000 range for strong local sponsors. On the upper end, Provo's tech-driven office and large-scale multifamily projects have attracted life company and CMBS executions well north of $50 million, particularly for stabilized Class A assets with creditworthy tenancy tied to Silicon Slopes employers. The market's strong rent growth and population fundamentals give lenders confidence to underwrite larger loan amounts at competitive leverage levels compared to slower-growth metros.
Closing timelines in Provo depend on asset type and loan structure, but most conventional commercial loans close in 45 to 60 days once a term sheet is signed. Bridge and construction loans tied to the numerous ground-up multifamily and office projects along the Silicon Slopes corridor can move faster with the right lender, sometimes in 30 to 45 days, while SBA 7(a) and 504 loans typically run 60 to 90 days given federal processing requirements. Utah's business-friendly regulatory environment and efficient county-level title and permitting processes generally keep timelines on the shorter end compared to coastal markets.
You do not need to be a Provo or Utah resident to obtain a commercial mortgage on a property located there. Out-of-state investors, including the growing wave of California-based technology companies, venture capital firms, and private equity groups relocating capital to Utah, regularly secure financing on Provo-area commercial assets without any local residency requirement. Lenders will evaluate the strength of the deal based on the property's cash flow, market fundamentals, and the borrower's overall financial profile, not their home state.
Provo's commercial lending market moves fast, and the lender relationships that matter most here, including regional banks like Zions and Glacier, credit unions with deep Utah County roots, and national life companies and debt funds attracted by Silicon Slopes fundamentals, are not equally accessible to all borrowers. As a broker with direct access to these capital sources, we know which lenders are actively pricing Utah Valley multifamily versus which are pulling back on office, and we use that intelligence to structure deals that close at the best available terms. In a market where BYU enrollment cycles, tech sector absorption rates, and population growth all influence underwriting, having a broker who understands those local nuances is a genuine competitive advantage.
