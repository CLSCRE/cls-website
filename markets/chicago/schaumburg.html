Commercial Real Estate Financing in Schaumburg

Schaumburg anchors the northwest suburban corridor along I-90 near O'Hare International Airport, and its commercial base was built on large corporate office campuses, most notably as the longtime North American headquarters for Motorola Solutions and Zurich North America, both of which maintain a significant presence in the village today. That corporate office legacy has produced one of the larger suburban office inventories in the Chicago metro, and like most suburban office markets nationally, Schaumburg has faced meaningful vacancy pressure since 2020 as companies right-sized footprints, creating both a challenge for legacy stabilized-office lending and an opportunity for sponsors pursuing value-add and alternative-use repositioning.

Retail remains a genuine strength: Woodfield Mall is one of the largest shopping malls in the United States by leasable area, and its continued draw supports a dense ring of adjacent big-box, restaurant, and lifestyle retail along Golf Road and Route 53. Industrial and logistics product along the I-90 corridor benefits directly from O'Hare's cargo operations and the broader northwest suburban distribution network, making it one of the more consistently financeable property types in the submarket regardless of the broader office cycle.

Active Property Types and Typical Deal Sizes

Industrial and logistics financing along the I-90 corridor is our most consistent Schaumburg category, typically ranging from $5 million to $35 million for distribution and light-manufacturing facilities benefiting from O'Hare proximity. These deals generally underwrite cleanly given stable e-commerce and logistics demand fundamentals that have held up better than office throughout the northwest suburbs.

Retail financing near Woodfield Mall, both mall-adjacent outparcels and nearby power centers, typically runs $4 million to $25 million. Office repositioning, converting or re-tenanting portions of the corporate campus legacy stock for medical, flex, or multi-tenant use, generally falls in the $8 million to $35 million range and requires lenders comfortable underwriting to a post-repositioning stabilized cash flow rather than legacy single-tenant occupancy. Hotel financing supporting both corporate travel near the office corridor and Woodfield Mall visitors typically runs $6 million to $25 million.

The Lender Ecosystem for Schaumburg Deals

Regional and national banks with northwest suburban branch presence remain highly competitive on industrial and logistics financing, given strong underlying fundamentals and clean collateral. CMBS conduits and life insurance companies compete for stabilized retail near Woodfield Mall, particularly where a national credit tenant anchors the rent roll.

Debt funds and bridge lenders dominate the office repositioning category for the same reason they do in other suburban Chicago office submarkets: conventional banks generally will not underwrite legacy corporate campus product without a de-risked leasing or conversion plan already in motion. Hospitality financing typically flows through regional banks and specialty hotel lenders familiar with the I-90 corridor's demand drivers.

Schaumburg Market Considerations

Cook County's commercial property tax structure applies to the portion of Schaumburg within Cook County, while sections of the broader northwest suburban corridor fall under different county jurisdictions, creating meaningful tax-rate variation across parcels that can look interchangeable at the street level. We verify the exact taxing jurisdiction and build a forward tax projection into every Schaumburg proforma rather than assuming uniform treatment across the submarket.

Office repositioning in Schaumburg, as in Oak Brook and other suburban corporate-campus submarkets, is basis-dependent: sponsors who acquire legacy office product at a low enough price to fund genuine conversion economics are seeing real interest from lenders, while stabilized-office financing at legacy rents remains difficult to place.
