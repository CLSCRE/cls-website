CLS CRE Lender Pulse Report

Q3 2026 CRE Lender Pricing Report

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at Commercial Lending Solutions

Report number: Q3 2026 | Coverage period: July 1, 2026 | Published: July 1, 2026

Commercial Real Estate Lending Conditions | July 2026. The opening of Q3 2026 finds the CRE debt markets in a notably bifurcated posture: life insurance companies and agency lenders are actively deploying at competitive rates for core and core-plus assets, while bridge debt funds are pricing transitional deals at meaningfully tighter spreads than six months ago. The 10-year Treasury has moved in a 25 basis point range since late May, sitting near 4.30 percent as Q3 opens, which has produced slight spread widening at the agency level relative to Q2's compressed levels. Life company allocation windows opened at the start of Q3 with some of the most aggressive industrial and NNN bidding we have seen since 2023. CMBS conduit issuance pace remains strong, keeping spreads on multifamily and industrial execution competitive. The single most consequential development entering Q3 is the renewed life co appetite for medical office and senior housing, two sectors that had been largely shut out of life co programs for over two years. Sponsors with quality assets in those sectors should be approaching life cos directly this quarter.

Section 1

Agency Multifamily (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac)

Fannie Mae DUS Conventional and Freddie Mac Optigo pricing on stabilized multifamily entering Q3 2026 reflects a slight spread widening versus Q2 as rate volatility through June unsettled the Treasury curve. The 10-year Treasury closed Q2 near 4.28 percent after a volatile stretch tied to fiscal debate and Fed communication, and agency lenders have widened spreads modestly to absorb that uncertainty. Q3 opening pricing on 10-year fixed stabilized multifamily is running 5.65 to 6.45 percent across deal size bands, with the SBL and small balance programs at the tighter end for quality assets.

Value-add bridge-to-agency executions remain one of the most active deal structures in the pipeline. Sponsors are using 24 to 36-month bridge loans from debt funds with forward commitments attached from Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, locking the takeout rate before renovation begins. This structure has become nearly standard on multifamily ground-up and heavy value-add where sponsors cannot yet meet agency stabilization requirements. Freddie Mac SBL programs below $7.5 million are particularly competitive for smaller operators, with streamlined underwriting and faster timelines than conventional DUS execution.

Freddie Mac Targeted Affordable Housing (TAH) pricing tightened approximately 15 basis points versus Q2 as TAH allocation windows opened at the start of Q3. Life company and agency competition for TAH deals is meaningful, and sponsors with qualified affordable housing assets should be running both channels simultaneously to drive best execution. The TAH program's cap exclusion benefits have become more valuable as conventional Freddie Mac conventional cap space tightens mid-year.

Broker's Note Seeing more sponsors use forward commitment structures to lock Q3 agency takeout rates before bridge loan closing -- the spread between bridge and perm is wide enough that locking the exit early is worth the forward commitment fee.

Section 2

Life Insurance Companies

Life insurance companies enter Q3 2026 as the most actively deploying institutional lender channel in the market. Q3 allocation windows opened at the start of July, and life cos are competing aggressively for core industrial, long-term NNN credit tenant, and stabilized multifamily in gateway markets. Rate ranges for core deals on industrial and multifamily are 5.20 to 6.10 percent for 10-year fixed at 55 to 65 percent LTV, with best execution reserved for relationship sponsors on trophy assets in Top 20 markets. The competitive intensity between life cos is the highest it has been since Q1 2024.

Medical office and senior housing are seeing genuine renewed life co interest entering Q3 2026, which is a meaningful shift. Several major life co allocators reopened medical office programs in Q2 after two-plus years of selective avoidance, and early Q3 quotes on well-leased medical office buildings in suburban markets are coming in at 5.55 to 6.20 percent for 10 to 15-year terms. Senior housing is more nuanced: life cos are targeting operator-quality senior housing with census above 90 percent and strong trailing EBITDAR coverage, but the credit discipline is real. Sponsors with senior housing at 85 to 90 percent census should still be shopping bridge debt funds first.

Office remains the notable absence from life co programs entering Q3 2026. The handful of life cos that have maintained office programs are limiting appetite to trophy Class A CBD buildings with investment-grade credit tenancy, long WALT, and top-20 market locations. Suburban and Class B office is effectively unavailable from life co lenders regardless of occupancy. Life co allocators are directing capital that would historically have gone to office toward industrial, NNN, and multifamily instead, which is part of why those sectors are seeing such aggressive execution.

Broker's Note The medical office reopening from life cos is real -- seeing quotes come in 40 to 60 basis points inside what the same sponsor would have gotten from a bank or CMBS conduit six months ago.

Section 3

CMBS / Conduit

CMBS conduit markets enter Q3 2026 with healthy issuance momentum. The conduit pipeline from Q2 cleared efficiently and B-piece appetite has remained strong, which is supporting competitive spread execution on quality collateral entering July. Industrial and multifamily CMBS spreads tightened approximately 10 basis points from Q2 levels as the supply-demand dynamic for well-underwritten conduit paper stayed favorable. All-in rates for stabilized industrial and multifamily conduit are running 5.90 to 6.40 percent entering Q3.

Office CMBS continues to be the fault line in the conduit market. Single-borrower CMBS remains executable for institutional-quality office assets with strong occupancy and long WALT -- the single-borrower execution gives conduit lenders control over collateral quality. Conduit pool CMBS is a different story: underwriters are broadly declining commodity office for conduit pools as ratings agencies have tightened subordination requirements on office-heavy pools, making the economics unattractive for conduit shops. Sponsors with Class B office needing conventional financing should be targeting bank balance sheet programs where available rather than CMBS.

NNN credit tenant CMBS continues to be one of the strongest executions in the conduit market. The combination of investment-grade credit, long lease terms, and clean cash flow profiles makes NNN paper attractive to B-piece buyers, and that demand translates into tighter spreads for borrowers. Sponsors with single-tenant NNN assets anchored by investment-grade retail, pharmacy, or quick-service food tenants are finding CMBS pricing competitive with or inside life co alternative rates on comparable LTVs.

Broker's Note On NNN credit tenant deals, running CMBS and life co quotes simultaneously is producing meaningful execution differences -- the winning channel varies by deal, and both should be on the table.

Section 4

Bridge / Debt Funds

Bridge debt fund pricing entering Q3 2026 reflects the tightest market conditions for transitional multifamily and industrial since mid-2024. Spreads on stabilized value-add multifamily are running SOFR plus 275 to 450 basis points depending on LTV and asset quality, with best execution available to 65 percent LTV on non-recourse structures for industrial and high-quality multifamily. The spread compression that characterized Q2 has largely held into early Q3, supported by continued private credit capital looking for deployment in the transitional lending space.

Value-add multifamily and industrial are the two sectors where debt funds are most aggressively competing for deal flow entering Q3 2026. Lenders that were slower to deploy in Q2 are now competing on pricing, structure, and speed to close to win mandates. Non-recourse is now available to 65 percent LTV on well-located industrial assets and value-add multifamily in major markets, a loosening from the 60 percent LTV non-recourse threshold that was standard through most of 2025. Recourse structures are available to 70 to 75 percent LTV for sponsors who will accept recourse during the business plan period.

Construction bridge, specifically speculative ground-up without a forward commitment attached, continues to price 50 to 75 basis points wider than stabilized value-add bridge. Debt funds pricing construction bridge are also requiring more sponsor equity contribution than in prior cycles -- most funds are targeting 25 to 30 percent equity from the sponsor on speculative construction rather than the 20 to 25 percent that was available in 2022 to 2023. This tighter equity requirement is a credit discipline response to construction cost overruns and lease-up delays observed in the 2023 to 2024 vintage construction loan cohort.

Broker's Note The non-recourse threshold moving from 60 to 65 percent LTV on quality assets is the most impactful structural change in bridge pricing this quarter -- it unlocks deals that previously required the sponsor to bring more equity.

Section 5

Construction Lending

Bank construction lending capacity entering Q3 2026 is selectively loosening for multifamily and industrial ground-up. Several regional banks that had paused or pulled back construction programs in 2024 and early 2025 have re-entered the market with refreshed allocations, particularly for multifamily in supply-constrained markets and industrial in logistics-focused submarkets. The re-entry is disciplined: banks are underwriting tighter LTC (generally 60 to 65 percent), requiring completion guarantees, and prioritizing sponsors with established relationships and prior construction track records. New-to-bank construction relationships are difficult to establish without a referral or existing deposit relationship.

Forward commitment usage has remained elevated entering Q3 2026, with approximately 42 percent of multifamily ground-up financings in the pipeline using a Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac forward commitment to lock the agency takeout rate before construction breaks ground. This structure provides sponsors with rate certainty on the permanent financing while construction risk is managed through the bridge or bank construction loan. The forward commitment fee (typically 0.75 to 1.50 percent of the permanent loan amount) is increasingly viewed as essential risk management rather than optional cost.

Build-to-rent construction financing in Q3 2026 is primarily sourced from regional banks and bridge debt funds. Banks are comfortable with BTR where the sponsor has demonstrated BTR operational experience and the submarket vacancy is below 7 percent. Debt funds are willing to go higher LTC (65 to 70 percent) on BTR if the sponsor profile is strong and there is a credible permanent exit. Retail and office construction lending remains essentially unavailable from bank balance sheets; the few deals getting done in those sectors are using debt fund execution at meaningfully higher spreads.

Broker's Note The bank construction re-entry is real but narrow -- seeing banks prioritize multifamily and industrial in strong submarkets and passing on nearly everything else; if your deal is not in those two categories, budget for debt fund pricing.

Section 6

SBA 504 / Small Business

SBA 504 CDC debenture rates entering Q3 2026 are approximately 6.10 to 6.35 percent for 20-year terms based on the underlying 10-year Treasury benchmark and SBA program adjustments. The 20-year debenture rate has moved modestly wider from Q2 levels as Treasury drifted up through late June. The 25-year debenture rate is running approximately 6.20 to 6.45 percent. Owner-occupied commercial real estate volume through SBA 504 remains steady, with manufacturing, professional service firms, and healthcare practices the most active user segments in the CLS CRE pipeline.

SBA 504 continues to deliver one of the most compelling cost-of-capital structures available to small business owners acquiring or refinancing owner-occupied commercial real estate. The fixed-rate debenture portion (typically 40 percent of total project cost) provides long-term rate certainty that bank first mortgages cannot match. Combined SBA 504 blended rates (first mortgage plus debenture) are running 6.50 to 7.20 percent all-in depending on the bank first mortgage pricing, meaningfully inside conventional owner-occupier financing on comparable properties. For qualifying owner-occupants, the down payment advantage (as low as 10 percent) is an additional structural benefit that conventional financing cannot replicate.

Note on program focus: SBA coverage is included in this report for completeness and because a portion of the CLS CRE pipeline includes owner-occupied commercial real estate. Commercial Lending Solutions (CLS CRE) is primarily focused on investor and construction deals -- bridge, DSCR, life company, agency, and construction lending for income-producing properties. SBA 504 and 7(a) are available for qualifying owner-occupants, but sponsors seeking SBA should confirm their deal meets the occupancy and use-of-proceeds requirements before proceeding.

Broker's Note SBA 504 is the correct answer for any qualifying owner-occupant -- the blended rate beats conventional by 100 basis points or more in most cases and the fixed-rate debenture is irreplaceable for long-term cost-of-capital certainty.

Methodology

How This Report Was Produced

This report aggregates pricing data from the CLS CRE pipeline as of July 1, 2026, including lender quotes received across permanent, bridge, and construction lending channels during Q2 2026 and opening Q3 2026 market conditions. Rate ranges reflect current market conditions based on active quote activity. Quotes are aggregated across product types with individual lender identities anonymized; lenders are categorized by source type only (Fannie Mae DUS, Freddie Mac Optigo, life insurance company, CMBS conduit, bridge debt fund, bank balance sheet, etc.). Pricing reflects standard structures with non-conforming structures excluded. The pipeline is national in scope with quotes from approximately 75 to 90 distinct lenders across all major CRE financing channels. Data is collected from active deal quotes, not from public secondary market or index data.

Detailed methodology, data definitions, and sample composition are documented at /research/methodology.html. For deal-specific pricing, contact Commercial Lending Solutions at loans@clscre.com or 310.708.0690.
