CMBS vs Life Company vs Agency Debt: Which Permanent Loan Fits

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at Commercial Lending Solutions

Quick answer: Agency debt from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac is the cheapest high-leverage choice, but only for multifamily and a few related residential property types. For every other stabilized property the choice is CMBS or a life insurance company: life company loans price lowest on high-quality assets with strong sponsors, and CMBS reaches higher leverage and more property types. As of November 24, 2026, the CLS CRE rate table shows life companies at 6.10 to 6.70 percent, agency at 6.16 to 7.11 percent and CMBS at 6.41 to 7.41 percent. Choose by property type first, leverage second, sponsor profile third, and compare exit costs before you compare coupons.

CMBS Wins When

When CMBS Is the Right Call

CMBS wins when leverage, property type or loan size rules out a life company and the property is not multifamily.

  • Office, retail, industrial or hotel property that does not meet the life company box
  • The sponsor needs more leverage than life companies offer
  • The loan is sized below life company minimums
  • The asset has some transition or tenant rollover that a life company will not underwrite
  • The sponsor accepts defeasance and master servicing in exchange for proceeds
Life Insurance Company Wins When

When Life Insurance Company Is the Right Call

A life company wins when the asset and sponsor are strong enough to earn the lowest coupon and the sponsor wants a long fixed term.

  • Stabilized, high-quality property with long leases or strong multifamily fundamentals
  • A sponsor with a track record and moderate leverage needs
  • Borrower wants 15 to 25 years of fixed-rate certainty
  • Direct lender servicing and consent flexibility matter for an actively managed asset
  • Multifamily at lower leverage, where a life company can price inside agency execution
Agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) Wins When

When Agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) Is the Right Call

Agency debt wins on stabilized multifamily when leverage and cost matter most.

  • Multifamily with 5 or more units, or a related residential program such as senior or student housing
  • The sponsor wants leverage up to 80 percent, or up to 85 percent through HUD programs
  • Affordable and workforce programs where Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac offer specialized execution
  • A supplemental loan later is part of the business plan
  • The sponsor wants non-recourse debt that is assumable with lender approval
Decision Framework

How to Choose Between CMBS, a Life Company and Agency Debt

Start with property type. Multifamily gives you all three options. Every other property type is CMBS or a life company, because agency lending is limited to multifamily and a few related programs.

Then look at leverage. Agency debt reaches up to 80 percent, CMBS reaches 60 to 75 percent depending on property type, and life companies usually stop at 55 to 65 percent. If the sponsor needs the proceeds, leverage often decides before price does.

Then look at the asset and the sponsor. Life companies pay for quality with a lower coupon and pass on assets they do not like. CMBS and agency lenders accept more variety and rely on structure (reserves, cash management, DSCR tests) to manage the risk.

Compare exit costs, not just coupons. CMBS usually means lockout and defeasance, life companies usually mean yield maintenance, and agency loans usually mean yield maintenance or a step-down. If you may sell or refinance within five years, the cheaper coupon can lose to the cheaper exit.

A practical shortcut. For multifamily of $2M or more, get an agency quote first and use a life company quote as the price check. For Class A or credit-tenant property above $10M, get a life company quote first and use CMBS as the leverage check. For everything else that is not multifamily, start with CMBS and see whether a life company will do the deal at a lower rate.

Costs people miss. CMBS often brings single-purpose borrower requirements, cash management and servicing fees. Life company rate locks often require a good-faith deposit that can be forfeited if you walk. Agency loans carry lender and agency fees and third-party reports. Ask each lender for a full cost sheet before comparing.

Illustrative Scenario

The Same $25M Apartment Building, Three Quotes

Consider a stabilized apartment building worth $25M. An agency quote at 75 percent LTV is $18.75M at 6.50 percent. A CMBS quote at 70 percent LTV is $17.5M at 6.90 percent. A life company quote at 60 percent LTV is $15.0M at 6.30 percent. On interest alone the life company loan is cheapest at about $945K a year, against about $1.219M for agency and $1.208M for CMBS. But agency delivers $3.75M more proceeds than the life company loan for about $274K more interest, which prices that extra capital at roughly 7.3 percent. If the sponsor needs the capital, agency wins. If the sponsor wants the lowest carry and does not need the proceeds, the life company wins. CMBS rarely wins on stabilized multifamily when agency is available.

This is an illustrative scenario with round numbers, not a specific CLS CRE closing. Actual terms depend on the property, sponsor and structure.

FAQ

CMBS vs Life Company vs Agency Debt FAQ

CMBS loans are pooled and sold to investors, so structure and servicing rules are rigid but leverage and property types are broad. Life company loans are held on the insurer's balance sheet, so pricing is low and servicing is direct but underwriting is selective. Agency loans are made under Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac programs for multifamily and carry the highest leverage and fastest timelines.
As of November 24, 2026, the CLS CRE rate table shows life companies lowest at 6.10 to 6.70 percent, agency at 6.16 to 7.11 percent and CMBS at 6.41 to 7.41 percent. Life companies only lend on the strongest assets and sponsors, so the lowest rate is not available on every deal.
Agency debt is usually best for stabilized multifamily because it combines the highest leverage, competitive pricing and fast timelines. A life company can win at lower leverage and a longer fixed term, and CMBS rarely wins when agency is available.
Agency lenders do not make these loans. Life companies are the low-cost choice for Class A and credit-tenant property with long leases and strong sponsors. CMBS is the usual path for everything else, especially when leverage matters or the asset has transition risk.

CMBS loans usually have a lockout followed by defeasance, which replaces the loan with a portfolio of securities and can be costly when rates fall. Life company loans usually use yield maintenance. Agency loans use yield maintenance or a declining step-down. Compare the cost of exit at your likely sale or refinance date.
