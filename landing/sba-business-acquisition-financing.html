SBA Is the Deal Maker When Conventional Won't Work

Most business acquisitions with commercial real estate fail to close because the buyer can't bring enough equity for a conventional loan. SBA 504 and SBA 7(a) solve this — they are specifically designed for owner-occupied business acquisitions and allow buyers to close with as little as 10% down.

SBA Business Acquisition FAQ

SBA 504 is structured as two loans: a first mortgage from a bank (typically 50% of project cost) and a second mortgage from a Certified Development Company (CDC, typically 40%), with the buyer bringing 10%. The SBA debenture rate on the CDC portion is fixed and typically below conventional rates. SBA 7(a) is a single loan (up to $5M) from an approved SBA lender and can cover real estate, equipment, working capital, and goodwill in one loan — more flexible but with a floating rate option. For deals with significant real estate, 504 usually offers better long-term economics. For deals where goodwill and other intangibles dominate, 7(a) is the typical choice.
Yes. SBA 7(a) can finance a combination of commercial real estate, business goodwill, equipment, inventory, and working capital in a single loan package. SBA 504 is primarily used for the real estate and long-lived equipment portion of a transaction. In many business acquisitions, a 7(a) loan covers all assets plus working capital, while a 504 is used when the real estate is the dominant asset and the buyer wants the fixed-rate SBA debenture on the CRE portion.
With a Preferred Lender and a complete, well-organized loan package, SBA transactions routinely close in 45–60 days. Preferred Lenders have delegated authority from the SBA and do not need to wait for SBA review of individual loan applications — this dramatically reduces the timeline. Non-preferred lenders can take 90–120 days. We submit to Preferred Lenders first and manage the documentation process to keep things moving. If your purchase agreement gives you 60 days, SBA is workable.
Yes. For real estate to qualify for SBA financing, the business must occupy at least 51% of the rentable square footage (for existing buildings) or 60% for new construction. The purpose of SBA CRE lending is to help small businesses own their own space. If the buyer will occupy only a portion and lease the rest, the owner-occupied threshold must be met. We verify this early in the process to ensure the deal structure is SBA-eligible before you invest time in the application.
Most SBA lenders look for a personal credit score of 680+, though some Preferred Lenders will approve borrowers in the 650–680 range with compensating factors. More important than the raw score is the overall credit story: no recent bankruptcies, no open federal tax liens, satisfactory payment history, and a clean criminal background. For business acquisitions, the target business's cash flow history (3 years of tax returns) is the primary underwriting driver — the business must demonstrate sufficient income to service the debt.
