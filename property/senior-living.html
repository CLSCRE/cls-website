Overview

Senior Living & Assisted Living Financing

Senior living underwriting is more complex than conventional multifamily because lenders evaluate operating metrics (occupancy, revenue per occupied unit, staffing ratios, regulatory compliance) in addition to traditional real estate fundamentals. Properties with strong operators, proven track records, and favorable demographic profiles in their trade areas attract the most competitive financing. HUD/FHA 232 programs offer the most aggressive permanent financing terms for stabilized facilities. Commercial Lending Solutions works with banks, HUD lenders, bridge lenders, and debt funds that specialize in healthcare and senior housing, helping owners navigate the unique requirements of this asset class.

FAQ

Senior Living Financing FAQ

Senior living facilities can access HUD/FHA 232 loans (the most competitive permanent option), bank permanent loans, bridge loans, CMBS financing, construction loans, mezzanine capital, and SBA 504 for owner-operated facilities. The best financing option depends on the facility type (IL, AL, MC, SNF), stabilization status, and operator track record.
HUD 232 is a government-insured mortgage program specifically for residential care facilities including assisted living, nursing homes, and board and care homes. It offers the most aggressive terms in senior housing: up to 85% LTV for refinances, 40-year fully amortizing terms, non-recourse, and some of the lowest fixed rates available. The trade-off is a longer closing timeline (4-6 months) and significant regulatory requirements.
Assisted living lenders evaluate occupancy rates, revenue per occupied unit, staffing ratios and labor costs, regulatory compliance history, state licensing status, operator experience, resident acuity mix, local demographic trends (75+ population growth), and competitive supply in the trade area. Strong operators with proven track records and facilities in markets with favorable demographics receive the best terms.

Most permanent lenders require 85-90%+ stabilized occupancy for senior living facilities, though this varies by care type. Independent living may qualify at 85%, while assisted living and memory care typically need 88-90%+ to demonstrate stabilization. Bridge lenders will finance facilities at 60-80% occupancy for lease-up or turnaround situations.
