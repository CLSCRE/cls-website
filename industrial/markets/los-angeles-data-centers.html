Data Centers Financing in Los Angeles

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at CLS CRE. Los Angeles, CA industrial market.

How Data Centers Financing Works in Los Angeles

Data center financing in Los Angeles operates within one of the nation's most constrained and valuable industrial markets, where power-intensive facilities compete for sites across Vernon, City of Commerce, Carson, and the broader LA industrial core. While Los Angeles ranks below Northern Virginia, Dallas, and Silicon Valley in hyperscale deployment, the market's proximity to Hollywood content creation, financial services downtown, and Pacific Rim connectivity creates strong demand for colocation and enterprise facilities. The region's industrial real estate fundamentals, with supply effectively capped by zoning restrictions and port-driven distribution demand, translate into some of the highest per-square-foot land costs nationally for data center development.

Unlike traditional industrial uses that benefit from LA's distribution advantages, data centers concentrate in submarkets with reliable power infrastructure and fiber connectivity rather than port proximity. Vernon and City of Commerce offer the heavy industrial zoning and electrical capacity necessary for hyperscale development, while Carson and Gardena provide secondary options for colocation operators. The market's power grid constraints and seismic considerations create additional site selection complexity compared to other major data center markets, but institutional demand from hyperscalers and colocation operators remains strong given LA's role as a key West Coast connectivity hub.

Lender Appetite and Capital Stack for Los Angeles Data Centers

Life insurance companies with data center specialty desks dominate permanent financing for stabilized LA data center assets, particularly those backed by investment-grade hyperscale tenants on long-term net leases. These lenders view LA industrial real estate favorably given the market's supply constraints and are extremely competitive for assets with credit tenancy structures. CMBS execution remains active for stabilized facilities above $15 million, especially hyperscale-leased assets where tenant credit and lease structure drive underwriting more than traditional industrial metrics.

Construction and development financing typically requires specialty data center debt funds or banks with specific expertise in power-intensive industrial construction. Regional banks compete selectively for smaller colocation projects or enterprise facilities, but the technical complexity and capital intensity of data center development generally necessitates specialty lenders. Portfolio financing through specialty REITs becomes attractive for operators with multiple LA-area facilities or national colocation platforms.

Current market conditions in 2026, with 10-year Treasury around 4.3% and SOFR near 3.6%, support permanent loan pricing in the mid-5% to low-6% range for credit-tenant structures, with life companies offering 75% to 80% LTV on stabilized hyperscale facilities. Construction loans typically price 200 to 300 basis points over SOFR with 70% to 75% loan-to-cost depending on pre-leasing and sponsor strength. Amortization periods extend to 25 or 30 years for investment-grade tenant structures, with yield maintenance or defeasance prepayment protection standard among institutional lenders.

Underwriting Criteria That Matter in Los Angeles

Tenant credit and power commitment agreements drive data center underwriting in Los Angeles more than traditional industrial metrics like rent per square foot or loading dock ratios. Lenders focus intensively on hyperscale tenant creditworthiness, lease term certainty, and the technical specifications of power and cooling infrastructure. Investment-grade tenants like major cloud providers on 10 to 15-year net leases represent the gold standard, while colocation operators require evaluation of their customer diversification and operational track record.

Power infrastructure receives particular scrutiny in LA given grid constraints and the high cost of electrical upgrades. Lenders evaluate committed power capacity, redundancy systems, and cooling efficiency as core underwriting factors, with development costs ranging from $500 to $1,500 per square foot for power and cooling infrastructure depending on tier classification and density requirements. Fiber connectivity and network access points become critical given LA's role in Pacific Rim telecommunications.

Environmental considerations carry heightened importance in LA's industrial submarkets, where legacy contamination and air quality regulations create additional compliance layers. Seismic engineering requirements for data center facilities exceed standard industrial construction, impacting both development costs and lender technical review processes. Zoning compliance becomes complex given the intersection of heavy industrial uses and data center power requirements across different municipal jurisdictions within the LA metro area.

Typical Deal Profile and Timeline

Realistic data center deals in Los Angeles typically range from $25 million to $200 million, with hyperscale build-to-suit projects potentially reaching $500 million or more depending on facility scale and tenant requirements. Sponsors generally need substantial data center development experience or strong institutional backing given the technical complexity and capital intensity involved. Life insurance companies and specialty lenders expect sponsors with successful track records in power-intensive industrial development or proven colocation operation.

Development projects in Vernon or City of Commerce targeting hyperscale tenants represent the upper end of deal flow, with all-in development costs often exceeding $1,000 per square foot when including land, shell construction, and full power infrastructure. Smaller colocation facilities or enterprise self-operated centers in secondary submarkets like Gardena or Industry may pencil at lower per-square-foot costs but still require substantial capital deployment given power and cooling requirements.

Timeline from LOI through closing typically extends 90 to 120 days for permanent financing on stabilized assets, while construction loan processes require 120 to 180 days given technical due diligence requirements and environmental review. Build-to-suit development projects with hyperscale pre-leasing may require 12 to 18 months from site control through construction loan funding, reflecting the complexity of power infrastructure coordination and municipal approval processes across LA's various industrial jurisdictions.

Common Execution Pitfalls Specific to Los Angeles

Power infrastructure coordination represents the most common execution challenge for LA data center projects, where utility capacity constraints and upgrade timelines often exceed initial projections. Sponsors frequently underestimate the complexity and cost of electrical service upgrades, particularly in Vernon and City of Commerce where existing grid capacity may require substantial utility coordination. Lenders have become increasingly sophisticated in evaluating power commitment letters and utility timeline representations given past project delays.

Municipal approval processes across LA's industrial submarkets create execution risks that sponsors from other markets often underestimate. Each jurisdiction maintains different heavy industrial zoning requirements and conditional use permit processes, with City of Commerce and Vernon having distinct regulatory frameworks despite geographic proximity. Environmental review timelines can extend significantly given California's regulatory environment and legacy industrial contamination concerns across many potential data center sites.

Appraisal challenges emerge frequently in LA data center transactions, where limited comparable sales and the specialized nature of power infrastructure create valuation complexity. Traditional industrial appraisers may lack experience with data center facilities, while the high land costs and specialized improvements require careful income approach modeling. Construction cost escalation in LA's constrained industrial market can impact project feasibility between initial underwriting and construction loan conversion.

Tenant credit verification and lease structure documentation require heightened attention in colocation projects, where multiple smaller tenants replace single credit hyperscale lessees. LA's diverse technology and media tenant base creates opportunities but also requires more complex underwriting of customer concentration and lease rollover risk compared to single-tenant hyperscale facilities.
