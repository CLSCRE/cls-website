Multifamily Loan Sizing Calculator

Lenders do not lend what you ask for. They lend the smallest amount that passes three tests: loan to value, debt service coverage and debt yield. Enter your property value and net operating income, pick a lender type, and see which test sets your loan.

Quick answer: A 40 unit building worth $9.5 million with $517,466 of underwritten NOI qualifies for $7.6 million on an 80% agency LTV, but only about $5.38 million on a 1.25x DSCR at 6.64% over 30 years. Coverage, not leverage, sets the loan. The calculator below is preloaded with that example so you can change the inputs and watch the answer move.

How the Calculator Sizes a Loan

  1. Loan to value: property value multiplied by the maximum LTV. Agency lenders go up to 80%, CMBS up to 75%, banks 65% to 75%, and life companies 50% to 65%.
  2. Debt service coverage: net operating income divided by the minimum DSCR, divided by the annual loan constant. The constant is the first year's principal and interest per dollar borrowed, so a 6.50% rate over 30 years is a 7.585% constant.
  3. Debt yield: net operating income divided by the minimum debt yield. CMBS and life company lenders commonly want 9% to 10% or better.
  4. Maximum loan: the smallest of the three. The test that produces it is the one to work on if you need more proceeds.

Multifamily Loan Sizing FAQ

Lenders size a multifamily loan to the smallest result of three tests. Loan to value multiplies the property value by the maximum LTV. Debt service coverage divides net operating income by the minimum DSCR and by the annual loan constant, which comes from the interest rate and amortization. Debt yield divides net operating income by the minimum debt yield. The calculator runs all three and reports the lowest as the maximum loan, along with the test that sets it.
At current rates the coverage test usually binds first. A 1.25x DSCR at a 6.5% rate and 30 year amortization supports roughly 10.5 times net operating income as a loan, while a 75% LTV on a building trading at a 5.5% cap rate supports about 13.6 times. Leverage only binds when rates fall, cap rates rise, or the building is valued conservatively. The multifamily underwriting guide at clscre.com/resources/multifamily-underwriting-guide.html walks through a full example.
Enter underwritten NOI, not the seller's trailing 12 month figure. Lenders rebuild income and expenses with a vacancy floor, a market management fee, reassessed property taxes and replacement reserves, which typically lowers NOI. If you only have the seller's number, run the NOI calculator first, or expect the lender's figure to come in lower than yours.

Your actual rate depends on leverage, property quality, term and the day you lock, so overwrite the rate field with a lender's quote when you have one.
