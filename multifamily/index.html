Underwriting Basics

How Lenders Size a Multifamily Loan

Lenders size to the smallest of three tests. At current rates the coverage test usually binds before the leverage cap, so the loan you receive is often well below the "up to 80 percent LTV" in a program headline.

Test 1

Loan to value (LTV)

Loan amount divided by appraised value. Agency reaches 80 percent, CMBS 75, banks 65 to 75, life companies 50 to 65.

LTV explained →

Test 2

Debt service coverage (DSCR)

NOI divided by annual debt service. Most permanent lenders require 1.25x, and life companies often want 1.35x or more.

DSCR explained →

Test 3

Debt yield

NOI divided by loan amount. CMBS and life companies look for 9 to 10 percent or better, bridge lenders accept 6 to 8 percent going in.

Debt yield explained →

Worked example: a 40 unit building, $9.5 million value

After the lender's adjustments (5 percent vacancy floor, 3 percent management fee, reassessed taxes and reserves) the seller's $638,900 NOI becomes $517,466. A 75 percent LTV test allows $7,125,000, but a 1.25x DSCR at 6.50 percent and 30 year amortization allows only about $5.46 million, which is 57 percent of value. Each $10,000 of NOI is worth about $105,500 of loan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What multifamily financing options does CLS CRE execute?

CLS CRE executes agency multifamily (Fannie Mae DUS, Freddie Mac Optigo including SBL, Target, Green Advantage, TAH), CMBS conduit, life insurance company permanent, bank permanent, value-add bridge financing from debt funds and mortgage REITs, ground-up market-rate construction, build-to-rent (BTR) single-family rental community financing, workforce multifamily programs, HUD 221(d)(4) construction-to-permanent, and HUD 223(f) permanent refinance.

Which lenders compete for multifamily in 2026?

Active multifamily capital sources in 2026 include the GSEs (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac via delegated lenders), CMBS conduits, life insurance companies, money center and regional banks for stabilized and construction, mortgage REITs and specialty debt funds for value-add bridge, and specialty BTR and workforce platforms. The most competitive execution depends on the specific deal profile.

What markets does CLS CRE cover for multifamily?

CLS CRE closes multifamily transactions nationwide across all 50 states, with active deal flow across California (Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego, San Jose, Sacramento), Sunbelt markets (Phoenix, Dallas, Austin, Nashville, Tampa, Charlotte, Atlanta, Miami), Mountain West (Denver, Salt Lake City, Boise), and Northeast (Boston, New York metro).

How does CLS CRE approach a multifamily refinance?

The approach depends on property stabilization, sponsor goals, and cash-out target. We model execution across agency, CMBS, life company, and bridge scenarios, factoring in prepayment flexibility, recourse treatment, proceeds maximization, and rate lock strategy. Comparing proposals across lender types shows the real spread in rate, proceeds, prepayment and recourse that a single-lender approach hides.

Why use a broker on a multifamily deal?

Multifamily financing options vary dramatically between agency, CMBS, life company, and bridge lenders. The same deal can see a meaningful spread in rate, proceeds and structure between the best and second-best execution. A broker maintaining active relationships across all lender types surfaces competitive offers a single-lender approach does not capture, and negotiates prepayment, recourse, and cash-out mechanics in the sponsor's favor.

How do lenders size a multifamily loan?

Lenders size a multifamily loan to the smallest result of three tests: maximum loan to value, minimum debt service coverage ratio (usually 1.25x) and minimum debt yield (typically 9 to 10 percent for CMBS and life companies). At current rates the coverage test usually binds first, so the loan is often well below the stated maximum leverage. See our multifamily underwriting guide for a worked example.
