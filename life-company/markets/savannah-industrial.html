Life Company Industrial Lending in Savannah: Why the Port Changes Everything

Savannah has undergone a structural transformation over the past decade that has permanently repositioned it among the top industrial markets in the United States. The Port of Savannah, operating the largest single container terminal in North America, drives an enormous volume of import and export throughput that demands warehouse, distribution, and logistics infrastructure at every scale. Life insurance companies allocating permanent capital to industrial in 2025 and 2026 are not treating Savannah as a secondary market. They are treating it as a primary target, with dedicated allocation competing for Class A product along the I-16 corridor, in Garden City, Pooler, and the emerging Effingham County and Rincon submarkets that have grown rapidly as port-proximate land becomes constrained closer to the terminal.

The Hyundai Metaplant America facility in Bryan County and its expanding electric vehicle supply chain ecosystem have added a second structural demand driver that was not present even five years ago. Tier-one and tier-two EV component suppliers are absorbing industrial space throughout the Savannah MSA, and life companies understand that this demand is durable and institutionally anchored in a way that speculative industrial in secondary markets is not. Amazon, along with a deep roster of third-party logistics operators serving the port, has further reinforced the tenant quality profile that life companies require. When a capital allocator in Hartford or Omaha is evaluating an industrial loan submission, Savannah now carries the same market credibility as Dallas or the Inland Empire.

That said, life company capital in this market is not indiscriminate. Older, functionally obsolete product in secondary Savannah submarkets without direct port or highway access will not attract life company interest regardless of occupancy. The capital is concentrating in modern bulk distribution and logistics facilities, last-mile assets in Pooler and Richmond Hill, and Class A light industrial parks with strong tenancy along the I-16 and I-95 corridors. Sponsors bringing well-leased, well-located assets to the market in 2025 and 2026 are finding life company execution genuinely competitive, with tight spreads reflecting the asset class and market strength.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Savannah Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for Savannah industrial is disciplined and consistent regardless of market enthusiasm. Leverage is the first screen. Standard life company LTV for stabilized industrial ranges from 55 to 65 percent, with NNN credit-tenant assets capable of reaching 70 percent in select situations. Sponsors accustomed to bank or debt fund leverage at 70 percent or above will need to recalibrate their equity stack before approaching life company capital. The lower leverage is not a weakness of the execution; it is the price of 10 to 25 year fixed-rate money with non-recourse structure and no mark-to-market risk.

Debt service coverage is typically underwritten at a minimum of 1.25x to 1.30x on in-place net operating income, with life companies applying their own expense loads and vacancy assumptions rather than simply accepting the borrower's proforma. For single-tenant NNN deals, the credit quality of the tenant and the remaining lease term relative to the loan term carry significant weight. A 15-year life company loan on a 7-year NNN lease creates term mismatch that most life companies will not accept without meaningful credit enhancement or structural protections.

On rate, the current environment places 10-year Treasury around 4.3 percent as of mid-2025, with life company industrial spreads for stabilized Class A product running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for most qualifying assets. Class A Savannah logistics tied to port-proximate tenancy or credit users can trade at the tight end of that range or potentially inside it, reflecting life company competition for the best deals. Terms of 10, 15, and 25 years are all available, with full-term interest-only periods possible on trophy distribution assets. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule. Non-recourse with standard bad-act carve-outs is the standard structure.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Financing Options in Savannah

Life company capital is not the right execution for every Savannah industrial deal, and understanding where it wins and where it does not is essential to capital stack planning. Life company financing is most competitive for sponsors who want long fixed-rate terms of 10 years or more, prize non-recourse structure, are holding the asset through multiple business cycles, and are financing stabilized properties with in-place cash flow. Class A logistics, credit-tenant NNN, and institutional-quality bulk distribution in Savannah are the clearest use cases.

CMBS can compete on non-recourse structure and sometimes on proceeds, but CMBS carries less structural flexibility, servicer complexity during the loan term, and prepayment mechanics that can be punishing on large payoffs. Bank balance sheet lenders offer recourse floating-rate construction and bridge lending that life companies do not compete with, and banks remain the right call for value-add acquisition or lease-up scenarios where stabilization is still ahead. Debt funds fill the gap for transitional assets, higher-leverage short-term needs, and situations where speed of execution outweighs cost of capital. Agency financing applies to multifamily, not industrial, and is not relevant to this asset class. For stabilized, long-hold Savannah industrial with a quality sponsor and clean lease profile, life company execution through a direct correspondent lender will typically produce the lowest all-in fixed cost of capital available in the market.
