Life Company Industrial Lending in Fort Worth

Fort Worth has emerged as one of the most actively targeted industrial markets for life insurance company permanent capital in the country, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. The city anchors the western half of the DFW metroplex and sits at the intersection of several freight and logistics corridors that life companies have been underwriting with conviction for years. AllianceTexas, the master-planned development surrounding Alliance Airport on Fort Worth's north side, is arguably the single most recognized life company industrial address in the region. Institutional tenants, modern Class A distribution facilities, and a proven submarket track record make AllianceTexas a near-automatic fit for life company underwriting criteria. The I-35W corridor extending north through Haslet, Justin, and Roanoke has absorbed significant industrial development and continues to attract the kind of credit-tenant logistics users that life companies favor for long-term fixed-rate commitments.

Beyond the northern logistics corridor, the Meacham Airport vicinity has become an increasingly active submarket for last-mile and light industrial product, benefiting from its proximity to central Fort Worth and the broader DFW population core. Life companies are selective here, favoring stabilized assets with institutional tenancy over speculative development, but well-leased product near Meacham has found strong life company execution. Across all of these submarkets, the common denominator is what Fort Worth offers that many secondary industrial markets cannot: scale, infrastructure, a demonstrated tenant base of national and global logistics operators, and population growth that underpins the long-term demand story. For a life company deploying 10 to 25 year fixed-rate capital, those are the characteristics that underwrite confidently.

Fort Worth's population growth, driven in part by corporate relocations from California and an expanding manufacturing base, has reinforced life company appetite for the market across asset classes. While this guide focuses on industrial, it is worth noting that life companies are also active on stabilized multifamily in West 7th and Sundance Square and on NNN suburban retail with credit anchors. For industrial sponsors specifically, the current environment in 2025 and 2026 is as favorable as it has been in recent memory. Industrial is among the most preferred asset classes for life company permanent capital deployment, and Fort Worth's logistics infrastructure keeps it near the top of most life company target lists.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Fort Worth Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting industrial in Fort Worth are operating from a consistent framework, and sponsors who understand that framework close faster and at better terms. Leverage is the first filter. Standard LTV for stabilized industrial runs 55 to 65 percent, with credit-tenant NNN assets potentially clearing 70 percent when the lease structure and tenant credit profile support it. Life companies are not stretch-leverage lenders. They are pricing permanence and credit quality, and the leverage parameters reflect that. Sponsors who come in requiring 70 to 75 percent LTV on a multi-tenant light industrial park with near-term rollover are not a life company candidate, full stop.

Debt service coverage is equally important. Life companies typically require 1.25x DSCR at a minimum, with preference for 1.30x or better on assets with any lease term concentration or rollover risk. For Class A logistics facilities with long-term credit tenants in AllianceTexas or along the I-35W corridor, underwriters may work with tighter coverage given the quality of the income stream, but the threshold rarely drops below 1.20x regardless of asset quality. On rate, life company industrial pricing in 2026 is indexed to the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.25 to 4.35 percent range, with life company spreads for most stabilized industrial assets running 125 to 175 basis points over the index. Class A logistics with investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenancy can price toward the tighter end of that range. The resulting all-in fixed rates are competitive with any permanent capital source in the market for the right asset.

Structurally, life companies are offering 10 to 25 year fixed-rate terms, 25 to 30 year amortization or full-term interest-only for trophy distribution product, non-recourse with standard carveouts, and yield maintenance or declining prepayment schedules. The non-recourse structure is a meaningful credit to many sponsors and should be factored into any honest comparison with bank or credit union balance sheet alternatives. Lease term is a critical underwriting input: life companies want remaining lease term that aligns with the loan term or outlasts it, particularly for single-tenant assets. A 10-year loan on a property with three years of remaining lease term is a difficult conversation regardless of the property's physical quality.

Typical Fort Worth Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial deal in Fort Worth looks something like this: a Class A bulk distribution or last-mile logistics facility in AllianceTexas, Haslet, or along the I-35W corridor, built within the last 10 to 15 years, fully leased or substantially leased to one to three tenants with strong credit profiles and remaining lease terms of at least 7 to 10 years. Loan size is typically $10 million and above, with the program accommodating deals up to $100 million for larger logistics assets. Sponsor profile is institutional or experienced private equity, with a track record in industrial and a balance sheet that supports non-recourse carveout obligations. The asset is stabilized, meaning occupancy is at or above 90 percent with in-place leases, not a forward commitment or a value-add play with lease-up risk.

Timeline from executed LOI to closing runs 45 to 75 days when working directly with a correspondent lender. Compare that to the 75 to 120 days or longer that sponsors routinely experience when a deal moves through a broker to a life company's home office and back. The difference is not hypothetical. It reflects the number of decision-making steps in the process. With a direct correspondent, those steps are compressed because they are happening under one roof. Third-party reports (appraisal, environmental, property condition assessment) are ordered immediately upon application and run in parallel with underwriting rather than sequentially behind it.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Fort Worth

Life company financing is not the right tool for every industrial deal in Fort Worth, and being honest about that upfront saves everyone time. For value-add acquisitions with significant lease-up risk, a debt fund or bridge lender is the appropriate execution. Life companies do not underwrite to pro forma occupancy or speculative rent growth. For sponsors who need 70 percent or higher leverage to make their returns work, CMBS or a regional bank balance sheet may be the more practical path, though those structures carry their own tradeoffs in terms of recourse exposure and prepayment flexibility.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is relevant for multifamily in Fort Worth but does not apply to industrial, so that comparison is sector-specific. CMBS is a viable alternative for stabilized industrial when loan size, leverage, or property characteristics push outside life company parameters, but CMBS execution typically comes with higher all-in cost, securitization-related servicing rigidity, and prepayment structures that are less borrower-friendly than yield maintenance on a 15 or 20 year life company loan. Bank balance sheet lenders are active in the Fort Worth industrial market and can offer competitive terms on smaller deals or recourse transactions, but they rarely match life company execution on term length, fixed-rate certainty, or non-recourse structure.

Life company financing wins most decisively when the sponsor has a long hold horizon, the property is stabilized with institutional tenancy, the loan size clears $10 million, and the priority is locked-in cost of capital over the full hold period rather than maximum leverage at close. For a Class A logistics facility in AllianceTexas with a 12-year NNN lease to a national tenant, there is no better permanent capital execution in the market.
