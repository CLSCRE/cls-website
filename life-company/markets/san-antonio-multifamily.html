Life Company Multifamily Lending in San Antonio

San Antonio has emerged as one of the more compelling secondary markets for life insurance company capital in the South Central region, and multifamily is at the center of that story. The city's economic foundation is unusually diversified for a market its size: Joint Base San Antonio anchors a military population that generates consistent, recession-resistant rental demand across multiple submarkets, while USAA, Valero Energy, and a rapidly expanding healthcare corridor along the Medical Center create a private-sector employment base that supports Class A and B rent growth. Life companies underwrite to long hold periods, and San Antonio's demographic trajectory, net in-migration from coastal markets, and population growth pushing into the far North Side and New Braunfels corridor give institutional lenders the long-term confidence they require.

Within multifamily specifically, life companies have been most active on stabilized Class A and B assets in the Pearl District, Stone Oak, North Central, and Medical Center submarkets. The Pearl District in particular has attracted life company attention as it has matured from an adaptive reuse experiment into one of the most supply-constrained infill locations in the city. Stone Oak and the Far North Side offer a different profile: newer vintage garden and mid-rise product with deep suburban demand, strong household incomes, and proximity to employment nodes that life company underwriters can model with confidence. The Medical Center submarket benefits from a near-permanent renter base tied to UT Health, University Health, and the large hospital systems clustered there. Each of these submarkets presents a different risk profile, but all share the characteristic that life companies value most: stable, institutional-quality income with a clear path to long-term hold.

Texas's regulatory environment matters here too. The absence of rent control at any level of government, combined with a landlord-friendly legal framework, gives life company underwriters the policy clarity they require when committing capital on a 15 or 25 year fixed term. San Antonio may not be a gateway market, but for the right asset in the right submarket, it competes favorably for life company allocations against other secondary Texas cities.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Antonio Multifamily Deals

Life companies are not balance sheet lenders chasing yield at the margin. They are long-duration capital sources that match fixed-rate liabilities against fixed-rate mortgage assets, and their underwriting standards reflect that mandate. For multifamily in San Antonio, the standard parameters begin with LTV in the 55 to 65 percent range. Strong sponsors with institutional-quality assets in core submarkets like the Pearl District or Stone Oak can occasionally push toward 65 percent, but life companies in secondary markets generally hold the line here more firmly than agency lenders. This is a feature, not a limitation, for sponsors who are not looking to maximize leverage at the cost of rate.

On the rate side, life company pricing is benchmarked to the 10-year Treasury with a spread that reflects asset quality, market, and term. With the 10-year Treasury trading in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range in early 2026, stabilized San Antonio multifamily is pricing in a spread range of roughly 125 to 175 basis points for most qualifying product, which typically produces all-in rates that land 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency execution. That differential compounds meaningfully over a 15 or 25 year fixed term, and it represents real economics for a long-term holder.

DSCR requirements are typically 1.25x at the in-place note rate for standard transactions, with some life company platforms underwriting to 1.20x for top-tier sponsorship and institutional-quality assets. Occupancy requirements are firm: most life companies want to see a minimum of 90 percent physical occupancy, sustained over a trailing period, before committing. San Antonio assets also need to demonstrate rent levels that are defensible against new supply, which means underwriters are attentive to pipeline construction in submarkets like the Far North Side, where new deliveries have been active. Non-recourse execution is standard, with carve-outs limited to fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy. Interest-only periods of one to three years are available for strong sponsorship and are worth negotiating at the term sheet stage.

Typical San Antonio Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

The life company multifamily deals we close in the San Antonio market tend to share a consistent profile. Asset size typically ranges from $8 million to $40 million, with the program accommodating deals up to $75 million for the right asset and sponsorship. Properties are generally 2010 vintage or newer, Class A or B, with a unit count that supports institutional management. Garden-style communities in Stone Oak or the Far North Side, mid-rise assets in the Pearl District corridor, and Medical Center-adjacent properties serving healthcare professionals are all strong fits. The common thread is stabilized, in-place income at or near market rents with verifiable lease-up history.

Sponsor profiles that life companies favor in this market include experienced operators with a track record of long-term multifamily ownership, clean financial statements, and liquidity sufficient to support the non-recourse carve-out obligations. This is not a value-add execution, and life companies are not the right capital source for a lease-up story. For a sponsor refinancing a stabilized asset into a 15 or 20 year fixed note with a plan to hold, this is the most cost-effective permanent execution available in the market today.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Antonio

For stabilized multifamily in San Antonio, the primary competition for life company capital is agency execution through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac. Agency programs offer higher leverage, generally up to 75 to 80 percent LTV, and are a better fit for sponsors who need more proceeds or are working with a shorter intended hold period. Agency also accommodates supplemental financing and has more flexible prepayment structures for certain deal profiles. If you are a sponsor who expects to sell or refinance within seven to ten years, agency execution is likely more efficient.

Life company financing wins on rate and term when the hold period is long and leverage requirements are moderate. A 55 to 60 percent LTV life company loan at 10 to 30 basis points inside agency, locked for 20 to 25 years with a non-recourse structure and interest-only availability, is a meaningfully better execution for a generational hold or a tax-exempt entity that cannot use agency programs.

CMBS is occasionally competitive on loan size for very large San Antonio transactions, but CMBS structures carry more restrictive cash management and special servicing mechanics that most experienced multifamily sponsors prefer to avoid. Bank balance sheet financing is relevant for floating-rate bridge needs or construction, not for permanent long-term fixed-rate capital. Debt funds operate at higher leverage and higher cost, filling the capital stack gap for value-add and transitional deals that life companies and agency simply will not touch.

If your San Antonio multifamily asset is stabilized, Class A or B, and you are managing a long-term hold strategy, life company correspondent execution is the conversation worth having first.
