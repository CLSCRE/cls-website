Life Company Office Lending in Spokane, WA

Spokane has quietly matured into a credible secondary market for life insurance company capital, though office remains the most selective asset class in the region. The city's economic foundation is more durable than many secondary markets of comparable size: Fairchild Air Force Base anchors a meaningful government and defense contractor employment base, Eastern Washington's agricultural economy provides stability independent of coastal tech cycles, and sustained in-migration from California and the Puget Sound corridor has driven population and household income growth that institutional lenders now take seriously. For office specifically, life companies are most active on Downtown Spokane Class A product with identifiable credit or government tenancy, where lease terms provide the long weighted average lease term underwriters require.

The post-2020 recalibration of life company office underwriting nationally has made Spokane deals both harder and more interesting to structure. Harder, because life companies everywhere have pulled back from speculative or multi-tenanted office with near-term rollover risk. More interesting, because Spokane's office market avoided the catastrophic vacancy spikes that hit Seattle, Portland, and San Francisco, and select Downtown Spokane assets with long-term government or regional credit tenants have continued to attract life company interest at competitive pricing. Medical office and professional service buildings with strong anchor tenants and sub-10 percent vacancy are the most executable deals in this market today.

Spokane Valley and Liberty Lake have emerged as secondary targets for life company office capital, particularly for single-tenant or dual-tenant suburban Class A product leased to regional employers or national credit tenants. The I-90 corridor connecting Spokane to the Idaho state line has drawn institutional attention across asset classes, and well-leased office along that corridor benefits from that broader credibility. Airway Heights, while primarily an industrial and logistics play given Fairchild proximity, occasionally produces single-tenant office deals tied to defense contractors that life companies find compelling for the right sponsorship.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Spokane Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Spokane office in 2026 is disciplined and specific. Loan-to-value expectations have compressed relative to pre-2020 norms: most life companies are underwriting at 50 to 60 percent LTV on stabilized office, with the most aggressive pricing reserved for under-leveraged structures in the 45 to 55 percent range. Borrowers accustomed to bank or CMBS leverage at 65 to 70 percent will find life company execution requires genuine equity commitment, but the rate and structure advantages for long-hold sponsors justify that constraint.

On the rate side, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.30 percent in 2026, life company office spreads for quality stabilized product in a market like Spokane typically run 150 to 200 basis points, producing all-in fixed rates in the mid-to-high 5 percent range for the most competitive deals. Suburban Class A with government or investment-grade credit tenants can tighten into the 125 to 150 basis point spread range. Urban Class A with diversified tenancy and stronger rollover risk is priced wider. Amortization runs 25 to 30 years, terms run 10 to 15 years fixed, and prepayment is yield maintenance standard. These are not flexible provisions: life company balance sheet capital requires long-duration commitments, and borrowers who need exit flexibility should consider other capital sources.

DSCR requirements have tightened alongside LTV. Most life companies are underwriting to minimum debt service coverage of 1.30 to 1.40 times on stabilized in-place cash flow, not pro forma rents. Weighted average lease term matters enormously: deals with a WALT under five years face significant hurdles, while deals with a WALT of seven years or more are underwritten with meaningfully more confidence. Occupancy thresholds are typically 90 percent or better at application. Recourse structure is non-recourse with standard carve-outs, though in the current office environment some life companies are requiring broader carve-out language than was common in prior cycles.

Typical Spokane Life Company Office Deal Profile

A realistic life company office deal in Spokane today looks like this: a Class A suburban or Downtown Spokane office building, 2005 vintage or newer, 85,000 to 200,000 square feet, 92 to 100 percent occupied, anchored by a government agency, regional health system, financial institution, or national credit tenant with a lease term extending at least seven years beyond the loan term. Loan sizes range from $8M to $40M in this market, with deals over $15M commanding the most competitive execution. Sponsors with institutional track records, clean balance sheets, and prior life company relationships are preferred, though private sponsors with strong sponsorship resumes and sufficient liquidity are underwritten on their merits.

Life Company Versus Other Permanent Options in Spokane

Life company financing is not the right answer for every Spokane office deal, and the honest comparison with other capital sources is worth making directly. For value-add office plays with near-term lease-up requirements, life companies are simply not the right execution: a bank balance sheet bridge loan or a debt fund bridge product is the appropriate tool, with life company permanent financing as the take-out once stabilization is achieved. Life companies lend on what is, not what will be.

CMBS is a legitimate alternative for stabilized Spokane office and will frequently underwrite to higher leverage than a life company, which matters for sponsors who need 65 percent or better. The tradeoff is a securitized structure with a special servicer relationship, less prepayment flexibility in the first several years, and execution that can be more volatile in uncertain credit markets. For sponsors with long hold periods and low leverage needs, life company fixed-rate execution typically outperforms CMBS on rate and structure quality. Bank balance sheet lending for office has become increasingly constrained since 2023 as banks manage office concentration limits; competitive bank terms for Spokane office are available but require established lender relationships and typically come with recourse requirements that life company execution does not.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is not applicable to office product and belongs to the multifamily conversation. Debt funds are active in Spokane primarily for transitional and value-add scenarios and price accordingly. For a stabilized, long-leased, low-leverage Spokane office asset with a 10-year-plus hold horizon, life company permanent financing remains the most competitive execution available, and the direct correspondent channel is the most efficient way to access it.
