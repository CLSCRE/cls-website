Life Company Net Lease Lending in Louisville: What the Market Looks Like in 2026

Louisville occupies a distinctive position in the life company net lease universe. The market's logistics infrastructure, anchored by UPS Worldport at Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport, has established the metro as one of the most recognized air cargo and distribution hubs in North America. Life companies understand Louisville's economic foundation well, and that familiarity translates into real capital conviction for the right net lease assets. Suburban retail corridors in St. Matthews, Jeffersontown, and along the I-65 logistics spine attract serious life company interest when the tenant is right, the lease is long, and the basis makes sense.

For single-tenant NNN product specifically, life companies are focused on investment-grade credit names with 10 or more years of remaining lease term. Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and Starbucks locations in the Louisville MSA fit naturally into life company portfolios because the predictable, long-duration cash flows mirror the liability profiles these institutions are managing. Suburban Louisville also benefits from no Kentucky rent control, clean fee simple ownership structures, and an improving retail trade area story in submarkets like Oldham County and the Highlands, all of which life company underwriters read as credit positives.

That said, Louisville is not a market where life companies write blank checks. Standalone fast food or tire shop NNN deals with weaker credit profiles or short remaining lease terms will not clear the bar. Life companies are selective here, and they should be. The deals that work are ones where the tenant, the location, and the lease structure give a long-term institutional lender confidence that the income stream will still be there in year 20. If your Louisville net lease deal checks those boxes, life company capital is likely the most competitive permanent financing available to you.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Louisville Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease in Louisville centers on a predictable set of criteria, and understanding those criteria before you approach a lender saves significant time. Loan-to-value typically lands between 60 and 70 percent, with the upper end of that range reserved for investment-grade tenants on long absolute NNN leases. Expect standard execution closer to 60 to 65 percent for secondary credit or shorter remaining lease terms. Life companies are long-term holders by nature, and their leverage comfort reflects that orientation.

Fixed terms run 10 to 25 years, with the term structured to match or slightly shadow the remaining lease expiration. A 15-year term on a lease with 16 years remaining is a common structure. On investment-grade credit tenants, full-term interest-only is regularly available, which meaningfully improves cash-on-cash returns for sponsors who are holding for the long run. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury around 4.3 percent, life company spreads for well-structured Louisville NNN deals are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points, depending on tenant credit quality, market, and lease structure. That all-in pricing remains meaningfully competitive with CMBS and bank balance sheet alternatives for stabilized, long-lease assets.

DSCR requirements are typically 1.20x to 1.25x minimum, though investment-grade tenants on absolute NNN leases often underwrite with room to spare given the predictability of the income. Non-recourse is standard across the program. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule is the norm, so sponsors planning a near-term disposition need to model prepayment cost carefully. For sponsors with a genuine long hold intention, the structure fits well because the debt and the business plan are aligned from day one.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Louisville

For stabilized, long-hold net lease assets with investment-grade tenants in Louisville, life company financing is typically the most competitive option available. Pricing at 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, full-term interest-only availability, non-recourse structure, and terms matching lease duration give long-term sponsors a debt instrument that fits their hold strategy more precisely than any other capital source.

CMBS can be competitive on pricing for larger loans, but prepayment mechanics, servicer complexity, and defeasance costs at exit create friction that many sponsors underestimate. Bank balance sheet financing offers flexibility on prepayment, but terms rarely extend beyond 10 years and are usually recourse, which is a meaningful constraint for institutional-quality net lease assets. Debt funds are the right call when leverage needs exceed 70 percent or when the hold period is genuinely short, but their pricing premium over life company rates is substantial on a stabilized credit tenant deal where the life company is competitive.

Life company execution is not the right answer when you need 75 percent or higher leverage, a value-add period, or a flexible prepayment window. For those scenarios, a bank, debt fund, or bridge lender fits better. But when the deal is stabilized, the tenant is investment grade, the lease is long, and the sponsor is holding, life company financing is almost always the place to start.
