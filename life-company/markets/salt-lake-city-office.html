Life Company Office Lending in Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City has emerged as one of the most closely watched office markets in the Mountain West, and life insurance companies have taken notice. The Silicon Slopes corridor stretching from Salt Lake CBD south through Draper, Lehi, and into the Provo-Orem metro has produced a concentration of technology, fintech, and professional services employment that is genuinely rare outside of coastal gateway cities. That employment base translates into the kind of creditworthy, long-term tenancy that life company underwriters value above almost anything else. For office sponsors in this market, that is a meaningful structural advantage when approaching permanent financing.

That said, life company office lending nationally has become considerably more selective since 2020, and Salt Lake City is not exempt from that discipline. Life companies remain active here, but they are focused on a specific segment: suburban Class A buildings along the I-15 corridor, campus-style product in the Silicon Slopes submarkets of Lehi and Draper, and urban Class A assets in the Salt Lake CBD with demonstrable rent rolls and limited near-term rollover. Government-tenanted or credit-tenanted buildings in Sandy, South Jordan, and the Point of the Mountain area represent the clearest path to life company execution today. Multi-tenanted suburban product with strong weighted average lease terms and low vacancy is fundable. Commodity mid-rise urban office with near-term lease expirations is not, regardless of market.

Life companies operating in Utah benefit from a regulatory and economic environment that is genuinely competitive. No rent control, a pro-business legislature, strong population in-migration from California and other high-cost states, and a comparatively low cost of occupancy relative to coastal office markets have kept absorption healthier here than in most Sun Belt peers. For sponsors holding well-leased suburban or Silicon Slopes office product, life company capital remains one of the most attractive permanent financing options available in 2026, provided the deal meets the tightened standards the industry has adopted across the board.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Salt Lake City Office Deals

Life company office underwriting in 2026 is disciplined, and sponsors who understand what these lenders require before approaching the market will save significant time and preserve credibility. Loan-to-value is typically constrained to 50 to 60 percent in the current environment, with the most competitive pricing reserved for deals structured at or below 55 percent. Under-leveraged structures are not penalized here; they are rewarded. Life companies are long-duration investors and they price risk accordingly. A borrower who comes in at 50 percent LTV on a Salt Lake City suburban Class A building is having a fundamentally different conversation than one pushing 65 percent.

On rate, the 10-year Treasury has been hovering in the 4.25 to 4.40 percent range in 2026, and life company spreads for stabilized office are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for qualifying product. Suburban Class A with government or investment-grade credit tenants commands the tighter end of that spread. Urban Class A assets with shorter WALTs or more tenancy concentration carry wider spreads, though they remain fundable for the right sponsorship profile. These are fixed-rate instruments, typically structured on 10 to 15 year terms with 25 to 30 year amortization, yield maintenance prepayment, and non-recourse structure subject to standard carve-outs. In the current office environment, underwriters may negotiate broader carve-out language than was typical pre-2020, and sponsors should be prepared for that conversation.

DSCR requirements generally fall in the 1.25x to 1.35x range at the lender's underwritten rate, and life companies will stress test occupancy assumptions carefully on any deal with significant near-term lease rollover. For Salt Lake City office, assets with WALTs of seven years or greater, occupancy above 90 percent, and tenants with demonstrated creditworthiness are the clearest fits. Medical office in the South Jordan and Sandy submarkets has also drawn consistent life company interest, given the healthcare sector's long lease structures and recession-resistant demand characteristics.

Typical Salt Lake City Life Company Office Deal Profile

A representative life company office deal in the Salt Lake City market in 2026 looks something like this: a Class A suburban office building along the I-15 corridor in Draper, Lehi, or South Jordan, built between 2010 and 2020, ranging from $15 million to $50 million in loan size. The property carries 93 percent or greater occupancy with a WALT of eight or more years, a mix of technology and professional services tenants, and a sponsor with a demonstrable track record of holding stabilized commercial assets in the Mountain West. Loan structure is a 12 to 15 year fixed-rate first mortgage at 50 to 55 percent LTV, non-recourse, 25 to 30 year amortization, yield maintenance prepayment.

Medical office in Sandy or South Jordan with health system anchor tenancy can also fit this profile, often with slightly more favorable spread treatment given the long-term lease structures typical of that sector. Government-tenanted office anywhere along the Wasatch Front, particularly buildings with GSA or state agency occupancy, represents the clearest life company execution available in this market today.

Timeline from signed LOI to closing as a direct correspondent lender typically runs 45 to 75 days for a well-prepared deal. For comparison, indirect submissions to life company home offices through brokers without a direct correspondent relationship commonly run 75 to 120 days or longer, with no guarantee the deal that emerges from that process resembles the term sheet that initiated it.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Salt Lake City

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Salt Lake City office deal, and sponsors deserve an honest comparison. Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is the dominant channel for multifamily assets along the Wasatch Front, but it is not available for office. CMBS is available for office in this market and can accommodate higher leverage than life companies will allow in 2026, typically up to 65 to 70 percent LTV for stabilized product. CMBS is worth considering when maximum proceeds are the priority or when the deal does not meet life company tenancy standards. The trade-off is less execution certainty, less structural flexibility, and a secondary market product that performs differently during the hold period.

Bank balance sheet lending from Utah's regional banks and credit unions remains active for smaller office deals, generally below $10 million, with floating rate structures and recourse requirements that do not suit long-term hold strategies. Debt funds can move faster and take more lease-up or transition risk than life companies, but they carry materially higher rates and fees, often 200 to 300 basis points above life company execution for comparable stabilized product.

Life company financing wins decisively for sponsors with the following profile: long intended hold, stabilized asset with creditworthy tenancy, desire for non-recourse fixed-rate debt with a long term, and loan size above $10 million. For Salt Lake City office, the Silicon Slopes suburban market and medical office segments are the most natural fits in 2026. Value-add plays, properties with significant near-term rollover, or deals requiring above 60 percent leverage belong in a different capital channel, and we will tell you that directly rather than waste your time on a process that will not close.
