Life Company Industrial Lending in Detroit

Detroit's industrial market has undergone a structural transformation that life insurance companies are paying close attention to. The EV manufacturing transition driven by Ford, GM, and Stellantis is reshaping the Metro Detroit industrial footprint in ways that extend well beyond the OEM facilities themselves. Tier-one and tier-two EV supply chain manufacturers are absorbing Class A industrial space across Macomb County, Oakland County, and Monroe County at a pace that has repositioned Metro Detroit as one of the more compelling industrial stories in the Midwest. Life companies funding 10- to 25-year permanent mortgages want assets with durable tenancy and defensible locations. The EV supply chain buildout is delivering exactly that, and institutional capital is following.

The I-75 and I-94 corridors remain the backbone of life company industrial interest in the Detroit MSA. Last-mile distribution facilities serving the dense residential population of suburban Oakland and Macomb Counties are attracting significant life company attention, particularly where tenants are investment-grade or credit-adjacent and lease terms align with the desired fixed-rate period. Sterling Heights, Auburn Hills, and the broader I-75 industrial corridor have seen consistent life company placement activity for stabilized bulk warehouse and logistics product. These are not speculative markets chasing cap rate compression -- they are established industrial submarkets with real tenant demand and infrastructure that life companies understand.

Life companies are selective in Detroit, as they are everywhere, but the selectivity here is more about asset quality and lease structure than market skepticism. Urban core industrial carries more scrutiny than suburban logistics. Older vintage flex or light industrial without strong tenancy will not clear life company underwriting standards regardless of how well the rest of the deal pencils. The life company opportunity in Metro Detroit is concentrated in stabilized, well-leased suburban industrial with credit tenants, modern clear heights, and locations that serve genuine supply chain or last-mile functions. Sponsors who understand that filter will find competitive execution in this market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Detroit Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for industrial in Detroit follows the same core discipline applied nationally, with local market overlays that reflect the specific dynamics described above. LTV typically lands between 55 and 65 percent for most stabilized industrial, with credit-tenant NNN structures eligible for up to 70 percent in the right circumstances. These are conservative leverage points by design -- life companies are permanent capital providers, not bridge lenders, and their underwriting reflects a long-duration view of asset performance rather than a repositioning thesis.

Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 25 years are standard, with longer terms most appropriate for credit-tenant NNN or single-tenant distribution facilities with lease structures that support the duration. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury holding in the range of 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Metro Detroit industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the benchmark for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics and distribution facilities with investment-grade tenancy can land at the tighter end of that range. That all-in execution remains competitive against CMBS and bank balance sheet for sponsors with long hold horizons.

DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x and move higher depending on LTV, asset class, and lease structure. Amortization runs 25 to 30 years, though full-term interest-only periods are available for trophy distribution product and strong sponsorship. Non-recourse is standard with customary carve-outs for bad acts and environmental. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which means these loans are designed for sponsors who intend to hold. Borrowers expecting to refinance in five years should look at other options.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. That distinction has real operational meaning that is worth understanding before you choose how to source this execution.

A mortgage broker placing an industrial deal with a life company submits that deal to the life company's home office or correspondent desk, where it enters a queue, gets assigned to an underwriter the sponsor has never met, and works through an approval process that the broker has limited ability to influence or accelerate. The broker is a conduit. The underwriting relationship belongs to the life company, not the broker, and the timeline reflects that structure. That process routinely runs 75 to 120 days or more from term sheet to closing, and it can extend further when deal complexity requires back-and-forth between the broker, the home office, and the borrower.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, commits to, and closes these loans in our own name. We are the lender of record. Our underwriting team reviews the deal internally, structures the term sheet, issues the commitment, and manages the closing process without routing the transaction through a third-party approval chain. There is a single underwriter relationship from first conversation through closing. When a deal needs structuring input -- on lease provisions, sponsor entity structure, reserve requirements, or prepayment mechanics -- that conversation happens directly with the decision-maker, not through an intermediary relaying messages to a home office.

For an EV supply chain industrial deal in Sterling Heights or a bulk warehouse refinance on the I-94 corridor, the practical difference is a timeline that typically runs 45 to 75 days from LOI to closing, versus the 75 to 120-plus days common in the indirect model. In a market where sellers and tenants have expectations about closing certainty, that gap matters.

Typical Detroit Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in Metro Detroit looks something like this: a stabilized Class A bulk warehouse or last-mile distribution facility in Macomb or Oakland County, built within the last 10 to 20 years, with 28- to 36-foot clear heights, adequate truck court depth, and a single tenant or multi-tenant roster with no near-term rollover risk. Loan size typically runs from $10 million to $50 million for most transactions in this market, though the program accommodates deals up to $100 million. Properties below $5 million in loan size are technically eligible but are better suited to bank or SBA execution given life company minimum deal economics.

Occupancy requirements are strict. Life companies want properties that are stabilized at or above 90 percent occupied with leases that run beyond the proposed loan term or at minimum carry renewal options from creditworthy tenants. Spec deals or properties with significant near-term vacancy do not fit this capital source regardless of market location. EV supply chain manufacturing facilities with long-term leases from tier-one automotive suppliers represent a particularly clean life company profile in the current Detroit market.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies prefer experienced industrial operators with clean credit history, demonstrated asset management track record, and entity structures that support non-recourse underwriting. First-time industrial sponsors or complex ownership structures require additional diligence and may not clear life company approval thresholds. Timeline from a complete application package to closing, working directly with Commercial Lending Solutions as the direct correspondent lender, typically runs 45 to 75 days.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Detroit

Life company execution is not always the right answer, and understanding where it wins and where it does not is part of structuring the right deal. For stabilized industrial in Metro Detroit, life company financing is most competitive when the sponsor has a long hold intent (10 years or more), the property is fully leased with credit tenancy, the loan request is above $10 million, and maximum leverage is not required. In those circumstances, the combination of non-recourse structure, long fixed-rate term, and competitive spreads over Treasury is difficult to beat with any other capital source.

CMBS offers a broadly similar structure and can reach slightly higher leverage in some scenarios, but pricing typically runs wider than life company execution for quality industrial, and the post-closing servicer relationship is less flexible on things like lease approvals and property modifications. Bank balance sheet financing is appropriate for shorter hold periods, higher-leverage scenarios, or properties that are not yet fully stabilized -- but bank loans carry recourse, floating-rate exposure (unless swapped), and typically max out at 5- to 7-year terms. Debt funds can move faster and reach higher LTVs but at a meaningful spread premium, making them better suited for transitional or value-add scenarios where life company underwriting would not apply regardless. Agency execution (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) is relevant for multifamily only and does not compete in the industrial space.

For EV supply chain industrial in Auburn Hills or I-94 logistics in Macomb County, where the sponsor has a stabilized asset and a long-term hold, life company correspondent capital is the execution to benchmark everything else against.
