Life Company Net Lease Lending in Des Moines

Des Moines occupies a genuinely unique position in the life company capital markets universe. Principal Financial, EMC Insurance, and other institutional investors are headquartered here, which means life company underwriters have decades of institutional familiarity with this market's fundamentals, its submarket dynamics, and its long-term growth trajectory. That embedded knowledge translates into consistent capital allocation to Des Moines commercial real estate, including NNN retail, across market cycles. For sponsors financing single-tenant net lease assets here, that familiarity matters: life companies are not learning this market on your deal.

On the net lease side specifically, life companies have been active buyers of suburban retail corridors anchored by investment-grade tenants across West Des Moines, Ankeny, Johnston, Urbandale, and Clive. The population growth and household income profiles across these western suburbs support the credit-quality retail tenants that life companies want to own for twenty years. A Dollar General on a ground lease in Ankeny, a Walgreens on a hard corner in West Des Moines, an AutoZone in Johnston sitting on an absolute NNN lease with twelve years remaining: these are assets that align precisely with what life company investment committees are approving. The predictable NOI, long lease duration, and credit strength of the tenant are exactly what a life company needs to match against long-duration liabilities.

Iowa's landlord-friendly statutes and the absence of rent control add a structural layer of underwriting comfort that life company investment committees genuinely factor in. For a lender holding a twenty-year fixed-rate note, state-level legal predictability matters. Des Moines also benefits from its position at the intersection of I-35 and I-80, which supports the logistics and industrial allocations that keep life company loan officers engaged with the local brokerage and ownership community year-round. That ongoing presence means Des Moines NNN retail deals are not outliers in a life company's portfolio: they are a consistent line item.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Des Moines Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting on NNN retail is structured around the lease, the tenant's credit, and the long-term durability of the income stream, in that order. In Des Moines, life companies are actively quoting investment-grade credit tenants with ten or more years of absolute NNN lease term remaining. Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, and similarly rated names command the tightest spreads and the most favorable structure. With the 10-year Treasury hovering around 4.3 percent heading into 2026, life company all-in rates for investment-grade NNN deals in Des Moines are pricing in the mid-to-upper 5 percent range depending on lease term, tenant, and LTV. Spreads for stabilized investment-grade assets are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, with best-execution reserved for the strongest credits and cleanest structures.

LTV expectations are disciplined. Standard underwriting runs 60 to 65 percent for most NNN assets. Investment-grade tenants with long lease terms can push to 70 percent, and in select cases to 75 percent for the strongest credits and longest remaining lease term. Life companies are not leverage players: they are pricing for safety and duration, and the LTV ceiling reflects that. DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x and move toward 1.35x on non-investment-grade or shorter-lease scenarios.

Term structure is a defining feature of life company financing. A twenty-year fixed rate is not unusual on a twenty-year NNN lease, and full-term interest-only is available for investment-grade tenants. That structure, long fixed-rate term with interest-only and non-recourse, is simply not replicable through bank balance sheet or CMBS on any consistent basis. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule, and the loan term is structured to match or trail the lease expiration. Blend-and-extend options for lease rollover management are part of the toolkit when the financing relationship is direct.

Typical Des Moines Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Des Moines market looks something like this: a single-tenant retail asset in the $3M to $15M range, constructed within the last fifteen years, occupied by an investment-grade credit tenant on an absolute NNN lease with twelve to twenty years of remaining term. The property is located in a high-traffic suburban corridor in West Des Moines, Ankeny, Johnston, or Urbandale. The sponsor is an experienced NNN investor or a 1031 exchange buyer with a clean balance sheet and a long-term hold horizon. They are not looking for aggressive leverage. They are looking for fixed-rate certainty, non-recourse structure, and a financing relationship that survives multiple lease cycles.

Loan sizing runs from our $2M minimum up through $50M for larger anchored retail positions. For portfolio applications, a $5M minimum is the practical floor. Occupancy at closing must be 100 percent with no pending co-tenancy issues or kick-out clauses that impair the NOI. Environmental clearance and a current appraisal from an approved MAI appraiser are standard requirements. The sponsor profile does not need to be institutional, but life companies want to see experience with the asset class and a net worth profile that supports the transaction size, even on non-recourse structure.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Des Moines

Life company financing wins decisively in a specific lane: long hold periods, stabilized investment-grade NNN assets, large loan sizes, and sponsors who value rate certainty over leverage. For a $10M Walgreens with eighteen years of lease term remaining, a life company non-recourse interest-only loan at a fixed rate for the full term is almost certainly the best execution in the market. No other permanent capital source combines non-recourse structure, full-term interest-only, twenty-year fixed rate, and institutional pricing at that loan size and asset quality.

Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is the dominant permanent option for multifamily in Des Moines but does not apply to NNN retail. CMBS can finance net lease assets and can push slightly higher leverage in some cases, but CMBS execution is slower, servicer relationships are impersonal, and the secondary market volatility in CMBS spreads creates pricing uncertainty that sophisticated long-term holders generally want to avoid. Bank balance sheet lending is available for NNN retail in Des Moines but typically comes with recourse, shorter fixed-rate periods, and amortization requirements that do not serve the long-duration investor. Debt funds can move faster and can take more risk, but their cost of capital is materially higher and recourse is often part of the structure.

Life company financing is not the right answer when a sponsor needs higher leverage, a shorter fixed period, or flexibility around a lease-up or renovation story. For value-add assets, transitional deals, or anything with lease-term below seven years, a debt fund or bridge lender is the appropriate first call. Life companies are buying long-duration income. If the deal does not deliver that, the execution will be slow and the pricing will not justify the process.
