Life Company Net Lease Lending in Grand Rapids, MI

Grand Rapids has quietly matured into a credible life company market, and the net lease sector is where that story is most legible. The metro's economic foundation is unusually diversified for a mid-sized Midwest city: the global concentration of office furniture manufacturing anchored by Herman Miller, Steelcase, and Haworth drives durable employment across the I-196 and US-131 industrial corridors, while Spectrum Health and a growing Medical Mile have built a healthcare ecosystem that supports stable suburban retail demand. Amway's presence and the broader West Michigan entrepreneurial culture have sustained above-average household formation and consumer spending relative to comparable metros. Life companies evaluating Grand Rapids NNN assets are not looking at a speculative market. They are looking at a regional economy with real anchors and a suburban retail base that has held up against e-commerce headwinds better than many comparable Midwest metros.

The 28th Street corridor in Kentwood is the center of gravity for life company net lease interest in this market. The strip's density of credit-tenanted single-tenant retail, its accessibility, and its positioning within one of Michigan's strongest suburban retail trade areas make it a natural fit for the long-duration, low-volatility capital profile life companies bring. Grandville and Walker have also attracted attention for well-located NNN properties with strong underlying real estate fundamentals. What life companies are not doing is chasing secondary locations or thin-credit tenants in suburban Grand Rapids. The market earns their attention precisely because they can find investment-grade tenants, long lease terms, and absolute NNN structures without having to take on speculative real estate risk. Michigan's absence of statewide rent control is a structural advantage that matters to long-duration lenders, keeping the regulatory picture clean over a 15 or 20 year hold.

That said, Grand Rapids is not a first-call market for every life company. Deal flow is thinner than Chicago or Detroit, which means the assets that do trade need to be clean. Life companies will underwrite carefully here, and they will pass on marginal locations or secondary credit tenants that might pass muster in a larger, higher-volume market. For the right asset, with the right tenant and the right lease structure, Grand Rapids NNN deals can absolutely clear life company credit committees and hold long-term in their portfolios.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Grand Rapids Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for Grand Rapids net lease starts with the tenant, not the real estate. Investment-grade credit is the baseline expectation for the best execution. Tenants like Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, Starbucks, and similar rated credits are the profile life companies have financed for decades because the cash flow is predictable, the lease structure eliminates operating variability, and the long-term income matches their actuarial liability requirements. Operators or regional tenants can still access life company capital in certain cases, but pricing and structure will reflect the added credit risk.

On leverage, life companies in this market are typically comfortable at 60 to 65 percent LTV for standard transactions, with the possibility of reaching 70 to 75 percent for investment-grade tenants carrying long remaining lease terms. The loan is sized primarily on the income stream, and DSCR minimums in the 1.25x to 1.35x range are standard. In a 2026 rate environment with the 10-year Treasury around 4.30 percent, life company spreads for stabilized, credit-tenanted NNN assets are running in the 125 to 175 basis point range, putting all-in fixed rates in territory that remains competitive against CMBS and bank execution for long-term holds. The fixed rate for the full term, whether 10, 15, or 20 years, is the defining value proposition. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, and the term is intentionally matched to the remaining lease to minimize rollover exposure during the loan period. Non-recourse is the standard structure. Interest-only for the full loan term is achievable for investment-grade tenants with strong remaining lease, which meaningfully improves cash-on-cash returns during the hold period.

Typical Grand Rapids Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Grand Rapids market involves a single-tenant NNN property in the $3M to $15M range, though our program accommodates deals up to $50M. The property is typically a 2000s or newer vintage, well-located along the 28th Street corridor in Kentwood, in Grandville near M-6, or in Walker near Alpine Avenue. The tenant is investment-grade with 12 or more years of remaining base lease term, an absolute NNN structure where the tenant absorbs all operating expenses, and a rent escalation schedule built into the lease. The sponsor is an experienced net lease investor or institutional owner with a clean track record, adequate liquidity, and a clear long-term hold strategy.

From a signed term sheet to closing, a direct correspondent life company transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days. That timeline reflects the efficiency of having a single underwriting relationship without the submission and home office review delays inherent in the brokered model. Third-party reports, title, and borrower documentation drive much of that timeline, and sponsors who come prepared with organized due diligence packages consistently hit the lower end of that range.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Financing Options in Grand Rapids

For stabilized, credit-tenanted NNN assets with long lease terms and a long-term hold strategy, life company execution is the most competitive permanent financing structure available in this market. The fixed-rate certainty over 10 to 25 years, non-recourse structure, and full-term interest-only availability for investment-grade tenants are difficult to match. CMBS can offer similar leverage at times, but comes with servicer complexity, transfer restrictions, and prepayment rigidity that experienced operators generally want to avoid on core assets. Bank balance sheet lenders in the Grand Rapids market are active on smaller transactions, but they bring recourse requirements, shorter terms, and refinance risk at the 5 to 7 year mark that life company borrowers specifically want to eliminate. Debt funds compete on leverage and closing speed for transitional or value-add scenarios, but their cost of capital is materially higher than life company pricing on stabilized assets, and the short-term structure creates rollover exposure that contradicts the investment thesis for a long-hold net lease strategy.

Agency execution through Fannie or Freddie is not applicable to single-tenant NNN retail and is the appropriate comparison only for the multifamily segment of the Grand Rapids life company market. Where life company execution is less competitive is in the value-add segment, higher-leverage refinances, and transactions with near-term lease rollover that life companies will price punitively or decline entirely. For those situations, bridge lenders and bank balance sheet are the more appropriate starting point.
