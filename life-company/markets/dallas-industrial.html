Life Company Industrial Lending in Dallas

Dallas has emerged as one of the most aggressively targeted Sun Belt markets for life insurance company permanent capital, and industrial is near the top of the priority list. The structural case is straightforward: no state income tax, sustained corporate in-migration, a pro-development regulatory environment with no rent control exposure, and one of the most active logistics corridors in the country anchored by DFW International Airport. Life companies underwrite to long-term fundamentals, and Dallas industrial checks every box. Vacancy compression across bulk warehouse and last-mile distribution has been sustained, absorption remains deep, and the tenant credit quality across major DFW submarkets supports the kind of long-duration NNN lease structures that life company capital was built for.

The Southern Tier industrial corridor and the DFW Airport submarket are the primary targets for life company permanent capital in this asset class. These are not speculative plays. Life companies are placing 10 to 25 year fixed-rate money against stabilized, fully leased Class A distribution and logistics product in these corridors because the demand drivers are durable and the exit risk at maturity is low. Last-mile facilities servicing the DFW metroplex, bulk distribution centers with credit-rated tenants, and light industrial parks with diversified occupancy in submarkets like Las Colinas and the broader Metroplex periphery all fall within scope. The depth of the Dallas industrial market means life companies can underwrite long-term with confidence, which translates directly into aggressive pricing for qualifying assets.

It is worth being direct about what life companies are not chasing in Dallas industrial: value-add repositioning, lease-up exposure, secondary locations with thin tenant demand, or short WALT profiles that create rollover risk inside the loan term. Life company permanent capital in Dallas is competitive precisely because it is disciplined. Sponsors bringing stabilized product with institutional-quality tenancy and a clear long-hold strategy will find life company execution in Dallas as competitive as anywhere in the country.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Dallas Industrial Deals

LTV tolerance for Dallas life company industrial sits in the 55 to 65 percent range for standard stabilized product. Credit-tenant NNN with long remaining lease terms can push toward 70 percent in the right structure, but the base case is conservative leverage with a strong in-place cash flow story. Life companies are not competing on proceeds. They are competing on rate, term certainty, and structural flexibility for sponsors who do not need maximum leverage.

On rate, the current environment for 2025 and into 2026 reflects a 10-year Treasury hovering in the mid-4 percent range. Life company spreads for stabilized Dallas industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over comparable Treasury for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics and distribution product with credit tenancy can tighten further, making life company execution among the most competitive all-in rate available for long-term industrial financing in this market. Term structures run 10 to 25 years fixed. Full-term interest-only periods are available for trophy distribution product with the right lease and sponsorship profile.

DSCR requirements typically floor at 1.25x on a stressed basis, with life companies preferring to see 1.30x or better at underwritten rents. Non-recourse structure is standard with carve-outs for bad acts and environmental. Amortization schedules run 25 to 30 years where applicable, though the interest-only option for qualifying deals is increasingly a negotiating point life companies are willing to move on in a competitive placement environment. Prepayment is yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which is the key structural consideration for sponsors planning an exit inside the loan term.

Typical Dallas Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The deals that perform best in life company underwriting for Dallas industrial share a consistent profile. Property size generally starts at $10 million in loan proceeds, with the sweet spot in the $15 million to $60 million range. Class A vintage, ideally 2010 or newer, with clear-height clearances appropriate to modern logistics use and dock-door ratios that reflect functional utility for distribution tenants. The Southern Tier and DFW Airport submarket account for the majority of target transactions, though Las Colinas and select Metroplex peripheral locations with documented tenant demand qualify.

Occupancy requirements are strict. Life companies want stabilized assets at or above 90 percent occupancy with remaining lease term sufficient to support the full fixed-rate period or close to it. Credit-tenant NNN is the cleanest execution. Multi-tenant light industrial with strong occupancy history and documented rollover coverage is workable but requires more underwriting support. Sponsor profile matters: life companies want experienced industrial operators with a clean credit history and demonstrated asset management capability.

Timeline from LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction runs approximately 45 to 75 days for qualifying assets. Brokers placing deals through a life company's indirect correspondent channel typically operate on a 75 to 120 day timeline, and that range can extend further depending on home office queue and condition resolution. For Dallas industrial sponsors working against a refi deadline or a value-maximization hold decision, that timing difference is meaningful.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Dallas Industrial

Life company financing is not the right tool for every Dallas industrial deal, and it is worth being honest about where other capital sources win. Agency financing does not apply to industrial. CMBS can match or beat life company proceeds at higher LTV, often 70 to 75 percent, which makes it the better choice for sponsors who need more leverage or are planning a near-term exit where yield maintenance economics on a life company loan are punitive. Bank balance sheet lenders can move faster on smaller deals, offer floating-rate structures, and are more accommodating on partial vacancy or short-term lease roll. Debt funds operate at the high-leverage end and are the right answer for value-add industrial where life company capital will not go.

Life company execution wins decisively when the hold period is long, the asset is stabilized, the tenancy is creditworthy, and the sponsor is optimizing for rate and structural certainty rather than maximum proceeds. For a Dallas industrial sponsor holding a Class A distribution center with a 10-year NNN lease and a long-term hold thesis, life company permanent capital is almost certainly the best all-in execution available in the current market. The non-recourse structure, the fixed rate certainty, and the compressed spreads on quality product make the comparison favorable in a way that does not exist at the value-add or shorter-hold end of the spectrum.
