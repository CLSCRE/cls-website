Life Company Net Lease Lending in Detroit, MI

Detroit's commercial real estate capital markets are in the middle of a genuine structural shift, and life insurance company lenders are paying attention. The region's economic narrative has moved well beyond the automotive sector's traditional cycles. Ford, GM, and Stellantis are committing billions to EV manufacturing retooling across Macomb, Oakland, and Monroe Counties, and that industrial transformation is pulling long-term institutional capital into Metro Detroit in ways that were not present a decade ago. Life companies, which have financed net lease real estate for decades because of its alignment with their long-duration liability structures, see suburban Detroit's credit-tenanted retail corridors as a logical extension of that broader institutional interest in the market.

For net lease specifically, life company appetite in the Detroit MSA concentrates in the suburban ring. Troy, Auburn Hills, Royal Oak, Birmingham, and Sterling Heights host the kind of suburban retail density that life companies underwrite most comfortably: pad sites and strip anchors occupied by investment-grade tenants serving stable residential trade areas. A Walgreens on a hard corner in Troy or a Dollar General on a net lease pad in Sterling Heights reads very differently to a life company underwriter than a value-add urban retail asset in Midtown or Corktown, where redevelopment upside and lease-up risk are part of the story. Life companies are not in the business of betting on upside. They want predictable NOI, creditworthy tenants, and leases long enough to match their hold periods.

That selectivity is worth naming directly. Detroit's urban core, while genuinely improving, remains a more complicated underwriting environment for life company capital in the net lease category. Occupancy volatility, shorter remaining lease terms on some urban retail, and credit-tenant scarcity in certain submarkets make the core a harder fit for this program. Sponsors with suburban NNN assets in the I-75 and I-94 corridor retail nodes, or with ground leases and absolute NNN structures on national credit tenants across Oakland and Macomb Counties, are positioning into life company capital's natural sweet spot.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Detroit Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease in this market is disciplined and consistent. Leverage typically sits between 60 and 70 percent of stabilized value, with room to approach 75 percent for investment-grade tenants with long remaining lease terms. At the base level, sponsors should underwrite to 60 to 65 percent LTV as the working assumption. DSCR requirements generally run 1.25x or better, though investment-grade tenants with full-term interest-only structures can create situations where DSCR coverage at origination is strong enough to support the IO treatment without amortization risk conversations.

Fixed-rate terms range from 10 to 25 years, and life companies structure these loans to match or shadow the remaining lease term wherever possible. A 15-year absolute NNN lease with a Walgreens or AutoZone as the obligor is a template life company deal. The term alignment matters because life companies are liability-matching institutions. A loan that outlasts the lease creates renewal risk they are not paid to absorb. Sponsors with shorter remaining lease terms should have a credible lease extension story or a blend-and-extend conversation underway before approaching this program.

On rate, the 2026 environment has the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.30 percent, and life company spreads for stabilized investment-grade NNN assets are running roughly 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for qualified transactions. That positions all-in fixed rates in a range that is meaningfully tighter than CMBS execution for the same asset and materially more favorable than most bank balance sheet or debt fund permanent pricing on a long-term basis. Non-recourse is standard in this program. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule structured to match lease expiration, which sponsors should factor into exit modeling if disposition before loan maturity is a possibility.

Typical Detroit Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Metro Detroit market looks something like this: a single-tenant NNN property in Troy or Auburn Hills, occupied by an investment-grade retailer with 12 or more years of remaining lease term on an absolute NNN basis. Property vintage is typically 1990s to 2010s, well-maintained, with no deferred capital requirements that would interrupt NOI. Loan size runs between $3 million and $20 million for most suburban Detroit single-tenant transactions, with portfolio aggregations reaching into the $30 to $50 million range.

Sponsors who perform well in this program tend to be experienced NNN investors and 1031 exchange buyers with clean ownership structures, straightforward SPE borrower entities, and no adverse credit history on commercial obligations. First-time buyers with smaller deal experience can qualify if the asset and tenant profile are strong, but life companies are relationship-oriented lenders who value sponsor stability. Timeline from executed LOI to closing runs 45 to 75 days for direct correspondent execution, assuming clean title, no environmental complications, and a responsive appraisal process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Detroit

Life company financing is the most competitive execution available in this market when the hold period is long, the tenant is investment-grade, the loan is stabilized, and the sponsor is comfortable with yield maintenance prepayment protection. No other permanent capital source consistently delivers the combination of non-recourse structure, full-term interest-only for credit tenants, 10 to 25 year fixed rates, and the spread tightness that life companies offer on qualifying net lease assets.

CMBS is the closest alternative for non-recourse permanent net lease debt. It can occasionally price competitively on credit-tenant deals, but CMBS servicing restrictions, cash management requirements, and the complexity of working through a securitized loan over a 10-year hold make life company execution preferable for sponsors who intend to own through the loan term. Bank balance sheet lenders in the Metro Detroit market are active in the $2 million to $8 million range but typically cap terms at 5 to 7 years and require recourse, which does not match the NNN hold model. Debt funds price wider than life companies on permanent stabilized debt and are better positioned for transitional or value-add situations. Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac applies to multifamily and is not relevant for net lease retail.

Where other capital sources win over life company financing: higher leverage requirements above 70 percent LTV, shorter preferred terms under 7 years, value-add business plans with near-term lease rollover, and smaller loans below $2 million where life company minimum deal thresholds create friction.
