Life Company Industrial Lending in Omaha: Why This Market Draws Permanent Capital

Omaha occupies an unusual position in the life company lending universe. Most secondary markets earn cautious allocations from life company investment committees. Omaha earns affinity. The city's identity as the institutional investor capital of the Great Plains, anchored by Berkshire Hathaway, Mutual of Omaha, and a deep bench of insurance and financial services employers, translates directly into lender familiarity and credit comfort. Life company underwriters do not treat Omaha as an unknown market requiring extra due diligence to justify. They treat it as a known quantity with a long track record of stable occupancy, rent growth, and sponsor quality.

For industrial specifically, the I-80 corridor and the Sarpy County logistics submarket are where life company capital concentrates. The Union Pacific intermodal footprint, access to regional distribution networks, and the growth of last-mile and bulk warehouse product along the southwest suburban ring have created exactly the kind of durable, institutional-grade industrial inventory that life companies target. Sarpy County assets in particular, covering Papillion, La Vista, and the broader Bellevue industrial zone, have drawn consistent life company allocations over the past several years. These properties feature modern clear heights, generous truck court depths, strong occupancy from regional and national credit tenants, and long weighted average lease terms. That combination sits squarely in the strike zone for life company permanent capital in 2025 and 2026.

The broader Omaha industrial market benefits from low vacancy, limited speculative development relative to larger Midwest markets, and a tenant base anchored by logistics, e-commerce fulfillment, and corporate distribution users. Life companies financing industrial in Omaha are not chasing a cyclical story. They are buying into a structurally undersupplied, demand-driven market with a long institutional track record. For sponsors holding Class A industrial assets in the I-80 corridor or Sarpy County, life company permanent financing is not just available. It is often the most competitive execution available in the market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Omaha Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for industrial in Omaha follows a consistent framework, but the details matter and sponsors who arrive prepared will move faster and capture better pricing. On leverage, most stabilized industrial assets will clear at 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value. Credit-tenant NNN deals with long remaining lease terms from investment-grade or near-investment-grade occupants can push toward 70 percent. The life company model is not built around maximum leverage. It is built around credit quality, cash flow certainty, and long-term asset durability. Sponsors needing 70 percent or higher leverage on a value-add or lease-up story will not find a match in this program.

Fixed-rate terms range from 10 to 25 years, with 10 and 15-year terms the most common for multi-tenant light industrial and flex. Single-tenant NNN assets leased to credit tenants can support 20 to 25-year fixed terms aligned with the lease. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury trading in the mid-4 percent range, life company spreads for well-leased Omaha industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over Treasury for most stabilized assets. Class A logistics and distribution product with strong tenancy can price at the tighter end of that range. These are among the most competitive fixed-rate spreads available for permanent industrial financing in any capital stack.

Debt service coverage expectations typically sit at 1.25x or higher on underwritten cash flow, with life companies stressing rents and expenses conservatively relative to bank or CMBS underwriting. Non-recourse structure is standard across the platform, with customary carve-outs for fraud, environmental, and waste. Full-term interest-only is available for trophy distribution product with compelling credit tenancy and strong sponsor history. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which matters for sponsors modeling eventual refinance or disposition activity over a long hold period.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital. That phrase carries real structural meaning that is worth unpacking, because it is the single most important differentiator in how we execute these transactions compared to a brokered placement.

A broker placing a life company deal submits the loan package to the life company's home office or correspondent desk, where it enters a queue for review by an underwriter the sponsor and broker have no prior relationship with, on a timeline the broker cannot control. Approval, modification requests, and commitment issuance all run through that third-party approval process. Every round of questions adds days. Every change to loan terms requires a new round of internal review at the lender's home office. The broker is an intermediary with limited ability to accelerate or influence the outcome.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, commits to, and closes these loans in our own name. The underwriting review happens here, with a single underwriter who knows the Omaha market, knows the program parameters, and has the authority to move from term sheet to commitment without routing the file through a home office for unknown-timeline approval. Sponsors are dealing directly with the decision-maker from the first conversation through closing. When a question arises in underwriting, it gets answered in hours, not deferred to a weekly credit committee at a home office three time zones away.

In practical terms, this means we move from term sheet to commitment in a timeframe that indirect borrowers simply cannot access. For sponsors with a closing deadline, a 1031 exchange timeline, or a construction loan maturity creating urgency, that difference is not a minor convenience. It is often the deciding factor in whether a deal closes on schedule.

Typical Omaha Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The life company industrial deals we close in Omaha share a recognizable profile. Loan sizes typically range from $5 million to $50 million, with strong lender interest for transactions at $10 million and above where the fixed cost of life company underwriting is proportionate to deal economics. Properties are generally 2000-vintage or newer, though well-maintained 1990s-era product with significant capital investment can qualify. Clear heights of 24 feet or greater are increasingly standard, with 28 to 32-foot clear preferred for bulk warehouse and distribution users.

Occupancy at closing needs to be stabilized, generally 90 percent or higher for multi-tenant product, with weighted average lease terms supporting the proposed loan term. Single-tenant credit NNN deals can close with shorter stabilized history if the tenant credit and lease structure are compelling. Sponsor profiles that move most efficiently through our underwriting are experienced industrial owners with multiple cycles of operating history, clean balance sheets, and demonstrated familiarity with life company reporting requirements. Timeline from signed LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days. Borrowers who have gone through the brokered life company process previously will recognize that timeline as materially faster than the 75 to 120 or more days common in indirect placements.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Omaha Industrial

Understanding when life company execution wins and when other capital sources are more appropriate is a core part of how we structure advice for Omaha industrial borrowers. Life company financing is the dominant choice when the sponsor is holding long-term, the asset is stabilized, the loan is $5 million or larger, and the priority is the lowest fixed rate with non-recourse structure and long-term payment certainty. For Class A I-80 or Sarpy County logistics with credit tenants, life company execution will typically beat CMBS on spread, beat bank balance sheet on term and non-recourse flexibility, and beat debt funds on cost of capital by a meaningful margin.

CMBS remains competitive for multi-tenant assets where the life company's conservative underwriting results in a materially lower loan proceeds than the sponsor requires. CMBS will generally go higher on leverage and can accommodate more complex lease structures, but pricing will be wider and the secondary market execution introduces its own timing variables. Bank balance sheet financing makes sense for shorter hold periods, assets still in a lease-up phase, or sponsors who need flexible prepayment without yield maintenance exposure. Debt funds cover the value-add and transitional space where life companies do not operate. If a Sarpy County industrial asset is 75 percent leased with a repositioning story, the right answer is a bridge lender until stabilization, followed by a life company permanent takeout.

Life company capital is not the right tool for every Omaha industrial deal. It is the right tool for the deals it is designed for, and in those situations it is difficult to beat on rate, structure, and long-term financing certainty.
