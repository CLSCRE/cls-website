Life Company Industrial Lending in San Diego

San Diego has emerged as one of the higher-priority West Coast markets for life insurance company permanent capital, and industrial product along the I-15 and I-5 corridors is capturing serious attention from correspondent lenders in 2025 and into 2026. The region's industrial fundamentals are anchored by a diversified demand base that few secondary markets can match: military logistics, biotech and pharmaceutical distribution, last-mile e-commerce serving one of the densest coastal populations in the country, and overflow demand from the perpetually constrained Los Angeles basin. Vacancy rates in San Diego's Class A industrial submarkets have remained well below the national average, and rent growth in key nodes has been durable enough to satisfy the long-duration underwriting standards that life companies apply to stabilized assets.

The submarkets that attract the most life company interest are Kearny Mesa, Sorrento Mesa, Miramar, and the northern corridor running through Carlsbad and Vista. These are established industrial nodes with functional infill locations, institutional-quality tenancy, and limited land for new competitive supply. Kearny Mesa in particular benefits from proximity to the I-15 and SR-163 interchange, making it a natural fit for distribution and light manufacturing users who serve both the city core and the broader county. Carlsbad and Vista attract logistics and light industrial users tied to the life sciences supply chain, a demand driver unique to San Diego that translates directly into stronger credit profiles for life company underwriters.

Life companies are not chasing San Diego industrial at any price. They are highly selective on vintage, clear span, dock configuration, and lease structure. A well-located Class A distribution facility with a credit tenant and ten or more years of lease term remaining is exactly the type of asset that generates competitive life company pricing. Older flex or multi-tenant light industrial parks with near-term rollover risk require a different capital source. Understanding where life company capital belongs in the San Diego industrial capital stack is the starting point for any serious financing conversation.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Diego Industrial Deals

Life companies apply a consistent underwriting framework to industrial assets regardless of market, but several factors matter specifically in San Diego. On leverage, the standard range is 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value for stabilized multi-tenant industrial parks with strong in-place occupancy. Credit-tenant NNN properties, particularly single-tenant distribution facilities with investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenancy, can access LTVs approaching 70 percent with the right lease term and location. These are not aggressive numbers by CMBS or bank standards, but they reflect the life company mandate to protect principal over a 10 to 25 year hold period.

On pricing, the 2026 rate environment places 10-year Treasury yields in the range of 4.25 to 4.50 percent, and life company spreads for stabilized San Diego industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury index for most qualified assets. Trophy last-mile distribution and Class A logistics with credit tenancy can achieve tighter spread execution. These all-in rates are meaningfully competitive with CMBS and bank balance sheet financing for the right deal, and the fixed-rate permanence over a full 10 to 25 year term provides a cost-of-capital certainty that floating-rate or shorter-term lenders simply cannot match.

DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x on a 25 to 30 year amortization schedule, with interest-only periods available for top-tier Class A logistics product. Non-recourse structure is standard, with carve-outs for standard bad acts. Life companies also scrutinize lease structures carefully. They want to see weighted average lease terms aligned with the loan term, tenant credit quality that supports the underwritten cash flow, and minimal near-term rollover concentrated in a single tenant. In a market like San Diego where industrial tenants are frequently tied to defense, biotech supply chains, or last-mile logistics, those credit profiles tend to underwrite favorably.

Typical San Diego Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in San Diego looks something like this: a Class A single-tenant distribution facility of 150,000 to 400,000 square feet in Kearny Mesa or Miramar, built within the last 15 years, occupied by a regional or national logistics or life sciences tenant with 8 to 12 years of remaining lease term. Loan size falls in the $10 million to $40 million range. The borrower is an experienced institutional or high-net-worth private sponsor with a track record in industrial CRE, seeking long-term fixed-rate permanent financing to replace a construction loan, an agency bridge, or a maturing bank term loan.

Occupancy at origination is at or near 100 percent with no near-term rollover risk. The property has been operating at stabilized cash flow for at least 12 months, and the sponsor can demonstrate clean title, a current appraisal, and Phase I environmental clearance. Timeline from executed letter of intent to closing through our direct correspondent process runs 45 to 75 days for deals that are properly packaged at submission. Deals submitted through indirect broker channels to a life company home office routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer, with the variance driven primarily by home office queue and handoff delays rather than deal complexity.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Diego

Life company financing is not the right answer for every San Diego industrial deal, and borrowers who understand the landscape make better capital decisions. Agency financing through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac applies only to multifamily, so it is not a competing option for industrial. CMBS is the most direct comparison for large stabilized industrial transactions, and it wins on leverage when a borrower needs 70 to 75 percent LTV and accepts the structural constraints of securitization. CMBS also tolerates shorter remaining lease terms and weaker tenancy profiles that life companies would decline. The tradeoffs are meaningful: CMBS spreads are typically wider than life company execution for the same deal, servicer relationships are less flexible over the loan term, and prepayment through defeasance is more complex than life company yield maintenance.

Bank balance sheet financing is competitive on rate for shorter-term industrial loans in the five to seven year range, and banks will take on lease-up risk or value-add profiles that life companies avoid. But banks cannot match life company execution on 20 to 25 year fixed-rate non-recourse terms, and bank relationships introduce refinance risk at maturity in a way that long-term life company financing eliminates. Debt funds are the right answer for transitional assets, higher leverage needs, or bridge situations pending stabilization. A San Diego industrial park with near-term lease rollover or a value-add repositioning underway belongs with a debt fund first, and a life company last. Once the asset is stabilized and the story is clean, that is the moment to call a direct correspondent lender.
