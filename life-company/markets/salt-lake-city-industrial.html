Life Company Industrial Lending in Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City has emerged as one of the most actively targeted markets for life insurance company permanent capital in the Mountain West, and industrial is at the center of that story. The Wasatch Front's I-15 and I-80 corridors have become critical logistics arteries serving a rapidly growing regional population, a booming e-commerce distribution network, and the manufacturing and supply chain demands of Utah's expanding technology sector. Life companies that spent the last decade concentrating on coastal gateway industrial are now competing hard for stabilized product along these corridors, drawn by Utah's pro-business regulatory environment, no rent control, strong rent growth, and a tenant base that skews toward credit-quality logistics and distribution operators.

The submarkets drawing the most life company attention for industrial are concentrated around the Point of the Mountain, Draper, South Jordan, and the I-15 industrial corridor running south toward American Fork and Lehi. These areas sit at the intersection of Utah's labor supply, highway infrastructure, and the Silicon Slopes technology economy, making them natural targets for last-mile distribution, light industrial parks, and bulk warehouse users. Lehi and American Fork in particular have attracted national logistics tenants who need proximity to the tech employment base. Properties in these submarkets with strong in-place tenancy, modern clear heights, and functional dock configurations are exactly the assets life companies want to hold for 15 to 25 years.

Northern Salt Lake County and West Valley industrial continue to attract life company interest as well, particularly for bulk distribution facilities with credit tenants on long-term NNN leases. Life companies are selective here: they want stabilized, well-leased assets with institutional-quality sponsors, not value-add repositioning plays. But for the right deal, Salt Lake City life company execution on industrial is among the most competitive permanent financing available in the Mountain West today.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Salt Lake City Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting industrial in Salt Lake City are applying conservative but competitive parameters that reflect their long-term hold mentality. Loan-to-value ratios typically land between 55 and 65 percent on stabilized assets, with credit-tenant NNN deals capable of reaching 70 percent given the lease credit and income certainty. These are not leverage-maximizing executions. Life companies are pricing risk based on long-term asset value stability and income durability, not peak-cycle valuations.

Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 25 years, with longer terms common on NNN leases where the lease term aligns with or exceeds the loan term. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury hovering around 4.30 percent, life company spreads on stabilized industrial typically run 125 to 175 basis points over the benchmark, with compressed spreads available for Class A logistics and distribution product with institutional tenancy. That puts all-in fixed rates in a range competitive with or tighter than CMBS on a credit-adjusted basis, with materially better prepayment flexibility on some structures.

DSCR requirements generally start at 1.25x on a debt service coverage basis at the fixed loan payment, with life companies stress-testing to market vacancy and replacement rent to ensure the income hold is durable. Non-recourse structure is standard, with typical carve-outs for fraud, environmental indemnity, and voluntary bankruptcy. Full-term interest-only is available for trophy logistics product with long-term credit tenancy. Utah's strong industrial rent growth and low vacancy along the Wasatch Front have supported valuations that make life company LTV parameters workable for most stabilized acquisitions and refinances in the current market.

Typical Salt Lake City Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in Salt Lake City today looks something like this: a Class A or late-vintage Class B bulk warehouse or light industrial park located in Draper, Lehi, South Jordan, or along the I-15 industrial corridor. The property is stabilized at 95 percent occupancy or better, with in-place leases running five years or longer on at least the anchor tenants. Clear heights of 28 feet and above for bulk product, or 24 feet for light industrial, are preferred. Dock-high configurations and functional yard depth matter for logistics-oriented tenants.

Deal sizes for life company execution typically start at 3 million dollars, with our preference and strongest execution at 10 million dollars and above. Most Salt Lake City life company industrial transactions we see are in the 10 to 50 million dollar range, though single-asset logistics facilities with credit tenants can run considerably higher. Sponsor profile matters: life companies want institutional or experienced private owners with clean credit, a track record in industrial, and liquidity sufficient to support the carve-out obligations. Timeline from a signed term sheet to closing typically runs 45 to 75 days through our direct correspondent process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Salt Lake City

Life company financing is not the right tool for every Salt Lake City industrial deal, and being direct about that is part of how we serve our clients. Life company execution is most competitive when the asset is stabilized, the hold period is long, the loan is large enough to justify the underwriting process, and the sponsor values rate certainty and non-recourse structure over maximum leverage. For a 20-year fixed-rate loan on a credit-tenant NNN distribution facility in Lehi, a life company will typically outperform CMBS on rate, outperform bank balance sheet on term, and provide a cleaner non-recourse structure than either.

CMBS is competitive when leverage requirements exceed life company parameters or when the sponsor needs more flexible underwriting on in-place cash flow. Bank balance sheet lending can win on execution speed and relationship pricing for smaller deals or sponsors with deep banking relationships, but banks are reluctant to offer terms beyond seven to ten years, creating refinance risk on long-hold industrial assets. Debt funds are the right answer for value-add or transitional industrial, where life companies will not lend. Agency financing does not apply to industrial. For stabilized, long-term industrial holds in the 10 million dollar and above range, life company correspondent execution is the benchmark that other capital sources are competing against in Salt Lake City right now.
