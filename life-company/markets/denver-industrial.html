Life Company Industrial Lending in Denver

Denver has emerged as one of the most reliable Mountain West targets for life insurance company permanent capital, and the industrial sector is no exception. The metro's structural fundamentals are compelling from an institutional underwriting standpoint: sustained population growth, a diversified employment base anchored by technology, energy, aerospace, and healthcare sectors, and a landlord-friendly regulatory environment with no rent control. For life company underwriters evaluating long-duration industrial exposure, Denver checks the boxes that matter most at the credit committee level.

Within the industrial sector, the I-70 East logistics corridor has become the focal point for life company capital allocation in Denver. The submarket runs from Commerce City through Aurora and into Brighton, and it has developed into one of the most active last-mile and bulk distribution nodes in the Mountain West. Class A logistics facilities along this corridor, particularly those leased to investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenants, represent exactly the kind of durable cash-flow collateral that life companies are structured to hold for 15 to 25 year terms. Aurora's expanding logistics market draws particular attention, driven by proximity to Denver International Airport and access to I-70, I-270, and E-470.

Light industrial parks with diversified tenancy in the established submarkets of Englewood and Lakewood also attract life company interest, particularly where occupancy is stabilized and weighted average lease terms support long-duration fixed-rate financing. Life companies are selective in Denver industrial, and that selectivity is concentrated on asset quality, lease structure, and sponsorship. They are not the right capital source for value-add industrial plays or vacant buildings being repositioned. But for stabilized Class A product in the right corridor, Denver is as active a life company industrial market as anywhere in the region.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Denver Industrial Deals

Life company capital for industrial in Denver is priced against the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the range of 4.25 to 4.40 percent through the first half of 2025 and is expected to remain in that general range through 2026. Spreads for stabilized Class A logistics and distribution facilities have compressed meaningfully, with well-structured deals in strong corridors pricing in the range of 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury. The result is all-in rates that are among the most competitive in the permanent capital market for industrial assets with strong tenancy.

Loan-to-value ratios for life company industrial in Denver typically land in the 55 to 65 percent range for standard stabilized product. Credit-tenant NNN structures, particularly those with investment-grade tenants and long remaining lease terms, can push toward 70 percent LTV in select cases. Debt service coverage requirements are generally underwritten at 1.30x or better on stabilized in-place income, with more conservative underwriting applied to assets with near-term lease rollover, even if current occupancy is high.

Term structures range from 10 to 25 years fixed-rate. For credit-tenant NNN product with long lease durations, life companies will often extend to the longer end of that range, matching asset cash flow with the liability duration they are trying to satisfy. Full-term interest-only periods are available for trophy distribution product where the cash flow and collateral quality justify it. Prepayment is typically structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which borrowers should underwrite carefully given the long hold periods these loans are designed to support. Non-recourse is standard, with customary bad-boy carve-outs.

Typical Denver Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The deals we close through our direct correspondent life company program in Denver generally share a consistent profile. Transaction sizes run from roughly $10 million to $75 million, with the strongest pricing and execution reserved for deals in the $15 million and above range where life company appetite is deepest. Properties are typically Class A vintage, built in the last 10 to 15 years, with modern clear heights, adequate trailer parking, and ESFR sprinkler systems that institutional tenants require.

Occupancy at application is typically 95 percent or better, with weighted average lease terms of at least five years remaining. Assets with single credit tenants on long-term NNN leases in the I-70 East corridor or the Aurora submarket represent ideal collateral. Multi-tenant light industrial parks in Englewood or Lakewood qualify where in-place rents are stable, tenancy is diversified, and the property is professionally managed.

Sponsor profile matters to life companies in a way it does not always matter to CMBS or debt fund capital. They want experienced industrial operators with a clean credit history, demonstrated asset management capability, and a balance sheet that supports the non-recourse carve-out structure. First-time sponsors or sponsors with concentrated leverage elsewhere in their portfolio may face additional scrutiny or be better served by other capital sources.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Denver Industrial

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Denver industrial deal, and any serious capital markets advisor should tell you where it wins and where it does not. Life company capital is most competitive for long-hold, stabilized industrial assets where the borrower wants maximum rate certainty over a 10 to 25 year fixed term, is comfortable with yield maintenance prepayment, and does not need high leverage. The non-recourse structure, compressed spreads on Class A logistics, and long-term fixed-rate certainty are genuinely difficult to replicate through other capital sources for the right asset.

CMBS offers a non-recourse permanent option with somewhat higher leverage and more flexible prepayment in the form of defeasance, but pricing is generally wider than life company execution for investment-grade quality collateral, and the servicer relationship over a long hold period is a real operational consideration. Bank balance sheet financing is competitive for shorter-term holds or transitional assets, and some Denver banks will go to 65 to 70 percent LTV on industrial, but fixed-rate terms beyond five to seven years are rare and swap structures add complexity. Debt funds fill the gap for value-add, lease-up, or higher-leverage scenarios where life company and CMBS programs simply do not fit, but they price accordingly, often 250 to 400 basis points wider than life company execution.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) is not applicable to industrial. For Denver industrial sponsors evaluating permanent financing options in 2025 and 2026, the honest framework is straightforward: if your asset is stabilized, Class A, in a strong submarket, and you are holding long, life company execution through a direct correspondent lender is very likely your best available pricing. If you need flexibility, higher leverage, or are working through a transition, other sources will serve you better.
