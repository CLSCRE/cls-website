Life Company Self Storage Lending in San Diego

San Diego has earned its place as a high-priority allocation market for life insurance company capital, and self storage is no exception to that calculus. The region's demand drivers are unusually durable: a permanent military tenant base anchored by Naval Air Station North Island, MCAS Miramar, and Camp Pendleton creates consistent household churn that feeds self storage demand across virtually every cycle. Add in a constrained coastal geography that limits new supply, a robust biotech and life sciences corridor from Torrey Pines through Sorrento Valley, and a renter-heavy population profile driven by cost barriers to homeownership, and you have a market where stabilized self storage assets produce the kind of income predictability life companies require.

Submarket selection matters significantly for life company appetite. Climate-controlled facilities in infill urban and suburban corridors, particularly along the I-5 and I-15 corridors touching Kearny Mesa, Sorrento Mesa, Mission Valley, and Carlsbad, attract the strongest institutional interest. These locations benefit from residential density, limited competitive supply pipelines, and the kind of household income profiles that support premium unit pricing. A climate-controlled facility in Kearny Mesa serving the surrounding tech and biotech workforce is a fundamentally different credit than a drive-up suburban facility in a secondary inland market. Life companies underwriting San Diego self storage are drawing that distinction carefully, and so are we.

Life company allocations to self storage have grown substantially over the past decade as institutions recalibrated away from office and regional retail and toward asset classes with demonstrated recession resilience. Self storage performed through 2008 to 2009 and again through 2020 in ways that permanently shifted institutional perception of the sector. In San Diego specifically, where population density and housing costs create structural storage demand, that narrative is especially compelling. Stabilized facilities with strong occupancy histories, climate-controlled inventory, and infill locations are receiving genuine attention from life company capital in 2026.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Diego Self Storage Deals

Life company underwriting for self storage in San Diego begins with occupancy. Stabilized performance at 85 percent or better on an economic basis is the baseline expectation, and lenders want to see that performance sustained across at least two to three years of operating history, not simply achieved in a recent lease-up sprint. Facilities that have held occupancy through different demand environments carry meaningfully more credit weight than those still proving their stabilization.

On the capital structure side, life companies are underwriting San Diego self storage at 55 to 65 percent LTV on stabilized value. That ceiling is not punitive given the long fixed-rate terms on offer, and it reflects the non-recourse structure that is standard for life company first mortgages. Debt service coverage requirements typically run in the 1.25x to 1.35x range on underwritten cash flows, and life companies apply their own cap rate and vacancy assumptions rather than accepting sponsor projections at face value. This conservatism is the tradeoff for the term, structure, and pricing that makes life company execution compelling for the right deal.

In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury hovering near 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized self storage in a market like San Diego are running in the 125 to 175 basis point range, producing all-in rates that remain competitive with or better than CMBS for the same asset class. Fixed-rate terms from 10 to 25 years with 25 to 30 year amortization provide the long duration that institutional owners and long-hold operators are specifically seeking. Prepayment is typically structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, so borrowers should model their exit assumptions carefully. Climate-controlled facilities and institutional-quality assets in infill San Diego submarkets are receiving the most favorable execution within that spread range.

Typical San Diego Life Company Self Storage Deal Profile

A realistic life company self storage transaction in San Diego in the current market looks something like this: a climate-controlled facility in a supply-constrained corridor such as Kearny Mesa, Mission Valley, or Carlsbad, built within the past 15 to 20 years, with stabilized occupancy above 85 percent on a consistent basis. Loan proceeds typically fall in the $3 million to $20 million range for most San Diego facilities, with larger portfolio transactions reaching the $50 million ceiling of the program. The facility should have professional management, clean operating history, and no significant deferred maintenance.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies prefer experienced self storage operators with at least one prior successful institutional financing. Strong net worth and liquidity relative to the loan amount, even in a non-recourse structure, signals the kind of credit quality that produces the best execution. Sponsors who are first-time borrowers with life companies can still qualify, but the underwriting scrutiny on the asset itself and its operating history will be correspondingly higher.

From a signed LOI to closing, direct correspondent transactions in this program typically close in 45 to 75 days for borrowers who deliver complete due diligence packages promptly. The single-underwriter model and our in-house commitment authority drive that timeline. Indirect placements through brokers submitting to a life company home office frequently run 90 to 120 days or more, with approval contingencies that can create real execution risk.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Diego Self Storage

Self storage falls outside the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac product sets, so agency financing is not a relevant comparison for this asset class. The competition for stabilized San Diego self storage debt is primarily CMBS, bank balance sheet loans, and debt funds.

CMBS can match or approach life company leverage at 65 to 70 percent LTV, but the tradeoff is a securitized structure that introduces servicer complexity, cash management requirements, and less flexible prepayment. For sponsors with a defined hold period and clean exit assumptions, CMBS is a legitimate alternative. For sponsors seeking maximum term length, non-recourse certainty, and a direct lender relationship, life company execution wins.

Bank balance sheet loans for self storage in San Diego are typically limited to 5 to 7 year terms, floating or short fixed-rate, with recourse requirements that many institutional sponsors find unacceptable for a permanent hold. They work well for transitional assets or borrowers who want optionality. They are not a substitute for life company financing on a stabilized, long-hold facility.

Debt funds can move quickly and go to higher leverage, but pricing reflects that risk, often 200 to 300 basis points or more above life company rates on a comparable asset. For a borrower who needs value-add proceeds or has occupancy still climbing toward stabilization, a debt fund bridge followed by a life company refinance is a rational two-step strategy. For a stabilized San Diego self storage asset in the hands of an experienced operator, debt fund pricing is a difficult justification against what life company capital offers.
