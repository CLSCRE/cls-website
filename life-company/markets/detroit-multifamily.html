Life Company Multifamily Lending in Detroit

Detroit's multifamily market has undergone a material repricing in life company appetite over the past several years, and the trajectory into 2026 reflects a more selective but meaningfully active deployment environment. Life insurance companies are not blanketing Metro Detroit with capital the way they approach coastal gateway markets, but for the right asset in the right submarket, life company execution is the most competitive permanent financing available. The story here is geographic and demographic: suburban Oakland and Macomb Counties are absorbing displaced urban core demand, benefiting from the EV manufacturing transition reshaping employment across the region, and producing the kind of stable, long-duration cash flow that life companies are built to finance.

The submarkets drawing the most consistent life company interest are concentrated along the northern suburban corridor: Troy, Royal Oak, Birmingham, Auburn Hills, and Ferndale are generating genuine transaction volume for stabilized Class A and B multifamily. These communities offer the income stability, barrier-to-entry land constraints, and creditworthy tenant profiles that life companies underwrite to. Sterling Heights and the broader Macomb County footprint are increasingly relevant as EV supply chain employment at Ford, GM, and Stellantis facilities creates durable household formation demand. Life company underwriters are tracking this employment base closely because it translates directly into rent support and occupancy durability at the property level.

The urban core is a different conversation. Downtown Detroit and Midtown have seen genuine recovery, but life companies remain cautious about concentrating long-duration capital in markets where rent growth is still proving its durability and operating cost structures remain elevated. Suburban multifamily in the northern Oakland County corridor is where life company conviction is strongest for this asset class in 2026. Sponsors with stabilized product in these submarkets have access to a genuine long-term capital option at pricing that agency execution will struggle to match for the right deal structure.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Detroit Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting for Metro Detroit multifamily is disciplined and consistent. The standard LTV range runs 55 to 65 percent for most stabilized assets in primary suburban submarkets, with selectivity on the high end reserved for the strongest sponsors and the cleanest markets. Higher LTV execution is possible but requires premier sponsorship, demonstrable submarket strength, and an occupancy and rent roll that leaves no interpretive room. Most Detroit suburban multifamily deals are sizing at 60 to 63 percent LTV in the current environment.

Debt service coverage is typically underwritten at a minimum of 1.25x, with many life companies requiring 1.30x or better for Midwest secondary markets. With the 10-year Treasury hovering around 4.3 percent entering 2026 and life company spreads for stabilized multifamily running 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, all-in fixed rates are landing in a range that pencils well for long-hold sponsors who are not chasing near-term refinance optionality. Life company rates are consistently running 10 to 30 basis points inside agency for comparable stabilized product, and that spread advantage is real and bankable for the right deal.

Term structures run 10 to 25 years fixed, with 10 and 12-year terms most common for sponsors who want optionality and 15 to 25-year terms favored by institutional holders and family office operators with genuine long-duration hold mandates. Non-recourse execution is standard, with carve-outs for fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy. Interest-only periods are available for strong sponsorship and lower leverage deals, and full-term IO is achievable on select transactions. Prepayment structures are typically declining schedules or yield maintenance, with lockout periods negotiable at origination.

Typical Detroit Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

The deals that close cleanly in the Metro Detroit life company market share a consistent profile. Property size runs from 80 to 300 units, with the program sweet spot between $8 million and $50 million in loan proceeds. Asset vintage is typically 1990 or newer for Class B and 2005 or newer for Class A, though well-maintained older product in core Oakland County submarkets can qualify with strong sponsorship and a documented capital improvement history. Occupancy at underwriting is 93 percent or better for a minimum of 90 days prior to rate lock, with a rent roll that reflects market rents rather than heavy concession exposure.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies in this market want experienced multifamily operators with clean credit, demonstrated asset management history, and a net worth that reflects the loan size. Institutional sponsors, family offices, and experienced private operators with five or more stabilized multifamily assets are the core borrower profile. First-time borrowers or sponsors with concentrated risk in distressed assets will find other capital sources more accommodating.

Geographically, the strongest deal flow is coming from Troy, Royal Oak, Birmingham, and Auburn Hills. Suburban Macomb County is generating increasing interest as EV employment figures firm up. A well-located 150-unit 2010-vintage Class B property in Troy or a newer Class A asset in Royal Oak is exactly what this program is designed to finance.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Detroit

For stabilized multifamily in Metro Detroit, the permanent financing comparison comes down to five options: life company correspondent, agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac), CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds. Each has a legitimate use case, and the right answer is deal-specific.

Agency execution through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac is the primary competitor for this asset class. Agency products offer higher leverage (up to 75 to 80 percent LTV) and strong pricing, but life company rates run 10 to 30 basis points tighter for comparable stabilized product at 60 to 65 percent LTV. For sponsors who do not need the leverage, the rate savings from life company execution are real, and the non-recourse structure and long fixed-term options are comparable. Agency is the better answer when a sponsor needs leverage above 65 percent or is financing affordable housing with LIHTC equity.

CMBS offers flexibility on asset type and geography, but floating-rate CMBS exposure and special servicer risk on modifications make it a weaker fit for long-hold stabilized strategies. Bank balance sheet financing is appropriate for shorter hold periods, transitional assets, or deals that need construction-to-permanent flexibility, but bank rates and recourse requirements make it structurally misaligned with the long-duration, non-recourse execution that life companies offer.

Debt funds fill the bridge and value-add lane. If a Detroit multifamily asset has occupancy below 90 percent, near-term lease-up risk, significant deferred maintenance, or a renovation program underway, life company capital is not the right fit. A debt fund bridge loan followed by a life company takeout is a logical sequence for sponsors who are repositioning assets in submarkets like Ferndale or Sterling Heights where the demand fundamentals are proven but the asset needs a cycle to stabilize.

Life company execution wins for stabilized assets, long hold periods, large loan sizes, and sponsors who value rate certainty over leverage. It is the most competitive permanent option available in this market for the deals it fits.
