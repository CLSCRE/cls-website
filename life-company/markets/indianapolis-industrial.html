Life Company Industrial Lending in Indianapolis

Indianapolis has quietly become one of the most sought-after industrial markets for life insurance company permanent capital in the country. The city's geographic position at the intersection of I-65, I-70, I-74, and I-69 creates a logistics hub that serves roughly 75 percent of the U.S. population within a two-day truck drive, and life companies have taken notice. That fundamental demand driver, combined with strong institutional tenant demand and disciplined new supply relative to coastal markets, makes Indianapolis industrial a genuine core allocation for life company portfolios heading into 2025 and 2026.

Life company allocations in Indianapolis have concentrated in two primary industrial corridors. The Whitestown and Zionsville submarket along the I-65 north corridor has drawn significant life company capital, particularly for Class A bulk distribution and logistics facilities with institutional-grade tenancy. The Plainfield and Avon submarket on the I-70 west corridor has similarly attracted permanent capital for large-bay warehouse and last-mile distribution product. Life companies have also been active in the Greenfield and I-70 east corridor as e-commerce distribution demand has pushed further out from the urban core. Secondary submarkets including light industrial parks in Carmel and Fishers have attracted life company attention where occupancy and lease structures support long-term hold underwriting.

The appeal of Indianapolis for life company capital is not simply geographic. Rental growth in the Indianapolis industrial market has been supported by a structurally low vacancy environment compared to oversupplied Sun Belt markets, and cap rate spreads over Treasuries remain wider than gateway markets, giving life companies a yield advantage without proportionally higher credit risk. For sponsors holding stabilized, well-leased industrial assets in Indianapolis, the current environment represents a favorable window for life company permanent financing at competitive fixed-rate terms.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Indianapolis Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting Indianapolis industrial deals in 2025 and 2026 are applying consistent and fairly disciplined criteria. Loan-to-value requirements typically run 55 to 65 percent for stabilized multi-tenant and single-tenant industrial assets, with credit-tenant NNN structures capable of supporting up to 70 percent LTV depending on the lease credit and remaining term. Life companies are not stretch-leverage lenders by design, and Indianapolis deals that arrive at the desk with equity-heavy capital stacks consistently win better pricing and structure.

Debt service coverage is typically underwritten at a minimum 1.25x, with stronger covenants (1.30x to 1.35x) for shorter remaining lease terms or multi-tenant occupancy risk. Life companies are underwriting to long-term stabilized cash flow, not to mark-to-market rent assumptions, which means proforma rents need to be supportable by current market evidence in the specific Indianapolis submarket. A Class A logistics building in Plainfield leased to a credit-grade tenant on a 10-year NNN lease will attract materially better execution than a light industrial park with near-term rollover risk, even if current occupancy is identical.

On rate structure, life companies are pricing most stabilized Indianapolis industrial deals at spreads of roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, which has traded near 4.3 percent through much of 2025. Class A logistics and bulk distribution with credit tenants can achieve tighter execution within that range. Terms run from 10 to 25 years fixed, with full-term interest-only available for trophy-quality distribution product with long-term institutional tenancy. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule, and non-recourse is standard with conventional carve-outs for fraud, environmental, and bankruptcy-related events.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Indianapolis

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Indianapolis industrial deal, and it is worth being direct about when it wins and when it does not. Life company capital is most competitive for stabilized assets with long-term, creditworthy tenancy, sponsors with long hold horizons, and loan sizes above $10 million where the pricing advantage over bank balance sheet and CMBS becomes meaningful. Non-recourse structure, full-term fixed rates, and competitive spreads make life company execution particularly well-suited to credit-tenant NNN deals, institutional-grade logistics facilities, and refinances where the sponsor is not planning a near-term disposition.

CMBS can compete on leverage and is more tolerant of shorter lease terms, but comes with securitization complexity, servicer risk, and prepayment structures that many sponsors find restrictive. Bank balance sheet lenders in Indianapolis offer flexibility and relationship pricing, but floating-rate exposure and recourse requirements are meaningful disadvantages for long-hold industrial assets. Debt funds provide bridge capital and higher leverage for value-add industrial, which life companies will not touch. If a Whitestown industrial park needs lease-up capital before permanent financing, a debt fund or bank bridge loan is the right first step, with a life company refinance as the exit. The strategies are complementary rather than competitive in that context.

For stabilized, well-leased Indianapolis industrial assets, the life company execution through a direct correspondent lender is consistently the most competitive permanent financing available in 2025 and 2026.
