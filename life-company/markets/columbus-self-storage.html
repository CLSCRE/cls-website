Life Company Self Storage Lending in Columbus, OH

Columbus has quietly become one of the more compelling Midwestern markets for life insurance company capital, and self storage is sharing in that attention. The city's economic foundation has shifted meaningfully over the past decade. Ohio State University anchors a consistent demand base across renter and consumer categories, but the Intel New Albany semiconductor mega-campus is the story that institutional lenders are tracking most closely. Intel's $20 billion-plus commitment to central Ohio has triggered a wave of semiconductor supply chain investment across the New Albany and I-270 corridors, drawing a new class of workforce and corporate relocations that directly support self storage demand in suburban submarkets. Life companies entering Columbus self storage deals today are underwriting a market with genuine population and income growth tailwinds, not just cyclical recovery.

From a submarket perspective, life company appetite concentrates around infill and suburban infill locations with demonstrated barriers to new supply. Facilities near Westerville, Dublin, and the Easton corridor have attracted institutional attention because land constraints and zoning complexity limit competitive new development. The Short North and Arena District generate demand from urban renters and small businesses, though site availability for self storage in those cores is limited. New Albany, driven directly by Intel-related workforce growth, is an emerging submarket where well-located, climate-controlled facilities are beginning to clear the stabilization threshold life companies require. For sponsors holding institutional-quality assets in these submarkets, Columbus now offers a genuine path to life company permanent financing at competitive fixed-rate terms.

It is worth noting that life companies remain selective. Columbus benefits from favorable fundamentals, but institutions are not chasing every self storage deal in the metro. Climate-controlled facilities with strong occupancy histories, clean lease-up curves, and locations in supply-constrained corridors are the assets drawing term sheets. Older non-climate-controlled facilities in secondary submarkets or assets still working through lease-up will face a narrower window with life company capital, regardless of market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Columbus Self Storage Deals

Life insurance companies approach self storage underwriting with discipline that reflects their liability-matching mandate. In Columbus specifically, lenders are targeting stabilized facilities with occupancy at or above 85 percent on a sustained basis, and the emphasis on "sustained" matters. A facility that cleared 85 percent occupancy six months ago will receive more scrutiny than one with a two-year track record at that threshold or above. Climate-controlled product is strongly preferred, and urban or suburban infill positioning is effectively a prerequisite for the most competitive pricing.

On the capital structure side, life companies are generally sizing to 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value for self storage, with DSCR requirements typically in the 1.30x to 1.40x range depending on sponsorship and asset quality. Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 20 years, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules. With the 10-year Treasury trading near 4.30 percent in the current 2026 rate environment, life company spreads for stabilized self storage are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the relevant Treasury benchmark, placing all-in rates in a competitive band relative to CMBS and meaningfully below floating-rate debt fund capital. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, reflecting the long-duration nature of the liability match. Non-recourse is standard for qualified sponsors and stabilized collateral. For Columbus deals specifically, lenders are paying close attention to submarket supply pipelines, given that development activity in some corridors has accelerated alongside Intel-related growth.

Typical Columbus Life Company Self Storage Deal Profile

A representative life company self storage transaction in Columbus looks something like this: a climate-controlled facility of 80,000 to 150,000 net rentable square feet, positioned in a suburban infill submarket such as Westerville, Dublin, or the New Albany corridor. The facility is stabilized at 88 to 93 percent occupancy with at least 18 to 24 months of operating history at that level. The vintage is 2010 or newer, with modern unit mix, digital access, and professional management in place. Loan size typically falls in the $5 million to $20 million range for this market, though our program accommodates deals up to $50 million for larger portfolios or multi-asset Columbus-area transactions.

Sponsor profile matters to life company lenders. First-time self storage borrowers or sponsors with complex organizational structures will face additional scrutiny, though strong collateral can offset some sponsor risk. Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Columbus Self Storage

Life company financing is not the right answer for every Columbus self storage deal, and understanding when it wins and when it does not will save a sponsor considerable time. Life company execution is most competitive for stabilized, long-hold assets where a borrower wants the certainty of a fixed rate locked for 15 to 20 years, non-recourse structure, and the lowest all-in cost of capital available for that risk profile. Sponsors planning to hold a facility for a full market cycle and who do not need high leverage will find life company pricing more attractive than any floating-rate alternative in the current environment.

CMBS is a reasonable comparison point for Columbus self storage. CMBS can reach slightly higher leverage in some cases and moves efficiently for deals where the borrower is comfortable with securitized servicing. However, CMBS prepayment flexibility is generally worse than life company yield maintenance structures for sponsors who may want optionality. Bank balance sheet financing competes on flexibility and relationship pricing for smaller deals, but banks are increasingly constrained on CRE concentration limits and term length. Debt funds offer speed and leverage for transitional or lease-up assets, but pricing is substantially higher than life company fixed rates for stabilized collateral. The honest answer is that value-add facilities, assets below 85 percent occupancy, or borrowers needing 70-plus percent leverage should look at bridge debt or bank financing before approaching a life company.
