Life Company Office Lending in Colorado Springs

Colorado Springs has emerged as one of the more resilient secondary markets for life company capital in the Mountain West, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. The city's employment base is anchored by one of the densest concentrations of military and defense infrastructure in the country: Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, Cheyenne Mountain, NORAD/NORTHCOM headquarters, and the broader Fort Carson footprint collectively insulate the local economy from the kind of demand volatility that has made life companies skittish about office elsewhere. Defense contractors and Space Force-adjacent technology firms occupy Class A office product along the I-25 corridor and in northern submarkets like Briargate and Northgate with lease structures that institutional lenders find genuinely attractive. For the right asset, life company capital in Colorado Springs is not a stretch.

That said, the post-2020 recalibration in life company office lending applies here as much as it does in Denver or any other market. Life companies have grown selective, and Colorado Springs is not immune to the broader repricing of office credit risk. Generic suburban product with short weighted average lease terms, multi-tenant vacancy exposure, or dated vintage will not meet current life company standards regardless of the market. What does get done here is specific: defense contractor single-tenant or anchor-tenanted Class A product in Briargate and Northgate, medical office near UCHealth and Penrose, and well-leased suburban Class A with credit tenancy along the Powers corridor or north of the Banning Lewis Ranch line. Sponsors who understand this distinction will move efficiently. Those expecting life company appetite to resemble the 2018 market will be disappointed.

Colorado Springs also benefits from a pricing dynamic that life companies notice. Cap rates and basis are measurably below Denver while the tenant credit quality in the defense and healthcare sectors competes favorably with larger markets. For a life company allocating to the Mountain West, a stabilized Class A office building in Briargate leased to a Tier 1 defense contractor at below-replacement-cost basis is a more attractive risk-adjusted credit than comparable product in a higher-basis urban market with more tenant uncertainty. That relative value story is part of why life company activity in Colorado Springs has remained steady even as office allocations nationally have contracted.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Colorado Springs Office Deals

Life company underwriting for office in Colorado Springs in 2026 centers on a narrow set of criteria, and sponsors should approach these conversations with clear eyes. Leverage is the starting point: most life company office executions in this market are pricing at 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value, and the deals commanding the best spread execution are coming in at the lower end of that range. Under-leveraged structures are not a weakness in this environment; they are a pricing lever. Sponsors with equity to put to work are in a better position than those trying to maximize proceeds.

On rate, life company office financing is priced off the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range through early 2026. Life company spreads for stabilized Colorado Springs office with strong tenancy are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for the most competitive credits, with wider spreads for assets that carry any leasing uncertainty or shorter WALT. The result is all-in fixed rates in the mid-to-upper 5 percent range for well-structured deals, which compares favorably to CMBS and significantly outperforms floating-rate debt fund capital on a total cost basis for sponsors planning a long hold.

DSCR requirements have tightened alongside leverage constraints. Life companies are generally requiring 1.30x or better on stabilized in-place cash flow, and underwriters are stress-testing lease rollover carefully. Assets with near-term lease expirations representing more than 20 percent of gross leasable area will face questions. Weighted average lease term is a primary filter: product with 7-plus years of WALT gets looked at seriously; shorter profiles require compensating factors. Non-recourse structure is standard, though sponsors should expect broader carve-out language in the current environment relative to pre-2020 deals. Term is typically 10 to 15 years fixed for stabilized product, with 25 to 30 year amortization and yield maintenance prepayment provisions.

Typical Colorado Springs Life Company Office Deal Profile

A realistic life company office transaction in Colorado Springs today looks something like this: a Briargate or Northgate Class A suburban office building, built between 2005 and 2018, single-tenant or anchor-tenanted at 90 percent or better occupancy, with a Tier 1 defense contractor or healthcare system as the primary credit tenant. Loan size is in the $8M to $30M range, with the most competitive execution starting at $15M and above. The sponsor has institutional experience with office assets, a clean balance sheet, and is not asking life company capital to carry a lease-up or a repositioning story. The property is stabilized, the cash flow is in-place, and the hold horizon is 10 years or longer.

Medical office near the UCHealth Memorial system or Penrose St. Francis also fits this profile well, particularly where the tenant mix includes hospital-affiliated physician groups on long-term leases. Security-Widefield product adjacent to Fort Carson has attracted interest where the tenancy is government or defense contractor driven. The Peterson SFB adjacent submarket is smaller but has produced fundable deals where the asset quality supports it.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Colorado Springs

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Colorado Springs office deal, and sponsors should understand where it wins and where other capital sources are more appropriate. For stabilized Class A product with long WALT, credit tenancy, and a sponsor planning a long hold, life company execution is typically the most competitive available: lower rates than CMBS, non-recourse structure cleaner than most bank balance sheet loans, and terms that eliminate refinancing risk for a decade or more.

CMBS is a reasonable alternative for deals that need slightly higher leverage or where the tenancy profile is a step below life company standards. CMBS will generally go to 65 to 70 percent LTV where life companies stop at 60, and the credit box is wider. The trade-off is rate, prepayment inflexibility, and servicer relationships that can complicate asset management decisions over time. For value-add office or assets with near-term lease rollover, neither life company nor CMBS is the right starting point. Bank balance sheet or debt fund capital is more appropriate for transitional situations, with the understanding that floating-rate exposure and shorter terms create refinancing risk that long-term fixed capital avoids entirely. Agency financing does not apply to office. For sponsors with a shorter target hold or a recapitalization strategy, a shorter-term bank loan may be the more rational structure even if the all-in cost is higher.
