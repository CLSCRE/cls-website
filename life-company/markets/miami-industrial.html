Life Company Industrial Lending in Miami

Miami's industrial market has undergone a structural repricing in the eyes of institutional capital over the past several years, and life insurance companies have taken notice. PortMiami is the deepest container port on the East Coast and the only port in the continental United States capable of handling post-Panamax vessels on both its inbound and outbound channels. That infrastructure reality, combined with South Florida's explosive population and business growth, has made the Doral, Hialeah, and Airport West corridors among the most aggressively underwritten industrial submarkets in the Sun Belt for permanent life company capital. Institutional lenders that historically concentrated their South Florida allocations on Brickell multifamily and Coral Gables office have quietly built significant industrial exposure across Miami-Dade in the current cycle.

What makes Miami particularly attractive for life company industrial underwriting is the combination of supply discipline and genuine demand drivers. The region is constrained geographically, with the Everglades to the west, the Atlantic to the east, and the urban core absorbing available land at a pace that limits new deliveries relative to demand. Last-mile distribution facilities serving the Miami metro, cold storage assets tied to PortMiami's perishable cargo volumes, and bulk warehouse serving the region's growing consumer base all fit cleanly within the life company credit box. Florida's legal environment adds another layer of underwriting clarity: no state income tax, no rent control at any level (state law preempts any local ordinance), and a well-functioning foreclosure and landlord-remedy framework that institutional lenders weight heavily when pricing permanent mortgage risk.

The international dimension of Miami's economy also supports life company appetite. The city's role as the capital of Latin American commerce means tenant rosters at quality industrial parks frequently include investment-grade and near-investment-grade operators with long operating histories. Credit-tenant NNN industrial in the Airport West and Doral corridors has attracted some of the most aggressive life company pricing seen in any South Florida commercial real estate category, with well-structured deals clearing at spreads that reflect genuine competition among institutional correspondent lenders for the right collateral.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Miami Industrial Deals

Life insurance company capital is permanent, patient capital, and the underwriting reflects that mandate. For Miami industrial transactions, lenders in this category are typically working within a 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value range on stabilized assets, with leverage stretching toward 70 percent for credit-tenant NNN product with long remaining lease term and investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenancy. These are not guideline suggestions -- they are genuine structural limits that reflect how life companies model long-term mortgage performance against their liability durations.

Fixed-rate term structures of 10 to 25 years are standard, with longer terms more common when tenancy supports it. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury trading in the low-to-mid 4 percent range, life company all-in pricing on stabilized Miami industrial assets is generally landing in a spread range of roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, though Class A logistics and distribution product with strong tenancy can trade inside that range when multiple life company correspondents are competing. DSCR requirements are typically 1.25x at minimum, with many programs underwriting to 1.30x or higher depending on market, tenancy concentration, and loan size. Non-recourse structure is standard for life company industrial product at this level, with conventional carve-outs for fraud, environmental, and structural misrepresentation.

Occupancy requirements are real: life companies are not value-add lenders. A property needs to be stabilized, meaning leased to creditworthy tenants with meaningful remaining term. In Miami specifically, lenders weigh tenant quality and lease structure carefully given the international nature of some tenancy. A well-structured single-tenant NNN deal with a recognizable credit operator in Doral will receive materially different execution than a multi-tenant light industrial park in a secondary Miami-Dade submarket with near-term rollover risk.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company industrial financing. That phrase carries a specific and consequential meaning that is worth explaining plainly, because it distinguishes how these transactions are handled from the way most commercial mortgage brokers operate in this space.

When a broker submits a deal to a life company, the file travels to that company's home office underwriting desk, where it enters a queue alongside other submissions from other correspondents and intermediaries. The underwriter reviewing the deal may have no prior relationship with the broker, limited familiarity with the specific submarket, and no particular urgency to move the file forward. Revisions, questions, and conditions flow back and forth between parties who have no direct working relationship. The process is opaque by design, and timelines extend accordingly.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, commits, and closes life company industrial loans in our own name. There is no submission to a home office for approval. There is no unknown underwriter reviewing a cold file. The borrower works directly with the team that is making the credit decision, structuring the term sheet, and taking the transaction through commitment. When a question arises about Miami industrial submarket dynamics, Airport West lease comps, or PortMiami cargo volume trends, it is answered by the people closing the loan, not routed through a correspondence chain. This translates directly into faster term sheet issuance, cleaner commitment letters, and a closing process that moves on the borrower's timeline rather than a home office queue's timeline. For time-sensitive acquisitions and refinancings in a competitive South Florida market, that structural difference matters in ways that are easy to quantify.

Typical Miami Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in Miami looks something like this: a stabilized Class A distribution facility in the Doral or Airport West corridor, 150,000 to 500,000 square feet, built or substantially renovated within the past 10 to 15 years, leased to one or two creditworthy tenants with 7 or more years of weighted average lease term remaining. Loan size will typically fall in the $10 million to $50 million range for single-asset transactions, though portfolio executions involving multiple Miami-Dade industrial properties can extend well above that threshold. The sponsor is an experienced institutional owner or private operator with a demonstrable track record in industrial real estate and a balance sheet that supports the non-recourse carve-out obligations.

From signed application and good-faith deposit to closing, a direct correspondent execution on a clean Miami industrial deal typically runs 45 to 75 days. A comparable deal structured through a broker placing the file with a life company correspondent desk frequently takes 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on the home office review cycle. For acquisitions with PSA deadlines or refinancings with construction loan maturities, that timing gap is not academic.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Miami

Life company financing is the strongest execution available for Miami industrial when the deal is stabilized, the hold period is long, and the loan size supports the institutional process. For a 15-year fixed-rate loan on a Class A Doral distribution facility leased to a creditworthy operator, life company pricing will generally beat CMBS on rate, beat bank balance sheet on term, and offer non-recourse structure that debt funds rarely provide at comparable all-in cost.

CMBS is a viable alternative for Miami industrial when flexibility on structure or timing is needed, and it can sometimes accommodate slightly higher leverage, but the securitization process adds complexity and the servicing structure over a 10-year term creates operational friction for active owners. Bank balance sheet lending remains relevant for shorter fixed-rate periods and for borrowers who value depository relationships, but banks are generally not writing 15 to 25 year fixed-rate non-recourse industrial paper in any volume. Debt funds compete effectively for transitional and value-add industrial, where a property is not yet life company-eligible, but their cost of capital on a stabilized permanent basis is rarely competitive with institutional life company spreads.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is not applicable to industrial product. Life company capital is effectively the gold standard for stabilized industrial permanent financing in Miami, and the asset class is currently among the most favored categories for life company deployment in 2025 and 2026.
