Life Company Multifamily Lending in San Diego

San Diego sits near the top of the priority market list for most life insurance company multifamily programs, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. The region's tenant base is among the most creditworthy in the country: active-duty military and DoD civilian employees from the largest concentration of naval installations on the West Coast provide a demand floor that is largely immune to tech layoffs and economic softness. Combine that with severely constrained land supply, a coastal geography that limits new deliveries, and a population that continues to grow, and you have a multifamily market that life companies are comfortable underwriting to long fixed-rate terms.

The submarkets that attract the most life company interest for multifamily are Class A and B properties in Mission Valley, Little Italy, UTC, and Del Mar. These locations benefit from proximity to the life sciences corridor anchored in Torrey Pines and Sorrento Valley, the largest biotech and pharmaceutical research cluster in the country outside of Boston. That employment base drives sustained demand for high-quality rental housing, and life companies underwriting 10 to 25 year fixed-rate paper want to know that the income stream supporting their debt has a durable employment foundation. In UTC and the coastal submarkets, that case writes itself.

One area that adds underwriting scrutiny is older multifamily stock within the City of San Diego. AB 1482 applies statewide, and the city's local rent ordinance imposes additional restrictions on rent increases for buildings constructed before February 1995. Life company underwriters will carefully examine rent rolls, in-place rents relative to market, and the sponsor's operating history for assets that fall under this regulatory framework. Properties with significant below-market rents and limited burn-off potential will face tighter proceeds or may not fit the program at all. Well-located, newer vintage assets in the submarkets listed above face none of these constraints and are the primary targets for this capital.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Diego Life Company Multifamily Deals

Life insurance companies are permanent lenders by mandate. They are matching long-dated liabilities against long-dated fixed-rate assets, which means deal selection is disciplined and underwriting standards are consistent. For San Diego multifamily, the typical loan-to-value range is 55 to 65 percent. Premier sponsors with strong track records in gateway coastal markets like San Diego can occasionally access up to 70 percent LTV, but this is the exception rather than the standard. Life companies are not competing for leverage; they are competing on rate and structure for deals that fit their credit parameters.

On rate, the 2026 environment has stabilized around a 10-year Treasury near 4.3 percent. Life company spreads for stabilized Class A and B multifamily in primary markets like San Diego typically run 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, placing all-in rates in a range that is consistently 10 to 30 basis points inside agency pricing for comparable stabilized product. That spread advantage compounds meaningfully over a 10 to 25 year fixed term. DSCR requirements generally run 1.20x to 1.30x at the underwritten rate, and life companies will stress test at a higher rate to confirm coverage. Properties with in-place DSCR well above the floor give underwriters confidence and may support interest-only periods for sponsorship with sufficient track record.

Non-recourse structure is standard. Carve-outs for fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy are market convention and will appear in every life company loan document. Beyond the financial metrics, life companies want seasoned sponsorship with owned and operated multifamily portfolios, institutional-quality property management, and a clear long-term hold thesis. A San Diego sponsor with a stabilized 150-unit Class A asset in Mission Valley and a 10-year hold plan is exactly the profile this capital was designed to serve.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

The distinction between a direct correspondent lender and a mortgage broker matters more in life company financing than in almost any other debt product, and it is worth explaining the mechanics precisely. Most life company deals in the market are originated by mortgage brokers who assemble an application and submit it to a life company's home office for review. The broker has no authority to commit capital, issue a binding term sheet, or control the underwriting timeline. A separate underwriting team at the life company reviews the submission, may request additional information, may apply an overlay that the broker did not anticipate, and issues its own credit decision on its own schedule. The borrower's primary contact is the broker, but the decision-maker is someone the borrower has never spoken to.

Commercial Lending Solutions operates as a direct correspondent lender for our life company program. We underwrite, commit to, and close these loans in our own name. The underwriting decision is made here, by a single underwriter who is also the point of contact for the transaction. There is no submission to a home office, no waiting for a credit committee at a remote location, and no third-party approval standing between a borrower's deal and a binding commitment. Our correspondent relationship with our life company capital source is a capital and funding arrangement, not an origination chain that adds time and opacity to the process.

In practice, this means San Diego sponsors dealing with Commercial Lending Solutions get a LOI to closing timeline of approximately 45 to 75 days on a properly prepared deal. Comparable deals submitted through a brokered channel routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer, and the timeline uncertainty is a structural feature of the model rather than an exception. For sponsors managing a refinance on a maturing loan or closing an acquisition with a defined timeline, the difference between these two paths is not a minor convenience. It is a material transaction risk.

Typical San Diego Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

The most common life company multifamily transactions we close in the San Diego market fall in the $8 million to $50 million range, though the program accommodates deals from $5 million to $75 million. The sweet spot is a 75 to 300 unit Class A or B asset built after 1995, fully stabilized at 93 percent occupancy or better for a minimum of 90 days, with a rent roll that reflects market-rate tenancies and minimal concessions. Lease-up properties and value-add assets in transition do not fit this program.

The typical sponsor profile is an experienced multifamily investor or developer with a demonstrated operating track record in California, a net worth at or above the loan amount, and liquidity sufficient to meet standard reserves requirements. Institutional operators and family offices with long-term hold strategies are the most natural fit. The financing structure typically includes a 10 to 15 year fixed-rate term with 25 to 30 year amortization, and interest-only periods of two to five years are available for deals with strong in-place coverage. Declining prepayment schedules or yield maintenance provisions will apply, consistent with the life company's asset-liability matching requirements.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Diego

For stabilized Class A and B multifamily in San Diego, life company execution is the most competitive permanent financing available when the sponsor has a long-term hold strategy, the loan size is above $5 million, and leverage needs are at or below 65 percent. The rate advantage over agency financing is real and consistent in the current environment, and the non-recourse structure with a flexible prepayment negotiation matches what sophisticated sponsors want on long-hold assets.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac remain strong options for sponsors who need higher leverage, want supplemental loan flexibility, or are financing smaller assets where the life company minimum deal size is a constraint. Agency programs also handle affordable and workforce housing overlays that life companies typically avoid. For value-add acquisitions, bridge lenders and debt funds provide the execution flexibility that life companies cannot match; life companies will not fund a property in lease-up or with deferred maintenance. CMBS offers a non-recourse fixed-rate option at scale but carries prepayment rigidity and servicing complexity that most borrowers find unattractive compared to life company paper when both are available. Bank balance sheet loans can price competitively on a floating-rate basis but rarely compete on 10 to 25 year fixed terms against life company correspondent capital.

The honest answer is that life company financing wins decisively for the right deal: stabilized, institutional quality, long hold, large enough to justify the execution, and sponsored by a borrower with the credit profile life companies require. When any of those conditions are absent, a different capital source is probably the better path, and we will tell you that directly.
