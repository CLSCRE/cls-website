Life Company Self Storage Lending in San Antonio

San Antonio has emerged as one of the more compelling secondary markets for life insurance company capital in Texas, and self storage has followed that trajectory. The city's fundamental demand drivers are unusually durable: a permanent military population anchored by Joint Base San Antonio, a large and growing healthcare employment base centered on the South Texas Medical Center, and consistent net in-migration from higher-cost metros. Each of these tenant profiles produces predictable storage demand. Military households relocate frequently. Healthcare workers rent apartments rather than buy homes. New arrivals to the market need transitional storage. Life companies underwriting stabilized self storage in San Antonio are not betting on speculative absorption; they are underwriting a proven demand base.

From a submarket standpoint, life company interest in San Antonio self storage concentrates in the same corridors where the broader capital markets are active: the Far North Side along US-281 and Loop 1604, North Central near the Stone Oak and Medical Center employment nodes, and established suburban infill locations with limited new supply. The Schertz and New Braunfels markets, sitting on the I-35 growth corridor between San Antonio and Austin, have drawn interest as well given population growth and constrained land supply. What life companies are less interested in is older, single-story, non-climate-controlled product on the south or west sides of the market, where absorption is less predictable and rent growth has lagged. Institutional capital in San Antonio is sector-selective, and self storage is no exception.

Texas's regulatory environment reinforces the case. No rent control, a landlord-friendly lien law framework for self storage operators, and a strong local economy anchored by USAA, Valero Energy, and Toyota manufacturing give life company credit committees a straightforward story to approve. San Antonio is not San Francisco or Austin in terms of headline growth rates, but for a life insurance company seeking 15 to 20 years of stable, predictable cash flow from a self storage asset, that is largely the point. Steady beats spectacular in a life company's duration-matching framework.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Antonio Self Storage Deals

Life insurance companies underwriting self storage in San Antonio operate within a well-defined credit box, and understanding that box before submitting a deal saves considerable time. Stabilized occupancy is the threshold requirement: 85 percent economic occupancy or better, typically demonstrated over a trailing 12-month period. Life companies are not bridge lenders and will not underwrite to pro forma stabilization, regardless of how compelling the rent growth story appears. The asset needs to be performing before permanent capital enters the picture.

On leverage, life companies are comfortable in the 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value range on stabilized San Antonio self storage. That disciplined LTV posture reflects both the asset class and the lender type. Life companies are not competing with debt funds on leverage; they are competing on rate, term, and structure. DSCR requirements typically run 1.25x to 1.35x at the underwritten rate, and life company underwriters will stress test cash flow assumptions, particularly on street rate volatility, which has been a meaningful factor in the self storage sector over the past two years. Climate-controlled facilities with demonstrated rental rate premiums underwrite more favorably than open-air drive-up product.

In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury in the low-to-mid 4 percent range, life company spreads on stabilized self storage in a market like San Antonio are running in the 150 to 200 basis point range, producing all-in fixed rates that remain competitive with CMBS execution and often outperform it on a total cost basis when accounting for non-recourse structure and prepayment flexibility. Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 20 years, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule depending on the correspondent program. Non-recourse is standard, with the typical carve-out package for bad acts and environmental matters. For a long-hold sponsor locking in permanent capital on a well-located San Antonio self storage asset, this is a difficult execution to beat.

A realistic life company self storage transaction in San Antonio looks something like this: a climate-controlled, multi-story or mixed-configuration facility built between 2010 and 2020, located on a high-traffic corridor on the North Side or in the Medical Center submarket, running at 88 to 93 percent economic occupancy on a trailing 12-month basis. Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Antonio Self Storage

Self storage is not an agency asset class. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac do not finance self storage, so that comparison applies only where a sponsor is choosing between asset classes for capital allocation purposes. For self storage-specific permanent financing in San Antonio, the real competitive set is life company, CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds.

CMBS is the most direct competitor to life company execution for stabilized self storage in this size range. CMBS can go to 70 to 75 percent LTV, which makes it the right answer for sponsors who need more leverage. The trade-off is less structural flexibility, servicer-administered loan management, and a secondary market execution that reprices with credit conditions. For sponsors who plan to hold an asset for 15 or 20 years and want a single, predictable lender relationship for the life of the loan, life company execution is typically preferable.

Bank balance sheet loans are competitive for smaller deals and shorter terms, particularly for sponsors with strong banking relationships. However, bank financing on self storage rarely exceeds a 10-year term, frequently carries recourse, and is subject to concentration limits that can limit availability precisely when sponsors need capital most. Debt funds are the right tool for bridge and value-add scenarios. If a San Antonio self storage facility is running at 70 percent occupancy and needs 18 months to stabilize, a debt fund is the appropriate capital source. Once stabilized, refinancing into a life company permanent loan is a natural exit from that capital stack.

Life company financing wins clearly when the asset is stabilized, the hold period is long, the sponsor values non-recourse structure, and the loan size is large enough to access institutional capital. In San Antonio's current market, those conditions describe a meaningful segment of the self storage owner universe.
