Life Company Net Lease Lending in Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City has emerged as one of the most actively pursued life company markets in the Mountain West, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. Utah's pro-business regulatory environment, absence of rent control, and the sustained employment growth driven by the Silicon Slopes technology and fintech corridor have created a population and income dynamic that life company credit committees find genuinely compelling. Single-tenant NNN retail serving the Wasatch Front suburban population, where household formation rates continue to outpace nearly every comparable metro, fits the life company investment thesis precisely: long leases, credit tenants, predictable cash flows, and a market with real demand underneath the asset.

For net lease specifically, the story is in the suburbs. The retail corridors along South Jordan, Sandy, Draper, Lehi, and American Fork have absorbed significant single-tenant development from investment-grade operators over the past decade. Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, and national quick-service restaurant brands have followed rooftop growth aggressively along the I-15 corridor, producing the kind of absolute NNN lease structures that life companies have underwritten for generations. These are not speculative locations. They are established suburban retail nodes serving dense, growing residential catchments with demographics that support long-term tenant viability.

Life company appetite in Salt Lake City is selective in one important respect: they want assets where the market supports the tenant, not just assets where the lease supports the loan. A Walgreens in South Jordan with twelve years of absolute NNN lease term and a growing suburban trade area is a fundamentally different credit story than a comparable lease in a stagnant Midwest market. Salt Lake City's growth profile gives life company underwriters confidence in residual value at lease expiration, which is ultimately what makes non-recourse, full-term interest-only execution possible at the pricing levels this market has commanded.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Salt Lake City Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for NNN assets in the Salt Lake City market centers on a consistent set of parameters, and borrowers who understand these thresholds before approaching the market close faster and on better terms. Leverage for most stabilized NNN transactions falls in the 60 to 65 percent LTV range, with the upper bound moving toward 70 to 75 percent only for investment-grade tenants with ten or more years of remaining absolute NNN lease term and demonstrably strong real estate fundamentals. Life companies are not leverage lenders. They are credit lenders, and the structure reflects that.

Debt service coverage requirements typically run 1.25x to 1.35x on a stressed basis, though investment-grade tenants with long leases and interest-only structures often clear this threshold comfortably given the spread between in-place rent and the debt constant. Fixed-rate terms are structured to match or trail the remaining lease term, commonly ranging from ten to twenty-five years, which eliminates refinance risk during the hold period and aligns the lender's and borrower's interests around the same terminal event. In the current 2026 rate environment, with the ten-year Treasury in the range of 4.25 to 4.35 percent and life company spreads for investment-grade NNN product ranging from approximately 125 to 175 basis points, all-in fixed rates for the strongest credits are landing in the low-to-mid 5 percent range. Sponsor credit matters less than tenant credit in this structure, but life companies do want experienced operators with clean track records. Non-recourse is standard for qualifying transactions, not a negotiated concession.

Typical Salt Lake City Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company NNN transaction in the Salt Lake City market looks something like this: a single-tenant retail asset in the $3 million to $15 million range, built within the past ten to twenty years, occupied by an investment-grade credit tenant operating under an absolute NNN lease with twelve or more years of remaining term. The property sits along an established retail corridor in one of the Wasatch Front growth submarkets, South Jordan, Draper, Sandy, Lehi, or American Fork, where population density and household income support long-term tenant viability well beyond the initial lease expiration. The loan structure is a ten to fifteen year fixed-rate first mortgage at 60 to 65 percent LTV, full-term interest-only, non-recourse, with yield maintenance prepayment protection aligned to the lease term.

The sponsor profile is typically an experienced private investor or smaller institutional owner with a long-term hold strategy, a clean balance sheet, and no interest in re-trading leverage or refinancing short-term debt. Life company capital is not the right fit for a value-add repositioning play. It is the right fit for a sponsor who has acquired a stabilized, credit-tenanted asset and wants to lock in low-cost, non-recourse permanent financing for the duration of the lease without the friction of a balloon maturity creating refinance exposure mid-hold.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Salt Lake City

For NNN retail in Salt Lake City, the relevant financing comparison is primarily against CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds. Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is a multifamily product and does not apply to single-tenant retail. CMBS is a real alternative for NNN transactions, particularly for larger loans or assets where the tenant credit is slightly below investment grade. CMBS can offer higher leverage in some cases and is generally more flexible on property type. The tradeoff is rate volatility tied to CMBS spreads, more complex prepayment structures, servicer relationships that become relevant over a long hold, and a securitized product that does not allow for the same structural flexibility life companies can offer on lease co-terminus terms and blend-and-extend provisions.

Bank balance sheet lending for NNN retail typically comes with shorter fixed-rate periods (five to seven years), recourse requirements, and refinance exposure at exactly the point in the lease term when the asset is most vulnerable. Debt funds offer speed and flexibility but price substantially higher than life company execution for stabilized credit assets.
