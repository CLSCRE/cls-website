Life Company Multifamily Lending in Colorado Springs

Colorado Springs has quietly become one of the more compelling secondary markets for life insurance company capital in the Mountain West, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. The city's economic foundation rests on a concentration of federal military and defense installations that is virtually unmatched outside the Pentagon itself. Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, Cheyenne Mountain Complex, and the headquarters of NORAD/NORTHCOM collectively anchor tens of thousands of direct military jobs and a secondary wave of defense contractor employment that has made Colorado Springs one of the most recession-resistant metros in the country. For life companies seeking stable, long-duration collateral, that employment profile translates directly into predictable multifamily demand and low tenant turnover risk.

The multifamily submarkets that attract life company attention in Colorado Springs are well-defined. Briargate and Northgate on the north end of the city have drawn Class A garden and wrap-style projects serving officer-grade military, defense contractor professionals, and technology sector employees tied to the Space Force mission. The Falcon and Powers Corridor submarkets offer slightly more affordable basis points with strong occupancy metrics driven by enlisted and NCO-grade tenants and civilian support staff. Security-Widefield, immediately adjacent to Peterson SFB, produces some of the most consistent occupancy data in the metro because of its proximity to the base's main gate. Life companies are attracted to all of these nodes, though they price them differently: Briargate and Northgate command the tightest spreads, while Security-Widefield and Falcon deals may require slightly more conservative leverage to clear credit committee.

Colorado's landlord-friendly regulatory environment is a meaningful underwriting factor that often goes unspoken. The state has no statewide rent control statute, and Colorado Springs has not pursued local rent stabilization. For a life company committing capital on a 15 or 20-year fixed-rate mortgage, the absence of rent control risk is not a minor footnote. It is a material credit positive. Combined with below-Denver basis and strong population growth driven by Space Force expansion, Colorado Springs is producing a volume of life company-eligible multifamily transactions that would have surprised observers from a decade ago.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Colorado Springs Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting in Colorado Springs follows the same credit discipline applied across the broader permanent lending platform, with some market-specific calibrations. LTV is typically structured between 55 and 65 percent for stabilized assets, with the lower end of that range applying to deals in secondary submarkets or with shorter operating histories. Premier sponsorship with a demonstrated track record in the Colorado market can push leverage toward 65 percent, though 70 percent LTV is reserved for exceptional deals in primary markets and is uncommon in Colorado Springs. Sponsors should underwrite to the 60 percent range as a planning number for most transactions.

Debt service coverage requirements are generally 1.25x at the underwritten rate on a 30-year amortization schedule, with some life companies underwriting to a stressed rate 25 to 50 basis points above the note rate for sizing purposes. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.30 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Colorado Springs multifamily are pricing in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the corresponding Treasury, depending on loan size, sponsorship quality, and submarket. That puts all-in fixed rates for well-structured deals meaningfully inside where CMBS is pricing and generally 10 to 30 basis points inside agency execution for comparable stabilized product.

Life companies want stabilized collateral. For Colorado Springs multifamily, that means physical occupancy consistently above 90 percent for a trailing 12-month period, with economic occupancy not masking concessions or short-term lease burn. Properties should be Class A or strong Class B vintage, generally 2000 or newer for most life company programs, though well-maintained 1990s vintage in core submarkets can qualify with appropriate reserves. Non-recourse structure is standard with customary bad-boy carve-outs. Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 25 years align well with the long-hold strategies that dominate institutional and family office ownership in this market.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital. That phrase carries specific operational meaning that matters to borrowers evaluating execution risk and timeline.

In the brokered model, a mortgage broker assembles a loan package and submits it to a life company's home office underwriting team for review, approval, and commitment. The borrower never speaks to the underwriter. The broker cannot commit capital. Every credit question, structuring discussion, and term negotiation routes through the broker to the life company and back. That process typically adds 30 to 60 days to a closing timeline, and it introduces approval risk at a point in the deal cycle where borrowers have often already committed time and due diligence expense.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites these loans in-house, issues commitments in our own name, and closes in our name. The borrower has a single underwriting relationship from the first conversation through closing. When a structuring question arises during due diligence, it gets resolved with the underwriter who issued the term sheet, not escalated to an unknown credit officer at a home office three time zones away. Our correspondent relationship with Symetra gives us the capital capacity and program parameters to execute on Colorado Springs multifamily transactions with the same certainty of execution that borrowers expect from a direct lender, because that is exactly what we are for these transactions.

The practical result is a closing timeline that typically runs 45 to 75 days from LOI to funding for a direct correspondent transaction, compared to 75 to 120 days or more for deals that require home office submission and approval. In a market where sellers expect defined closing timelines and extension penalties are real, that difference is not abstract.

Typical Colorado Springs Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

The life company multifamily transactions we close in Colorado Springs tend to cluster in a recognizable profile. Loan sizes range from $5 million to $35 million, with the sweet spot in the $8 million to $25 million range where life company execution is most clearly differentiated from agency and bank alternatives. Properties are typically garden-style or wrap apartment communities in the 80 to 250-unit range, built between 2000 and 2018, located in Briargate, Northgate, Falcon, or the Peterson SFB corridor.

Sponsorship is usually an experienced Colorado or regional multifamily owner with a portfolio demonstrating competent property management and a long-term hold strategy. Sponsors pursuing a 5-year value-add play are not the right fit for life company execution. Sponsors refinancing a stabilized asset they intend to hold for 15 years, or acquiring a turnkey Class A community with institutional-quality operations, are exactly the borrower profile life companies are competing to serve. Occupancy should be seasoned above 90 percent with clean rent rolls. From a signed LOI, a well-prepared borrower can expect to close in 45 to 60 days under our direct correspondent process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Colorado Springs

Life company execution earns its place on the capital stack for a specific deal profile. For stabilized Colorado Springs multifamily above $5 million with a long intended hold period, life company rates are generally the tightest available in the permanent lending market, running inside agency pricing while offering longer fixed-rate terms than most Fannie and Freddie executions. Non-recourse, 20-year fixed-rate financing at life company spreads is a product that agency, CMBS, and bank balance sheet lenders simply cannot replicate.

Agency execution through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac remains competitive for smaller loans, deals with moderate value-add components, or sponsors who need supplemental financing flexibility. Agency pricing is close to life company for sub-$5 million transactions, and the streamlined agency process can outperform on timeline for smaller deals. CMBS is generally wider in rate and carries less favorable prepayment mechanics, making it a secondary option for most Colorado Springs multifamily borrowers who have access to life company terms. Bank balance sheet lending is the right call for bridge scenarios, construction takeouts, or deals that need higher leverage than 65 percent. Debt funds occupy the value-add and transitional space where life companies will not go.

The honest summary: if your Colorado Springs multifamily deal is stabilized, well-located, and you intend to hold it for a decade or more, life company execution through a direct correspondent lender is almost always the most cost-effective permanent financing structure available. If you need 75 percent LTV, a two-year term, or flexibility around occupancy, other sources are the better fit.
