Life Company Self Storage Lending in New York: What the Market Looks Like in 2026

New York City represents the single largest life insurance company CRE lending market in the country, and the competitive dynamics for self storage capital here reflect that depth. Life companies that have meaningfully increased their self storage allocations over the past decade are bringing that appetite into the five boroughs and the immediate metro, where infill land constraints, persistent household density, and limited new supply pipelines have created exactly the kind of stabilized, recession-resilient income profiles that institutional capital prizes. The same secular forces that made self storage a standout performer through 2008 and through 2020 are well understood by life company portfolio managers, and New York's structural supply barriers reinforce the thesis at the asset level.

That said, life company appetite in New York is calibrated to specific submarkets and asset quality tiers. Climate-controlled, institutional-quality facilities in urban infill locations across Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx represent the clearest path to life company execution. A well-occupied, climate-controlled facility in a location like Long Island City, Williamsburg, or a dense Bronx corridor carries meaningfully stronger institutional appeal than a suburban conversion on the periphery of the metro. Life companies are underwriting collateral coverage carefully in this market, and the high land values that characterize New York's built environment work in borrowers' favor at conservative LTV levels. The assets that attract the sharpest life company pricing here are facilities that look, operate, and report like institutional-grade real estate, because that is what life company underwriters are mandated to hold.

It is also worth noting where life company appetite in New York has narrowed. Post-2020 office exposure has become highly selective, limited to Class A trophy product in Manhattan's most liquid corridors. Pre-1974 rent-stabilized multifamily has largely fallen outside life company parameters following HSTPA. The reallocation of that capital has to go somewhere, and alternative property types with proven income stability, including self storage, have been direct beneficiaries of that portfolio rebalancing. For stabilized self storage in New York, the institutional capital environment in 2026 is genuinely favorable.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in New York Self Storage Deals

Life company underwriting for self storage in New York follows a consistent framework, and sponsors should understand the parameters before approaching this capital source. Stabilized occupancy is the foundational requirement: 85 percent occupied or above is the standard threshold, and underwriters want to see that occupancy sustained over multiple reporting periods, not achieved in the trailing month. Climate-controlled facilities receive preferential treatment both in terms of capital availability and pricing, reflecting the income premium and tenant stickiness those assets demonstrate relative to drive-up product.

On leverage, life companies are underwriting New York self storage at 55 to 65 percent LTV on a stabilized basis. Higher-quality assets in core locations can access the top of that range; assets with any operational complexity or shorter track records will be underwritten more conservatively. DSCR requirements typically land at 1.25x or above at the underwritten rate, and life company underwriters apply their own stabilized income assumptions rather than accepting trailing rent rolls at face value. Sponsors accustomed to agency or bank underwriting should expect a more methodical income analysis here.

Terms run 10 to 20 years fixed, which is one of the genuine structural advantages life company capital offers relative to alternatives. On the rate side, life company spreads for stabilized self storage in New York are generally running 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, which at current Treasury levels in the 4.3 percent range translates to all-in fixed rates that are competitive with CMBS and materially below debt fund pricing. Prepayment is yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which means life company financing is structured for sponsors who intend to hold, not those anticipating a near-term exit. Non-recourse is standard on properly structured transactions, and that structure is available without the carve-out complexity that sometimes characterizes CMBS documentation.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage: What It Actually Means

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital. That distinction is specific and consequential, and it is worth explaining in concrete terms rather than abstract claims.

In the brokered model, a mortgage broker identifies a life company program, packages a deal, and submits it to the lender's home office for review by an underwriter the borrower has never spoken to, on a timeline the broker cannot control. The broker has no authority to commit capital, no ability to negotiate structure in real time, and no standing to accelerate the internal approval process when a deal needs to move. Every question that arises during underwriting requires a round trip through the broker back to the borrower and then back to the home office. That friction is structural, not incidental.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites self storage transactions in-house, commits to financing in the life insurance company's name, and closes the loan directly. There is no submission to a home office, no unknown underwriter conducting a parallel review, and no third-party approval process that can stall or derail a transaction. When our team issues a term sheet on a New York self storage deal, that term sheet reflects our own credit authority. When questions arise during due diligence, they are resolved within our team. The borrower has a single point of contact who is also the decision-maker. For deals in the $5 million to $50 million range where timing and structure matter, that distinction is not a marketing point. It is a material operational difference that affects both the speed and the predictability of execution.

What a Typical New York Life Company Self Storage Deal Looks Like

A realistic life company self storage transaction in New York in 2026 looks something like this: a climate-controlled, multi-story facility in a dense outer borough submarket, built or substantially renovated within the past 15 years, operating at 88 to 95 percent occupancy on a stabilized basis, owned by an experienced operator with a track record in institutional-quality self storage. Loan sizes in this market typically fall between $5 million and $35 million for stand-alone facilities, though portfolio transactions can reach the upper end of the $50 million program ceiling.

Sponsors in this capital stack are typically experienced owner-operators or institutional platforms that understand long-term holds, value fixed-rate certainty, and have already cycled through a value-add phase. Life company capital is not the right fit for a recently acquired facility still burning through lease-up. It is the right fit for a sponsor who has stabilized an asset and wants to lock in long-term, non-recourse debt with a sophisticated institutional counterparty.

From executed term sheet to closing, direct correspondent transactions at Commercial Lending Solutions typically run 45 to 75 days on straightforward deals. That compares to 75 to 120 days or more for indirect placements where the home office review timeline is outside any broker's control. In a market like New York, where borrowers often have existing relationships and competitive refinancing options, that timeline difference is a real factor in execution.

Life Company Versus Other Permanent Capital Options in New York

Self storage in New York does not qualify for Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac financing, so agency capital is not a relevant comparison for this asset class. The realistic alternatives are CMBS, bank balance sheet lending, and debt funds, and each has a context where it wins.

CMBS can reach higher leverage than life companies, with some executions going to 70 to 75 percent LTV, and CMBS does not require the same income stability track record at the time of commitment. For sponsors who need higher leverage or have an asset that is not quite at life company stabilization standards, CMBS is a viable path. The tradeoff is a more complex loan structure, securitization-driven servicing, less flexibility on modifications, and all-in pricing that is generally wider than life company execution on comparable deals.

Bank balance sheet lending in New York can be competitive on shorter-term floating-rate products, particularly for sponsors with existing banking relationships. For a five-year refinance with prepayment flexibility, a bank may outperform a life company. For a sponsor who wants 15-year fixed-rate non-recourse certainty, the bank balance sheet comparison breaks down quickly.

Debt funds occupy the high-leverage, higher-cost end of the spectrum and are most relevant for transitional or value-add situations. On a stabilized New York self storage asset seeking permanent financing, debt fund pricing is not competitive with life company execution under current market conditions.

Life company financing wins clearly on stabilized, high-quality assets where the sponsor's priority is long-term fixed-rate certainty, non-recourse structure, and the lowest cost of permanent capital. That is the sweet spot this program is designed for.
