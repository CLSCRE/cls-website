Life Company Industrial Lending in Tucson

Tucson is not a market where life insurance companies deploy capital indiscriminately. It is a market where they deploy it selectively and, when the asset fits, aggressively. The anchor of that selectivity is Raytheon Missiles and Defense, Arizona's largest private employer, whose sprawling presence in Tucson's southeast industrial corridor creates a defense-adjacent manufacturing and logistics ecosystem that life companies find genuinely compelling. Industrial product tied to defense supply chain, precision manufacturing, or logistics serving Raytheon's vendor base occupies a different underwriting tier than generic warehouse in a secondary market. Life companies understand the depth of that employment base and price accordingly.

Beyond the Raytheon cluster, Tucson's industrial market benefits from its position as a distribution and logistics node between Phoenix and the U.S.-Mexico border. The Marana submarket along I-10 has attracted regional distribution users and last-mile operators serving the broader Tucson metro. Sahuarita, anchored by its own industrial and manufacturing activity, draws tenants in electronics, mining equipment, and precision components. These submarkets present the kind of stabilized, single-tenant or multi-tenant light industrial product that fits life company underwriting criteria: longer weighted average lease terms, creditworthy tenancy, and functional assets in supply-constrained locations. Life companies are also watching the Foothills and Oracle Road corridors for flex and light industrial product serving healthcare and technology tenants, particularly those with ties to Banner Health and the University of Arizona research enterprise.

The honest caveat about Tucson is that life company appetite here is narrower than in Phoenix. Deal size, tenancy quality, and lease structure matter more than they would in a primary market with deeper liquidity. A generic multi-tenant industrial park with short-term leases and local-credit tenants will not clear life company underwriting in Tucson. A Class A bulk distribution facility on I-10 in Marana with a regional or national tenant and seven-plus years of lease term remaining is a different conversation entirely. Sponsors who understand that distinction are the ones who benefit from life company execution in this market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Tucson Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting industrial in Tucson in 2025 and 2026 are focused on a specific set of deal characteristics. Leverage is constrained: standard LTV runs 55 to 65 percent for stabilized product, with credit-tenant NNN deals potentially reaching 70 percent when the lease term aligns with or exceeds the loan term. These are not high-leverage executions. Sponsors who need 70 to 75 percent proceeds on a speculative or transitional asset should look elsewhere. Life company capital is priced for certainty, not stretch.

On rate, the current environment has the 10-year Treasury in the 4.25 to 4.40 percent range, with life company spreads for stabilized industrial running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the benchmark for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics and distribution product with strong tenancy can price at the tight end of that range or inside it, making life company execution among the most competitive permanent capital available for the right deal. Terms run 10 to 25 years fixed, with longer terms reserved for credit-tenant NNN structures. Full-term interest-only is available for trophy logistics product with institutional-quality sponsorship and lease profiles.

DSCR requirements typically run 1.25 to 1.35 times on a 25 to 30 year amortization schedule, though IO structures are underwritten on a coverage basis tied to current net operating income. Non-recourse is standard, with carve-outs for the usual bad acts. In the Tucson context, life companies are also focused on lease rollover exposure relative to loan maturity. A deal with significant near-term rollover in a market with less leasing depth than Phoenix will face harder scrutiny on residual value and re-leasing assumptions. Deals with single tenants in defense-related or healthcare-adjacent industrial, where the replacement cost and re-leasing story is credible, tend to underwrite more cleanly.

A realistic life company industrial deal in the Tucson market looks something like this: a stabilized single-tenant or two-tenant industrial or flex-industrial property in the $5M to $30M range, located in Marana, Sahuarita, or the southeast Tucson industrial corridor. The sponsor is an experienced industrial owner or institutional partnership with a demonstrable track record in the asset class and a clear hold strategy aligned with a 10-year-plus fixed term.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Tucson

Life company financing is not always the right answer for industrial in Tucson. Understanding when it wins and when it does not is part of using it correctly.

Against CMBS, life company execution is typically superior on rate, structure, and flexibility for deals above $5M with strong tenancy. CMBS wins on higher leverage and less restrictive reserve and cash management requirements. For sponsors who need 70-plus percent LTV or who have lease rollover exposure that does not fit life company credit criteria, CMBS is often the practical answer.

Bank balance sheet works well for Tucson industrial deals in the $3M to $8M range where the sponsor has an existing banking relationship or depository business to offer. Banks are also more flexible on transitional or value-add assets. The trade-off is shorter fixed terms, typically three to seven years, and recourse requirements that most institutional sponsors prefer to avoid.

Debt funds fill the gap for value-add, lease-up, or higher-leverage situations. They are not a competitor to life company permanent capital because they are solving a different problem. A sponsor who finishes a renovation and stabilization with a debt fund loan should be thinking about a life company refinance at stabilization, not extending with the debt fund.

Agency financing does not apply to industrial. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are multifamily lenders. For Tucson industrial, the permanent capital conversation is life company, CMBS, bank, or insurance company separate account, and life company correspondent execution from a direct lender is the most efficient path to long-term fixed-rate non-recourse capital for qualifying assets.
