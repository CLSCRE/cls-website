Life Company Industrial Lending in Chattanooga: A Market Built for Long-Term Capital

Chattanooga has quietly assembled the ingredients that life insurance company capital finds most compelling in a secondary market: anchor demand drivers, interstate logistics infrastructure, and a diversifying employment base that extends well beyond any single employer. The Volkswagen Tennessee manufacturing plant in the I-75 corridor anchors the automotive supply chain industrial market, pulling tier-one and tier-two suppliers into the Ooltewah and East Hamilton County submarkets. Amazon's regional distribution presence reinforces last-mile and bulk logistics demand along both I-75 and I-24. Life companies tracking net absorption and lease-up velocity in those corridors are seeing numbers that compete favorably with some larger Sun Belt metros.

The I-24 logistics corridor west toward Nashville and the I-75 industrial spine running north toward Cleveland, Tennessee are the two axes where life company allocations concentrate for industrial product. Class A distribution facilities with clear heights above 32 feet, modern dock configurations, and creditworthy tenants on leases of seven years or longer are what institutional capital is underwriting here. Ooltewah and the Hamilton Place area have emerged as preferred locations for regional distribution and light industrial park product, where tenancy quality has improved measurably over the past cycle. Hixson, while primarily residential in character, sees some flex and service-industrial demand that can qualify under the right lease structure.

Life company lenders are selective about Chattanooga in the way they are selective about most secondary markets: the deal has to clear a higher bar on tenancy quality and lease term than a comparable asset in Atlanta or Nashville would face. That selectivity, however, has not translated into withdrawal. Tennessee's landlord-friendly regulatory environment, the absence of rent control, and the state's broader industrial growth story have kept Chattanooga on the approved market list for multiple life company programs. For sponsors with the right product, this is an executable market with genuine institutional appetite.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Chattanooga Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for Chattanooga industrial follows the same structural framework applied across the broader Southeast, with some secondary market adjustments that sponsors should understand before approaching the market. Leverage is the first filter. Standard industrial execution runs at 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value on stabilized product. Credit-tenant NNN leases with investment-grade obligors can push that ceiling to 70 percent, which is meaningful for sponsors optimizing the capital stack on a build-to-suit or long-term single-tenant asset. Spec industrial or properties with near-term rollover will generally be underwritten toward the lower end of that range regardless of market.

Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 25 years are the norm, with longer terms reserved for credit-tenant structures where the lease and loan terms are closely matched. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury in the range of 4.25 to 4.35 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Chattanooga industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over Treasury for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics with institutional-grade tenancy can tighten inside that range, making life company execution genuinely competitive on an all-in cost basis relative to alternatives. Interest-only periods for the full loan term are available on trophy distribution product and are increasingly common as life companies compete for high-quality deal flow.

DSCR requirements typically sit at 1.25x or better at the note rate, with some programs underwriting to a stress rate. Non-recourse structure is standard, with customary carve-outs for fraud, environmental indemnity, and bankruptcy-related bad acts. Sponsors should arrive at the conversation with clean operating history, a lease abstract showing weighted average lease term, and a clear capital expenditure and deferred maintenance accounting. Life companies are not turnaround capital; they are pricing stabilized cash flow, and anything that introduces uncertainty into the income stream will widen the spread or move the deal out of allocation entirely.

Typical Chattanooga Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The life company industrial deal that moves efficiently in Chattanooga today shares a recognizable profile. Loan size typically falls between $10 million and $50 million, though our program accommodates deals from $5 million up to $100 million for the right asset. The property is Class A or strong Class B, built within the past 15 years, located within reasonable proximity to I-75 or I-24, and occupied by a tenant or tenant mix with a weighted average lease term of at least five to seven years. Single-tenant NNN assets with creditworthy obligors, particularly those tied to the automotive supply chain or regional distribution networks, represent the most straightforward underwriting path.

Sponsors in this profile are typically institutional owners, family office investors, or experienced regional operators with a track record of commercial ownership and clean financial statements. Life companies are not asset-based lenders in the way a bank or debt fund might be; sponsor quality enters the credit picture even on non-recourse structures because it affects how carve-outs are evaluated and how the lender perceives execution risk during the hold period.

Timeline from signed term sheet to closing for a direct correspondent transaction in this market runs 45 to 75 days under normal conditions. Comparable transactions placed through a brokered channel frequently require 75 to 120 days or more, with greater variability depending on the life company's internal pipeline and allocation cycle.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Chattanooga

Life company financing is not the right answer for every Chattanooga industrial deal, and understanding where it wins and where it loses is part of making a sound capital decision. Life company execution is most competitive for stabilized, long-hold assets where the fixed-rate certainty and non-recourse structure have genuine value, where loan size justifies the closing costs and prepayment structure, and where tenancy quality supports the underwriting. Credit-tenant NNN logistics assets, Class A distribution facilities with institutional tenancy, and well-leased light industrial parks in established corridors are natural fits.

CMBS can occasionally compete on rate for larger stabilized deals, but the servicing structure, reserve requirements, and prepayment inflexibility make it a worse execution for most long-term holders. Bank balance sheet financing offers more flexibility on structure and recourse but carries floating-rate exposure, shorter terms, and renewal risk that creates refinancing uncertainty. Debt funds fill the value-add and transitional space that life companies will not touch, at a cost of capital that reflects the additional risk. For sponsors with lease-up exposure, near-term rollover, or a business plan that involves repositioning, a debt fund or bridge lender is the right first conversation, with life company takeout as the exit once the asset stabilizes.

Agency execution through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is not applicable to industrial product and remains the dominant channel for Chattanooga multifamily, particularly in the Northshore and Downtown corridors where life companies are also active as a competing permanent capital source.
