Life Company Industrial Lending in New York: Market Context

New York City represents the largest life company CRE lending market in the country, and industrial product in the outer boroughs has become one of the most compelling targets for permanent correspondent capital heading into 2025 and 2026. While life insurers have pulled back meaningfully on pre-2020 vintage Manhattan office, their appetite for well-located last-mile distribution, bulk warehouse, and light industrial parks with strong tenancy in the Bronx, Queens, and JFK/Jamaica corridor has only grown. The structural tailwinds are real: supply constraints in the outer boroughs, sustained e-commerce and logistics demand, and the near-impossibility of assembling large industrial footprints close to the urban core keep occupancy tight and rent growth credible. Life companies underwriting these fundamentals find them durable, which is exactly the kind of collateral they are designed to hold for 10 to 25 year terms.

The submarkets drawing the most consistent life company attention on the industrial side are the Bronx industrial corridor, the JFK and Jamaica logistics zone, Long Island City, and select pockets of Staten Island with direct highway access. These are not speculative bets. They are infill locations with demonstrated absorption, limited new supply pipelines, and in many cases credit or near-credit tenancy. High land values throughout the five boroughs also provide collateral coverage that supports conservative life company LTV parameters even on assets where the income alone might not fully justify the basis. That land value floor matters when a life insurer is underwriting a 20-year fixed commitment.

Life companies have become more selective across New York generally since 2020, particularly on older office product and pre-1974 rent-stabilized multifamily subject to HSTPA restrictions. Industrial has moved in the opposite direction. Class A logistics facilities, last-mile distribution centers, and modernized bulk warehouse assets with institutional-grade tenancy represent some of the tightest spread executions available in the life company correspondent market right now, and New York outer borough product qualifies when the fundamentals are there.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in New York Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for New York industrial is not fundamentally different from their national framework, but the market context shapes several variables. On leverage, the standard range runs 55 to 65 percent LTV for stabilized industrial. Credit-tenant NNN deals with long remaining lease terms and investment-grade obligors can reach 70 percent, though that is the ceiling and not the expectation. Life companies in this market are underwriting replacement cost carefully given land values and construction costs, and a deal that clears 55 percent LTV in most markets might pencil tighter in New York simply because of the basis. Sponsors need to be clear-eyed about that going in.

On rate, the current environment for 2026 closings reflects a 10-year Treasury in the range of 4.3 percent, with life company spreads for stabilized industrial running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics with credit tenancy and long lease terms can price at the tighter end of that range or tighter still, which is one reason life company execution is genuinely competitive for the right industrial deal. These are 10 to 25 year fixed-rate loans, and the certainty of that fixed rate over a long hold period is a major part of the value proposition for sponsors who are not planning near-term disposition.

DSCR requirements typically fall in the 1.25x to 1.35x range on actual in-place income, with life companies stress-testing at a loaded interest rate in their sensitivity analysis. Occupancy thresholds are generally 90 percent or better for stabilized treatment, though fully-leased single-tenant industrial with a credit obligor gets the best execution. Non-recourse structure is standard with customary carve-outs for bad acts, environmental indemnities, and SPE covenants. Full-term interest-only is available for trophy distribution product and long-term NNN deals where the cash flow and credit profile support it. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule is the norm, meaning these loans are designed for sponsors who are genuinely holding.

Typical New York Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in New York through our direct correspondent program typically looks like this: a stabilized bulk warehouse or last-mile distribution facility in the Bronx, Queens, or JFK corridor, 100,000 to 500,000 square feet, built or substantially renovated post-2000, occupied by one to three tenants with remaining weighted average lease term of seven years or longer. Loan size generally falls between $10 million and $75 million for these assets, though our program accommodates deals from $3 million to $100 million. Sponsors are institutional or experienced private owners with clean balance sheets, relevant asset management experience, and a demonstrable long-term hold strategy.

Single-tenant NNN industrial with a credit-grade obligor and 10-plus years of remaining term is the most competitive profile in this market and will attract the tightest pricing and the most favorable structure, including potential interest-only. Multi-tenant light industrial parks with strong historical occupancy and below-market rents offering mark-to-market upside also qualify, though life companies underwrite conservatively on projected income and want demonstrated in-place performance, not pro forma cash flow. Timeline from executed LOI to closing in our direct correspondent model is typically 45 to 75 days for a clean deal with complete due diligence materials available at submission.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in New York Industrial

Life company financing wins in a specific and well-defined lane. For stabilized New York industrial assets with long-term tenancy, sponsors who are holding through a full cycle, and deal sizes above $10 million, life company correspondent capital is frequently the most competitive permanent execution available. The combination of long fixed terms, non-recourse structure, and spread-based pricing that is genuinely tight for quality collateral is difficult to replicate elsewhere.

CMBS is the closest structural competitor for non-recourse permanent financing, and it remains relevant for deals where life company credit standards or LTV ceilings are a constraint. CMBS typically offers higher leverage (up to 70 to 75 percent) and somewhat more flexibility on tenancy profile, but the execution involves a securitization process with less certainty of structure at closing, and the servicer relationship over a 10-year term is a real consideration for sponsors who have been through workouts. Bank balance sheet lenders compete on 5 to 7 year terms with floating or hybrid structures but rarely provide the fixed-rate certainty or the 10 to 25 year term that life companies offer. Debt funds fill the value-add and transitional gap with bridge structures, but at meaningfully higher rates and with recourse or full-leverage covenants that a stabilized asset does not require. Agency execution (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) is multifamily-only and not applicable to industrial. For a stabilized New York industrial asset with a long-term sponsor and quality tenancy, the life company correspondent market is the right conversation to be having.
