Life Company Office Lending in Providence: A Selective but Active Market

Providence occupies an unusual position in the life company lending universe. It is not a primary market where life companies deploy capital reflexively, but it is far from ignored. The city's institutional anchors, particularly Brown University, the Rhode Island School of Design, and Lifespan Health System, create a durable demand base for office and medical office space that life company underwriters recognize and value. The 45-minute proximity to Boston matters too: Rhode Island assets price at a discount to comparable Bay State product while drawing from some of the same employment and tenant base, a spread that sophisticated life company investors have learned to exploit.

Post-2020, life company office underwriting tightened everywhere, and Providence was no exception. General office without institutional-grade tenancy or long weighted average lease terms has become difficult to finance through life company capital in this market. What remains fundable, and fundable at competitive rates, is a narrower but well-defined category: medical and academic office anchored by Lifespan, Care New England, or one of the university systems; Class A suburban product in Johnston, Cranston, or Warwick with credit or government tenants; and Downtown and Jewelry District office with demonstrated occupancy stability. Sponsors who understand where life company capital is focused in this market can still access among the most favorable long-term fixed-rate execution available.

The Jewelry District has attracted particular attention as Providence's innovation corridor, with medical and biotech-adjacent office drawing tenant interest that translates into the kind of lease structures life companies want to see. Suburban Route 6 and I-95 corridor office with government or quasi-public tenancy also fits the profile. What life companies are largely stepping back from in Providence, as in most secondary markets, is conventional multi-tenanted downtown office with near-term lease rollover and no clear re-leasing story. The selectivity is real, but for the right asset and sponsor, the Providence market offers a legitimate life company execution opportunity.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Providence Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Providence office deals in the current environment centers on a narrow set of credit characteristics. Leverage expectations have reset materially since the low-rate era. LTVs in the 50 to 60 percent range are the current standard, and borrowers who bring under-leveraged structures, whether through significant equity or seasoned ownership, are rewarded with better pricing. Deals at or above 65 percent LTV face meaningful execution risk or require substantial credit-tenant offsets to clear life company credit committees.

Term structure for stabilized Providence office is typically 10 to 15 years fixed, with 25 to 30-year amortization. Life companies are not short-term lenders, and their execution is most competitive for sponsors who share that orientation. With the 10-year Treasury trading in the mid-4 percent range in 2026, life company spreads for most stabilized secondary-market office product are running in the 150 to 200 basis point range, though credit-tenanted and medical office assets can tighten inside that band. All-in fixed rates should be modeled directionally off those components rather than any specific quoted figure, which will move with the underlying index.

DSCR requirements are typically 1.25x or higher, with some life companies requiring 1.30x or better for office product in non-primary markets. Non-recourse execution is standard and is one of the structural advantages life company capital offers over bank balance sheet lending, though carve-out provisions have broadened somewhat in the current credit environment. Lease quality is underwritten intensively: weighted average lease term remaining, tenant credit, rollover concentration, and rent-to-market comparisons are all scrutinized. A Lifespan-anchored medical office building with a 12-year WALT looks very different to a life company than a Downtown Providence multi-tenant building with four leases rolling in two years, and pricing and proceeds will reflect that distinction.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital, and the distinction between that structure and a brokered arrangement is not semantic. It has direct consequences for deal speed, underwriting certainty, and the quality of guidance a sponsor receives before committing time and resources to a process.

In a brokered model, a mortgage broker submits a loan request to a life company's home office, typically as one of many competing deals in the queue. The broker does not control the underwriting timeline, does not know the specific underwriter reviewing the file, and cannot offer a binding commitment. Feedback loops are slow, conditions are often surfaced late in the process, and sponsors can invest 60 to 90 days in a deal before receiving a definitive answer. The broker is an intermediary between the capital and the borrower, with limited ability to influence the outcome once the submission is made.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, commits to, and closes life company loans in its own name. There is no submission to a home office for approval, no unknown underwriter on the other side of the process, and no third-party credit committee that can surface new conditions at the 11th hour. Our underwriting team applies life company credit standards directly, which means we can tell a sponsor in the first conversation whether a Providence office deal fits the program or not. That candor has real value. Sponsors who receive honest upfront guidance avoid months of wasted process and can pursue alternative execution when the life company product is not the right fit. This is what being a direct correspondent lender means in practice, and it is the foundation of how we work with borrowers on office transactions in this market.

Typical Providence Life Company Office Deal Profile

A representative life company office transaction in the Providence market looks something like this: a Class A medical or academic office building in the Jewelry District or a suburban campus in Johnston or Cranston, built or substantially renovated within the last 15 to 20 years, 90 percent or better occupied, with a WALT of at least seven to ten years anchored by an institutional tenant. Loan size typically falls in the $7 million to $30 million range for the Providence market, with the most competitive pricing available at $15 million and above.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies want experienced owners with clean credit histories, institutional-grade property management, and balance sheets that support the non-recourse carve-out obligations. First-time sponsors or those with complex or distressed credit histories face a more difficult path in life company underwriting regardless of asset quality.

Timeline from signed term sheet to closing for a direct correspondent life company loan is typically 45 to 75 days for a clean deal. That compares favorably to the 90 to 120-plus days common in brokered life company processes, where the absence of a direct underwriting relationship adds friction at every stage.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Financing Options in Providence

Life company execution is not always the right answer for Providence office borrowers, and understanding when it wins and when it does not is part of working with a lender who operates transparently. Life company capital is most competitive for stabilized, long-hold assets with credit tenancy, large loan sizes relative to the local market, sponsors seeking maximum term certainty, and borrowers who want non-recourse structure without the securitization complexity of CMBS.

CMBS can offer comparable non-recourse execution and occasionally higher proceeds, but it carries securitization fees, less flexibility on loan modifications post-closing, and servicer relationships that some sponsors find cumbersome over a long hold. Bank balance sheet lending offers more flexibility on structure and speed but typically requires recourse and shorter terms, making it a better fit for value-add or transitional office where the business plan does not support a 10 to 15-year fixed commitment. Debt funds can move faster and reach higher leverage, but at a meaningful cost-of-capital premium that erodes returns on stabilized assets. For medical office or credit-tenanted suburban product in the Providence market on a long hold, life company execution typically beats all of these alternatives on all-in cost and structural terms. For value-add office with lease-up risk, shorter hold periods, or leverage requirements above 60 percent, one of the competing sources is likely a better fit.

Life companies in Providence typically target stabilized assets in the $5M to $75M stabilized office with strong tenancy range. How do life company rates compare to CMBS and bank financing for life company office in Providence?

For stabilized life company office assets with strong fundamentals, life company rates are typically inside CMBS by 15 to 40 basis points and inside bank balance sheet pricing on longer fixed-rate terms. The trade-off is stricter underwriting standards and prepayment penalties (yield maintenance is standard). Life company execution is most competitive for borrowers who want long-term hold certainty over flexibility.
