Life Company Net Lease Lending in Colorado Springs

Colorado Springs has quietly become one of the more active secondary markets for life insurance company capital in the Mountain West, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. The city's economy is anchored by one of the densest concentrations of military and defense infrastructure in the country: Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, Cheyenne Mountain Complex, and the headquarters of NORAD and USNORTHCOM collectively employ tens of thousands of active duty personnel, defense contractors, and civilian support workers. That employment base creates exactly the kind of durable, non-discretionary retail demand that life companies want to see behind a net lease credit story. A Walgreens on the Powers Corridor or a Dollar General serving the Security-Widefield submarket carries a different demand profile than a comparable asset in a market dependent on a single private employer.

Life companies are attracted to Colorado Springs for a specific reason beyond the military footprint: the pricing dynamic relative to Denver. Cap rates in Colorado Springs generally run 25 to 75 basis points wider than comparable Denver assets, which means better going-in debt yield coverage on the same credit tenant, the same lease term, and the same non-recourse structure. For a life company underwriting to a 30-year liability match, that spread matters. The trade is not a compromise. Colorado has no rent control, the legal environment is landlord-friendly, and the state's overall economic trajectory continues to attract institutional attention. For single-tenant NNN assets specifically, the submarkets generating the most life company interest are the Powers Corridor retail strip, the Briargate and Northgate master-planned communities along the northern I-25 spine, and military-adjacent retail nodes in the Falcon and Security-Widefield areas.

That said, life company capital in Colorado Springs is selective. This is not a market where a lender will stretch on a secondary credit tenant, a short lease term, or a location without demonstrated trade area depth. The military stability creates a floor, not a ceiling, for underwriting standards. Assets that perform well are properties with investment-grade tenants, absolute NNN lease structures, 10 or more years of remaining term, and locations with genuine population density and traffic counts. Life company lenders who know this market understand that the Briargate submarket functions differently from the downtown core, and that I-25 access is a meaningful differentiator for logistics and service retail tenants with delivery dependencies.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Colorado Springs Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease assets in Colorado Springs follows a consistent framework, and sponsors should understand the parameters before engaging a lender. On leverage, the standard range is 55 to 65 percent LTV for most transactions, with the ability to reach 70 to 75 percent for investment-grade tenants with long remaining lease terms and strong DSCR coverage. Life companies are not high-leverage lenders. Their competitive position is on price and structure, not on proceeds. If a sponsor needs 75 percent or above, life company capital is not the right tool regardless of tenant credit.

On rate, the 2026 environment places the 10-year Treasury around 4.30 percent. Life company spreads for well-located, investment-grade net lease assets in a market like Colorado Springs typically run 125 to 175 basis points over the matched Treasury, with the tightest execution reserved for Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and similar national credit tenants on long absolute NNN leases. All-in rates for those deals are competitive with or inside CMBS execution when you account for the structural differences: non-recourse, full-term interest-only for qualified tenants, no defeasance requirement, and yield maintenance prepayment that is structurally cleaner for long-hold strategies. DSCR requirements generally run 1.25x or better at the loan constant, which at current rate levels is achievable at 65 percent LTV for most investment-grade Colorado Springs assets trading at market cap rates.

Lease term is the single most important underwriting variable after tenant credit. Life companies want the loan term to match or be covered by the remaining lease term. A 15-year fixed loan requires meaningful lease coverage. A 10-year loan on a 12-year lease is a conversation; a 15-year loan on a 7-year lease is not. Sponsors with near-term lease rollover need to address that before approaching life company capital, or bring a blend-and-extend structure to the table at origination.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Colorado Springs

Life company financing wins decisively in a specific scenario: long-hold, stabilized, investment-grade net lease assets where a sponsor wants the lowest fixed rate, non-recourse structure, and a loan term matched to the lease. In that scenario, life company execution is better than CMBS on structure, better than bank balance sheet on term and rate, and better than debt funds on cost of capital.

CMBS is a legitimate alternative for Colorado Springs net lease deals, particularly in the $5M to $15M range where CMBS execution is efficient and life company interest may be lighter. CMBS offers higher proceeds at 70 to 75 percent LTV and more flexibility on secondary credit tenants, but defeasance prepayment is a meaningful constraint for sponsors who may sell or refinance before maturity, and the servicer relationship in a CMBS deal is not the lender relationship.

Bank balance sheet lending is appropriate for shorter-term business plans, value-add repositioning, or deals where a sponsor needs more leverage than life companies will provide. Bank pricing on stabilized net lease may compete on rate in certain environments, but 3 to 5 year terms with extension options do not match the liability profile of a long-term net lease investor the way a 15 or 20 year fixed-rate life company structure does.

Debt funds occupy the high-leverage, higher-rate space. They are the right tool for transitional assets, lease-up situations, or sponsors who need 75 to 80 percent proceeds and can absorb floating-rate risk. For a stabilized Dollar General with 15 years remaining on a NNN lease in Security-Widefield, a debt fund is the wrong capital source. A life company correspondent loan is the right one.

Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is specific to multifamily and does not apply to single-tenant net lease retail. If you are financing military-adjacent multifamily in Colorado Springs, agency execution is typically the most efficient permanent capital available and should be evaluated separately from the life company net lease program.
