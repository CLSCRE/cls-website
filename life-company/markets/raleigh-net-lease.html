Life Company Net Lease Lending in Raleigh, NC

Raleigh and the broader Research Triangle have moved decisively into the first tier of life company target markets over the past several years, and net lease assets in this corridor are benefiting directly from that capital flow. The drivers are structural, not cyclical. Apple's North Campus commitment, sustained Amazon and Google investment, and the enduring employment anchor of NC State, Duke, and UNC have produced a labor market that life company underwriters view as durable across a 20-year hold horizon. That long-view underwriting mentality is exactly how life companies think, and Raleigh increasingly fits their model. Wake County's pro-development zoning posture and North Carolina's absence of rent control or tenant-friendly landlord statutes give life company credit committees clean, predictable assumptions when they underwrite net lease properties here.

For single-tenant NNN assets specifically, life company capital is most active in suburban retail corridors where traffic counts are strong and tenant credit is investment grade. Brier Creek, Cary, Apex, and the Morrisville submarket along the I-540 technology corridor are producing the deal volume that life companies want to see. A Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, or Starbucks in a high-traffic node along I-540 or US-1 checks every box on a life company's acquisition criteria: credit tenant, absolute NNN lease, predictable NOI, and a submarket with genuine long-term demand drivers. Anchored retail in North Hills and along the NC-54 corridor in Cary is also attracting life company attention when the anchor credit and lease term align.

Life companies are selective in Raleigh, not absent. Deals that trade below investment-grade tenant credit, carry significant near-term lease rollover, or sit in secondary locations without strong traffic and demographic fundamentals will not clear life company credit committees regardless of how attractive the in-place yield appears. Sponsors who understand this selectivity and bring the right assets to the right lender move quickly. Those who do not understand it lose time working through channels that are not a genuine fit.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Raleigh Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting on Raleigh net lease transactions follows a consistent framework that experienced sponsors should understand before approaching this market. Loan-to-value typically runs 60 to 70 percent, with the upper end available for investment-grade credit tenants carrying long remaining lease terms. On an absolute NNN asset with a corporate-guaranteed lease and 15 or more years of term remaining, life companies will occasionally reach toward 75 percent LTV, but 60 to 65 percent is the standard working assumption for deal sizing. These are not leverage-maximizing loans. They are long-duration, low-risk instruments priced accordingly.

Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 25 years are standard, and the best execution almost always involves matching the loan term to the remaining lease term. A 15-year fixed loan on a 15-year Dollar General lease in Apex eliminates balloon risk, eliminates rollover risk at maturity, and creates the kind of clean duration matching that life company investment committees value. In the current 2026 rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury around 4.30 percent, life company spreads on investment-grade NNN tenants in strong markets like Raleigh are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the relevant Treasury, depending on tenant credit, lease structure, and property location. That all-in rate is meaningfully competitive with CMBS and bank balance sheet alternatives for stabilized, long-hold assets.

DSCR requirements typically land at 1.25x or better at origination, though for investment-grade credit tenants with full-term interest-only structures, effective DSCR coverage can be evaluated differently given the contractual certainty of the rent stream. Non-recourse structure is standard across life company net lease loans. Carve-outs for standard bad acts apply, but life company execution does not require personal guarantees from the sponsoring entity. That non-recourse feature is a material differentiator versus bank balance sheet lending, where recourse is often a condition of credit approval regardless of deal quality.

Typical Raleigh Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Raleigh market today looks something like this. Loan size ranges from $3 million to $20 million for single-tenant assets, with portfolio transactions reaching $50 million or more. The underlying property is typically a 2010 or newer single-tenant retail or QSR building on a hard corner or high-traffic suburban corridor in Brier Creek, Cary, Apex, or along I-540. The tenant is investment-grade, the lease is absolute NNN with no landlord obligations, and remaining lease term is 10 years minimum, with 12 to 20 years preferred. Occupancy is 100 percent by definition on single-tenant assets; the underwriting question is lease quality, not occupancy rate.

Sponsor profile matters but is not the primary credit. Life company net lease underwriting is tenant-credit-forward. A well-organized, experienced sponsoring entity with clean financials and a track record of net lease ownership will close faster, but life company credit committees are primarily buying the tenant's covenant, not the sponsor's balance sheet. Timeline from executed LOI to closing as a direct correspondent lender runs 45 to 75 days for a clean deal. Comparable transactions placed through brokered channels typically require 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on the home office queue and the deal's complexity.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Raleigh

Life company execution wins on net lease assets when three conditions align: the tenant is investment grade, the hold period is long, and leverage requirements are moderate. Against CMBS, life company financing offers a cleaner execution process, a real relationship with the lender, and non-recourse structure without the securitization overhead. CMBS pricing can be competitive but the conduit process introduces servicing complexity and less flexible prepayment structures that disadvantage long-hold sponsors. Life company yield maintenance is predictable and can be modeled cleanly at acquisition.

Bank balance sheet loans are recourse, shorter-term, and typically require deposit relationships or additional cross-collateralization for larger loans. They are better suited to transitional or value-add situations where life company criteria are not yet met. Debt funds offer speed and higher leverage but at a cost of capital that rarely makes sense for stabilized, long-term-hold net lease assets. Agency execution through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is not applicable to net lease retail assets regardless of how the deal is structured.

Life company financing is the wrong tool when leverage needs exceed 70 to 75 percent, when the tenant is below investment grade, when lease term is shorter than 7 to 10 years, or when the sponsor anticipates a sale or refinance within 5 years. In those situations, bank balance sheet or debt fund execution is more appropriate. But for the right net lease asset in Raleigh with a credit tenant and a long-term hold strategy, life company correspondent capital is the most competitive and most appropriate execution available in the market today.
