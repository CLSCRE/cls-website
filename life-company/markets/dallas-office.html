Life Company Office Lending in Dallas: What the Market Looks Like Today

Dallas has earned its place as one of the premier Sun Belt markets for life insurance company permanent capital, and that reputation holds even as office lending has become more selective nationally. The fundamentals driving life company interest in Dallas are structural, not cyclical: no state income tax, sustained corporate in-migration from high-cost coastal markets, a pro-development regulatory environment, and one of the strongest employment bases in the country. For life company underwriters evaluating long-term hold risk on a 10 to 15 year fixed-rate mortgage, those factors matter considerably.

For office specifically, life company capital in Dallas has migrated toward the submarkets that have demonstrated resilience since 2020. Legacy Town Center, Uptown Dallas, and Las Colinas continue to attract life company interest for the right product, particularly Class A buildings with credit or government tenants and long weighted average lease terms. Suburban Class A in Frisco, Plano, Allen, and McKinney has also remained fundable for quality sponsorship, largely because the tenant base in those submarkets skews toward financial services, healthcare, and technology firms that have maintained or expanded their footprints rather than contracted them. Urban Class A in the CBD is a more nuanced conversation, but it is not a closed door for the right deal.

The honest characterization of the Dallas life company office market in 2025 and into 2026 is selective, not shut. Life companies have tightened their asset-level criteria, but they have not exited the market. What they have done is reduce their tolerance for speculative occupancy, near-term lease rollover, and secondary location within a submarket. A Class A building in Legacy or Uptown with strong tenancy and a weighted average lease term north of seven years is a very different conversation than a Class B suburban asset with a single tenant whose lease expires in three years.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Dallas Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Dallas office is built around permanence and predictability. These are long-term balance sheet lenders, not yield-chasing debt funds, and their criteria reflect that. In the current environment, LTV expectations for office have compressed to the 50 to 60 percent range for most transactions, with better pricing available for sponsors who come in under-leveraged. The days of 65 to 70 percent LTV life company office execution are largely behind us for most product types, though exceptionally strong deals with government-credit tenancy can still push toward the higher end of that range.

On the rate side, life company spreads for stabilized Dallas office are generally running in the 150 to 200 basis point range over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading around 4.3 percent entering 2026. That puts all-in fixed rates roughly in the mid-to-upper 5 percent range for the most competitive executions, with weaker sponsorship or secondary submarket assets pricing wider. The fixed-rate term is typically 10 to 15 years for stabilized product, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules. Prepayment is yield maintenance, which means life company debt is most cost-effective for sponsors with a genuine long-term hold strategy.

DSCR requirements have also tightened. Life companies are generally underwriting to a minimum 1.25x DSCR on in-place cash flows, and some are stress-testing to higher coverage ratios depending on the lease term profile. Non-recourse structure remains standard, though carve-out language has broadened somewhat since 2020, particularly around environmental representations and material lease modifications. For Dallas specifically, life company underwriters place considerable weight on submarket vacancy trends, tenant credit quality, and the remaining lease term relative to the loan term. A deal where the anchor tenant's lease expires before loan maturity is going to face significant scrutiny regardless of current occupancy.

Typical Dallas Life Company Office Deal Profile

The life company office deals we are closing in the Dallas market share several common characteristics. Deal size typically falls in the $10 million to $50 million range, with our most competitive executions at $15 million and above. Property vintage is generally 2000 or newer, though well-maintained assets with recent capital investment can qualify depending on location and tenancy. Class A designation is the baseline, with select Class B medical office and government-tenanted suburban product also qualifying in the right submarkets.

Occupancy at origination is typically 90 percent or higher, with in-place leases from credit or institutional tenants anchoring the underwriting. Weighted average lease term at closing should generally exceed five years, with preference for deals where the WALT extends well into the loan term. Sponsor profile matters to life company lenders: they are looking for experienced commercial real estate operators with a track record on comparable assets, clean balance sheets, and the financial strength to satisfy the non-recourse carve-out structure.

Timeline from accepted term sheet to closing on a direct correspondent basis typically runs 45 to 75 days for a well-prepared transaction. Borrowers coming from an indirect brokered process, or those shopping among multiple life companies through intermediaries, should plan for 90 to 120 days at minimum, often longer if home office review surfaces questions that require sponsor response and resubmission.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Dallas Office

For stabilized Dallas office, life company financing competes primarily against CMBS, bank balance sheet lending, and debt funds. Each has a role depending on the deal profile, and being honest about those tradeoffs is more useful than reflexively pushing life company execution.

CMBS can offer higher leverage (up to 65 to 70 percent in some structures) and more flexible underwriting for secondary assets, but the execution is slower in practice, prepayment through defeasance or yield maintenance is equally restrictive, and servicer relationships post-closing are a known friction point. For a sponsor with a clear exit horizon shorter than five years, CMBS flexibility may not justify the cost.

Bank balance sheet lending offers more structural flexibility, shorter terms (typically 3 to 7 years), and sometimes better pricing on smaller deals, but recourse requirements remain common and banks have pulled back materially on office exposure since 2022. Finding a bank willing to hold a $25 million office loan on balance sheet in Dallas today is a different conversation than it was three years ago.

Debt funds can close faster and go higher on leverage, but they price for that flexibility. All-in rates from debt funds on office in Dallas are frequently 200 to 300 basis points above life company execution for comparable deals, which is a meaningful cost differential on a long hold. For value-add office plays where the asset is not yet stabilized, debt funds are often the only realistic option. For stabilized Class A product with credit tenancy and a long hold horizon, life company financing is generally the most competitive permanent capital available in this market.
