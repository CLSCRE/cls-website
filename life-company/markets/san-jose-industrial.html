Life Company Industrial Lending in San Jose

San Jose occupies a singular position in the life company capital markets landscape. As the civic and commercial core of Silicon Valley, the South Bay commands attention from institutional lenders that rarely bother with secondary or tertiary industrial markets. The demand generators here are not abstract: Apple, Cisco, Intel, Nvidia, Google, and Adobe anchor an employer base that drives sustained logistics consumption, last-mile distribution demand, and light industrial absorption across the I-880 and US-101 corridors. Life insurance companies underwrote industrial conservatively for decades, but in 2025 and 2026 the asset class has become one of the most actively pursued categories in their permanent mortgage portfolios, and San Jose is near the top of their preferred market list.

The I-880 industrial corridor running through Milpitas, North San Jose, and into the East Bay is the primary submarket where life company capital is most aggressively priced. Bulk warehouse and Class A distribution product along this corridor attracts the tightest spreads in the life company stack, reflecting the depth of the tenant base and the scarcity of functional, modern logistics product in a supply-constrained coastal market. Milpitas, in particular, has emerged as a preferred node for last-mile distribution given its proximity to both the Port of Oakland freight network and Silicon Valley's dense residential population. Infill light industrial parks in North San Jose with multi-tenant occupancy and strong credit tenancy also qualify, provided lease structures and occupancy metrics meet life company stabilization thresholds.

Life companies are selective in this market, not absent. Properties with deferred maintenance, short weighted average lease terms, or significant rollover exposure within the first five years of the loan term will face either pricing adjustments or outright passes from life company credit desks. San Jose's land costs and construction costs create a natural replacement cost floor that supports valuations, but life company underwriters focus on in-place cash flow and lease credit quality before they weigh market fundamentals. Sponsors bringing stabilized, modern industrial product with institutional-grade tenancy will find life company execution here to be among the most competitive permanent financing available anywhere in the country.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Jose Industrial Deals

Life company credit criteria for industrial in San Jose are consistent with the broader program parameters, applied through the lens of a market they know well. Loan-to-value ratios typically fall in the 55 to 65 percent range on stabilized product, with credit-tenant NNN deals sometimes reaching 70 percent where lease structure and tenant financial strength support it. Lenders in this program are not stretching for leverage; they are competing on rate, structure, and certainty of execution for sponsors who have the equity to take a conservative LTV in exchange for the best fixed-rate coupon available in the permanent market.

On the rate side, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.30 percent in the current environment, life company spreads on stabilized industrial in a market like San Jose typically run in the 125 to 175 basis point range depending on property quality, lease term, and tenant credit. Class A logistics with long-term NNN leases to credit tenants can trade at the tight end of that range or tighter. The result is all-in fixed rates that remain highly competitive against CMBS and bank balance sheet alternatives for the right deal, especially when non-recourse structure and full-term fixed rate certainty are factored into the comparison.

DSCR requirements generally sit at 1.25x or better on standard amortizing structures, with interest-only periods available for trophy distribution product that meets specific asset quality and tenancy thresholds. Loan terms run 10 to 25 years fixed, with the longer end of that range reserved for credit-tenant NNN transactions where lease term aligns with the loan maturity. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which matters for hold period planning. Non-recourse with standard carveouts is the norm across the program. For a San Jose industrial owner with a stabilized, well-leased asset and a long-term hold strategy, these terms are structurally superior to most alternatives in the current market.

Typical San Jose Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in San Jose might look like this: a $15 million to $40 million first mortgage on a Class A bulk distribution or last-mile logistics facility along the I-880 corridor in Milpitas or North San Jose, built within the last 15 years, with 95 percent or greater occupancy, a weighted average lease term of seven years or more, and one or more investment-grade or nationally recognized tenants. The sponsor is typically an institutional owner, a regional developer with a long-term hold strategy, or a private equity group recapitalizing a stabilized asset after lease-up. The loan request is non-recourse, fixed rate for 10 to 15 years, with either a 25 to 30-year amortization or an interest-only period negotiated at origination.

Light industrial parks in North San Jose with multi-tenant occupancy can qualify as well, provided the overall occupancy and rent roll stability meet life company thresholds. Flex industrial with a heavy office component receives more scrutiny in the current environment given broader office market headwinds, but pure industrial or warehouse-dominant flex product remains on the approved list. Minimum deal size for the program is $3 million, though our preference and the life company's preference align around $10 million and above, where the economics justify the underwriting depth these lenders require.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Jose

Industrial is not an agency-eligible asset class, so the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac comparison is relevant only for sponsors with mixed portfolios evaluating capital allocation across property types. For industrial specifically, the permanent financing alternatives are life company, CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds.

CMBS offers higher leverage in some cases and more flexibility on property type and occupancy, but the fixed-rate execution is generally priced wider than life company for quality assets, the non-recourse structure comes with more restrictive servicing and cash management requirements, and the defeasance prepayment mechanism adds complexity for sponsors who anticipate asset sales. For a San Jose industrial owner with a clean, stabilized deal and a 10-plus year hold, life company execution will nearly always produce a better coupon and cleaner loan documents than CMBS.

Bank balance sheet lending offers relationship flexibility and sometimes higher leverage, but floating-rate exposure and 3 to 5-year term structures create refinance risk that institutional owners increasingly price carefully. Debt funds are the right call for value-add, transitional, or lease-up scenarios where a life company will not lend at all. They are not competitive on rate or structure for stabilized, long-term hold industrial in a market like San Jose.

Life company execution wins when the deal is stabilized, the hold period is long, the loan size is meaningful, and the sponsor values fixed-rate certainty and non-recourse structure over leverage. In San Jose's industrial market in 2025 and 2026, those conditions describe a significant share of the institutional quality product changing hands or being refinanced.
