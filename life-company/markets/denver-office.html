Life Company Office Lending in Denver, CO

Denver has earned a durable position on the target list for life insurance company capital, and that holds true even in a more selective post-2020 office environment. The fundamentals that drive life company underwriting confidence are present here: no rent control, a diversified employment base anchored by technology, energy, aerospace, and federal government agencies, and sustained in-migration that continues to support commercial occupancy across asset classes. For office specifically, life companies remain active in Denver's best suburban submarkets, particularly the Denver Tech Center and Centennial corridor, where Class A product with long weighted average lease terms and creditworthy tenancy has historically demonstrated the rent stability life companies require.

That said, the Denver office story requires honest framing in 2026. Urban core office, including portions of LoDo and the central business district, faces the same structural headwinds as peer markets nationwide. Life companies are not blanket avoiding Denver office, but they are underwriting to a higher standard than they applied pre-pandemic. Medical office and government-tenanted suburban product in submarkets like Englewood, Lakewood, and the DTC command the most competitive execution. Multi-tenanted suburban Class A with low vacancy and a strong in-place WALT continues to attract life company interest. Class B in transitional submarkets is a harder conversation, and borrowers should have realistic expectations before beginning the process.

The submarkets drawing the most active life company attention for office right now cluster along the I-25 corridor south of downtown. The DTC remains the benchmark suburban office submarket in Colorado, and Centennial has developed enough depth in its tenant base to support serious life company underwriting. Life companies that are active in Denver's industrial and multifamily space, given Aurora's logistics market and the LoDo-to-Highlands residential corridor, are often the same lenders evaluating quality suburban office on a selective basis. That institutional familiarity with the Denver market works in a borrower's favor when the deal characteristics are right.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Denver Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Denver office in 2026 starts with the tenancy story. Credit tenants, government leases, and medical users anchoring multi-tenanted rosters carry the most weight. A weighted average lease term of seven years or better is a reasonable threshold for competitive execution; deals with shorter WALT or near-term rollover will face either meaningful pricing adjustments or a pass. Single-tenant assets can work when the tenant credit is institutional grade and the lease structure is absolute net, but life companies are not extending that benefit of the doubt to speculative or secondary credit profiles in this cycle.

On leverage, borrowers should arrive with expectations calibrated to current market reality. LTV in the 50 to 60 percent range is the operating standard for Denver office today. Well-structured deals with under-leveraged requests, strong tenancy, and long-hold sponsors can access the lower end of the spread range, but life companies are not stretching to 65 percent on office the way they might on multifamily or industrial in this market. DSCR requirements are typically 1.30x or better at underwritten cash flow, and underwriters are stress-testing vacancy assumptions rather than crediting full in-place occupancy without a meaningful cushion.

Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 15 years remain the standard structure for stabilized Denver office, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules. With the 10-year Treasury trading near 4.3 percent in the current rate environment, all-in fixed rates for qualifying suburban Class A product are pricing in a range reflective of life company spreads generally running 125 to 175 basis points over the benchmark for well-structured deals, with wider spreads for urban product or shorter lease terms. Prepayment is yield maintenance as a standard condition. Non-recourse structures are available, though carve-out provisions have broadened somewhat in the current environment and borrowers should review those terms carefully before committing.

Typical Denver Life Company Office Deal Profile

The office deals that close efficiently through Commercial Lending Solutions' direct correspondent program in Denver tend to share a recognizable profile. Transaction size is typically $10M to $50M, with the most competitive execution emerging at $15M and above where life company capital is fully optimized. Property vintage is generally 2000 or newer, though well-maintained 1990s suburban product with recent capital improvements and strong in-place occupancy can qualify. Class A suburban assets in the DTC and Centennial corridor are the clearest fit; medical office and government-tenanted product in Englewood and Lakewood represent adjacent opportunities that life companies are actively underwriting.

Occupancy requirements at underwriting are typically 90 percent or better, with a WALT supporting the full loan term or close to it. Sponsors are experienced commercial real estate operators with demonstrated long-hold strategies. Life companies are not the right capital source for a sponsor planning an exit in three to five years; the yield maintenance prepayment structure reflects that alignment of interest.

Timeline from executed LOI to closing runs 45 to 75 days for a direct correspondent transaction. Borrowers coming from a brokered process or a bank balance sheet submission should anticipate that a direct correspondent timeline compresses the process meaningfully compared to the 75 to 120 or more days that an indirect or home office submission process typically requires.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Denver Office

Life company financing is the right tool for a specific kind of Denver office deal, and it is worth being clear about where other capital sources are more appropriate. CMBS is available for stabilized office but carries securitization mechanics, servicer relationships, and prepayment structures that many long-hold borrowers find less favorable than life company yield maintenance; CMBS can also work for deals that do not meet life company tenancy standards. Bank balance sheet lenders remain active in the Denver market and can accommodate shorter terms, partial recourse structures, and more flexible prepayment, but floating rate exposure and balance sheet capacity constraints make banks less competitive for long-term stabilized executions in the current rate environment.

Debt funds fill the value-add and transitional space that life companies will not touch: shorter-term bridge financing for office assets with occupancy below life company thresholds, lease-up stories, or repositioning business plans. For those deals, a debt fund or construction bridge lender is the right starting point, with a life company refinance as the target exit once the asset stabilizes.

Agency execution (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) does not apply to office and is specific to multifamily. For Denver borrowers who own multifamily alongside office in their portfolio, the distinction matters: life company execution for multifamily in Denver's core submarkets is competitive with agency on large loans with long hold requirements, but agency products carry different prepayment and leverage profiles that often win for borrowers with different exit assumptions.

Life company financing wins clearly when the deal is stabilized, the hold period is long, the loan size is $15M or above, the tenancy is creditworthy, and the borrower's priority is rate certainty and non-recourse structure over maximum proceeds. In Denver's current office market, that describes a narrower universe of assets than it did in prior cycles, but those deals exist and they close at competitive terms through direct correspondent execution.
