Life Company Industrial Lending in Charlotte, NC

Charlotte has emerged as one of the Southeast's most compelling targets for life insurance company industrial capital, and the reasoning is straightforward to any underwriter who has spent time in the market. The region sits at the intersection of two of the most active logistics corridors in the Carolinas: the I-85 industrial spine running northeast toward Concord and Kannapolis, and the I-485 outer loop serving last-mile distribution demand generated by Charlotte's rapidly expanding suburban population base. Life companies allocating permanent capital in 2025 and 2026 are not looking for growth stories. They are looking for stabilized, income-producing assets in markets with durable demand drivers, and Charlotte's industrial market delivers exactly that.

The underlying economic fundamentals reinforce the case. Charlotte's concentration of major financial institutions, including Bank of America, Truist, and Wells Fargo, anchors a white-collar employment base that supports consumer spending and, by extension, distribution activity serving the metro. Population growth across the northern and southern suburbs continues to compress last-mile delivery times for carriers and third-party logistics operators, which translates directly into leasing demand for well-located bulk warehouse and distribution facilities in Concord, Huntersville, and the Matthews submarket. Life companies underwriting these assets in Charlotte are working with a market narrative that requires relatively little explanation. The demand drivers are structural, the rent growth has been real, and the tenant credit profiles in newer Class A projects tend to be institutional.

That said, life company capital is not indiscriminate. Older light industrial parks with short lease terms, functional obsolescence, or weak tenancy will not attract life company interest regardless of how strong the Charlotte macro story looks. The assets that generate the most competitive life company execution in this market are Class A logistics facilities along the I-85 corridor, credit-tenant NNN distribution buildings in the Concord and Kannapolis submarkets, and well-leased bulk warehouse product where the remaining lease term supports a 10-year or longer fixed-rate commitment. Sponsors who understand where the life company appetite starts and stops in Charlotte will spend far less time chasing capital that was never going to close.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Charlotte Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting industrial assets in Charlotte are working within a consistent set of structural parameters that reflect the broader permanence of their capital. Loan-to-value ratios for stabilized industrial assets typically sit in the 55 to 65 percent range, with credit-tenant NNN structures occasionally supporting leverage up to 70 percent where the lease term and tenant credit justify the exception. These are not aggressive leverage providers. Sponsors coming out of a construction loan or a value-add bridge structure expecting to refinance at 70 to 75 percent loan-to-cost will generally find the life company market a mismatch unless significant equity has been created during the hold period.

On the rate side, the 2026 environment has life company industrial pricing coming in at spreads of roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range. Class A logistics and distribution product with strong tenancy can tighten inside that range, making life company execution genuinely competitive against CMBS and bank balance sheet alternatives for the right deal. Fixed-rate terms run from 10 to 25 years, with full-term interest-only periods available for trophy distribution assets where the cash flow profile supports it. Amortization schedules, when required, are typically 25 to 30 years.

Debt service coverage requirements generally run from 1.25x to 1.35x on a trailing or in-place basis, and life companies underwriting Charlotte industrial assets will stress the rent roll carefully. Lease term relative to the loan maturity matters enormously. A 10-year loan on a distribution facility with seven years of lease term remaining will face a much harder look than the same deal with a lease extending through or beyond the maturity date. Non-recourse structure is standard in this program, with carve-outs for standard bad acts. Sponsors with a clean credit history, institutional-quality reporting, and a demonstrated track record in industrial ownership are the profile that moves through life company underwriting most efficiently.

Typical Charlotte Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The life company industrial deals we close in the Charlotte market tend to share a recognizable profile. Loan sizes typically fall between $10 million and $60 million, though our program accommodates transactions from $3 million up to $100 million for the right asset. The property is generally a Class A or Class B-plus industrial facility built within the last 15 years, located in one of the primary logistics corridors: I-85 toward Concord and Kannapolis, the I-485 distribution ring, or established light industrial parks in Huntersville or Matthews.

Occupancy requirements for life company execution are effectively stabilized, meaning 90 percent or better with a lease roll profile that does not create near-term concentration risk inside the loan term. Single-tenant NNN distribution buildings with investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenants are among the most efficient executions we see in this market. Sponsor profile matters: life companies are underwriting the borrower as well as the asset, and institutional-quality reporting, clean financials, and relevant industrial ownership experience all contribute to a smoother process.

Timeline from signed term sheet to closing for a direct correspondent life company transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days depending on due diligence complexity. Comparable transactions placed by brokers through a life company's correspondent or home office desk routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer, primarily because of the added communication layers and approval steps that a direct relationship eliminates.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Charlotte Industrial

For stabilized Charlotte industrial assets with strong tenancy and long hold periods, life company execution is difficult to beat on rate, structure, and certainty of close for loans in the $10 million and above range. The non-recourse structure, long fixed-rate term, and potential for interest-only periods are features that other capital sources do not consistently offer together at competitive pricing.

CMBS is the closest competitor for non-recourse, fixed-rate permanent debt on stabilized industrial assets. Life company execution will generally outperform CMBS on rate for high-quality deals, and the servicing relationship during the loan term is meaningfully cleaner with life company debt than with a securitized product. Bank balance sheet lenders can offer flexibility and speed, but they typically require recourse, carry floating rate exposure or shorter fixed-rate windows, and are less competitive on pricing for larger permanent loans. Debt funds are well suited for transitional or value-add industrial assets, bridge financing, or higher-leverage situations where life company parameters do not fit, but their cost of capital reflects that flexibility.

Life company capital is genuinely the wrong tool for value-add plays, sub-90-percent occupancy situations, or deals where the business plan calls for a three-to-five year hold with a near-term disposition. Yield maintenance prepayment provisions mean that life company debt carries real prepayment costs in a rate decline scenario. Sponsors with shorter exit horizons or meaningful lease-up risk ahead should be looking at bank balance sheet or debt fund options first. When the deal is stabilized, the hold is long, and the loan size clears the threshold, life company execution in Charlotte is consistently among the most competitive in the market.
