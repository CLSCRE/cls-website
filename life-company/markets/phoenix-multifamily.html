Life Company Multifamily Lending in Phoenix: Market Overview

Phoenix has emerged as one of the most actively targeted Sun Belt markets for life insurance company permanent capital, and the reasons are structural rather than cyclical. Population inflows from California, the Pacific Northwest, and the Midwest have sustained renter demand across a broad swath of metro submarkets, while the arrival of TSMC Arizona, Intel's semiconductor expansion in Chandler, and a growing advanced manufacturing corridor in the West Valley have added a durable employment base that life company underwriters find genuinely compelling. For permanent lenders with 10 to 25 year investment horizons, Phoenix's no-rent-control operating environment and diversified economic drivers represent exactly the kind of predictable cash flow profile that matches their liability structure.

Life company capital in Phoenix is most active on Class A multifamily, with particular concentration in Tempe, Scottsdale, Chandler, and Gilbert. These submarkets offer the combination of institutional-quality product, strong renter demographics, and proximity to major employment nodes that life company credit committees prioritize. Goodyear and Peoria have also drawn attention as rent growth has extended outward from the urban core and new supply in those corridors has been absorbed at a pace that supports stabilized underwriting. North Phoenix and Mesa represent secondary targets where the right asset with the right sponsorship can clear life company credit standards, though the bar for occupancy and vintage is higher in those locations.

It is worth noting that life companies have spent the past 18 to 24 months working through the supply digestion that hit Class A Phoenix multifamily in 2023 and 2024. That cycle has largely run its course in the core submarkets, and life companies are re-engaging on stabilized product with greater conviction heading into 2026. They remain selective, however. They are not writing loans on lease-up assets, value-add plays with meaningful vacancy, or properties with deferred capital needs. The Phoenix deals clearing life company credit today are stabilized, well-maintained assets with strong in-place rent rolls and sponsors with demonstrable operating track records in the market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Phoenix Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting in Phoenix starts with stabilization. Most lenders in this channel require physical occupancy above 90 percent for a minimum of 90 days prior to loan commitment, with in-place rents that can support underwritten cash flow without relying on lease-up projections or market rent assumptions that exceed actual achieved rents at the subject or immediate comps. For Phoenix Class A assets, that standard is achievable in the right submarkets, but sponsors need to arrive at the conversation with clean trailing financials rather than a pro forma story.

On leverage, life companies are generally writing Phoenix multifamily at 55 to 65 percent LTV for standard deals, with the lower end of that range more common on assets where in-place cash flow is tighter or the submarket is newer to the life company's approved target list. Premier sponsors with strong balance sheets and a track record of institutional multifamily operations can access up to 65 percent with more favorable terms in core submarkets like Scottsdale and Tempe. Debt service coverage requirements typically run 1.25x to 1.35x on a stabilized basis, with some lenders requiring a minimum stressed DSCR tested against a rate floor.

On rate, life company all-in pricing for Phoenix stabilized multifamily is currently tracking 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency execution, with spreads over the 10-year Treasury generally in the 125 to 175 basis point range for most stabilized Class A and B assets. With the 10-year Treasury in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range heading into 2026, sponsors should model all-in fixed rates in a range consistent with those spreads. Terms run 10 to 25 years fixed, with 25 to 30 year amortization, and interest-only periods are available for deals with strong sponsorship and lower leverage. Non-recourse is standard, with the customary carve-outs for fraud, voluntary bankruptcy, and environmental liability.

Questions go back and forth between the broker and the home office, structure requests require escalation, and approval timelines stretch. Borrowers in that process often do not know exactly where their deal stands or who is making the final credit call.

As a direct correspondent lender, we eliminate that layer entirely. Our borrowers have a single underwriting relationship, a single point of contact for credit questions and structure decisions, and a closing timeline that reflects our direct authority rather than the pace of a home office review queue. That is not a marketing claim. It is a function of how the correspondent structure is legally and operationally organized, and it shows up in deal timelines, term sheet specificity, and the ability to have substantive credit conversations before a formal application is submitted.

Typical Phoenix Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

The life company multifamily deals we close in Phoenix typically fall in the $8 million to $50 million range, though our program extends from $3 million at the floor to $75 million for the right asset and sponsor. The sweet spot is a 150 to 400 unit Class A or strong Class B asset built within the last 15 years, stabilized above 92 percent occupancy, located in a core submarket with demonstrated rent growth and institutional comparable transactions. Tempe, Scottsdale, Chandler, and Goodyear have produced the most consistent deal flow under this profile.

Sponsors on these transactions are typically experienced multifamily operators with Phoenix market history, institutional net worth relative to the loan amount, and a clear long-term hold strategy. Life company capital is a poor match for sponsors planning a 3 to 5 year exit, given prepayment structure. It is an excellent match for operators who want to lock in long-term fixed-rate debt, exit floating rate exposure, and structure permanent financing that matches their expected ownership horizon.

From executed term sheet to closing, our direct correspondent process typically runs 45 to 75 days. Deals routed through brokers to a life company home office more commonly take 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on the lender's pipeline and internal review process. For sponsors managing acquisition timelines or refinance windows, that difference is material.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Phoenix Multifamily

For stabilized Phoenix multifamily above $5 million with a long hold strategy, life company execution competes directly with agency lending through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Agency remains the dominant permanent financing channel for Phoenix multifamily at most leverage points, and for deals where a borrower needs 70 to 80 percent LTV, agency will generally win on proceeds. Life company execution wins on rate for qualifying deals at 55 to 65 percent LTV, and on flexibility of term structure for sponsors who want options that agency programs do not offer, including longer fixed periods and more negotiable prepayment structures.

CMBS is available for Phoenix multifamily but is rarely the optimal execution for stabilized Class A and B assets at the loan sizes where life company capital is competitive. CMBS pricing is generally wider than life company spreads on comparable product, and the securitization structure introduces servicing complexity that long-term holders often prefer to avoid. CMBS makes more sense for asset types or deal structures outside the life company credit box.

Bank balance sheet and debt fund capital are better suited to value-add, transitional, or shorter-hold Phoenix multifamily deals. Banks can move faster on smaller loans and tolerate more occupancy variability, while debt funds offer bridge capital with flexible structures for assets in transition. Neither is competitive with life company permanent financing on a long-term stabilized basis when rate, term, and non-recourse execution are the primary criteria.
