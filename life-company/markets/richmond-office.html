Life Company Office Lending in Richmond: What the Market Looks Like Today

Richmond occupies an interesting position in the life company office lending landscape. It is not a primary gateway market, but it is far from ignored. Life insurance companies active in Virginia view Richmond as a stable secondary market underpinned by durable institutional demand drivers: state government employment concentrated in the Capitol District, the Virginia Commonwealth University Medical Center complex anchoring significant medical office demand, and Capital One Financial's long-established suburban campus in the West End. These are exactly the kinds of credit-quality occupancy stories that life company underwriters want to see before committing long-term fixed-rate capital to an office asset in the current environment.

Post-2020, life company office lending nationally has become considerably more selective, and Richmond is no exception. The broad suburban office market in markets like Henrico and Chesterfield carries real occupancy uncertainty, particularly for properties without government or investment-grade credit tenants. However, Class A medical office properties near the MCV and VCU Health campuses continue to attract life company attention, as do well-leased suburban properties in Short Pump and Midlothian with strong weighted average lease terms and creditworthy anchor tenants. These submarkets have demonstrated resilience that generic suburban office cannot claim right now.

Life companies are being honest about what they will and will not touch in the current Richmond office market. Multi-tenanted Class A properties with low vacancy, long WALTs, and government or credit anchor tenants represent the fundable tier. Older Class B product in secondary corridors without a compelling tenancy story is simply not meeting life company credit standards today, regardless of price. Sponsors considering a life company execution for Richmond office need to enter that conversation with clear eyes about where the bar is set.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Richmond Office Deals

The underwriting framework life companies apply to Richmond office deals in 2026 reflects both broader sector caution and the specific dynamics of this market. Leverage expectations have compressed from historical norms. Most lenders are operating in a 50 to 60 percent LTV range for office, with the lower end of that range commanding meaningfully better execution on rate and structure. Sponsors who can bring equity to the table and avoid pushing leverage are the ones winning the best terms. Debt yields north of 8 percent are a common threshold requirement in the current environment, and DSCR expectations of 1.35 to 1.50 times are standard for well-leased product.

On rate, life company office spreads in 2026 are running roughly 150 to 200 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.25 to 4.50 percent range. That translates to all-in fixed rates in the mid-to-high 6 percent range for qualifying product, though spread variation is meaningful depending on asset quality, tenancy, and LTV. Medical office with government-affiliated or health system tenants in Richmond commands the tightest spreads. Suburban Class A with strong private-sector credit tenants typically prices in the middle of that range. These are not guarantees; actual execution depends on deal-specific underwriting.

Life companies offering 10 to 25 year fixed-rate terms expect yield maintenance prepayment provisions as standard. Non-recourse structures are available, though sponsors should expect potentially broader carve-outs in the current environment given heightened lender sensitivity to office sector risk. Properties should carry institutional-quality property management, current environmental clearance, and a lease roll profile that demonstrates income stability over the loan term. In Richmond specifically, properties with state agency or VCU Health tenancy have the clearest path to life company approval.

Loan sizes typically start at $5 million but the program becomes most competitive at $15 million and above. The properties are generally 1990s vintage or newer Class A, located in established suburban corridors like Short Pump, the West End, or the MCV health campus area. Occupancy at closing is 90 percent or better, with a weighted average lease term that extends meaningfully into the loan period. The best deals have at least one anchor tenant with investment-grade credit or a government covenant.

Life Company Versus Other Permanent Options in Richmond

Life company financing is not the right answer for every Richmond office deal, and understanding when it wins and when it does not is part of working with a lender who gives honest guidance.

CMBS is a legitimate alternative for office deals where pricing matters more than relationship and the sponsor is comfortable with a securitized structure. CMBS can sometimes accommodate slightly higher leverage than life companies are currently offering, and it does not require the same depth of sponsor relationship. The tradeoffs are servicer complexity, less flexibility on structure, and prepayment constraints that differ from yield maintenance in ways that matter at disposition.

Bank balance sheet lending remains active in Richmond for office at smaller loan sizes and shorter terms. Regional banks and community lenders will do five to seven year terms with recourse, which works for sponsors who want more flexibility than life company yield maintenance allows. The rate is typically less competitive for long-term fixed, and the term does not match a long-hold business plan the way a 15 or 20 year life company execution does.

Debt funds will take on office deals that life companies and CMBS pass on, including value-add, transitional, and lower-occupancy scenarios. The cost of capital is higher, but for deals that do not meet life company stabilization standards, a debt fund bridge with a defined path to permanent financing is often the right structure.

Life company execution is most competitive when the hold period is long, the property is stabilized with credit tenancy, the loan size is $15 million or above, and the sponsor's priority is the lowest long-term fixed cost of capital available in the market. For that profile of deal in Richmond, no other execution source currently matches it.
