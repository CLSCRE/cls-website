Life Company Net Lease Lending in Denver, CO

Denver has established itself as one of the Mountain West's most active life company target markets, and net lease assets have benefited directly from that institutional appetite. Life insurance companies are drawn to Denver for reasons that align precisely with their underwriting discipline: no rent control, consistent population inflows, a diversified employment base anchored by technology and energy, and a retail corridor system that supports the kind of credit tenant absorption that makes absolute NNN properties pencil at institutional leverage. When a Walgreens or AutoZone occupies a freestanding building in Aurora or Lakewood on a 15-year absolute NNN lease, the income stream looks almost identical to a fixed-income instrument, which is exactly how life companies underwrite it.

The submarkets that attract the heaviest life company interest for net lease retail are concentrated along the suburban corridors where single-tenant credit retailers have planted flags for the long term. Englewood, Lakewood, Aurora, and the Denver Tech Center submarket see consistent deal flow on QSR pads, pharmacy net lease, dollar store portfolios, and auto parts locations. Brighton and the broader I-70 East logistics corridor draw life company capital toward industrial net lease as well, though freestanding suburban retail remains the core NNN product type. Cherry Creek and LoDo command premium pricing, but the suburban trade area corridors are where most net lease deal volume originates because the land basis and tenant mix support the long lease structures life companies require.

Life companies have financed net lease real estate for decades, and Denver's trajectory reinforces that commitment. Population growth from domestic migration, a maturing tech sector, and sustained in-migration from California and the Mountain West feed the consumer base that credit tenants underwrite when they sign 15 to 20 year absolute NNN leases in suburban Denver. That demographic stability is what converts a single-tenant retail building into an institutional fixed-income asset in a life company's portfolio, and it is why life companies continue to allocate to Colorado net lease at competitive spread levels even when broader credit markets tighten.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Denver Net Lease Deals

Life company net lease underwriting in Denver is tenant-first and structure-second. The credit quality of the guarantor on the lease determines nearly every economic variable in the deal. Investment-grade tenants, Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, and their peers, command the tightest spreads and the most favorable structures. In the current 2026 rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury hovering near 4.3 percent, life company spreads for investment-grade NNN assets in strong markets like Denver are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the benchmark, producing all-in fixed rates that remain meaningfully competitive with CMBS and significantly more attractive on structure than bank balance sheet product.

Leverage is disciplined. Life companies typically size net lease loans at 60 to 65 percent LTV for standard stabilized deals, with upper-end proceeds reaching 70 to 75 percent for investment-grade tenants with 12 or more years of remaining primary term. The income coverage requirement is typically a 1.25 to 1.35 times DSCR on an underwritten basis, though investment-grade tenants often allow interest-only structures that compress the effective debt service and improve coverage metrics. Interest-only for the full loan term is common on strong-credit NNN deals and represents one of the most attractive structural features life company capital offers relative to any amortizing alternative.

Lease term alignment is critical. Life companies want the loan maturity to match or trail the primary lease expiration, which is why 10 to 25 year fixed-rate terms are the standard structure. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule protects the lender's long-dated coupon. For sponsors planning a long-term hold or a sale-leaseback where the tenant has signed a 20-year initial term, the loan structure almost designs itself. Blend and extend provisions are also available for deals approaching lease rollover, giving sponsors a tool to manage the capital stack alongside lease renegotiations rather than facing a refinance cliff.

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. That distinction is not semantic. As a direct correspondent, we underwrite, commit to, and close these loans in the life insurance company's name using life company capital, with our underwriter making credit decisions directly. The life insurance company is the lender of record. This is structurally different from a mortgage broker who packages a deal and submits it to a life company's home office, where it enters an intake queue, gets reviewed by an underwriter the sponsor has never met, and moves through an approval process the broker cannot meaningfully influence or accelerate.

In practical terms, the direct correspondent model compresses the decision timeline and sharpens the structure conversation. When a sponsor brings us a Dollar General in Aurora or an AutoZone in Englewood, we are not writing a memo and waiting for a third-party approval. We know the credit parameters our life company wants to own, we know what structures clear internal approval, and we can communicate a credible term sheet position within days rather than weeks. Those issues are removed from the equation when the originator and the lender are the same party.

For net lease specifically, where deal structure is almost entirely driven by lease document review and tenant credit analysis, the ability to have a direct conversation between the sponsor's team and the underwriting decision-maker produces materially better outcomes on structure, pricing, and closing certainty.

Typical Denver Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in Denver looks something like this: a freestanding single-tenant retail property built in the 2010s or early 2020s, occupied by an investment-grade credit tenant on a 15 to 20 year absolute NNN lease with 12 or more years of primary term remaining, located in a suburban trade area in Aurora, Lakewood, Englewood, or along the DTC corridor. Loan size typically falls between $5 million and $20 million, though portfolio transactions aggregating multiple Colorado net lease assets can reach $50 million within a single correspondent facility. The sponsor profile is typically an experienced private owner, family office, or regional REIT with a demonstrated hold strategy and no near-term value-add execution required at the property level.

Timeline from signed LOI to closing for a direct correspondent life company loan runs approximately 45 to 75 days. Brokered life company submissions, where the deal travels through a mortgage broker to a home office underwriting team and back, routinely stretch to 75 to 120 days or longer. For 1031 exchange buyers working against identification and closing deadlines, the direct correspondent timeline is often the deciding factor in lender selection.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Denver

For stabilized net lease assets with investment-grade tenants and long remaining lease terms, life company execution is the most competitive permanent financing available in the Denver market. The combination of non-recourse structure, interest-only for the full term, 10 to 25 year fixed rate, and institutional pricing that reflects the creditworthiness of the tenant rather than the speculative value of the real estate is simply not replicated by other capital sources.

CMBS is the closest competitor, offering non-recourse fixed-rate execution on a similar asset type. However, CMBS pricing typically runs wider than life company spreads for investment-grade tenants, and CMBS structures carry servicer complexities, cash management lockboxes, and modification limitations that life company loans avoid. Bank balance sheet product offers flexibility but comes with shorter terms, typically 5 to 7 years, recourse requirements, and floating rate exposure that most long-term net lease owners specifically want to eliminate. Debt funds price to return targets that result in rates 150 to 300 basis points above life company levels, making them appropriate for value-add or transitional situations but not for stabilized NNN product. Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac does not apply to net lease retail.

Life company execution loses its competitive edge when a sponsor needs high leverage above 70 percent, when the lease term remaining is under 10 years, when the tenant credit is below investment grade, or when the business plan is value-add rather than long-term stabilized hold. In those scenarios, CMBS, bank bridge, or debt fund capital is the appropriate starting point.
