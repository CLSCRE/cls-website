Life Company Net Lease Lending in Seattle: Market Context

Seattle has earned a permanent place on the short list of markets where life insurance companies actively pursue net lease allocations. The region's economic foundation, anchored by Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing, and a dense concentration of technology and logistics employers, produces the kind of long-term rent stability that life company underwriters value above almost everything else. When a Dollar General or AutoZone sits on a suburban Seattle parcel with twelve years of absolute NNN term remaining, the predictable cash flow profile maps cleanly onto a life company's liability structure in ways that few other investment types can match. That alignment is why life companies have stayed active in this market through multiple rate cycles.

Within the greater Seattle metro, life company attention for net lease product concentrates in a specific set of submarkets. Suburban retail corridors in Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Renton, and Lynnwood consistently attract institutional interest because the underlying consumer demographics are among the strongest in the Pacific Northwest. The Kent Valley and SODO corridors, while more industrial in character, also generate single-tenant net lease transactions where logistics and distribution users occupy freestanding facilities under long-term NNN leases. South Lake Union and Capitol Hill skew toward multifamily and mixed-use, but well-located ground-floor retail with credit tenants in those submarkets can qualify for life company consideration when the lease structure is right.

Washington state's broader regulatory climate adds one layer of selectivity. Seattle's 2024 rent stabilization ordinance has made life companies more cautious on older multifamily, but it has no direct bearing on single-tenant net lease underwriting. If anything, the ordinance has redirected some institutional capital toward NNN retail and industrial net lease, where rent escalations are contractually defined in the lease itself rather than subject to local policy shifts. For sponsors holding or acquiring credit-tenanted net lease assets in the Seattle metro, the current moment represents a genuine window into life company capital at competitive long-term rates.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Seattle Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease in Seattle starts with the tenant credit rating and works outward from there. Investment-grade tenants, think Walgreens, AutoZone, Dollar General, Starbucks, Taco Bell corporate, command the tightest spreads and the most favorable structural terms because the lease itself functions as the primary credit instrument. Life companies are not underwriting the real estate in isolation; they are underwriting a long-term contractual income stream backed by a creditworthy obligor. That distinction drives everything.

On leverage, standard life company executions in this market land between 60 and 70 percent loan-to-value for most stabilized single-tenant net lease assets. Investment-grade tenants with ten or more years of remaining lease term can push toward 75 percent in select cases, though most life company programs self-limit well below that ceiling as a portfolio discipline. Debt service coverage ratios typically need to clear 1.25x at a minimum, with 1.30x to 1.40x being more common in practice for non-recourse execution. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury trading in the mid-four percent range and life company spreads for investment-grade NNN assets running roughly 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark, all-in fixed rates for the strongest credits are landing in a range that remains highly competitive against other permanent capital sources for long-hold sponsors.

Term structure is a defining feature of life company net lease financing. These are 10 to 25 year fixed-rate loans, typically structured to match or slightly precede the lease expiration date. Interest-only for the full term is standard for investment-grade credit tenants and represents a meaningful cash-on-cash advantage over amortizing alternatives. Prepayment is governed by yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which matters for sponsors who intend to hold through the full lease cycle. Non-recourse is the standard structure across virtually all life company net lease executions. For Seattle sponsors building long-term hold portfolios around credit tenants, these terms are difficult to replicate elsewhere in the capital stack.

Typical Seattle Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Seattle metro currently looks something like this: a single-tenant freestanding retail property in the $3M to $20M range, situated in a suburban corridor in Bellevue, Kirkland, Renton, or Lynnwood. The tenant is an investment-grade credit operator with ten to fifteen years of remaining absolute NNN lease term. The property was built within the last fifteen years or substantially renovated, sits on a well-located hard corner or pad position, and carries zero near-term capital expenditure exposure given the NNN structure. The borrower is typically an experienced net lease investor or a family office building a long-term income portfolio, not a first-time buyer seeking maximum leverage.

On the capital side, these deals close at 60 to 65 percent LTV with full-term interest-only debt service and a 15 to 20 year fixed-rate term that matches remaining lease duration. Non-recourse execution is standard. Sponsors using Commercial Lending Solutions as direct correspondent lender typically move from signed LOI to closing commitment in 45 to 60 days, a timeline that reflects the absence of intermediary hand-offs and the direct underwriting relationship we carry with our life company capital source.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Seattle

Life company financing wins on specific terms for specific deals, and it is worth being direct about where it fits and where it does not. For stabilized, investment-grade net lease assets held over a long horizon, life company execution is genuinely difficult to beat. The combination of long fixed terms, non-recourse structure, interest-only debt service, and competitive spreads is not available anywhere else at comparable cost. CMBS can approach some of these characteristics but typically carries less favorable prepayment terms and requires securitization-driven standardization that can conflict with deal-specific structuring needs. Bank balance sheet lenders in Seattle are active and competitive for relationship-driven transactions, but most cap at five to seven year terms, require partial recourse, and cannot match the 15 to 25 year fixed-rate certainty that life companies provide.

Debt funds are the right answer for value-add situations, transitional assets, or deals that need higher leverage than 65 to 70 percent. They price that risk accordingly. Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is the dominant execution for multifamily and does not apply to single-tenant net lease retail or industrial. When a sponsor's priority is maximizing leverage, accessing short-term bridge capital, or financing a property with below-investment-grade tenancy or short lease term, life company financing is not the right fit and a candid advisor will say so clearly.
