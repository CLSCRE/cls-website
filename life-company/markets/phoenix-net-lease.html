Life Company Net Lease Lending in Phoenix: Why the Sun Belt's Fastest-Growing Metro Attracts Permanent Capital

Phoenix has become one of the most consistently targeted markets for life insurance company permanent capital in the western United States, and net lease retail sits near the top of the allocation list. The fundamentals are straightforward: population growth that has outpaced nearly every other major metro over the past decade, a diversified employment base anchored by semiconductor manufacturing, logistics, and healthcare, and a regulatory environment that imposes zero rent control. For life companies underwriting 15 to 25 year fixed positions, that combination of demand drivers and landlord-friendly policy creates exactly the kind of predictable, low-volatility income stream their actuarial models require.

The TSMC Arizona fab campus in North Phoenix and Intel's continued investment in Chandler have accelerated suburban growth in corridors that already had strong retail fundamentals. Chandler, Gilbert, and Mesa are producing NNN retail deals with investment-grade tenants, long remaining lease terms, and population density growth that supports credit tenant renewals. Scottsdale and North Phoenix submarkets are drawing medical office and pharmacy tenants with the income profile life companies underwrite most aggressively. The West Valley, particularly Goodyear and Buckeye, is still maturing on the retail side but drawing attention for industrial-adjacent NNN uses tied to the logistics corridor buildout.

Life company capital in Phoenix is selective, not unlimited. Underwriters are focused on suburban NNN retail with genuine trade area support, not speculative pad sites in emerging corridors. A Dollar General or AutoZone on a hard corner in Chandler with 14 years of absolute NNN lease remaining is a different conversation than a regional QSR in a transitional location. The market has earned life company attention because it has consistently performed, and that means underwriters know the submarkets well enough to distinguish between deals that fit and deals that do not.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Phoenix Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for Phoenix NNN properties is disciplined and consistent. Loan-to-value typically lands in the 60 to 65 percent range for most suburban retail NNN transactions, with selective movement toward 70 to 75 percent reserved for investment-grade tenants with 15 or more years of absolute NNN lease remaining. The 10-year Treasury at roughly 4.3 percent in 2026 context places life company all-in rates for quality Phoenix NNN deals in the low-to-mid 5 percent range, with spreads for investment-grade tenants running approximately 125 to 150 basis points over the benchmark. Weaker credit or shorter lease terms push spreads wider, into the 175 basis point range or beyond.

Debt service coverage requirements typically start at 1.25x, with 1.30x or better expected on deals without investment-grade sponsorship or in submarkets the life company has not previously placed capital. Interest-only for the full term is available and commonly structured for Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, and other recognized investment-grade credits. For tenants outside the top credit tier, partial-term IO or a standard amortization schedule is the more likely outcome. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, and terms are matched to remaining lease expiration so the mortgage and the lease do not create a mismatch problem at rollover.

Non-recourse is standard on life company execution and is one of the structural advantages sponsors value most. The underlying lease quality and property fundamentals carry the credit, not a personal guaranty. For Phoenix specifically, life company underwriters are paying close attention to lease structure, confirming absolute NNN with no landlord obligations, and they are scrutinizing trade area population density and tenant sales performance where available. A Phoenix market deal that checks every structural box will move efficiently through the process. One with lease carveouts, co-tenancy clauses, or a tenant credit that does not clear investment grade will encounter friction regardless of how well the market is regarded.

Typical Phoenix Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company NNN deal in Phoenix today looks something like this: a single-tenant retail pad in Chandler, Gilbert, or Scottsdale, occupied by a national investment-grade tenant with 12 to 18 years of absolute NNN lease remaining. The loan amount falls between $3 million and $15 million, the property was built within the past 10 to 15 years, and the sponsor is an experienced net lease investor with a clean balance sheet and familiarity with life company documentation requirements. The deal is acquired or refinanced at a stabilized cap rate that supports 60 to 65 percent LTV at current underwriting.

From a signed LOI to a funded closing, direct correspondent execution in this market typically runs 45 to 75 days. That timeline accounts for title, survey, environmental, appraisal, and lease review. By comparison, a broker submitting the same deal to a life company's home office for approval is typically looking at 75 to 120 days or longer, with the added risk of a credit committee revision that changes structure after the sponsor has already committed. For Phoenix deals tied to 1031 deadlines or competitive acquisition timelines, that difference is not academic.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Capital Options in Phoenix NNN

Life company execution is the most competitive option in this market when the deal involves a stabilized investment-grade NNN asset, a long hold period, a loan above $3 million, and a sponsor who values non-recourse fixed-rate certainty over maximum leverage. No other capital source consistently delivers 15 to 25 year fixed-rate non-recourse financing at life company spreads for this asset class. CMBS can execute on NNN retail and offers non-recourse structure, but CMBS execution carries servicer risk, less flexible prepayment, and is generally less relationship-driven. Bank balance sheet lenders are competitive on smaller loans and shorter terms but rarely commit beyond 10 years and typically require recourse. Debt funds can move faster and lend at higher leverage, but they price accordingly and are better suited for value-add or transitional situations where the asset is not yet life company ready.

Agency financing through Fannie or Freddie does not apply to NNN retail and is reserved for multifamily. For sponsors with a Phoenix NNN asset that needs a bridge period before a long-term placement, the correct sequence is often a shorter-term bank or debt fund loan to stabilize or extend the lease, followed by a life company permanent loan once the asset qualifies. A direct correspondent lender can advise on that sequencing from day one rather than waiting until the deal is already packaged for placement.
