Life Company Net Lease Lending in Madison, WI

Madison occupies a distinctive position in the life company lending universe. It is not a primary gateway market, but it is far from a tertiary pass-through either. Life insurance companies have grown increasingly comfortable allocating capital here because the underlying demand drivers are institutional in character: the University of Wisconsin anchors a perpetual enrollment economy on the east side and downtown, Epic Systems draws a concentrated, high-income workforce to the Verona and west-side corridors, and state government employment provides a baseline of economic stability that few mid-size metros can match. For net lease specifically, that employment base translates into exactly the kind of predictable consumer spending that supports investment-grade retail tenants at the suburban nodes life companies prefer.

Within the Madison MSA, the submarkets generating the most life company interest for single-tenant NNN retail are the suburban commercial corridors along the west and southwest sides: Middleton, Fitchburg, and Verona capture a disproportionate share of deal flow because the household incomes, traffic counts, and tenant covenants align with life company credit criteria. A Dollar General shadow-anchored by an Epic employee concentration, a Walgreens at a hard-corner Middleton intersection, or a corporate AutoZone on a Fitchburg arterial all represent the kind of asset where life company capital finds its best execution. Waunakee and Monona round out the secondary nodes where the right credit tenant on a clean NNN structure will clear life company underwriting without friction.

Life companies have been selective in Madison on net lease, as they are everywhere in 2026. They are not chasing yield, and they are not filling allocation buckets with marginal credits. What they want in this market is simple: an investment-grade tenant, a long remaining lease term, absolute NNN structure, and a location that would re-tenant without a fight if the current occupant vacated. Madison's suburban retail corridors, particularly those benefiting from Epic-driven rooftop growth in Verona and Fitchburg, clear that bar consistently when the sponsorship and basis are right.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Madison Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for single-tenant NNN in Madison is built around a conservative framework that rewards certainty over yield. LTV runs 60 to 70 percent for most transactions, with up to 75 percent available for the strongest investment-grade tenants carrying 15 or more years of remaining term on an absolute NNN lease. Life companies are not leverage maximizers. A sponsor pushing for 75 percent LTC on a value-add net lease opportunity will not find a life company solution; that is a debt fund or construction lender conversation.

Term and lease alignment are non-negotiable. Life companies structure loans to match or run inside the remaining lease term. A 15-year fixed rate on a tenant with 17 years remaining is a clean execution. A 10-year fixed on a tenant with 11 years remaining works. Requesting a 20-year term against a 14-year lease does not. With the 10-year Treasury trading in the 4.25 to 4.35 percent range in early 2026, life company all-in rates for investment-grade NNN tenants in a market like Madison are clearing in the low to mid 5 percent range, with spreads to Treasury running approximately 125 to 175 basis points depending on tenant credit quality, lease duration, and location fundamentals. For the top-tier credits on long leases, that spread compresses. For secondary credits or shorter remaining terms, it widens accordingly.

DSCR requirements typically sit at 1.25x or better at origination. Because life companies commonly underwrite investment-grade deals on an interest-only basis for the full term, the IO-only payment structure needs to produce that coverage at the underwritten rent. Non-recourse is standard across this program. Life companies are not asking principals to sign personal guarantees on stabilized, credit-tenanted net lease assets; the property and the tenant covenant are the collateral, full stop.

Typical Madison Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Madison market looks something like this: a freestanding single-tenant retail property in the $3M to $15M range, built within the last 15 years, absolute NNN lease structure with an investment-grade national tenant, 12 to 20 years of remaining term, suburban location in Middleton, Fitchburg, Verona, or Waunakee with strong traffic counts and income demographics. The sponsor is typically an experienced private net lease investor or a 1031 exchange buyer with a clean balance sheet and no heavy leverage elsewhere in the capital structure.

The loan structure on that profile would carry a fixed rate term matched to remaining lease, full-term interest only if the tenant credit is investment grade, non-recourse, and yield maintenance prepayment designed to align with lease expiration rather than a calendar date. Loan sizing runs $2M on the low end, with $5M and above preferred for efficiency on both sides. Portfolio packages of Madison-area net lease assets are well-suited to this program when the underlying credits and locations are consistent. Timeline from executed LOI to closing is 45 to 75 days in a direct correspondent execution, assuming clean title and a responsive borrower team on due diligence delivery.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Madison

Life company financing is not always the right answer, and understanding when it wins is as important as knowing what it offers. For single-tenant NNN with investment-grade tenants, long remaining lease terms, and hold periods of ten years or more, life company pricing and structure are difficult to beat. The combination of non-recourse, full-term IO, long fixed-rate terms, and tight spreads for top credits creates a capital cost advantage that CMBS cannot consistently match and that bank balance sheet lenders will not provide on a 20-year fixed basis.

CMBS can compete on NNN deals where the life company boxes are not fully checked. Shorter remaining lease terms, secondary credits, or deals that need slightly higher leverage at 70 to 75 percent LTV may find a better bid in the CMBS market. Bank balance sheet lenders in Madison remain active on NNN for relationships but rarely offer the term length or non-recourse structure that long-hold investors require. Debt funds price wider and are better suited to bridge, value-add, or transitional situations where a life company would simply not engage. Agency products are not relevant for single-tenant retail and are a separate conversation entirely for the Madison multifamily market. The life company program is purpose-built for stabilized, long-lease, credit-tenanted net lease assets, and that is where it wins cleanly.
