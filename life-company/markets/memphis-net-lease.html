Life Company Net Lease Lending in Memphis

Memphis occupies a distinctive position in the life insurance company lending universe, and that position is rooted in something more durable than a single economic cycle. The city sits at the convergence of I-40, I-55, and I-69, hosts the global headquarters of FedEx, and anchors the Memphis Aerotropolis, one of the most active air cargo ecosystems in the world. Life companies have responded accordingly, allocating aggressively into Memphis industrial and logistics assets over the past decade. That same institutional confidence extends to the net lease retail market, where Memphis functions as the regional commercial hub for a multi-state trade area stretching from western Tennessee into northern Mississippi and eastern Arkansas. For credit tenants with strong suburban footprints, Memphis is a serious life company market.

The suburban corridors that life companies find most compelling for net lease retail follow the population density and household income patterns concentrated in East Memphis, Germantown, Collierville, and Bartlett. These are not transitional neighborhoods or speculative retail locations. Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, Starbucks, and similar investment-grade tenants operating in these submarkets are serving established trade areas with long operating histories at the site level. That combination of credit strength and demonstrated location performance is precisely what life company underwriters want to see. Southaven, Mississippi, just south of the state line, also draws life company attention given its position as one of the fastest-growing retail submarkets in the greater Memphis MSA.

It is worth noting that life company net lease allocation in Memphis does not exist in isolation from the broader industrial story. When a life company already has significant exposure in the Airport corridor or along Shelby Drive through logistics and distribution assets, they understand the Memphis market at a granular level. That existing market knowledge shortens the analytical distance between submission and commitment for net lease retail deals in the same MSA. Memphis is not a market where a life company is learning the geography for the first time. That familiarity benefits borrowers in ways that show up directly in underwriting speed and structure.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Memphis Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease assets in Memphis is disciplined and consistent. Loan-to-value typically ranges from 60 to 65 percent for standard NNN deals, with selective movement toward 70 to 75 percent reserved for investment-grade tenants carrying 15 or more years of remaining lease term and absolute NNN lease structures. Life companies are not leverage-maximization lenders. Their mandate is long-duration, low-risk fixed income, and the loan terms they offer reflect that orientation. Term lengths of 10 to 25 years are standard, with the preferred structure matching the term to the remaining lease expiration to eliminate rollover risk during the loan period.

On rate, the 2026 environment has life company net lease spreads generally running 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.25 to 4.50 percent range. Investment-grade single-tenant deals with long remaining lease terms and strong site metrics can price at the tighter end of that range. The combination produces all-in fixed rates that remain highly competitive against CMBS and bank execution for the right asset. Full-term interest-only is routinely available for investment-grade tenants. For non-investment-grade or weaker credit profiles, amortization requirements vary, and the floor on credit quality matters significantly to the underwriting outcome.

DSCR requirements are typically set at a minimum of 1.25x on a stressed basis, though stronger credit tenants with flat or modest rent escalations can sometimes be underwritten at tighter coverage given the predictability of the income stream. Non-recourse is the standard structure for life company net lease loans. Carve-outs are standard and non-negotiable, but full recourse obligations are not part of this program. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule, and borrowers should enter the transaction understanding that life company loans are designed to be held to maturity, not refinanced in year three.

Typical Memphis Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease loan in Memphis looks something like this: a single-tenant retail property in Germantown or Collierville occupied by an investment-grade tenant under an absolute NNN lease with 12 to 18 years of remaining term, 1,500 to 15,000 square feet, strong daily-needs or automotive service use, built in the last 10 to 20 years, and priced in the $2.5 million to $15 million range. The sponsor is typically an experienced net lease investor or family office with a clear hold strategy, limited near-term liquidity needs, and an interest in locking in long-duration fixed-rate debt with non-recourse protection.

Timeline from LOI to closing on a direct correspondent transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days. Borrowers who have worked through brokered life company channels will recognize that 45 to 75 day window as materially faster than the 75 to 120 day plus timelines common when a deal is routed through a correspondent or correspondent's correspondent to a home office underwriting team. The difference is process efficiency, not shortcuts. The same quality standards apply. The direct correspondent structure simply eliminates the handoff delays.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Financing Options in Memphis

For net lease assets, life company execution is most competitive when the borrower is holding long, the tenant is investment-grade, and the loan size is above $5 million. In those conditions, life company pricing, IO availability, non-recourse structure, and term flexibility are difficult to match through other capital sources. CMBS can price competitively on rate but introduces servicer complexity, defeasance requirements, and a secondary market execution process that removes control from the borrower after closing. Bank balance sheet financing is more flexible on structure but typically limited to 5 to 10 year terms with amortization requirements, which reintroduces refinancing risk for long-hold investors.

Debt funds compete at higher leverage points, typically 70 to 80 percent LTV, but price accordingly and rarely match the long-term fixed rate certainty that life company capital provides. For sponsors who need 75 percent leverage on a value-add net lease asset with near-term lease rollover, a debt fund or bridge lender may be the right starting point. For a stabilized, investment-grade tenanted asset in East Memphis or Germantown where the business plan is a 10 to 15 year hold, life company financing is almost always the best execution available in the market.

Agency financing is relevant for Memphis multifamily but does not apply to net lease retail. The comparison is a non-starter for single-tenant NNN assets, and borrowers evaluating the capital stack for those properties should evaluate life company, CMBS, and bank execution on their respective merits for the specific asset and hold strategy.
