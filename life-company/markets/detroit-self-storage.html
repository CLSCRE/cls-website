Life Company Self Storage Lending in Detroit: A Selective But Active Capital Source

Detroit's commercial real estate capital markets have historically been dominated by industrial, logistics, and suburban multifamily when it comes to life company placement. The metro's ongoing transformation around EV manufacturing supply chain assets in Macomb and Oakland Counties has kept institutional capital focused on industrial product along the I-75 and I-94 corridors, and suburban multifamily in Troy, Royal Oak, Birmingham, and Ferndale continues to attract life company appetite as the urban core recovery plays out. Against that backdrop, self storage has emerged as a legitimate addition to life company allocations in the Detroit market, provided the asset and submarket meet the specific filters institutional capital applies here.

Life companies active in Michigan are not writing self storage across the board. What they are targeting is climate-controlled, institutional-quality product in dense suburban infill locations where population and household formation are stable or growing. Sterling Heights, Auburn Hills, Troy, and the broader Oakland County corridor represent the strongest alignment between life company risk appetite and self storage demand drivers. These are markets where EV industry employment, established residential density, and limited new supply create the income stability and demand defensibility that life companies require. Facilities along the I-75 suburban corridor, serving established residential trade areas rather than transitional urban zones, fit the thesis most cleanly.

The Detroit urban core presents a more selective picture. Life companies financing self storage in this market will underwrite downtown and midtown Detroit assets carefully, weighting occupancy history, competition within a defined radius, and the stability of the surrounding residential base. Properties serving genuinely dense, stabilized neighborhoods with demonstrated absorption can qualify, but the threshold for proof of performance is higher than in established suburban submarkets. Sponsors with assets in Macomb County or the I-75 suburban corridor will generally find faster traction with institutional capital than those presenting transitional urban locations, regardless of current occupancy.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Detroit Self Storage Deals

The underwriting standards life companies apply to self storage in the Detroit market are consistent with their national program criteria, with some local nuances that sophisticated sponsors should understand before approaching institutional capital. Stabilized occupancy of 85 percent or better is the baseline. Life companies are not providing lease-up or transitional capital. They are writing long-term permanent first mortgages on assets with demonstrated, durable income streams, and they will want to see at least two to three years of operating history at or near stabilization.

Loan-to-value for self storage in this market runs 55 to 65 percent. Climate-controlled, institutional-quality assets in strong suburban submarkets like Troy or Auburn Hills may approach the upper end of that range; tertiary or mixed-quality product will be underwritten more conservatively. Debt service coverage requirements typically sit at 1.30x to 1.40x on trailing net operating income, and life companies will stress-test that income against vacancy assumptions that reflect local market conditions rather than simply underwriting current occupancy at face value.

On rate and term, the 2026 environment places 10-year Treasury yields around 4.3 percent. Life company spreads for stabilized self storage in established markets are running in the range of 125 to 175 basis points over Treasury, depending on asset quality, location, and sponsor strength. The result is all-in fixed rates that remain competitive with alternative permanent financing sources, particularly for sponsors holding through a full cycle. Terms run 10 to 25 years fixed, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules. Non-recourse is standard execution for qualifying stabilized assets. Prepayment is typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which sponsors planning a near-term exit should factor into their hold strategy before committing to this execution.

Typical Detroit Life Company Self Storage Deal Profile

The life company self storage deals Commercial Lending Solutions is underwriting in the Detroit metro share a consistent profile. Loan sizes typically fall in the $3 million to $20 million range for individual assets, though portfolio transactions or larger institutional-quality facilities can reach $50 million. Property vintage tends to be 2000 or newer, with climate-controlled units comprising a significant share of net rentable area. Single-story drive-up only facilities in lower-demand suburban trade areas generally do not achieve life company execution; climate-controlled multistory or mixed-configuration assets in high-density suburban corridors are the sweet spot.

Sponsor profile matters to life company underwriters. Experienced self storage operators with multiple facilities, clean financial statements, and a demonstrated track record of managing through cycles are the target. Life companies writing non-recourse paper are underwriting the asset and the operator simultaneously. Sponsors presenting a first-time self storage acquisition, even at strong occupancy, will face higher documentation thresholds than established operators with portfolio history.

The Detroit submarkets generating the most qualifying deal flow for this program include Sterling Heights, Troy, Auburn Hills, and the broader Oakland County suburban corridor, where residential density, income demographics, and supply discipline align with life company underwriting filters.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Financing Options in Detroit

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Detroit self storage deal, and sponsors should evaluate the full menu of permanent capital options with that honesty in mind. For self storage specifically, agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac is not available, as those programs are restricted to multifamily. That makes the relevant comparison CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds.

CMBS can provide higher leverage than life companies, often reaching 65 to 75 percent LTV, and may accommodate properties that fall short of life company quality or occupancy thresholds. The tradeoff is execution risk, servicer inflexibility post-closing, and prepayment structures that can limit refinance or sale optionality. For stabilized, long-hold assets where the sponsor is not planning an exit in the near term, CMBS execution rarely beats life company pricing or structure.

Bank balance sheet financing remains active for self storage in Michigan but is typically shorter-term, 5 to 7 years, with recourse requirements and floating or shorter fixed-rate periods. For sponsors with near-term refinance or sale plans, or those who need higher leverage than life companies will provide, bank execution may be the better fit. Debt funds fill the bridge and value-add space that life companies categorically avoid. If an asset is below 85 percent occupancy, in lease-up, or undergoing renovation, a debt fund is the appropriate source. Life company capital enters after stabilization is demonstrated and permanent financing is the appropriate product.

Life company execution wins most clearly for sponsors with stabilized, high-quality assets, long intended hold periods, and a preference for certainty of execution at competitive fixed rates. Non-recourse structure, long fixed terms, and institutional pricing are the defining advantages. In the Detroit suburban self storage market, where qualified assets do exist and institutional capital is actively allocating, life company financing is the permanent execution benchmark for sponsors who qualify.
