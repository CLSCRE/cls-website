Life Company Net Lease Lending in Omaha: Market Context

Omaha occupies a distinctive position in the life company lending universe, and that position is no accident. The city's institutional DNA, anchored by Berkshire Hathaway, Mutual of Omaha, and a deep bench of financial services firms, gives life company capital allocators an inherent comfort with the market that you simply do not find in secondary cities of comparable size. Life companies understand Omaha's economic base, its employment stability, and its long track record of absorbing real estate without the volatility that plagues gateway markets. For net lease assets, that familiarity translates directly into execution: competitive spreads, reliable term sheets, and consistent appetite across market cycles.

Within Omaha, life company interest in net lease retail tracks closely with the suburban growth corridors that have defined the market over the past decade. Papillion, La Vista, and the broader Sarpy County footprint have attracted the national credit tenants that life companies prefer: Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, Starbucks, and similar investment-grade operators whose leases provide the long-duration, predictable cash flows that match life company liability structures. West Omaha retail nodes along Dodge Street and around 144th Street carry institutional-quality characteristics, and corporate-anchored trade areas in those corridors consistently clear life company underwriting. Bellevue and the I-80 access corridors are also increasingly active as population growth in those submarkets supports new net lease development.

The Omaha life company market is not without its selectivity. Assets with shorter lease terms, sub-investment-grade tenants, or locations in declining retail corridors will face a harder look from life company capital, regardless of the borrower's relationship history. Life companies underwriting net lease in this market are buying the lease as much as the real estate, and Omaha's strong fundamentals do not override a weak tenant or a lease with less than ten years remaining. For sponsors with the right assets, however, the market's stability and institutional familiarity create a genuine execution advantage relative to less-established secondary markets.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Omaha Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease in Omaha starts with the tenant and works outward. Investment-grade credit ratings are not a preference; they are effectively a requirement for the most favorable execution. Tenants like Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and Starbucks carry the kind of corporate guarantees and balance sheet strength that allow life companies to underwrite to the lease rather than applying traditional real estate stress scenarios. For those tenants, LTV coverage in the 65 to 75 percent range is achievable, and full-term interest-only structures are on the table. For tenants rated below investment grade or with non-rated corporate parents, life companies will tighten to 60 to 65 percent LTV and will typically require some level of amortization.

On term, life companies want to match the loan maturity to the lease expiration or come as close to it as the structure allows. Ten to twenty-five year fixed-rate terms are standard in this program, and the preferred lease structure is absolute NNN with corporate guaranty and no landlord obligations. Remaining lease term at closing should be at minimum ten years, and longer is meaningfully better from a life company pricing perspective. Leases with blend and extend options or early renewal provisions can be structured into the loan, but the baseline lease must be clean.

On rate, the 2026 environment with the ten-year Treasury in the 4.3 percent range means life company all-in fixed rates for investment-grade NNN assets are landing in a range that reflects spreads of roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the ten-year, depending on tenant credit, lease term, and LTV. That pricing is among the most competitive available for permanent net lease financing. DSCR requirements for this program typically start at 1.20x and step up modestly for lower-rated tenants or shorter remaining lease terms. Non-recourse execution is standard, with carve-outs for standard bad acts only.

The practical difference for Omaha sponsors is meaningful. Typical Omaha Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in Omaha today looks something like this: a single-tenant retail asset in Sarpy County or West Omaha, occupied by an investment-grade tenant on an absolute NNN lease with twelve to eighteen years of remaining term. The property was built within the last ten to fifteen years, is in institutional-quality condition, and requires no landlord capital over the life of the loan. Loan size falls in the $5 million to $15 million range for most single-asset deals, though portfolio executions with multiple Omaha or regional assets can range meaningfully higher.

The sponsor profile that life companies respond to in this market typically includes a track record of owning net lease real estate, a clean financial statement, and a clear long-term hold strategy. Net lease life company financing is a long-duration capital tool; sponsors planning a seven-year hold and a 1031 exchange are better served by CMBS or a shorter-term bank product. Sponsors who intend to own the asset to lease expiration and want the most competitive fixed-rate execution for that hold are exactly who this program is designed for.

Timeline from a signed LOI to closing through a direct correspondent lender runs 45 to 75 days in most cases, driven primarily by appraisal and title. The same transaction submitted through a brokered correspondent model typically takes 75 to 120 days or more, reflecting the additional approval layers in the process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Omaha

Life company financing is not the right answer for every Omaha net lease deal, and understanding where it wins and where it does not is part of working with a lender who is actually trying to get deals closed correctly.

Against CMBS, life company execution wins on rate, structure simplicity, and non-recourse certainty for stabilized investment-grade net lease assets. CMBS can go higher on leverage (up to 75 percent and occasionally beyond) and can accommodate shorter remaining lease terms, but the securitized structure introduces servicer relationships, lockbox mechanics, and reserve requirements that institutional net lease sponsors frequently prefer to avoid. For assets that clear life company underwriting, the choice is usually straightforward.

Bank balance sheet financing is a reasonable option for smaller net lease deals below the $2 million threshold or for sponsors who need recourse flexibility, shorter fixed-rate terms, or construction-to-perm structures. Banks are also the right tool when a net lease asset has a lease rollover within five years or a tenant credit profile that does not clear life company standards. The tradeoff is rate: bank pricing in the current environment will generally not match life company execution on a twenty-year fixed basis.

Debt funds occupy the higher-leverage, higher-rate end of the spectrum. For net lease assets requiring bridge financing, lease-up capital, or value-add repositioning before permanent financing, debt funds serve a purpose. For stabilized NNN product with a credit tenant and a long lease, they are the most expensive capital available and rarely competitive.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is not applicable to net lease retail. For sponsors with mixed portfolios that include multifamily in Aksarben or Midtown alongside net lease assets, agency and life company executions can run in parallel depending on asset type.
