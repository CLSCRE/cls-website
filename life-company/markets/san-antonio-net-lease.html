Life Company Net Lease Lending in San Antonio

San Antonio has quietly become one of the more active life company markets in Texas for net lease retail, and the structural reasons are straightforward. The city's economy is anchored by a combination of military employment at JBSA, a massive healthcare corridor centered on the Medical Center, and a manufacturing base headlined by Toyota's North American truck plant. That employment diversity produces exactly the kind of stable, suburban household formation that drives foot traffic to the credit tenants life companies want to finance. Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, and fast-casual QSR operators have expanded aggressively across San Antonio's far North Side and along the 281 corridor, and life company capital has followed.

The suburban growth story is the key underwriting narrative. New Braunfels and Schertz have absorbed significant population overflow from San Antonio's core, creating demand for the kind of necessity-based retail that life companies favor. A new-construction Dollar General on a 15-year absolute NNN lease in Schertz reads nearly identically to one in the Dallas suburbs from a life company's perspective, but San Antonio assets have historically carried slightly tighter competition for acquisition capital, which has kept fundamentals clean. Life companies are not chasing yield in San Antonio. They are chasing long-term lease certainty in a high-growth Texas market with no rent control and a landlord-favorable legal environment.

The Far North Side submarkets, particularly the 1604 loop corridor and Stone Oak area, represent the sweet spot for life company net lease activity. Anchored strip centers with investment-grade grocery or pharmacy anchors, freestanding QSR pads with 20-plus years of lease term remaining, and AutoZone or O'Reilly locations on long absolute NNN leases all generate strong interest. The Medical Center adjacency adds a separate demand driver for pharmacy and urgent care net lease assets, which have been a growing category for life company correspondent programs over the past several years.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Antonio Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for San Antonio net lease deals centers on a short list of non-negotiable criteria. Lease term is the gating factor. Most life companies want to see a minimum of 10 years of remaining lease term at origination, with a strong preference for absolute NNN structure where the tenant absorbs taxes, insurance, and maintenance. Investment-grade credit tenants, rated BBB- or better, unlock the best execution: full-term interest-only structures, LTV up to 70 to 75 percent, and pricing at the tighter end of the spread range. Non-investment-grade or franchisee credit tenants are not automatic disqualifiers, but they will compress proceeds and widen spreads materially.

On the capital structure side, life companies are underwriting San Antonio net lease assets at 60 to 65 percent LTV for standard credit tenants and up to 70 to 75 percent for long-lease investment-grade credits. DSCR requirements typically fall in the 1.25x to 1.35x range, though full-term interest-only structures on IG tenants can reduce the effective coverage hurdle. Fixed-rate terms range from 10 to 25 years and are almost always structured to align with the lease expiration date, which eliminates refinance risk at rollover. Non-recourse is the standard structure across the life company correspondent universe.

On rate environment, 10-year Treasury rates have been trading in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range through early 2026. Life company correspondent spreads for stabilized investment-grade net lease assets are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, placing all-in fixed rates in a range that remains highly competitive relative to CMBS and bank execution for long-term holds. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule is standard, so sponsors should model for long holds. These are not bridge loans. Life company net lease financing is a permanent capital decision.

Typical San Antonio Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in San Antonio looks something like this: a freestanding drug store, discount retailer, or QSR pad site built within the last 10 to 15 years, located on a hard corner along a high-traffic arterial on the Far North Side or within the 1604 corridor. Loan size typically falls between $3M and $15M for single-tenant assets, with portfolio transactions ranging up to $30M or beyond. The lease has 12 to 20 years of remaining term on an absolute NNN structure with a rated investment-grade tenant. The sponsor is an experienced net lease investor or a family office with a long-hold strategy and no near-term disposition pressure.

Occupancy requirements are straightforward for net lease: the asset is either occupied or it is not a life company deal. A dark building, even with a lease in place, requires alternative execution until the tenant is operating. Sponsors should also expect life companies to scrutinize lease renewal options, termination clauses, and co-tenancy provisions carefully. A lease with a tenant kick-out tied to anchor co-tenancy is a materially different underwriting proposition than a clean absolute NNN with no outs. From application to closing, direct correspondent execution at Commercial Lending Solutions runs 45 to 75 days for clean deals with complete documentation packages.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Antonio

Life company financing wins on specific terms. For a stabilized, investment-grade net lease asset in San Antonio with a 15-year lease and a long-hold sponsor, no other capital source matches the combination of rate, term, non-recourse structure, and interest-only availability. CMBS can get close on rate but introduces servicer complexity, defeasance mechanics, and yield maintenance structures that are less flexible than the life company correspondent model. Bank balance sheet lenders in San Antonio are competitive for shorter fixed periods, typically 5 to 7 years, but almost always require full recourse and amortization, which degrades cash-on-cash returns on low-cap-rate net lease assets.

Debt funds serve a different part of the capital stack. They are not competing with life companies on stabilized net lease. They win on transitional assets, value-add plays, and situations requiring leverage above 70 percent or closing timelines under 30 days. If a San Antonio sponsor is acquiring a net lease asset with a below-market lease that burns off in 5 years, a debt fund bridge may be the right first step, with life company permanent financing as the exit once the lease is restructured and extended. Agency financing through Fannie and Freddie is not relevant for net lease retail; that channel is specific to multifamily.

The honest summary: life company correspondent financing is the right tool for patient capital, stabilized assets, long lease terms, and sponsors who want maximum certainty of execution on a non-recourse, fixed-rate permanent loan. When leverage requirements exceed 70 percent, lease term is under 10 years, or the asset is in transition, other sources will outperform.
