Life Company Net Lease Lending in Columbus, OH

Columbus has moved from a secondary life company target to a genuinely competitive market over the past several years, and the drivers are structural rather than cyclical. Ohio State University anchors a permanently large renter and retail consumer base, but the more significant long-term catalyst is the Intel Ohio mega-campus in New Albany. Intel's $20 billion-plus semiconductor fabrication investment is reshaping central Ohio's employment and population growth trajectory in ways that life companies find directly relevant to underwriting long-term fixed income. When a market's economic base is being permanently expanded by that magnitude of capital investment, a 20-year net lease on a suburban Columbus Walgreens or AutoZone looks considerably more durable than it might have five years ago.

For net lease specifically, life company capital has been most active in the suburban retail corridors surrounding the city's strongest population nodes. The Easton submarket, Dublin along US-33, Westerville, and the I-270 beltway retail pads have all produced life company-eligible net lease transactions. These are not urban infill assets with complicated land situations -- they are well-located, single-tenant pads or small anchored centers with investment-grade credit, long lease terms, and the kind of predictable NOI that matches a life company's liability profile precisely. The Intel supply chain buildout in New Albany is also beginning to generate a new category of credit industrial net lease -- semiconductor equipment and logistics users on long absolute NNN leases -- that life companies are actively evaluating as a distinct asset class.

Columbus is not without selectivity pressures. Life companies targeting this market want demonstrable trade area depth, not just a national credit tenant name. A Dollar General on a strong commuter corridor in Westerville underwrites differently than the same tenant in a marginal rural location 45 minutes south of downtown. In Columbus, the urban and first-ring suburban submarkets produce the deals life companies want to own for 15 to 25 years. Sponsors should approach life company financing here with realistic expectations about where on the metro's demand map their asset sits.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Columbus Net Lease Deals

Life company net lease underwriting in Columbus follows national program parameters, but local market context shapes where within those parameters a deal prices and structures. Leverage is the first filter. Standard LTV for most Columbus net lease transactions runs 55 to 65 percent, with room up to 70 to 75 percent reserved for investment-grade tenants on leases with 15 or more years of remaining term. Life companies are not stretch lenders -- they are matching long-duration assets to long-duration liabilities, and conservative leverage is intrinsic to that model, not a concession to market conditions.

Tenant credit quality is the second filter and arguably the more important one in practice. Investment-grade credits -- Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, O'Reilly Auto Parts, national grocery anchors -- command the tightest spreads life companies offer. In the current rate environment with the 10-year Treasury in the 4.25 to 4.50 percent range, life company spreads for strong investment-grade net lease in Columbus are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, producing fixed rates meaningfully below what CMBS or bank balance sheet lenders are quoting on comparable collateral. That spread advantage is the primary reason to pursue life company execution on the right deal.

Lease structure matters as much as tenant credit. Life companies want absolute NNN leases with no landlord obligations, no co-tenancy clauses, and lease terms that extend at least five years beyond the proposed loan maturity. A 20-year loan on a lease with 18 years remaining does not underwrite cleanly. DSCR requirements typically run 1.25x at a minimum, with stronger coverage preferred on non-investment-grade tenants. Non-recourse structure is standard across the program -- this is not a program that converts to recourse for weaker deals. Full-term interest-only is achievable for the strongest investment-grade credits and is increasingly the default ask from experienced sponsors who understand the product.

Typical Columbus Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in Columbus looks something like this: a $3 million to $12 million single-tenant retail property on a hard corner or established retail pad, built between 2005 and 2020, occupied by an investment-grade national credit tenant on an absolute NNN lease with 12 to 20 years of remaining term. The sponsor is typically an experienced private investor or family office holding for long-term income rather than a value-add operator. The financing objective is a fixed-rate, non-recourse permanent loan that eliminates refinancing risk and generates clean positive leverage against the property's cap rate.

Loan sizes on Columbus transactions typically range from $2 million on smaller single-tenant pads to $20 million or more on anchored suburban retail centers. The $5 million to $15 million range is where volume concentrates. Life companies prefer stabilized, occupied assets -- this is not a program for properties in lease-up or with near-term rollover exposure. Columbus's Easton corridor, Dublin retail nodes, and Westerville commercial strips regularly produce eligible collateral. Timeline from accepted LOI to closing runs 45 to 75 days for a direct correspondent execution, compared to 75 to 120 or more days when a deal moves through a brokered submission process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Columbus

For net lease specifically, life company financing wins on rate and structure when the deal qualifies, but it is not the right tool for every situation. Against CMBS, life company execution offers better pricing for investment-grade credits, a cleaner non-recourse structure without CMBS-specific carveout exposure, and no securitization risk in the servicing relationship. CMBS becomes competitive when deals fall below life company credit thresholds or require higher leverage than 70 percent. Bank balance sheet lenders in Columbus are active in the net lease space but typically offer shorter terms (5 to 10 years), floating or shorter fixed-rate periods, and recourse requirements that do not match the long-hold investor's objectives. Debt funds offer speed and leverage but price well above life company rates -- appropriate for transitional situations, not stabilized net lease holds.

Life company financing is most clearly the right answer when the borrower has a long hold horizon, an investment-grade tenant, a lease term that extends well beyond the loan, and no need for leverage above 70 percent. When those conditions are present in Columbus, no other capital source produces comparable rate, term, and structure simultaneously. When any of those conditions are missing -- value-add repositioning, sub-investment-grade tenants, higher leverage needs, short-term holds -- other sources will serve the deal better.
