Life Company Industrial Lending in Spokane: What the Market Looks Like in 2025 and 2026

Spokane's industrial market has steadily moved from regional afterthought to a genuine target for life company permanent capital, and the fundamentals driving that shift are durable. The Inland Northwest's position as a distribution hub serving Eastern Washington, Northern Idaho, and Western Montana gives Spokane Valley and the I-90 industrial corridor real logistics utility that institutional lenders recognize. Last-mile and regional distribution facilities with strong tenancy profiles are exactly the kind of asset that life companies are aggressively pursuing in 2025 and 2026, and Spokane offers that product at price points and cap rates that still support strong underwriting.

Life company interest in Spokane industrial concentrates heavily in Spokane Valley and the Liberty Lake submarket, where newer bulk warehouse and light industrial parks have absorbed well and carry the lease quality and property condition that permanent lenders require. Airway Heights, adjacent to Fairchild Air Force Base, has also attracted attention for industrial and logistics uses tied to defense logistics and the broader manufacturing presence in that corridor. The I-90 corridor threading through the metro is the spine of life company industrial activity here, offering the infrastructure access and tenant depth that moves a deal from consideration to commitment.

California migration, ongoing port diversification by regional distributors, and a cost structure significantly below Seattle and the Puget Sound have all contributed to Spokane's improving industrial fundamentals. Life companies remain selective, as they always are in secondary markets, and they will underwrite Spokane with slightly wider spreads than primary Pacific Northwest markets like Portland or Seattle. But for stabilized, well-leased industrial product in the right submarket, life company capital is not only available in Spokane, it is competitive with virtually any permanent financing alternative in the market today.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Spokane Industrial Deals

Life companies underwriting Spokane industrial deals apply the same fundamental discipline they use in primary markets, with secondary-market adjustments to LTV and spread. Standard leverage for stabilized industrial in this market runs 55 to 65 percent of value, with the lower end of that range applying to single-tenant assets with shorter lease terms and the upper end reserved for credit-tenant NNN structures, where terms can reach 70 percent. Life companies are not a high-leverage execution, and sponsors entering this conversation expecting agency-style proceeds will need to recalibrate.

Fixed-rate terms in the 10 to 25 year range are standard, with 10 and 15 year fixed terms the most common election for Spokane industrial. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury in the range of 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Spokane industrial are running roughly 150 to 200 basis points over benchmark, reflecting the secondary market premium relative to primary logistics markets. Class A bulk distribution or credit-tenant NNN product can compress toward the tighter end of that spread range, while older vintage light industrial parks or multi-tenant product with rollover risk will price wider. DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x and can move to 1.30x or higher depending on lease term, tenant credit, and property specifics.

Non-recourse financing is standard in the life company execution, with carve-outs for bad acts and environmental indemnity as the baseline. For trophy distribution product with long-term credit tenancy, full-term interest-only is achievable and represents one of the most attractive structural features of life company execution for well-qualified assets. Occupancy and lease term are the gating factors in Spokane. Life companies want to see stabilized occupancy with a weighted average lease term that supports the fixed-rate period, with credit-tenant NNN leases representing the cleanest path to maximum proceeds and optimal pricing.

As a direct correspondent lender, we can move from a qualified term sheet to a binding commitment on a timeline that reflects our own underwriting capacity, not an external queue. Typical Spokane Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in Spokane looks something like this: a stabilized warehouse or bulk distribution facility in Spokane Valley or Liberty Lake, built within the last 10 to 15 years, single-tenant or multi-tenant with strong in-place leases, and occupancy at or near 100 percent. Loan sizes from $5 million to $30 million are most common in this market, though our program accommodates deals from $3 million up to $100 million for larger logistics assets. Sponsors are typically experienced institutional or regional investors with a demonstrated hold period strategy that aligns with a long-term fixed-rate structure.

Lease quality matters more than almost any other variable. A Spokane industrial deal with a single investment-grade or regionally dominant tenant on a 10-plus year NNN lease is a fundamentally different underwriting conversation than a multi-tenant light industrial park with near-term rollover. The former can access the full range of life company structure including non-recourse, interest-only, and maximum LTV. The latter will require deeper credit analysis and may result in more conservative terms.

Timeline from signed application to closing for a direct correspondent transaction in this asset class typically runs 45 to 75 days depending on third-party report sequencing and title complexity. Brokered life company placements routinely run 75 to 120 days or more, and that timeline variance is almost entirely attributable to the submission and review cycle at the life company's home office.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Spokane Industrial

Life company financing wins decisively in Spokane industrial when the deal involves a long hold period, stabilized in-place cash flow, a preference for non-recourse structure, and loan sizing above what most local or regional banks want to hold on balance sheet. For a $15 million Class A distribution facility in Spokane Valley with a credit tenant on a 15-year NNN lease, life company execution will typically produce a better fixed-rate, better structure, and better long-term economics than any competing option.

CMBS can compete on leverage and non-recourse structure, but pricing volatility and servicer rigidity on modifications make CMBS a second choice for sponsors who value flexibility over the hold period. Bank balance sheet financing offers relationship value and speed for smaller deals, but fixed-rate terms are typically limited to 5 to 7 years with a balloon, and most regional banks in the Spokane market are not competitive on larger industrial loans. Debt funds fill a different role entirely, excelling in value-add, lease-up, or transitional scenarios where life companies simply will not participate.

Life company capital is not the right tool for every Spokane industrial deal. If the property needs renovation, if occupancy is below stabilization, or if the sponsor needs 70-plus percent leverage, another capital source will serve better. But for stabilized, well-leased industrial product with a sponsor positioned for a long-term hold, life company permanent capital remains the most efficient and lowest-cost institutional execution available in this market.
