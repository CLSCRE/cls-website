Life Company Office Lending in San Antonio

San Antonio's office market tells a more nuanced story than the broad national narrative around post-pandemic office distress. The city's economic foundation, anchored by JBSA military installations, USAA, Valero Energy, and a rapidly expanding healthcare and medical research corridor, has insulated certain office submarkets from the vacancy spikes that have plagued coastal gateway markets. Life insurance companies, which have become materially more selective about office lending since 2020, continue to find fundable product in San Antonio precisely because of this institutional tenant base and the city's population-driven demand dynamics.

The submarkets that draw the most life company attention are the Medical Center corridor, where healthcare-anchored office and medical office buildings carry long-term lease structures that align naturally with life company investment horizons, and the Stone Oak and Far North Side suburban corridors, where Class A suburban product with creditworthy tenants has maintained occupancy better than the urban core. North Central office, particularly buildings with government or quasi-government tenancy connected to the military ecosystem, also represents a fundable profile. Life companies are not writing blank checks on San Antonio office, but the right asset in the right submarket with the right lease roll story remains executable at competitive long-term fixed-rate pricing.

It is worth being direct: life company appetite for San Antonio urban core office, particularly mid-block CBD product with meaningful near-term lease expirations, is limited in the current environment. What the market rewards is suburban Class A with weighted average lease terms of seven years or longer, medical office with institutional healthcare tenants, and multi-tenanted product where no single tenant controls more than 35 to 40 percent of the rent roll. Sponsors who understand where the selectivity lies will save significant time and capital in the financing process.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Antonio Office Deals

Life company underwriting for San Antonio office in the current cycle centers on a handful of non-negotiable structural requirements. Leverage has compressed meaningfully since 2021. Expect maximum LTV in the 55 to 65 percent range for well-leased Class A suburban product, with the lower end of that range applying to assets with any near-term lease roll or urban core exposure. Sponsors who bring under-leveraged structures, 50 percent LTV or below, gain real pricing benefit and a materially faster path through credit.

On rate, the current 10-year Treasury environment near 4.3 percent places life company fixed-rate spreads for stabilized San Antonio office in the 125 to 175 basis point range, depending on asset quality, tenancy profile, and loan size. That translates to all-in fixed rates in a range that remains competitive with CMBS and bank balance sheet for long-hold sponsors who are pricing certainty of execution over the next 10 to 15 years. Yield maintenance prepayment is standard and should be modeled from day one. DSCR requirements have tightened; most life companies want to see 1.35x or better at the in-place rent roll, not pro forma occupancy, and they will stress test lease renewals independently.

Non-recourse structure is standard for life company financing and remains a core reason institutional sponsors favor this capital source. In the current market, carve-outs may be slightly broader than in prior cycles, particularly around environmental indemnities and lease modification rights, but the full-recourse structure common to bank balance sheet lending is not part of this program. Term is typically 10 to 15 years fixed on stabilized product. Medical office and government-tenanted assets may support terms at the longer end of that range given the predictability of lease structures tied to those tenant categories.

Typical San Antonio Life Company Office Deal Profile

A realistic life company office transaction in San Antonio today looks something like this: a suburban Class A office building of 80,000 to 250,000 square feet located in Stone Oak, the Medical Center corridor, or North Central, built or substantially renovated after 2005, with occupancy of 90 percent or above and a weighted average lease term of at least seven years. Loan size typically falls between $10 million and $50 million, with execution becoming most competitive at $15 million and above where the fixed cost of life company due diligence amortizes efficiently. Medical office buildings leased to major health systems or government-tenanted assets connected to the JBSA ecosystem represent the strongest current profiles.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies want experienced commercial real estate owners with institutional-quality asset management practices, clean credit history, and a track record of managing similar product. This is not a program for first-time owners of office assets regardless of the underlying real estate quality. For qualified sponsors with the right asset, timeline from letter of intent to closing runs approximately 45 to 75 days through our direct correspondent process, compared to 90 to 120 days or longer for indirect submissions through a broker without a correspondent relationship.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Antonio

Life company financing is not the right answer for every San Antonio office deal, and understanding when it wins and when it does not will save sponsors significant time. Life company execution is most competitive for long-hold stabilized assets where 10 to 15 year fixed-rate non-recourse debt aligns with the sponsor's hold strategy, for larger loan sizes where the pricing advantage over bank balance sheet is meaningful, and for credit-tenanted product where the life company's preference for predictable cash flows is a structural advantage rather than a constraint.

CMBS is a viable alternative for sponsors who need slightly higher leverage, have more complicated lease structures, or are financing assets that fall outside life company asset preferences. CMBS executes well in San Antonio for certain CBD and suburban office profiles but carries less favorable prepayment flexibility and a servicer relationship that some sponsors find operationally burdensome. Bank balance sheet lending remains active in San Antonio for recourse borrowers with strong banking relationships, particularly for deals under $15 million or assets with shorter-term lease structures where the bank's floating-rate product can be more efficient during a value-add lease-up period. Debt funds offer speed and flexibility for transitional office but at materially higher cost of capital and typically with recourse requirements. For stabilized Class A suburban office with institutional tenants and a long-hold strategy in San Antonio, life company direct correspondent execution is the most competitive capital stack available today.
