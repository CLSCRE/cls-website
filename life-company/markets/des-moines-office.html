Life Company Office Lending in Des Moines, IA

Des Moines occupies a genuinely unusual position in the life company lending landscape. Unlike most secondary markets where life insurance capital flows in from distant home offices staffed by underwriters who have never walked a submarket, Des Moines is home to several major life insurance companies, including Principal Financial Group and EMC Insurance. That institutional familiarity with local CRE fundamentals translates directly into more consistent capital allocation and a lender base that understands the difference between a Class A suburban office campus in West Des Moines and a commodity mid-rise downtown. For well-qualified office transactions, that matters more than most borrowers realize.

That said, life company office lending across all markets has become materially more selective since 2020, and Des Moines is not exempt from that discipline. The primary life company appetite in this market has shifted toward industrial and logistics assets along the I-35 and I-80 corridors, Class A multifamily in West Des Moines, Ankeny, Johnston, and Urbandale, and NNN suburban retail with credit tenancy. Office remains fundable, but the bar has moved. Suburban Class A product with long weighted average lease terms, government or investment-grade credit tenants, and demonstrated post-2020 occupancy stability is what clears the underwriting threshold. Multi-tenanted suburban office in Clive, West Des Moines, or Urbandale with a diversified, creditworthy rent roll continues to attract life company interest. Older urban product with near-term lease rollover does not.

Sponsors evaluating life company execution for a Des Moines office asset should think carefully about tenancy quality before entering a process. Life companies in this market are making long-duration loans, typically 10 to 15 years, and their underwriting reflects that time horizon. Vacancy risk, lease term remaining, and tenant credit profile are weighted heavily. If a property clears those filters, Des Moines life company execution can deliver among the most competitive fixed-rate permanent financing available in the Midwest at this tier of the market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Des Moines Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Des Moines office in the current environment centers on a handful of non-negotiable criteria. Leverage is the first filter. Typical LTV requirements have tightened to the 50 to 60 percent range for office, with the most competitive pricing reserved for under-leveraged structures closer to 50 percent. Borrowers accustomed to 65 to 70 percent LTV execution from banks or CMBS will need to recalibrate expectations. Life companies are pricing for hold, not for margin, and they want a cushion.

Debt service coverage is equally disciplined. Most life company programs today require a minimum DSCR in the 1.30x to 1.40x range for office, with some programs setting floors higher depending on asset profile. At current 10-year Treasury levels near 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Class A office are running approximately 150 to 200 basis points over the index, depending on market, tenancy, and leverage. That pricing is generally competitive versus CMBS for the right deal, particularly given the non-recourse structure and the absence of securitization-related servicing friction.

Lease term is central to life company credit analysis. For a 10-year fixed loan, a life company wants to see weighted average lease term that extends meaningfully beyond loan maturity, or at minimum, anchor tenants with strong credit and demonstrated renewal history. Government-tenanted office, medical office, and single-tenant net lease office with investment-grade occupancy are the most straightforward execution scenarios. Multi-tenanted suburban office with a stable, diversified rent roll and occupancy above 90 percent is fundable, but requires more underwriting narrative. Amortization typically runs 25 to 30 years on a 30-year schedule, with yield maintenance as the standard prepayment structure. Non-recourse with carve-outs is the norm, though carve-out language has broadened somewhat in the current market environment.

Typical Des Moines Life Company Office Deal Profile

A representative life company office transaction in Des Moines today looks something like this: a suburban Class A office campus in West Des Moines or Urbandale, built or substantially renovated post-2005, ranging from $10M to $40M in loan size. Occupancy is 90 percent or higher, with a weighted average lease term of at least seven years remaining. The anchor tenant is a regional employer, a financial services company, a healthcare system, or a government agency. Sponsorship is an experienced local or regional owner with a clean credit history and meaningful equity in the deal.

Medical office in Johnston or Ankeny tied to a health system anchor is another profile that clears the bar consistently. Single-tenant net lease office with an investment-grade tenant and 10-plus years remaining on the lease is among the cleanest execution scenarios in the current market.

Timeline from LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days for a complete application on a qualifying asset. Borrowers who have gone through the brokered process, where a deal travels from originator to home office and back with multiple rounds of review, know that timeline often stretches to 90 to 120 days or longer, with meaningful uncertainty at each stage.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Des Moines

Life company financing is not the right tool for every Des Moines office deal, and no credible lender should suggest otherwise. Understanding when it wins and when it does not is part of underwriting a financing strategy.

Life company execution is most competitive for stabilized, Class A suburban office with long WALT, strong credit tenancy, and a sponsor intending to hold the asset for the full loan term. The fixed-rate certainty over 10 to 15 years, non-recourse structure, and absence of CMBS servicing complexity are meaningful advantages for a long-duration hold. For loans in the $15M to $75M range, life company pricing at current spreads is generally inside CMBS for creditworthy deals, and the structure is cleaner.

CMBS wins when leverage needs exceed what a life company will underwrite, or when the property has characteristics that require a more mechanical underwriting approach rather than relationship-based credit judgment. Bank balance sheet lending is appropriate for shorter terms, value-add transition periods, or smaller loan sizes where the relationship lending model offers flexibility that neither life companies nor CMBS can match. Debt funds are the right answer when a sponsor needs bridge capital for a lease-up or repositioning play before the asset earns life company execution. Agency lending, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, is specific to multifamily and not relevant to office.

If your Des Moines office asset is stabilized, well-tenanted, and positioned for a long hold, life company financing through a direct correspondent lender is worth a serious underwriting conversation before any other permanent option.
