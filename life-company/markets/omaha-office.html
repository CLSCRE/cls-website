Life Company Office Lending in Omaha: A Market Built for Institutional Capital

Omaha occupies a distinct position in the life company lending universe. The city's deep roots in the insurance and financial services industry, anchored by names like Berkshire Hathaway, Mutual of Omaha, and Union Pacific, give it a credibility with institutional lenders that most mid-sized markets cannot claim. Life companies understand Omaha's economic DNA because many of them share it. That familiarity translates into consistent capital allocation even when office lending broadly has pulled back since 2020. For the right asset in the right submarket, life company financing remains available and competitive here in ways it simply is not in comparable markets.

West Omaha corporate office is the primary target for life company allocations in this asset class. The suburban corridors west of 108th Street have attracted the kind of institutional-grade tenancy, long weighted average lease terms, and modern Class A product that life company underwriters require. Government-tenanted and credit-tenanted properties in this corridor check every box. The Bellevue market, supported in part by Offutt Air Force Base proximity and defense contractor presence, also draws consistent interest from life company programs willing to underwrite government-adjacent office demand. Medical office across the metro, including assets tied to Nebraska Medicine and CHI Health's footprint, represents another category where life company underwriting standards are more forgiving than they are for conventional multi-tenanted suburban product.

That said, Omaha's office market is not immune to the post-pandemic reassessment that has affected life company appetite nationally. Urban core office, including parts of the downtown central business district, faces more scrutiny today than it did five years ago. Life companies are asking harder questions about tenancy rollover risk, parking ratios, and flight-to-quality dynamics before committing to urban assets. Sponsors pursuing downtown Omaha office financing through a life company program should expect deeper underwriting conversations and potentially more conservative proceeds than they would see for a fully leased West Omaha Class A building with a 10-year weighted average lease term. The capital is still available, but the bar is higher and the guidance you get at the front end of a deal matters considerably.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Omaha Office Deals

Life company underwriting for Omaha office in 2026 centers on a short list of hard filters. Leverage is the first and most important. In the current environment, expect sizing conversations to begin at 50 to 60 percent loan-to-value for standard stabilized product. Well-leased suburban Class A assets with investment-grade or credit tenants can push toward 60 to 65 percent, but sponsors who arrive expecting pre-2020 leverage levels will find the math does not work. Under-leveraged structures command meaningfully better pricing, and many experienced sponsors in this market are deliberately under-leveraging to capture rate advantages rather than stretching for proceeds.

On rate, life company execution for stabilized Omaha office is currently benchmarked to 10-year Treasury levels that have settled in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range, with life company spreads running approximately 125 to 175 basis points above that benchmark for most qualifying assets. Credit-tenanted single-tenant or anchor-tenanted assets can trade inside that range. Multi-tenanted product with near-term rollover exposure will price wider. The all-in fixed rate for a well-structured West Omaha Class A deal with strong tenancy is meaningfully better than what CMBS or bank balance sheet financing delivers for the same asset, which is precisely why long-hold sponsors pursue the life company execution when they qualify for it.

DSCR requirements have tightened alongside the rate environment. Life companies are underwriting to minimum debt service coverage ratios of 1.35x to 1.40x at the loan constant, and many programs are stress-testing occupancy to 85 or 90 percent before arriving at their underwritten cash flow. Lease term matters as much as current occupancy. A building at 95 percent occupied with three leases rolling in 18 months will get a very different treatment than a comparable building at 88 percent occupied with 9-year average remaining lease terms. Term structure on life company loans in this market runs 10 to 15 years fixed, with 25 to 30-year amortization schedules standard. Non-recourse structure is available for qualifying assets, though carve-out provisions have become somewhat broader than they were in the pre-2020 cycle. Prepayment flexibility is limited: yield maintenance is standard, and sponsors should underwrite their hold period accordingly before pursuing life company execution.

If the deal needs restructuring, that conversation happens at a distance. If the life company's appetite shifts during the process, the broker learns about it when the borrower does.

In the current environment, where life company office underwriting has tightened and the range of qualifying assets is narrower than it was three years ago, upfront guidance is worth more than it has ever been. Typical Omaha Life Company Office Deal Profile

The life company office deals that close in Omaha through our correspondent program share a recognizable profile. Deal size typically falls between $10 million and $50 million, with our program most competitive on transactions at $15 million and above where execution costs are well-justified by rate savings. Properties are generally 2000-vintage or newer Class A suburban office, well-maintained, professionally managed, and located in West Omaha office corridors or in medical office parks connected to established health systems. Occupancy at application runs 90 percent or better, with a weighted average lease term of at least five to seven years remaining. Tenants include regional corporate headquarters, financial services firms, healthcare operators, and government or quasi-government users.

Sponsor profile matters to life company underwriters more than many borrowers expect. Institutional and experienced private sponsors with demonstrated long-term hold strategies and clean credit histories fit the program. Life companies are not in the value-add business, and they are not writing loans for sponsors who view permanent financing as a bridge to a near-term disposition. Timeline from executed LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction in this market runs 45 to 75 days for well-prepared deals. Comparable transactions routed through an indirect brokered channel typically run 75 to 120 days or longer, and that assumes the deal clears home office underwriting without material re-trading.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Omaha

Life company financing wins on a specific set of criteria, and experienced Omaha sponsors know when to pursue it and when to look elsewhere. For stabilized, long-hold office assets with strong tenancy and sub-65 percent leverage requirements, life company execution delivers the best fixed rate available in the market, true non-recourse structure, and certainty of execution from a relationship lender. No other permanent source matches all three simultaneously for this asset class.

CMBS can compete on leverage and proceeds, particularly for larger loans, but introduces servicing complexity, imposes more rigid covenant structures, and lacks the flexibility in loan modification or partial release scenarios that direct relationship lenders provide. Bank balance sheet financing offers speed and relationship flexibility but typically requires recourse, shorter terms, and floating rate exposure that long-hold sponsors do not want on stabilized assets. Debt funds fill the value-add and transitional space that life companies will not touch, at cost structures that make them unsuitable for stabilized holds. For Omaha multifamily specifically, agency execution through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac remains the dominant channel, and life companies acknowledge that reality by focusing their Omaha multifamily appetite on assets that do not cleanly fit agency parameters.

The honest answer is that life company office financing in Omaha is the right tool for a specific kind of deal. If your asset is stabilized, your hold period is long, your leverage needs are moderate, and your tenancy is institutional quality, this is the most competitive permanent execution available to you in this market today.
