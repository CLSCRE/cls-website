Life Company Industrial Lending in Houston

Houston is one of the most active life company industrial markets in the country, and that status is directly tied to the Port of Houston. The port complex anchors one of the most consequential logistics corridors in North America, and the industrial submarkets surrounding it, specifically Bayport, Deer Park, and Pasadena, generate the kind of long-duration, credit-backed lease structures that life insurance companies are built to hold. When a life company allocates permanent capital to industrial in 2025 and 2026, Houston port-adjacent product is near the top of its target list. Bulk distribution facilities, Class A logistics parks, and last-mile assets with investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenancy are drawing compressed spreads from multiple life company platforms competing for the same paper.

Beyond the port corridor, the broader Houston industrial market benefits from structural advantages that life company credit committees appreciate. Texas has no state income tax, no zoning code, and no rent control framework, which gives institutional lenders clean optionality on exit and reduces regulatory risk in underwriting. Houston's position as the energy capital of the country has historically driven demand for industrial and logistics space from oilfield services, chemical distribution, and heavy equipment tenants, and that demand base has diversified meaningfully over the past decade into e-commerce fulfillment, cold storage, and advanced manufacturing. The Katy and Sugar Land corridors on the west side have absorbed significant Class A industrial development, and well-leased product in those submarkets is generating strong life company interest.

It is worth noting where life companies remain selective in Houston. Office has been under a cloud since the 2015 energy downturn, and life company allocations to Houston office remain limited relative to the city's size. Retail is evaluated deal by deal, with NNN suburban assets performing well and power center product facing more scrutiny. Industrial and logistics are a different conversation entirely. Life companies are net buyers of risk in this asset class in Houston right now, and the spread environment reflects that conviction.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Houston Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for Houston industrial is disciplined and consistent. Leverage typically lands in the 55 to 65 percent LTV range for stabilized assets, with credit-tenant NNN structures capable of reaching 70 percent under the right lease profile. These are not high-leverage executions, and sponsors entering the life company conversation need to be appropriately capitalized or positioned with an existing equity base that supports that stack. The return profile of life company debt is designed for long-hold sponsors, not opportunistic recapitalizations at peak leverage.

Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 25 years, with longer terms more accessible when the underlying lease structure supports it. A 15 or 20 year fixed-rate execution on a single-tenant NNN industrial asset with an investment-grade guarantor is a natural match for life company capital. For multi-tenant industrial parks, 10 to 15 year terms are more common, and the underwriting will focus on weighted average lease term, rollover exposure, and market rent relative to in-place rents. DSCR requirements generally run 1.25x to 1.35x at underwritten cash flow, with some life companies applying a haircut to variable lease structures or near-term rollover risk.

On rate, life company industrial pricing in 2026 is closely benchmarked to the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading in the 4.2 to 4.4 percent range. Life company spreads on Class A Houston industrial have been running in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the 10-year for well-qualified deals, making the all-in rate competitive with or inside CMBS execution for comparable leverage. Trophy distribution product with long-term credit tenancy can access full-term interest-only periods, which is a meaningful structural benefit relative to most bank or CMBS alternatives. Non-recourse with standard carve-outs is the baseline structure across virtually all life company industrial executions.

Typical Houston Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The life company industrial deals we close in Houston tend to share a recognizable profile. Deal size runs from $10 million to $75 million, with the most competitive execution reserved for assets in the $15 million and above range where life company capital is most actively allocated. Properties are typically 2010 vintage or newer, though well-maintained 2000s-era assets with strong tenancy can qualify. Class A bulk distribution in the port corridor, last-mile facilities in Katy or north Houston, and multi-tenant light industrial parks with sub-5 percent vacancy and staggered lease maturities are the most common collateral types.

Occupancy requirements are generally 90 percent or better at application, with in-place leases running at least three years of remaining term on the majority of the rent roll. Single-tenant NNN assets with investment-grade or nationally recognized tenants can access the most aggressive terms. Sponsors are typically institutional or experienced private owners with prior life company relationships, clean credit histories, and liquidity adequate to meet post-closing reserve requirements. Timeline from LOI to closing on a direct correspondent transaction in this market is typically 45 to 75 days. Indirect transactions placed through a broker with a life company correspondent desk more commonly run 75 to 120 days or longer.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Houston

For stabilized Houston industrial assets, life company financing is often the most competitive permanent execution available, but it is not always the right tool. Understanding when life company capital wins and when other sources are better suited is part of structuring a deal correctly from the start.

CMBS is the most direct competitor for life company industrial in Houston. For deals that fall outside life company credit parameters, including higher leverage requirements, shorter remaining lease term, or secondary market locations, CMBS can provide execution that life companies will not. CMBS also allows more flexibility on recourse and structure in some cases. The trade-off is prepayment inflexibility and servicer complexity that long-term holders often find frustrating. For sponsors who plan to hold an asset for 10 to 20 years, life company yield maintenance is typically more manageable than CMBS defeasance.

Bank balance sheet lending is viable for smaller Houston industrial deals, particularly in the $3 million to $8 million range where life companies have less appetite. Banks can also accommodate shorter fixed-rate periods, which suits sponsors who anticipate refinancing or selling within five to seven years. The leverage available on bank loans is often comparable to life company execution, but the floating-rate risk on bank paper has been a meaningful concern in the current rate environment.

Debt funds offer higher leverage and faster execution for value-add industrial acquisitions, lease-up plays, or assets that are not yet stabilized enough for life company underwriting. The cost of capital is significantly higher, but debt funds fill a genuine structural gap for transitional assets. Once a Houston industrial property has stabilized, refinancing into life company permanent capital is a natural next step.

Agency financing (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is not relevant for industrial assets and is mentioned here only for completeness: agency capital is restricted to multifamily, and Houston's industrial market has no direct agency equivalent. For multifamily in Houston, agency execution is typically the benchmark, and life companies compete on a deal-by-deal basis in the inner loop and Energy Corridor for institutional-quality apartment assets.
