Life Company Multifamily Lending in Spokane: What the Market Looks Like in 2026

Spokane has quietly become one of the more compelling secondary markets for life insurance company capital in the Pacific Northwest. Eastern Washington's diversified economic base, anchored by Fairchild Air Force Base, a growing healthcare and university employment sector, and sustained in-migration from higher-cost California markets, has produced the kind of stable, demand-driven multifamily fundamentals that life companies require. This is not speculative growth capital chasing a story. Life company lenders are drawn to Spokane precisely because the demand drivers are durable and the rent growth, while measured, is not dependent on a single employer or a boom cycle.

For multifamily specifically, life companies are most active on stabilized Class A and B product in South Hill, Downtown Spokane, and Liberty Lake. South Hill continues to attract professional households priced out of Seattle-side markets. Liberty Lake has established itself as a genuine suburban live-work submarket with a tenant base that supports premium rents. Downtown Spokane has seen meaningful adaptive reuse and ground-up delivery, and the best of that product is now hitting the stabilization benchmarks life companies require. Airway Heights, while militarily supported, tends to attract more specialized capital given its concentration risk. Life company appetite there is selective.

Washington's 2024 rent stabilization legislation adds a layer of underwriting selectivity to older Spokane multifamily stock, particularly pre-1990s vintage product where rent upside was a meaningful component of the value thesis. Life companies have responded by sharpening their focus on newer product where rents are already at or near market and the regulatory exposure is reduced. This does not represent a retreat from Spokane. It represents the kind of disciplined capital allocation that makes life company execution attractive to sponsors who have already done the hard work of building a stabilized, well-leased asset.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Spokane Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting for Spokane multifamily centers on stabilization, credit quality, and long-term cash flow visibility. The standard LTV range for this market runs 55 to 65 percent, with most lenders landing in the 60 percent range for a typical stabilized Class A or B asset. Sponsors with demonstrated track records in the Inland Northwest and strong balance sheets can access the upper end of that range. Higher leverage is rarely available through the life company channel, and sponsors who need 70 percent or above should be looking at agency execution or bank balance sheet alternatives.

Fixed-rate terms of 10 to 25 years are the structural hallmark of life company lending, and that long-duration capital is the primary reason sophisticated sponsors choose this execution. With the 10-year Treasury hovering around 4.3 percent in early 2026, life company spreads on stabilized Spokane multifamily are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, which typically places all-in rates 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency pricing for the same stabilized product. For a sponsor financing a 20-year hold in Liberty Lake or South Hill, that spread advantage compounds meaningfully over time.

DSCR requirements generally start at 1.25x on a 25 to 30 year amortization schedule, with some life companies offering interest-only periods for well-capitalized sponsors on stronger deals. Occupancy thresholds are firm: most life companies require a minimum of 90 percent physical occupancy for a trailing period of 90 days or more at application. Properties still in lease-up or carrying meaningful concession exposure will not clear the bar. Non-recourse is standard, with conventional bad-boy carve-outs covering fraud, environmental, and voluntary bankruptcy. Washington's landlord-tenant regulatory environment is factored into the underwriting, particularly for assets with older rent rolls that may have not yet tested market rents under the new framework.

Typical Spokane Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

A representative life company multifamily transaction in Spokane looks something like this: a 120 to 200 unit Class A apartment community in South Hill or Liberty Lake, delivered within the last ten years, stabilized at 93 to 96 percent occupancy with a clean rent roll and minimal concession exposure. Loan size typically falls between $8 million and $30 million, which sits squarely in the sweet spot of the program. The sponsor has multiple multifamily assets under management, a net worth comfortably in excess of the loan amount, and liquidity that meets or exceeds standard life company thresholds.

The financing structure on a deal like this would typically include a 15 to 20 year fixed-rate term, 30 year amortization with a potential interest-only period for the first two to three years for a qualified sponsor, non-recourse with standard carve-outs, and a declining prepayment schedule or yield maintenance provision aligned with the hold strategy. From receipt of a complete application package to closing, the direct correspondent process runs 45 to 75 days on a clean deal. Sponsors who have their due diligence materials organized at the outset consistently hit the shorter end of that range.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Spokane

Life company execution wins on stabilized, long-hold, larger multifamily deals in Spokane when pricing precision and non-recourse permanence are the priority. Against agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, life company rates are typically 10 to 30 basis points tighter for comparable stabilized product, the loan documents are simpler, and the relationship is more direct. Agency wins on higher leverage, shorter terms, and loans under $5 million where the correspondent minimum deal size creates friction.

CMBS is structurally competitive on larger transactions but carries more servicing complexity, less flexibility in prepayment structure, and a secondary market pricing dynamic that can introduce volatility at origination. Bank balance sheet lending is the right tool for transitional assets, shorter hold periods, or sponsors who need recourse flexibility or construction bridge financing. Life company capital does not compete on those terms and is not designed to.

Debt funds are appropriate for value-add Spokane multifamily where occupancy is below 88 percent, where the business plan requires a renovation period, or where the sponsor needs leverage above 65 percent. Once that asset stabilizes and the value-add execution is complete, refinancing into life company correspondent capital is frequently the optimal exit from a bridge loan. We work with sponsors at both stages of that cycle.
