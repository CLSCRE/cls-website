Life Company Office Lending in Phoenix: What the Market Looks Like in 2026

Phoenix occupies a favorable position in the life company capital pecking order, and that holds true even for office, where national life company appetite has narrowed considerably since 2020. The macro case for Phoenix remains intact: sustained population growth, a broadening employment base anchored by semiconductor investment from TSMC and Intel, and a legislative environment with no rent control and a predictable regulatory framework. Life companies allocating permanent capital to Sun Belt markets consistently rank Phoenix alongside Dallas, Nashville, and Raleigh as preferred deployment targets. That backdrop does not make every Phoenix office deal financeable, but it does mean that quality product with the right tenancy profile receives genuine attention from life company credit committees.

The selectivity in Phoenix office lending today is asset-specific rather than market-specific. Life companies remain cautious on urban core product where remote work patterns have softened absorption and where lease rollover risk is harder to underwrite with confidence. Suburban Class A in Scottsdale, Chandler, Gilbert, and North Phoenix is a different conversation. These submarkets have absorbed tenant flight-to-quality demand, maintained healthier occupancy rates than the CBD, and attracted credit-quality tenants on longer initial lease terms. Medical office and medical office buildings concentrated in Scottsdale and Chandler are particularly attractive to life company capital given the mission-critical nature of the space and the institutional-grade tenancy that typically accompanies it. Government-tenanted suburban product anywhere in the metro fits the life company credit profile closely.

Sponsors approaching this market should enter with realistic expectations around leverage and lease seasoning. Life companies are not the lender for a Phoenix office asset at 70 percent LTV with a tenant roster rolling in three years. They are, however, among the most competitive sources of permanent capital for a well-occupied, long-WALT suburban or medical office asset in the 15,000-to-100,000-square-foot range with institutional sponsorship. For the right deal, life company execution in Phoenix delivers rate and term advantages that no other capital source currently matches.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Phoenix Office Deals

Underwriting standards for life company office lending have tightened since 2022 and the criteria being applied in 2026 reflect a more conservative baseline than the prior cycle. LTV for Phoenix office currently runs 50 to 60 percent for most stabilized product, with the best pricing reserved for deals structured at or below 55 percent. Sponsors who approach life companies expecting 65 percent leverage on office will find that execution is either unavailable or priced as a concession. Under-leveraged structures are not penalized; they are rewarded with spread compression.

On the rate side, life company office pricing in the current environment reflects a 10-year Treasury trading in the 4.25 to 4.40 percent range with life company spreads for stabilized Phoenix office typically running 130 to 175 basis points over the benchmark, depending on asset quality, tenancy, and loan size. Suburban Class A with credit tenants or government occupancy commands the tighter end of that spread. Multi-tenanted product with shorter remaining WALT or urban exposure prices wider. All-in rates for the most competitive Phoenix office executions are landing in the low-to-mid 5 percent range for 10-year fixed-rate product as of this writing, though borrowers should build in appropriate rate movement assumptions between application and closing.

DSCR requirements for life company office are generally set at 1.25x at underwritten cash flow, with some lenders applying a stress test at a rate above the note rate for sizing purposes. Occupancy floors matter: most life company credit committees want to see 90 percent or better physical occupancy and a weighted average lease term of no less than five years at origination, with preference for seven-plus. Phoenix medical office and government-tenanted suburban product routinely clears these thresholds. Standard multi-tenanted suburban office with near-term rollover exposure requires more work at the credit level. Non-recourse structure with standard bad-boy carve-outs is the baseline; in the current office market, some life companies are applying slightly broader carve-out language, and borrowers should review that language carefully during the commitment stage.

Typical Phoenix Life Company Office Deal Profile

A representative life company office transaction in Phoenix in the current market looks something like this: a suburban Class A building or campus in Scottsdale, Chandler, or Gilbert, built or substantially renovated after 2005, occupied at 92 percent or better, with a WALT of six years or longer anchored by one or two institutional or investment-grade tenants. Loan size typically falls between $10 million and $50 million, with the most competitive execution at $15 million and above. Medical office buildings in Scottsdale and Chandler with hospital system or multi-specialty group tenancy fit this profile closely and are among the most actively underwritten office subtypes in our Phoenix correspondent pipeline.

Sponsor profile matters as much as asset quality. Life companies want experienced commercial real estate operators with clean credit history, demonstrated asset management capability in the relevant product type, and a hold strategy aligned with the loan term. A 10 or 15-year fixed-rate non-recourse loan is a relationship instrument, and life company credit committees evaluate the sponsor accordingly. Timeline from LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days for a clean deal. Borrowers working through an indirect broker channel should budget 75 to 120 days or longer for the same loan due to home office submission and review cycles.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Phoenix Office

Life company financing is not the right execution for every Phoenix office deal, and being clear about that is part of the value a direct correspondent lender offers. For value-add office with occupancy below 85 percent or significant near-term rollover, a debt fund or bank bridge loan is the appropriate capital source. Life companies are permanent lenders and their underwriting reflects stabilized, in-place cash flow with minimal credit risk. They are not structured to accommodate a lease-up story.

CMBS is a competing option for larger stabilized Phoenix office loans and can occasionally reach higher leverage than life company execution. The trade-off is servicer rigidity, less favorable prepayment flexibility, and a more complex execution process. For sponsors who anticipate any possibility of asset-level changes during the loan term, life company servicing relationships are materially easier to work with than CMBS special servicer interactions.

Bank balance sheet lending for Phoenix office is available but typically limited to recourse structures, shorter fixed-rate terms, and lower loan sizes. Regional and community banks are appropriate for smaller deals or sponsors with existing depository relationships. For a $15 million or larger non-recourse office loan with a 10-year fixed rate, life company execution is generally superior to bank balance sheet on both pricing and structure. Debt funds fill the gap for transitional deals but carry floating rate exposure and fees that make them unsuitable for a long-term hold strategy.

Life company financing wins on pricing, term certainty, and non-recourse structure for stabilized, long-hold Phoenix office assets with institutional tenancy. For everything else, there is a better tool, and we will tell you which one fits your deal.
