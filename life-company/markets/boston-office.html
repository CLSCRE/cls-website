Life Company Office Lending in Boston: A First-Tier Market With Selective Standards

Boston has long been among the most coveted markets for life insurance company capital, and that status has not changed materially even as the national office market has faced significant headwinds since 2020. Life companies are active in Greater Boston precisely because the market's fundamentals are underwritten by something more durable than a single industry cycle: a deep concentration of academic medical centers, life sciences anchors, financial services firms, and federal agencies creates a tenant base that institutional lenders understand and underwrite with confidence. That said, life company appetite for standard office in Boston has narrowed considerably, and sponsors who approach this capital source without a clear picture of current standards risk wasting months on a deal that was never going to close.

The submarkets that attract life company attention in the Boston office context are specific. The Route 128 and I-95 technology and biotech corridor, spanning nodes in Waltham, Watertown, Burlington, and Framingham, remains fundable for suburban Class A product where tenancy is institutional and weighted average lease terms are long. Government-tenanted assets, medical office, and multi-tenanted buildings with creditworthy anchors are the most competitive candidates. Urban Class A in the Seaport and Back Bay can still get done with the right sponsorship and lease structure, but life companies are demanding more conservatism on leverage and are scrutinizing rent rolls far more carefully than they did five years ago. Cambridge and Kendall Square present a hybrid dynamic: pure lab and life sciences product commands significant life company interest, but standard office in those submarkets requires the same scrutiny applied elsewhere.

The broader Boston life company market remains highly competitive for multifamily, life sciences, and industrial, and that liquidity benefits office sponsors indirectly. Life companies active in Greater Boston have established relationships, local market knowledge, and approved asset management infrastructure already in place, which reduces friction on well-structured office deals that meet current credit standards. The question is not whether life company capital is available in Boston. It is whether your specific asset fits what life companies are actually buying today.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Boston Office Deals

Life companies underwriting Boston office in 2026 are operating in an environment where the 10-year Treasury is trading in the range of 4.3 percent and life company spreads for stabilized, well-tenanted office are generally running 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark. All-in fixed rates for the strongest deals are landing in a range that is still meaningfully below what bank balance sheet and most CMBS execution looks like today, which is where life company financing earns its position in the capital stack. But that pricing advantage is reserved for deals that meet tight credit criteria.

On leverage, life companies are currently underwriting Boston office at 50 to 60 percent loan-to-value, with the lower end of that range commanding better pricing and broader lender interest. Under-leveraged structures, where a sponsor brings significant equity or is refinancing a long-held asset with embedded appreciation, are the most competitive candidates. Debt service coverage requirements are typically running 1.30x to 1.40x on a stressed basis, and underwriters are stress-testing rent rolls by applying vacancy haircuts and mark-to-market analysis on any near-term lease expirations. A building with a weighted average lease term under five years is going to face significant scrutiny regardless of current occupancy.

Loan terms are typically 10 to 15 years fixed, with 25 to 30 year amortization. Life companies are not writing shorter-term floating-rate paper on office. If your business plan requires flexibility or a refinance event in three to five years, life company capital is the wrong tool. Prepayment is yield maintenance, meaning the lender's economics are protected for the full term. The non-recourse structure is standard, though carve-outs in the current market may be somewhat broader than what borrowers were seeing in 2018 and 2019. Sponsors should expect lender counsel to negotiate those carve-outs carefully, and strong sponsorship track records remain a meaningful factor in how that conversation goes.

Typical Boston Life Company Office Deal Profile

The life company office deal that gets done in Greater Boston today has a recognizable profile. Transaction size typically falls between $15 million and $75 million, with deals in the $20 million to $50 million range representing the most active segment of our pipeline. The asset is suburban Class A along the Route 128 corridor or in a well-located urban submarket, built or substantially renovated post-2000, with occupancy north of 90 percent at closing and a rent roll anchored by creditworthy tenants on leases with meaningful term remaining. Medical office and government-tenanted product commands the most competitive execution. Multi-tenanted assets with staggered lease expirations and strong historical occupancy are fundable at the right leverage.

Sponsor profile matters more than it did in the prior cycle. Life companies want to see experienced ownership, a clean track record on comparable assets, and a clear hold strategy aligned with the loan term. First-time commercial borrowers or sponsors with complicated credit histories are not the target profile for this program. Minimum deal size is $5 million, but the program is most efficient and most competitive above $15 million.

Life Company Versus Other Permanent Options in Boston

Life company execution is the right answer for a specific set of office deals in Boston, but it is not the universal solution, and sponsors are best served by understanding where it wins and where it does not.

Against CMBS, life company financing offers meaningfully better non-recourse carve-out terms, a cleaner servicing relationship, and often better pricing on larger, stabilized transactions. CMBS wins on leverage flexibility and on deals where the sponsor needs more proceeds than a life company will provide at 55 to 60 percent LTV. For value-add office with near-term lease rollover, CMBS or bank balance sheet is typically the more appropriate source.

Bank balance sheet lending in Boston remains active for relationship borrowers, and for floating-rate or shorter-term bridge needs, a bank or debt fund is the right call. Life company capital is not a fit for transitional assets, renovation plays, or anything with meaningful lease-up risk. Agency financing applies only to multifamily and is not relevant for office.

Debt funds can close faster and go higher on leverage, but they price significantly wider than life companies on permanent capital, and their floating-rate structures introduce refinance risk that does not exist on a 10 or 15 year life company fixed. For a long-hold stabilized office asset where a sponsor wants certainty of execution, competitive fixed-rate pricing, and a non-recourse structure, life company capital remains the most efficient tool in the market.
