Life Company Multifamily Lending in Tampa: Market Context

Tampa Bay has emerged as one of the more active life company lending markets in the Southeast, and the trajectory is not accidental. Sustained in-migration from high-tax Northeast and Midwest markets, a no-income-tax Florida environment, and one of the strongest rent growth profiles in the Sun Belt over the last decade have made the Tampa metro a consistent underwriting target for life insurance company capital. Unlike some secondary markets where life companies participate selectively, Tampa now draws regular attention from institutional lenders who view the market as a durable, long-term hold environment rather than a cyclical bet.

Life companies are most active on stabilized Class A and B multifamily assets concentrated in submarkets with demonstrated absorption depth: Channelside and Ybor City for urban infill product, Westshore and South Tampa for mid-rise and garden assets with strong employment proximity, and the St. Petersburg and Clearwater corridors for well-occupied suburban communities serving the growing professional and retiree base across Pinellas County. Assets in Hillsborough County with easy access to the I-75 and I-4 interchange zone are also attracting institutional interest, particularly where workforce housing fundamentals remain tight. Underwriters are not chasing fringe locations. The capital targets proven submarkets with low vacancy histories and demonstrable rent growth, not transitional neighborhoods.

Florida's landlord-friendly legal environment and the absence of rent control legislation make Tampa multifamily structurally appealing for the long fixed-rate terms that define life company paper. A borrower placing 25-year fixed-rate non-recourse debt on a stabilized Westshore mid-rise is doing so in a state where the regulatory environment is unlikely to erode cash flow predictability over that hold period. That risk calculus matters to life company credit committees, and it is one reason Tampa consistently clears underwriting hurdles that slower-growth markets do not.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Tampa Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting in Tampa starts with stabilization. These lenders are not basis lenders and they are not repositioning lenders. They want assets that are performing, meaning sustained occupancy at or above 90 to 93 percent across a trailing 12-month period, market-rate leases with manageable near-term rollover, and a rent roll that reflects actual market conditions rather than pro forma assumptions. Class A properties in Channelside or Westshore with institutionally managed operations are the clearest path to approval. Class B assets in strong suburban locations can qualify, but they require tighter occupancy floors and more conservative cash flow underwriting.

On the capital stack, life companies in Tampa are typically underwriting to 55 to 65 percent LTV for most stabilized assets. Premier sponsorship in a demonstrably strong submarket can push toward 65 to 70 percent, but that ceiling is not routine. DSCR requirements generally run 1.25x to 1.35x at the underwritten rate, and life company underwriters stress-test cash flow using actual in-place income rather than market projections. For 2026 execution, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.30 percent, life company spreads on stabilized Tampa multifamily are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, placing all-in fixed rates in a range that is typically 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency execution for the same stabilized product.

Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 25 years with 25 to 30 year amortization, and full-term interest-only is available for high-quality sponsorship on strong assets. Prepayment structures are either a declining schedule or yield maintenance, with lockout periods negotiable at origination. Non-recourse is standard, with carve-outs limited to fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy. For sponsors with long-term hold strategies and institutional-quality assets, this structure is difficult to replicate with any other capital source in the Tampa market.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. That distinction has real mechanics behind it, and it matters in ways that are concrete, not theoretical. As a direct correspondent, we underwrite, commit, and close these loans in our own name. The life insurance company capital funds the loan, but the credit decision, the term sheet, and the commitment letter come from us, not from a home office underwriter at an insurance company that has never walked the asset or spoken with the sponsor.

The difference between this model and the brokered model is structural. A mortgage broker submitting a deal to a life company's home office is, in effect, pitching a deal to an unknown underwriter operating under internal credit parameters the broker cannot fully control or anticipate. That process involves a submission, an internal review, a credit committee presentation, and a response timeline driven by the life company's internal workflow rather than the deal's actual urgency. The borrower has no direct relationship with the decision-maker. Questions about structure, exceptions, or timing go through an intermediary. Timeline risk is real, and deals do fall out of that process for reasons that have nothing to do with the quality of the asset.

As a direct correspondent lender, we own the underwriting relationship. One underwriter, one credit process, one decision. When a Tampa sponsor asks about an exception to the standard prepayment structure or wants to discuss interest-only on a Class A Westshore asset, that conversation happens directly with the people making the credit call. That access compresses the timeline and produces better-structured commitments. It also means that when we issue a term sheet, it reflects a genuine credit view, not an estimate of what a home office might approve.

Typical Tampa Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

A representative life company multifamily transaction in Tampa looks something like this: a 200 to 350 unit Class A or B-plus garden or mid-rise asset in Westshore, South Tampa, or the St. Petersburg corridor, built between 2005 and 2020, running at 93 to 97 percent occupancy on a trailing basis, with a rent roll reflecting current market rates and limited near-term lease concentration. Loan sizing typically falls in the $8 million to $40 million range, where life company execution is most competitive against agency alternatives.

Sponsorship matters. Life companies in Tampa want operators with a demonstrated track record in Florida multifamily, clean balance sheets, and institutional reporting capabilities. First-time sponsors or sponsors without relevant asset-class experience will face significant headwinds regardless of asset quality. Net worth and liquidity requirements are real and are verified, not waived.

Timeline from signed LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction runs 45 to 75 days on a well-organized deal. Transactions that require submission to a life company's home office for approval routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on the life company's internal credit calendar and deal volume. For sponsors with rate lock requirements or acquisition timelines, that difference is often determinative.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Tampa

For stabilized multifamily in Tampa, life company financing competes most directly with Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac agency execution. Agency is a strong alternative and should not be dismissed. Agency products offer higher leverage (up to 80 percent LTV in some cases), more flexible prepayment structures, and deep familiarity in the broker and lender market. For sponsors who need leverage above 65 percent, or who want step-down prepayment flexibility over a shorter hold period, agency execution will often win. Life company execution wins on rate for stabilized, lower-leverage deals where the spread advantage of 10 to 30 basis points over agency compounds materially across a 15 or 25 year term. On a $20 million loan held for 20 years, that spread differential is not cosmetic.

CMBS is a viable alternative for larger transactions or for assets with characteristics that sit outside life company credit boxes, including higher vacancy, transitional leasing, or sponsorship with credit complexity. CMBS pricing is generally wider than life company for comparable stabilized product, and the servicing and special servicing structure introduces operational friction that long-term holders often find unattractive. Bank balance sheet lending in Tampa is available for smaller deals and bridge situations but does not compete on rate or term for permanent financing. Debt funds serve value-add and transitional deals where neither life companies nor agency lenders will participate.

The honest summary: if you own a stabilized, well-occupied Class A or B multifamily asset in a core Tampa submarket and your strategy is a long-term hold at conservative leverage, life company financing is the most competitive permanent execution available in this market today.
