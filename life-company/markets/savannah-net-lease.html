Life Company Net Lease Lending in Savannah, GA

Savannah has undergone a structural transformation over the past decade that has fundamentally reshaped how life insurance companies allocate capital across Georgia. The Port of Savannah, now the largest single container terminal in North America, has drawn a wave of institutional investment that extends well beyond the industrial sector. As major users including Amazon and Hyundai's Metaplant America in nearby Bryan County anchor the region's logistics and electric vehicle supply chain ecosystem, the supporting retail and service infrastructure has expanded rapidly across Pooler, Richmond Hill, Garden City, and the Effingham County corridor. That expansion has produced a pipeline of NNN and net-leased retail assets with the exact credit profile life insurance companies target: investment-grade tenants, long absolute NNN leases, and suburban markets with durable population growth driven by employment, not speculation.

For net lease specifically, life companies are drawn to Savannah's suburban retail submarkets because the underlying demand drivers are genuine. Pooler has become one of the fastest-growing communities in Georgia, with national credit tenants including Dollar General, AutoZone, Walgreens, and Starbucks executing long-term leases to capture the household formation surge tied to Hyundai Metaplant employment and port logistics jobs. Richmond Hill and Rincon are showing similar dynamics. These are not speculative lease-up plays. They are stabilized, long-duration income streams with institutional-grade tenants in markets that life companies are actively tracking. The result is that Savannah net lease deals, particularly those in the Pooler and Effingham County growth corridors, are landing on life company allocation lists alongside the industrial assets that dominate the broader Savannah story.

Life company appetite in Savannah for net lease is selective but real. Assets must be well-located within established retail nodes, not tertiary pads with exposure to a single traffic pattern. Tenants must be investment-grade or near-investment-grade with strong unit-level economics. And lease terms must be long enough to match life company liability horizons. When those criteria are met, Savannah net lease deals compete for some of the most efficient permanent capital in the market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Savannah Net Lease Deals

Life companies underwriting net lease deals in Savannah in 2026 are working from a consistent playbook. LTV tolerances typically run 60 to 70 percent, with true investment-grade tenants on long absolute NNN leases occasionally reaching 75 percent. The more important metric is debt service coverage, where life companies generally want to see a minimum 1.25x DSCR with stronger requirements, sometimes 1.35x or higher, for tenants below investment-grade thresholds. With the 10-year Treasury holding near 4.3 percent and life company spreads for stabilized NNN assets in the 125 to 175 basis point range, the all-in fixed rate on a well-structured Savannah deal reflects a cost of capital that is genuinely competitive for long-hold sponsors who do not want to refinance every five to seven years.

Term structure is a primary underwriting variable. Life companies will lend for 10 to 25 years fixed, but they want the loan term to match or slightly trail the remaining lease term. A Walgreens with 18 years of remaining absolute NNN term is an ideal candidate for a 15-year fixed-rate, non-recourse structure. A deal with eight years of remaining lease and no extension options is going to face real resistance regardless of the tenant's credit profile. Full-term interest-only is achievable for investment-grade tenants on strong locations, which significantly improves cash-on-cash returns for sponsors who are holding for the income, not the amortization.

Prepayment flexibility is limited in the life company structure. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule is standard, which means this capital is appropriate for sponsors with genuine long-term hold intentions. For a Savannah investor placing a Dollar General or AutoZone into a long-term portfolio, that structure is a feature. For anyone who anticipates selling or recapitalizing within five years, other capital sources are more appropriate.

Typical Savannah Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease deal in the Savannah market in 2026 looks something like this: a single-tenant retail property in Pooler or Richmond Hill, constructed within the last 10 to 15 years, occupied by an investment-grade credit tenant on an absolute NNN lease with 12 or more years of remaining term. Loan size typically falls between $3 million and $15 million for a single-asset transaction, with portfolio executions occasionally running higher. Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Savannah

Life company financing is not the right tool for every Savannah net lease deal, and understanding where it wins and where it does not is part of making the right capital decision.

Against CMBS, life company execution is superior for investment-grade credit tenants with long lease terms. CMBS is a securitized product with standardized structures and limited flexibility. Life company pricing on true NNN deals with strong tenants is typically tighter than CMBS spreads, the non-recourse structure is cleaner, and the prepayment certainty is better for long-hold sponsors. CMBS becomes more competitive for secondary credit tenants or deals that do not meet life company LTV or lease-term requirements.

Bank balance sheet financing can offer more flexibility on LTV and lease term, but banks are pricing floating-rate or shorter fixed-rate products that do not match the long-term hold strategy most net lease investors are pursuing. A seven-year bank loan on a 15-year NNN lease creates refinance risk that a 15-year life company fixed-rate structure eliminates entirely.

Debt funds are the right answer for value-add situations, short lease terms, or sponsors who need leverage above 70 percent. They are not competing with life company pricing on stabilized, investment-grade NNN assets, and they should not be. The cost of capital in a debt fund execution reflects the risk the lender is taking. Life company pricing reflects the stability of the cash flow.

Agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac applies to multifamily assets and is not a direct comparison for net lease retail. For Savannah multifamily in submarkets like Downtown, Pooler, or Richmond Hill, agency execution remains competitive, but that is a separate program and a separate analysis.
