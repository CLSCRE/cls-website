Life Company Net Lease Lending in Cincinnati, OH

Cincinnati occupies a compelling position in the Midwest life company capital landscape, and net lease assets in this market have drawn consistent institutional attention from life company correspondents for good reason. The regional economy anchored by Procter and Gamble, Kroger, and a deep logistics infrastructure along the I-71 and I-75 corridors gives life company underwriters exactly what they want to see: long-tenured corporate occupiers, predictable consumer-driven demand, and lease structures that align with institutional hold periods. For single-tenant NNN assets, the Cincinnati suburban ring from West Chester to Mason to Kenwood represents some of the most financeable retail in the Midwest, with investment-grade tenants, high household incomes, and stable population trends that support lease renewal assumptions at maturity.

The Northern Kentucky submarkets, particularly Florence and Covington, extend the Cincinnati MSA's reach into a geography that life companies increasingly treat as a seamless part of the same investment thesis. Florence, in particular, has emerged as a prime target for life company capital on NNN retail given its position as a regional retail hub serving both Ohio and Kentucky residents, with traffic counts and household income demographics that support investment-grade tenant underwriting. The absence of rent control anywhere in Ohio further strengthens the life company case for long-duration net lease exposure in this market.

Where life companies exercise selectivity in Cincinnati is largely a function of lease term and tenant credit. A Walgreens or Dollar General on an absolute NNN lease with 12 or more years remaining in Kenwood or West Chester is a natural fit for life company correspondent capital. A secondary-credit tenant on a modified gross lease in a tertiary submarket requires significantly more structuring work. Life company appetite in this market is genuine but disciplined, and sponsors benefit from working with a lender who already knows where that line is drawn before the first conversation begins.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Cincinnati Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for Cincinnati net lease starts with the tenant, not the building. Investment-grade credit tenants including Walgreens, AutoZone, Dollar General, Starbucks, and similar nationally recognized operators command the strongest life company pricing and the most favorable loan structure. For these tenants, loan-to-value typically ranges from 60 to 70 percent, with the upper end of that range available when lease term remaining extends beyond 15 years and the lease is structured as absolute NNN with no landlord obligations. Non-investment-grade tenants are not automatically disqualifying, but they compress proceeds and require more structural support.

On rates, life company spreads for stabilized investment-grade NNN in Cincinnati are running in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading near 4.30 percent through early 2026. All-in fixed rates for the strongest credit tenant deals are sitting in the mid-to-upper 5s, with less creditworthy tenants or shorter lease terms pushing toward 6 percent or beyond depending on structure. These are long-duration fixed instruments, typically 10 to 25 year terms, structured to match or closely track remaining lease term so that both the lender and the borrower face the lease renewal question at the same time.

DSCR requirements for life company net lease financing in this market typically fall in the 1.25x to 1.35x range at origination, though full-term interest-only structures for investment-grade tenants mean the cash-on-cash carry during the hold period is often materially better than amortizing alternatives. Non-recourse is standard across the program. Prepayment is almost universally yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which means sponsors need to be underwriting a genuine long-term hold strategy when they choose life company capital. These loans are not built for a three-year exit.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions operates as a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. That distinction is not a marketing description. It has specific mechanical meaning: when we underwrite a net lease deal in Cincinnati, we underwrite it internally against our life company's criteria, issue a commitment in our own name, and close the loan as the lender of record. The life company capital is behind us, but the underwriting decision, the term sheet, and the closing process all flow through our shop, not through a home office review cycle that we have no visibility into or control over.

Brokers who place life company deals do something structurally different. They package a loan request and submit it to a life company's home office, where an underwriter the sponsor has never spoken to reviews the deal, may re-trade terms, and operates on a timeline driven by that institution's internal pipeline and committee schedule. Sponsors working through that model often wait 30 to 45 days just to receive a preliminary indication, and the person who sold them on the loan has limited ability to advocate for deal-specific nuances once the file is in someone else's hands.

As a direct correspondent lender, we eliminate that layer entirely. One underwriting relationship, one set of credit criteria, and direct line-of-sight from the first conversation to the closing table. For Cincinnati net lease sponsors, that means faster term sheets, more predictable timelines, and the ability to have a real conversation about deal structure rather than waiting to see what comes back from a black box. When a lease has an unusual landlord obligation or a tenant has a unique corporate structure, those are conversations we can have directly with the decision-maker, not questions we submit and hope get answered favorably.

Typical Cincinnati Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in Cincinnati looks something like this: a single-tenant retail property in West Chester or Mason occupied by a national investment-grade retailer, built or renovated within the last 10 to 15 years, with 12 to 18 years of absolute NNN lease term remaining. Loan size is typically in the $3M to $15M range for single-asset transactions, though portfolio executions of multiple Cincinnati-area NNN assets can push well above that. The sponsor profile tends toward experienced net lease investors who understand the yield maintenance structure and are not underwriting a short-term flip.

Timeline from a signed LOI to closing on a direct correspondent transaction in this market typically runs 45 to 75 days, assuming clean title, a straightforward ALTA survey, and no environmental issues. That compares to 75 to 120 days or longer for deals placed through the brokered life company channel, where submission, review, and committee approvals add weeks to a process the sponsor cannot accelerate. For deals with 1031 exchange timelines or seller deadlines, the direct correspondent timeline is often the difference between making the exchange and missing it.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Cincinnati

Life company financing wins most directly against CMBS for Cincinnati NNN deals where the sponsor has a genuine long-term hold horizon, wants non-recourse execution, and is not expecting to sell or refinance within five years. CMBS can match non-recourse and often offers slightly higher leverage, but defeasance costs at exit and servicer friction during the hold period are real drawbacks that experienced sponsors weigh carefully. For investment-grade credit tenants with strong lease terms, life company pricing is typically tighter than CMBS by 20 to 40 basis points, and the absence of a REMIC structure simplifies loan assumption and disposition planning.

Bank balance sheet debt offers more flexibility on prepayment and can handle transitional situations that life companies will not touch, but banks rarely offer terms beyond seven years on commercial real estate, and the floating rate exposure on shorter fixed periods introduces refinance risk at exactly the wrong time for a long-duration net lease asset. Debt funds fill value-add and transitional roles that life companies are not in the business of filling. If the lease has fewer than five years remaining or the tenant is below investment grade, a debt fund or bridge lender is the right conversation. If the asset is stabilized, the tenant is creditworthy, and the sponsor intends to hold, life company correspondent capital is generally the most efficient permanent financing available in this market.
