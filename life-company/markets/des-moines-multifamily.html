Life Company Multifamily Lending in Des Moines

Des Moines occupies an unusual position in the life company capital markets universe. Unlike most secondary markets where institutional lenders allocate selectively and only during favorable spread environments, Des Moines benefits from something structural: several of the largest life insurance companies in the country, including Principal Financial Group, EMC Insurance, and Meredith Corporation's legacy capital platforms, are headquartered here. That institutional familiarity with local market fundamentals translates directly into consistent, deep allocation to Des Moines commercial real estate, including multifamily, across full market cycles. Life companies that know a market at the institutional level tend to lend through volatility rather than pulling back at the first sign of macro pressure.

For multifamily specifically, life company capital flows most actively toward the suburban growth corridors that have driven the metro's population and income gains over the past decade. West Des Moines, Ankeny, Johnston, and Urbandale represent the primary targets: suburban submarkets with strong household formation, employer density, and rent growth supported by in-migration from higher-cost Midwest metros. Class A and upper-tier Class B product in these corridors checks every box life company underwriters prioritize: stabilized occupancy, institutional-quality construction, tenants with above-average household incomes, and a regulatory environment in Iowa that carries no rent control risk. Iowa's landlord-friendly statutes matter to permanent lenders underwriting 15 to 25 year hold periods.

The Clive submarket and the broader western suburbs have also attracted meaningful life company attention as mixed-use and transit-proximate multifamily product has matured there. For borrowers with institutional-quality assets in these corridors, life company execution is not simply one option among several. For stabilized, long-hold multifamily in the $8M to $50M range, it is typically the most competitive permanent structure available, both on rate and on non-recourse terms.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Des Moines Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting in Des Moines multifamily is disciplined and consistent. These are buy-and-hold investors matching long-duration liabilities to long-duration assets, which shapes every underwriting parameter. LTV for most stabilized assets in the Des Moines suburbs will land between 55 and 65 percent. Premier sponsors with deep multifamily operating track records and gateway-market portfolios can occasionally access up to 70 percent, but that is the exception and requires strong in-place cash flow to support it. Life companies are not competing for the top of the capital stack; they are competing on rate and structure for borrowers who value certainty and term over maximum proceeds.

In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Des Moines multifamily are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury for the chosen term. That positions life company all-in rates 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency execution for the same stabilized, non-value-add product, which is a material pricing advantage on a 10 to 25 year fixed instrument. DSCR minimums typically run 1.25x on the underwritten net operating income, with stronger underwriting assumptions applied to vacancy and management expense than a bank lender might use. Life companies underwrite to own, not to syndicate, and their conservatism on DSCR reflects that.

Fixed terms of 10 to 25 years with 25 to 30 year amortization are standard. Full-term interest-only is available for select deals with exceptional sponsorship and low leverage. Non-recourse structure is standard, with carve-outs limited to bad-boy triggers: fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy. Prepayment flexibility varies by lender and structure; declining prepayment schedules and yield maintenance are both available, and lockout periods are negotiable at origination. Borrowers with long-term hold strategies who want to lock cost of capital for a decade or more, without signing a personal guarantee, are precisely the profile this capital is designed to serve.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital. That designation has a specific, concrete meaning that distinguishes it from the brokered life company market, and that distinction matters operationally for borrowers.

In the brokered model, a mortgage broker originates a deal and submits the loan package to a life company's home office for underwriting, approval, and commitment. The borrower's sponsor narrative, property data, and credit story pass through an intermediary and land with an underwriter the borrower has never spoken to, at a company the broker may or may not have a deep relationship with. That process introduces approval uncertainty, timeline variability, and a communication chain that slows decisions. The home office may have allocation constraints the broker is not aware of. The underwriter reviewing the package has no relationship with the sponsor. Deal structure questions require back-and-forth that adds days or weeks to every decision point.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites in-house, issues commitments in our own name, and closes these loans directly. There is no submission to a home office. There is no unknown underwriter reviewing the package at a third party. The borrower has a single underwriting relationship from term sheet through closing. That translates to faster decisions, cleaner structure conversations, and closing timelines that typically run 45 to 75 days from LOI to close, compared to 75 to 120 days or more for deals moving through an indirect submission process. For borrowers in competitive acquisitions or those managing refinance timing around a maturing note, the timeline difference is not marginal. It is often the deciding factor in which capital source wins the deal.

Typical Des Moines Life Company Multifamily Deal Profile

A representative life company multifamily transaction in Des Moines looks something like this: a 150 to 300 unit Class A or strong Class B garden or mid-rise community in West Des Moines, Ankeny, Johnston, or Urbandale, built within the last 15 years, stabilized at 93 percent or better occupancy, with in-place rents at or near market. Loan sizing falls in the $8M to $35M range for most suburban product, with larger transactions on institutional-scale communities. The sponsor holds an institutional or family-office-level track record in multifamily, with a clear long-term hold intent and no near-term exit strategy that conflicts with the fixed-term structure.

Occupancy must be stabilized at close. Life company capital does not fund lease-up. Recency of construction matters but is not a hard cutoff; well-maintained 1990s and early 2000s vintage product with updated interiors and a documented capital improvement history can qualify. Environmental compliance is reviewed carefully, and title and survey requirements are institutional-grade. The closing process, handled directly by Commercial Lending Solutions as the direct correspondent lender, runs efficiently because the underwriting decisions are made internally, not pending external approval.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Des Moines

Borrowers in Des Moines stabilized multifamily have several permanent capital options, and life company execution does not win every deal. Understanding where it competes strongest and where other sources outperform is straightforward.

Against agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac), life company execution is most competitive on larger loans above $8M to $10M where the pricing advantage over agency is most visible, on 15 to 25 year fixed terms where agency products offer fewer options, and for borrowers who want non-recourse without the reporting and property management compliance requirements that agency servicing carries. For smaller loans, shorter terms, or borrowers who need 75 to 80 percent LTV, agency wins on proceeds and flexibility.

Against CMBS, life company execution wins on rate, non-recourse carve-out quality, and relationship. CMBS spreads for stabilized multifamily in secondary markets are generally wider than life company all-in rates, and CMBS comes with more restrictive servicing, harder prepayment structures, and less flexibility on deal-specific structure. CMBS makes sense when life company proceeds are insufficient or the asset does not meet life company credit standards.

Against bank balance sheet and debt funds, life company wins on term length and rate for stabilized assets. Banks rarely go beyond 5 to 7 year fixed terms on commercial real estate, and debt funds price at significant premiums to life company or agency rates. Value-add assets, bridge situations, or deals requiring 70 to 80 percent leverage belong with a debt fund or bank construction program. Life company capital is the permanent takeout, not the transition vehicle.
