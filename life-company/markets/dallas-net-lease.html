Life Company Net Lease Lending in Dallas, TX

Dallas has earned its position as one of the premier Sun Belt markets for life insurance company permanent capital, and net lease assets are no exception. The combination of zero state income tax, sustained corporate in-migration, and a regulatory environment that does not penalize property ownership creates the kind of underwriting certainty life companies require. When a life company underwrites a 20-year fixed-rate loan against a net lease asset, they are not just underwriting the tenant's credit. They are underwriting the market. Dallas checks that box with consistency that few other metros can match.

For single-tenant NNN and anchored retail specifically, the story concentrates in the high-traffic suburban corridors that have absorbed the population growth driving Dallas's expansion. The Legacy Town Center and Frisco submarket along the Dallas North Tollway corridor, the Allen and McKinney retail nodes along US-75, and established suburban trade areas in Plano and Las Colinas all represent exactly the kind of infill and near-infill locations where life companies want to place long-duration capital. These are not speculative intersections. They are proven retail nodes with demonstrated traffic counts, dense residential catchments, and tenant demand from the investment-grade retailers that life companies specifically target.

The net lease product aligns almost structurally with what life companies need from their real estate portfolios. Investment-grade tenants such as Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and Starbucks generate predictable, contractual cash flows over lease terms that often extend 10 to 20 years. That cash flow predictability mirrors the liability profile of a life insurance company's policy obligations in a way that multifamily rent rolls and office tenant rosters simply cannot replicate. Dallas's continued job growth, population inflow, and consumer spending fundamentals provide the demand-side confidence that makes a life company comfortable placing capital at competitive spreads in a market they intend to hold for decades.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Dallas Net Lease Deals

Life companies underwriting net lease assets in Dallas are running a fundamentals-first process. The starting point is tenant credit. Investment-grade rated tenants with 10 or more years of remaining lease term on an absolute NNN structure are the core target. Life companies do not want to manage real estate, and an absolute NNN lease on a credit tenant effectively removes property management from the equation for the duration of the loan. Lease term remaining at loan maturity is equally important. Structuring the loan term to match or trail the lease expiration is a standard practice, and Dallas's active retail corridors make it easier to build that case for the tenant's ongoing site commitment.

On the capital structure side, life companies in this market are generally underwriting to 60 to 70 percent LTV on stabilized NNN assets, with room to move toward 75 percent for the strongest investment-grade tenants with long remaining lease terms. Debt service coverage ratios typically need to land at or above 1.25x, and for investment-grade credit tenants, full-term interest-only is not an exception. It is the standard structure. The predictable NOI justifies it, and life companies price these loans accordingly.

From a rate environment perspective, heading into 2026 with the 10-year Treasury trading in the 4.25 to 4.35 percent range, life company spreads on stabilized investment-grade NNN assets in a market like Dallas are running roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year. That positions all-in fixed rates for the most competitive deals in the mid-to-upper five percent range, with pricing tightening for the strongest credit tenants and best-in-class suburban locations. Non-recourse execution is standard across the program. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule tied to the loan term, with the structure designed to match lease expiration and give sponsors flexibility on exit or refinance timing.

Typical Dallas Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Dallas market looks something like this: a single-tenant property in the $3M to $15M range, built within the last 10 to 20 years, located on a high-traffic suburban arterial in a submarket like Frisco, Plano, McKinney, or Las Colinas. The tenant is a publicly traded investment-grade credit. The lease has 12 to 18 years of remaining term on an absolute NNN structure. The sponsor is an institutional owner or a high-net-worth private investor seeking long-term fixed-rate debt, often as part of a 1031 exchange or portfolio recapitalization.

Loan sizing typically falls between $2M and $20M on a single-asset basis, with portfolio executions extending toward $50M when multiple NNN assets are cross-collateralized or individually quoted. Closing timelines for a direct correspondent transaction run 45 to 75 days from a signed LOI when title, environmental, and lease documentation are in order from day one. Sponsors who come to the table with clean due diligence packages and organized lease abstracts consistently hit the lower end of that range.

Life Company Versus Other Permanent Options in Dallas

Life company financing is not the right solution for every Dallas net lease deal, and experienced sponsors understand where each capital source performs best. For single-tenant NNN assets with investment-grade tenants, long lease terms, and a hold strategy of 10 years or more, life company execution is difficult to beat. The combination of non-recourse structure, fixed-rate certainty over the full loan term, and interest-only availability for qualified tenants produces a debt profile that is genuinely difficult to replicate elsewhere.

CMBS can finance net lease assets at comparable leverage in some cases, but the servicing structure, defeasance requirements, and lack of relationship flexibility make CMBS a less attractive option for sponsors who anticipate any lease-related complexity during the loan term. Bank balance sheet lending typically caps out at 5 to 7 year terms with recourse requirements, which creates refinance risk and personal liability exposure that long-term net lease investors generally prefer to avoid. Debt funds offer speed and flexibility but price at spreads that make them uncompetitive against life company paper on stabilized, credit-tenanted assets.

Where life companies lose is on leverage, speed for distressed or transitional assets, and value-add execution. A sponsor acquiring a net lease asset with near-term lease rollover, a sub-investment-grade tenant, or an above-market basis will find debt funds or bridge lenders more receptive. Life companies are permanent capital lenders, not turnaround capital. The stronger the tenancy and the longer the lease, the more competitive life company pricing becomes relative to every alternative in the market.
