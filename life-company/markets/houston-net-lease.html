Life Company Net Lease Lending in Houston

Houston is one of the most active life company CRE markets in the South, and net lease assets in the metro have drawn consistent correspondent capital for decades. The reasons are structural: Houston's sprawling suburban footprint, strong population growth, and a retail base anchored by necessity-driven and credit-grade tenants make single-tenant NNN assets here a natural fit for life company deployment. The metro's economic diversification across energy, healthcare, logistics, and professional services creates a durable consumer base that supports the long-term occupancy assumptions life companies underwrite to. Suburban corridors in Katy, Sugar Land, and the broader West Houston market have produced a reliable pipeline of Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and national QSR assets with 10-plus year absolute NNN leases that align almost exactly with a life company's liability matching objectives.

Houston's lack of a zoning code is a meaningful underwriting consideration that life companies take seriously. Clean exit optionality matters to lenders holding paper for 15 or 20 years, and Houston's permissive development environment reduces the regulatory friction that can complicate disposition scenarios in other large markets. Life companies are also drawn to the fact that Texas carries no rent control statute, which keeps lease structures clean and enforces the predictability that drives life company pricing. Within the metro, the strongest NNN activity for life company capital tends to concentrate along major retail corridors in the Energy Corridor, Katy, and Sugar Land, where credit tenants occupy well-located pad sites with strong traffic counts and long lease tails.

It is worth being direct about where life company appetite has retracted in Houston. Office product, particularly suburban office with energy-sector exposure, has faced meaningful headwinds since the 2015 oil downturn and has not fully recovered. Life companies remain cautious here and will not underwrite speculative office lease-up in most submarkets. Net lease retail with investment-grade credit tenants is a different conversation entirely, and sponsors approaching this market with the right asset type will find life company pricing and terms that are difficult to replicate through any other capital source.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Houston Net Lease Deals

The underwriting criteria for life company net lease financing in Houston follows the national framework but with local calibration. Leverage is typically structured at 55 to 65 percent LTV for standard assets, with execution at 70 to 75 percent available for investment-grade tenants with long remaining lease terms and strong real estate fundamentals. Life companies are not leverage-driven lenders, and sponsors seeking 75 percent or higher should look at CMBS or bank alternatives. The capital efficiency on a life company execution comes from rate and structure, not loan-to-value.

On pricing, the 10-year Treasury has traded in the 4.25 to 4.40 percent range through early 2026, and life company spreads for investment-grade net lease assets in markets like Houston have generally priced in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the corresponding Treasury, depending on tenant credit, lease term, and market liquidity. Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and similar investment-grade credits command the tighter end of that range. DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x and can move to 1.15x for the strongest credit tenants with long leases. Full-term interest-only is a standard feature on investment-grade deals and represents a meaningful economic advantage over amortizing alternatives. Loan terms run 10 to 25 years, typically structured to match or closely approximate the remaining lease term, which removes balloon risk from the capital stack at a point when lease rollover would otherwise create refinancing uncertainty.

Tenant credit is the single most important variable in life company net lease underwriting. Absolute NNN leases with investment-grade obligors and 10-plus years of remaining term represent the clearest path to life company execution. Secondary credit tenants are financeable but will price wider and face more rigorous real estate underwriting, including stronger site metrics and lease guarantee structures. The property itself needs to be well-located within its submarket, with demonstrable alternative use value that supports the life company's long-term collateral position.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. This distinction is not semantic, and it matters in concrete ways that affect how a deal moves from term sheet to close. As a direct correspondent, we underwrite, commit to, and close these loans in our own name. The credit decision is made in-house. There is no submission package traveling to a home office underwriter, no third-party approval committee reviewing our work, and no intermediary who needs to be convinced of a deal's merits before a commitment can be issued.

Brokers who submit net lease deals to a life company's home office are operating in a fundamentally different model. In that process, a deal lands at an underwriter the broker has no direct relationship with, competes for attention in a queue with other submissions, and can stall or die at any stage of the approval chain without the sponsor having meaningful visibility into why. Recourse structures, prepayment terms, and lease credit assessments all get filtered through an underwriter who may have a different read on the market than the originator who sourced the deal. None of that intermediary friction exists in a direct correspondent execution.

For Houston net lease sponsors, this means that when we issue a term sheet, we are committing our own correspondent capital, not previewing what we hope a life company's home office might approve. A sponsor negotiating terms is negotiating directly with the lender of record. That single-underwriter relationship also means we can give a clear answer quickly on credit edge cases, whether that is a tenant with near-term lease expiration, a blend-and-extend scenario, or a property with a unique site characteristic that requires judgment rather than formulaic credit scoring. The mechanics of a direct correspondent execution are simply different from the brokered model, and for a sophisticated sponsor with a time-sensitive acquisition or refinance, that difference is material.

Typical Houston Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease execution in the Houston market tends to look like this: a single-tenant retail pad or freestanding building in the $3M to $15M range, occupied by an investment-grade tenant such as a pharmacy, dollar store, auto parts retailer, or quick-service restaurant chain, with a remaining lease term of 12 to 18 years on an absolute NNN structure. The property is typically a 2010 or newer vintage, well-located on a hard corner or high-traffic arterial in a strong suburban submarket such as Katy, Sugar Land, or the western Energy Corridor retail corridor. Vintage matters: life companies prefer assets with minimal deferred maintenance and a clean environmental profile, and 1980s or 1990s assets carry more scrutiny regardless of tenant quality.

The sponsor profile for life company execution is a long-term holder, typically an experienced private investor, family office, or institutional owner seeking permanent fixed-rate debt and a capital structure that matches their hold period. Loan terms in the $3M to $20M range are common in Houston; larger portfolio executions in the $25M to $50M range are available for sponsors aggregating multiple assets under a single credit structure. Timeline from signed LOI to closing on a direct correspondent execution typically runs 45 to 75 days. Brokered submissions to a life company's home office frequently run 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on the home office underwriting queue and approval cycle.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Houston

For single-tenant NNN retail in Houston, life company financing is the most competitive execution available when the property, tenant, and sponsor align with the criteria above. The combination of non-recourse structure, full-term interest-only, and long fixed-rate terms at tight spreads is not replicated by any other capital source for this asset class. Bank balance sheet lenders can compete on leverage but rarely match life company pricing on term, and they almost never offer true non-recourse on NNN retail without meaningful carve-out exposure. CMBS can execute at higher LTVs and offers non-recourse structure, but prepayment mechanics are less flexible and the securitization process introduces execution risk that life company correspondent capital does not carry.

Debt funds fill a different part of the capital stack. They are the right answer for value-add acquisitions, lease-up scenarios, or properties with credit tenants below investment grade where life companies will not engage. If a Houston sponsor is acquiring a single-tenant asset with a 4-year lease remaining and a non-rated tenant, a debt fund or bank bridge loan is the appropriate tool. Agency financing is relevant for multifamily and does not apply to net lease retail. The honest summary is this: if the asset is stabilized, the tenant is investment grade, the hold period is long, and the sponsor wants the best fixed-rate cost of capital available in a non-recourse structure, life company correspondent financing is the right answer. For anything else, the conversation starts with a different program.
