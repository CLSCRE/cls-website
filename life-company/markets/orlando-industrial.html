Life Company Industrial Lending in Orlando

Orlando has quietly become one of the more compelling life company markets in the Southeast, and industrial is a core reason why. The I-4 corridor and Florida Turnpike interchange zones have attracted significant logistics, distribution, and light industrial development over the past several years, driven by population growth, port connectivity through Tampa and Jacksonville, and the region's emergence as a last-mile hub for Central Florida's expanding suburban consumer base. Life companies that once treated Orlando as a tertiary market now actively compete for stabilized Class A product along these corridors, particularly in Osceola County, the Turnpike interchange near Kissimmee, and the established distribution nodes north of the city toward Sanford and Lake Mary.

What distinguishes Orlando in 2025 and 2026 is the breadth of demand drivers supporting industrial occupancy beyond the obvious tourism economy. Healthcare expansion in Lake Nona, tech sector growth, and the sustained in-migration that has made Central Florida one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the country all create durable last-mile and regional distribution demand. Life companies underwriting industrial in this market are not betting solely on a single demand driver. They are underwriting a diversified occupier base, a landlord-favorable legal environment with no rent control, and a physical location that sits at the intersection of Florida's two primary transportation arteries. That combination is exactly what life company permanent capital is designed to finance.

Class A bulk warehouse and distribution facilities along I-4 and the Turnpike attract the tightest spreads and the most competitive life company execution. Light industrial parks with strong multi-tenant occupancy in Altamonte Springs, Sanford, and the Lake Mary submarket also generate genuine life company interest, particularly where tenancy is stable and weighted average lease term supports a 10-year or longer fixed-rate commitment. Older vintage product, shallow-bay configurations, or properties with near-term rollover concentration will face more selective underwriting, but well-leased modern product in these submarkets is as financeable with life company capital today as it has been at any point in the current cycle.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Orlando Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for Central Florida industrial centers on a straightforward set of criteria, but the application of those criteria is more nuanced than a simple checklist. Leverage is typically sized between 55 and 65 percent of stabilized value. Credit-tenant NNN structures with investment-grade or near-investment-grade occupancy can reach 70 percent in some cases, reflecting the cash flow certainty that life companies require to extend maximum proceeds. Deals below 55 percent LTV sometimes generate more competitive pricing, particularly for trophy logistics facilities where the life company views downside protection as minimal.

Fixed-rate terms range from 10 to 25 years. Ten and 15-year terms are most common for stabilized multi-tenant light industrial. Longer terms of 20 to 25 years are reserved primarily for credit-tenant NNN transactions where the lease term aligns with or exceeds the loan term, a structure that effectively converts the industrial loan into a bond-like instrument in the life company's portfolio. On the rate side, with the 10-year Treasury in the 4.25 to 4.35 percent range in 2026, stabilized Class A industrial pricing from a life company correspondent typically falls in the 150 to 225 basis point spread range over the relevant Treasury index, translating to all-in fixed rates that remain competitive against most alternative permanent capital sources for this asset class. Credit-tenant or trophy distribution product at lower leverage can price through that range.

DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x on a fully amortizing basis for standard industrial, with some flexibility to 1.20x for credit-tenant structures or full-term interest-only product. Interest-only periods, including full-term IO, are available for Class A logistics and distribution facilities that meet the asset quality and sponsorship thresholds life companies apply to their most favored transactions. Non-recourse is standard, subject to customary bad-act carve-outs. Prepayment is typically structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule, which borrowers should model carefully for assets they intend to sell before loan maturity.

Typical Orlando Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

A representative life company industrial transaction in Central Florida looks something like this: a Class A rear-load or cross-dock distribution facility in the 150,000 to 500,000 square foot range, constructed within the last 10 to 15 years, located along the I-4 or Turnpike corridor in Osceola County or the Sanford-Lake Mary submarket. The property is stabilized at 95 percent occupancy or above, with a weighted average lease term of seven years or more remaining. The lead tenant or tenants carry credit profiles that a life company underwriter can underwrite to directly.

Loan sizing typically falls between $10 million and $60 million for most Central Florida industrial transactions, though the program accommodates deals up to $100 million for larger logistics facilities. Sponsorship matters. Life companies in 2025 and 2026 prefer experienced institutional or established regional operators with demonstrated property management capability and a balance sheet that supports the non-recourse structure without introducing counterparty risk concerns. Timeline from executed LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction in this profile is typically 45 to 75 days, subject to appraisal and environmental delivery.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Orlando

Life company financing is not the right execution for every industrial deal in Orlando, and understanding where it wins and where it does not is important for positioning a transaction correctly from the start.

Against CMBS, life company execution typically wins on rate, structure, and lender relationship for stabilized Class A product in the $10 million to $100 million range. CMBS can accommodate higher leverage and more complex collateral structures, but the rate premium, reserve requirements, and servicer relationship trade-offs make it a secondary option when life company capital is available. For value-add industrial with near-term lease-up risk, CMBS is generally more flexible, but life companies will not compete on that profile.

Bank balance sheet financing is competitive for smaller industrial deals, shorter fixed-rate periods, and borrowers with strong depository relationships. Banks can move quickly and structure around specific borrower needs, but floating-rate risk, shorter amortization, and term limitations make them a poor substitute for life company permanent financing on long-hold stabilized assets. Debt funds offer speed and leverage, often at 65 to 75 percent LTV with floating rates, and they are the right tool for transitional industrial or lease-up plays. They are not designed to compete on permanent fixed-rate pricing for stabilized product.

Agency execution is not available for industrial assets. Life company capital is the dominant institutional permanent financing option for Class A industrial in the $10 million and above range, and in Central Florida's current market, it is priced to reflect that position.
