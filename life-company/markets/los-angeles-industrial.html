Life Company Industrial Lending in Los Angeles

Los Angeles has been one of the most active life company CRE markets in the country for decades, and industrial has emerged as the asset class where that capital competes most aggressively right now. The structural drivers are well understood at this point: the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach handle roughly 40 percent of U.S. containerized imports, last-mile demand in a market of 10 million people is structurally undersupplied, and land constraints across the South Bay, the LAX corridor, and the Inland Empire have produced rent growth and occupancy metrics that life company underwriters find compelling. These are the kinds of markets where permanent capital wants to be, and life companies have responded accordingly.

The submarkets life companies favor for industrial in the Los Angeles basin are specific. Class A logistics and bulk distribution in the Inland Empire, particularly along the I-10 and I-15 corridors, have attracted significant life company allocations given their scale, institutional tenancy, and long-term lease structures. South Bay assets near the port complex and the LAX cargo hub are similarly favored, particularly when they carry credit-tenant NNN leases. Light industrial parks in El Segundo, Burbank, and the eastern San Fernando Valley also qualify when tenancy is strong and the physical product is institutional grade. Life companies are not value-add players, and they are not interested in functionally obsolete buildings with near-term rollover. The Los Angeles market's high barriers to entry and deep tenant base mean that well-located, stabilized assets here tend to meet life company underwriting criteria more readily than in secondary markets.

For 2025 and into 2026, industrial is among the most favored asset classes for life company permanent capital nationally, and the Los Angeles market amplifies that preference. Compressed spreads for Class A logistics and distribution facilities reflect genuine competition among life companies for allocation into this asset class. Sponsors holding stabilized industrial in Los Angeles with long-weighted average lease terms should be actively evaluating life company execution against every other permanent financing option in the market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Los Angeles Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for industrial in Los Angeles is straightforward in its priorities and demanding in its execution. Loan-to-value ratios typically fall between 55 and 65 percent on stabilized assets, with credit-tenant NNN product potentially reaching 70 percent given the bond-like cash flow structure. These are not high-leverage executions. Life companies are pricing for long-term permanent hold scenarios, and the LTV parameters reflect that orientation. Sponsors coming to this market looking for 70 to 75 percent leverage on a value-add play should be talking to debt funds, not life companies.

On the rate side, with the 10-year Treasury trading in the range of 4.3 percent in 2026, life company spreads for stabilized industrial in a market like Los Angeles are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points for most qualifying assets. Class A logistics with long-term credit tenancy can price at the tighter end of that range or tighter still when life company appetite is strong. These are fixed-rate executions for terms of 10 to 25 years, with 25 to 30 year amortization schedules or full-term interest-only available for trophy distribution product. The fixed-rate, long-term structure is the defining feature of life company debt and the primary reason sponsors with long hold horizons pursue it.

DSCR requirements generally start at 1.25x on a stressed basis, with life companies using their own underwritten rents and vacancy assumptions rather than accepting in-place numbers at face value. Occupancy at loan closing is typically required to be 90 percent or better, and lease term remaining is scrutinized carefully. A 500,000 square foot distribution facility with a single tenant whose lease expires in year three of a 15-year loan term is going to face additional underwriting requirements regardless of the in-place cash flow. Non-recourse with standard bad-boy carve-outs is the default structure. Prepayment is yield maintenance or a declining schedule depending on the specific life company and deal structure.

The Los Angeles life company industrial deals that close efficiently through our correspondent program share a consistent profile. The property is Class A or institutional-grade Class B, built within the last 20 years, with clear heights appropriate to current logistics requirements. Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Los Angeles

Life company execution is not the right tool for every Los Angeles industrial deal, and sponsors benefit from understanding where it wins and where it does not. Against CMBS, life company financing is typically more competitive on rate for stabilized, institutional-quality assets in the $10 million and above range, offers more flexible prepayment negotiation, and does not carry the servicer rigidity that makes CMBS problematic for sponsors who may need to modify lease terms or request lender consents during the loan term. For long hold, stabilized assets, life company wins almost every time.

Against bank balance sheet lenders, life company financing offers longer fixed-rate terms and non-recourse structure that most bank products cannot match on industrial. Banks compete on flexibility, speed, and recourse for smaller deals or sponsors who need construction or bridge components. For a sponsor refinancing a stabilized South Bay distribution facility into a 15-year fixed rate, a bank is not the right conversation.

Debt funds compete on speed and leverage, not rate. When a sponsor needs 70 to 75 percent LTV, has near-term lease rollover, or is in a value-add execution, a debt fund is the appropriate capital source. Life companies are not going to solve a leverage problem or a lease-up situation. Agency financing applies to multifamily and is not relevant to industrial. The life company execution wins decisively when the asset is stabilized, the sponsor has a long hold horizon, and the priority is locking in the lowest long-term fixed rate with a non-recourse structure in a market where institutional capital wants to be.
