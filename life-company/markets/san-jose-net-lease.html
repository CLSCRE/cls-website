Life Company Net Lease Lending in San Jose

San Jose sits at the center of one of the most closely watched commercial real estate markets in the country, and life insurance companies know it. The Silicon Valley ecosystem anchored by Apple, Google, Cisco, Adobe, Nvidia, and Intel produces employer density and household income levels that underpin retail demand in ways most Sun Belt markets cannot replicate. For single-tenant net lease assets, that backdrop matters enormously: the same tech-driven consumer base that sustains above-average spending at credit tenants like Starbucks, AutoZone, and Walgreens also makes San Jose submarkets attractive to institutional capital with a long investment horizon. Life companies are selective here, but when they commit, they commit fully.

The submarkets that generate the most life company interest for net lease include North San Jose and Santa Clara, where proximity to major campuses concentrates daytime population and supports service retail; Willow Glen and Campbell, which offer dense residential density with strong income demographics; and Cupertino and Sunnyvale, where the Apple and Google footprints drive sustained foot traffic along major retail corridors. Assets positioned along the I-880 and Highway 101 corridors also benefit from logistics worker density that supports convenience retail and QSR tenants. Life companies financing net lease in San Jose are not buying California exposure generically. They are buying specific site-level fundamentals in proven nodes.

That selectivity cuts both ways. Life company capital in San Jose rewards sponsors who own the right property in the right location with among the tightest spreads available in the permanent financing market. It does not accommodate lease-up risk, speculative tenancy, or tertiary corridors regardless of the sponsor's balance sheet. The bar for execution is high, but for stabilized single-tenant NNN assets with investment-grade credit and substantial lease term remaining, no other capital source matches the cost and structure a direct life company correspondent can deliver in this market.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in San Jose Net Lease Deals

Life companies underwriting net lease in San Jose in 2026 are working from a base 10-year Treasury in the range of 4.25 to 4.35 percent, with spreads for investment-grade credit tenants on stabilized NNN assets ranging from approximately 125 to 175 basis points depending on tenant credit, lease duration, and site quality. All-in fixed rates for the strongest deals sit in a range that remains meaningfully inside CMBS and most bank balance sheet execution, which is exactly the pricing advantage long-hold sponsors are seeking when they approach correspondent lenders.

On structure, life companies in this market are underwriting to LTVs of 60 to 70 percent as a standard range, with the upper end of 70 to 75 percent reserved for investment-grade credit tenants carrying 15 or more years of remaining lease term on an absolute NNN basis. DSCR requirements typically start at 1.25x on a debt service basis, though full-term interest-only structures are commonly available for IG tenants, which can substantially improve cash-on-cash returns for sponsors who qualify. Loan terms run 10 to 25 years and are structured to align with or slightly trail the remaining lease term. Non-recourse is standard. Yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule is typical, making these structures best suited to sponsors with genuine long-term hold intentions.

The tenant profile is the single most important underwriting variable. Investment-grade names including Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, and similar national credit operators command the tightest spreads and the most favorable structural terms. Franchisee-guaranteed leases and non-rated tenants receive more conservative treatment on both LTV and pricing. In a market like San Jose where real estate values are inherently high, the combination of lower leverage and full-term IO can still produce attractive debt service coverage and sponsor economics, but the underwriting discipline is real.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company capital. That distinction has concrete operational meaning that separates it from the brokered mortgage model and from the experience most borrowers have had placing deals with life companies through intermediaries.

In the brokered model, a mortgage broker prepares a submission package and sends it to one or more life company home offices for review. The borrower then waits for an underwriter at a company they have never spoken to, reviewing criteria they cannot fully anticipate, inside a credit committee process with timelines the broker cannot control. Feedback comes through a third party. Pricing adjustments require another round trip. If the deal requires structural creativity, that negotiation happens at a distance through an intermediary who may or may not have the standing to push back effectively.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, issues the term sheet, commits, and closes these loans in its own name using life company correspondent capital. The underwriting relationship is direct. There is a single underwriter who knows the San Jose market, knows what the capital source wants to own, and can structure the deal accordingly before it ever goes to a credit committee. That translates to faster decisions, cleaner term sheets, and the ability to resolve deal-specific issues during the origination process rather than after a submission has been reviewed by people who have no direct relationship with the borrower. For a life company net lease transaction in San Jose, where timing can affect whether a sponsor meets a 1031 exchange deadline or secures rate lock at a favorable point in the rate cycle, that operational difference is not marginal. It is frequently decisive.

Typical San Jose Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in San Jose originates from a stabilized single-tenant retail asset in the $3M to $20M range, though deals up to $50M are within program parameters. The property is typically a 2000s or newer construction freestanding building, absolute NNN leased to an investment-grade retailer with 12 or more years of remaining primary term, located on a signalized hard corner or within a dominant retail node in one of the submarkets noted above. The borrower is an experienced CRE investor or family office with a clean balance sheet, no material prior defaults, and a genuine long-term hold strategy. First-time borrowers and sponsors with complex credit histories face a higher bar.

From a signed letter of intent to closing, a direct correspondent lender can typically execute in 45 to 75 days on a well-documented transaction. Deals placed through intermediaries with life company home offices routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer due to submission queue times, credit committee scheduling, and the inherent inefficiency of communication through a third party. In a 1031 exchange context or in a rate lock situation, that timeline difference has direct economic consequences.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in San Jose

For single-tenant NNN assets, life company correspondent financing is the most competitive permanent option when the deal is stabilized, the tenant is investment-grade, the loan is $2M or above, and the sponsor is holding long. No other capital source delivers the combination of non-recourse structure, full-term interest-only availability, and pricing at spreads of 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury on the same consistent basis.

CMBS can compete on leverage, occasionally reaching 70 to 75 percent LTV, and handles non-investment-grade tenants more comfortably than life companies. However, CMBS imposes more restrictive loan administration, cash management requirements, and servicer relationships that sophisticated borrowers frequently find onerous. Bank balance sheet lending offers flexibility on recourse and short-term structures but cannot match life company pricing on long-term fixed-rate permanent debt and typically caps out at 5 to 7 year terms. Debt funds are the right tool for bridge scenarios, lease-up execution, and value-add repositioning, but their cost of capital is not competitive against stabilized net lease assets with credit tenants. Agency financing is not applicable to net lease retail assets.

Life company execution is not the right answer for every deal. If the lease term is short, the tenant is not rated, the LTV requirement exceeds 70 percent, or the sponsor needs recourse to access higher leverage, other sources will be more efficient. The life company program is a precision instrument for the stabilized, long-term, credit-tenant net lease scenario. In San Jose, that scenario appears frequently enough to make life company correspondent relationships a core part of any serious net lease investor's capital stack.
