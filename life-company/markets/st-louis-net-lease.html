Life Company Net Lease Lending in St. Louis

St. Louis occupies a distinctive position in the life company capital landscape for net lease assets. The metro sits at the convergence of four major interstate corridors -- I-44, I-55, I-64, and I-70 -- making it one of the most strategically located logistics markets in the central United States. That infrastructure reality translates directly into life company appetite. Single-tenant retail and NNN assets anchored by investment-grade credit tenants along these corridors consistently attract life company interest because the underlying real estate fundamentals justify a 15 or 20-year fixed-rate commitment. Life companies are not chasing yield; they are matching long-dated liabilities to predictable income streams, and a 20-year absolute NNN lease with a Walgreens or Dollar General in a suburban St. Louis trade area fits that mandate precisely.

The submarkets that generate the most consistent life company interest for net lease assets are concentrated in West County, Chesterfield, Kirkwood, and along the primary retail corridors in Brentwood and Richmond Heights. These are established, dense suburban markets with proven retail demand, strong household income demographics, and limited new supply. A freestanding AutoZone or Starbucks in Chesterfield or a Dollar General in an outer Kirkwood corridor represents exactly the kind of real estate a life company wants to own for two decades: simple collateral, credit tenant, and no management complexity. Earth City and the I-70 logistics corridors are dominant for industrial net lease product, particularly assets with Boeing defense-related or freight tenants, where life company capital has been active for years.

Life companies are selective in St. Louis, as they are everywhere. They are not a high-volume execution channel, and they are not a fit for every deal that crosses a broker's desk. What they are is the most competitive permanent financing available for stabilized, investment-grade NNN assets with long remaining lease terms. Sponsors who understand that positioning -- and who come to the table with clean collateral, a creditworthy tenant, and a long-term hold strategy -- will find life company pricing compelling relative to any other source in the market today.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in St. Louis Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for St. Louis net lease assets is disciplined and consistent. Loan-to-value expectations run 60 to 70 percent for most transactions, with the upper end of that range reserved for investment-grade tenants with 10 or more years of remaining lease term. Absolute NNN structure is the strong preference -- the life company is not in the business of managing landlord obligations, and lease structures that introduce any operating expense exposure will be scrutinized accordingly. DSCR requirements typically fall in the 1.25 to 1.35 range, though full-term interest-only structures are available for investment-grade credit tenants, which compresses the effective DSCR threshold on those transactions.

Rate environment context matters for St. Louis sponsors evaluating life company execution in 2026. With the 10-year Treasury hovering in the 4.3 percent range, life company spreads for investment-grade NNN tenants are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over that benchmark for most stabilized suburban retail assets. The result is fixed-rate permanent financing in a range that is meaningfully competitive against CMBS for long-hold sponsors, particularly when the non-recourse structure and interest-only availability are factored in. Term length is structured to match the remaining lease, typically ranging from 10 to 25 years, and prepayment is yield maintenance or a declining schedule negotiated at origination. There is no short-term horizon with life company capital -- these are buy-and-hold structures designed for sponsors who intend to own the asset for the duration.

Tenant credit quality is the single most important underwriting variable. Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, Starbucks, and similarly rated national credit tenants are the core of life company net lease appetite in St. Louis. Weaker or unrated tenants will face more conservative LTV and may not qualify for interest-only structures. Lease term remaining at origination should be 10 years minimum; shorter remaining terms require a specific rationale or blend-and-extend structure to clear underwriting.

Typical St. Louis Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the St. Louis market looks something like this: a freestanding single-tenant retail asset in Chesterfield or Brentwood, occupied by an investment-grade national credit tenant under an absolute NNN lease with 12 to 18 years remaining. The property was built between 2005 and 2018, is well-located on a primary retail corridor, and the sponsor is a regional private investor or family office pursuing permanent fixed-rate debt to match a long-term hold. Loan size typically falls in the $3M to $15M range for single-asset transactions, with portfolio executions running to $25M or beyond where multiple properties are cross-collateralized.

Sponsor profile matters. Life companies prefer experienced commercial real estate owners with clean balance sheets and relevant asset management history. First-time buyers of net lease assets are not automatic disqualifiers, but they require a stronger overall credit picture to offset the experience gap.

Timeline from accepted term sheet to closing runs 45 to 75 days for a direct correspondent transaction. Brokered submissions to life company home offices routinely run 75 to 120 days or longer, depending on committee schedules and submission queue depth. For sponsors working within a 1031 exchange timeline or managing a rate-lock window, that difference is not academic.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in St. Louis

Life company financing is not the right execution for every St. Louis net lease deal, and sponsors are best served by understanding where it wins and where it does not.

Against CMBS, life company execution is more competitive for investment-grade credit tenants with long remaining lease terms, larger loan sizes, and sponsors who prioritize non-recourse fixed-rate debt over maximum proceeds. CMBS can go to higher leverage and is more tolerant of shorter remaining lease terms, but securitized debt introduces servicing complexity and less flexibility in any future disposition or refinance scenario. For a sponsor holding a Walgreens for 15 years with no near-term sale plan, life company pricing and structure typically wins.

Bank balance sheet debt is generally not a competitive alternative for long-term net lease financing. Banks do not offer 15 or 20-year fixed-rate terms, and their variable-rate or shorter-term structures create refinance risk that life company capital eliminates by design.

Debt funds are a fit for value-add, transitional, or lease-up scenarios where life company underwriting standards cannot be met. If a St. Louis retail asset has a tenant with under 7 years remaining or a below-investment-grade credit profile, a debt fund or bridge lender is the appropriate channel. Life companies are not a rescue execution for difficult deals.

For net lease assets that meet the credit tenant, long lease, and stabilized occupancy threshold, no other financing source in St. Louis matches life company execution on rate, term, and non-recourse structure simultaneously.
