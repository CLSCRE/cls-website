Life Company Industrial Lending in Tampa: A Market Built for Permanent Capital

Tampa Bay has emerged as one of the more active life company underwriting markets in the Southeast, and industrial is near the top of the target list. The I-4 and I-75 corridors feeding into Hillsborough County have absorbed significant logistics and distribution investment over the past several years, driven by population growth, port expansion at Port Tampa Bay, and the continued migration of distribution activity southward from higher-cost markets. Life companies that were selectively active in Tampa five years ago are now competing aggressively for stabilized Class A industrial in this market, particularly bulk warehouse and last-mile distribution facilities with strong tenancy and long weighted average lease terms.

What makes Tampa genuinely attractive to life company capital is the combination of in-migration, a landlord-friendly regulatory environment, and the metro's role as a distribution hub for the broader I-4 corridor stretching toward Orlando. Florida's absence of rent control and its consistent absorption of Northeast and Midwest population have made Tampa a preferred underwriting environment for permanent lenders with long investment horizons. For industrial specifically, the submarkets along I-4 east of downtown Tampa and the Hillsborough County logistics nodes along I-75 represent the highest concentration of life company deal flow in this asset class. Facilities serving the port, the airport industrial district, and the growing last-mile network feeding residential density in Westshore and South Tampa are all in scope for the right sponsor and property profile.

Life companies are not chasing every deal in this market. They are selective by nature, and Tampa industrial is no exception. The assets that attract firm commitments are stabilized, institutional-quality properties with credit or near-credit tenancy, modern clear heights, adequate truck court depth, and location within established logistics corridors. Functionally obsolete product, short remaining lease terms, or properties with significant deferred maintenance will not clear life company credit regardless of market strength. When the asset and sponsorship are right, however, Tampa industrial offers a compelling case for permanent capital placement.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Tampa Industrial Deals

Life company underwriting for Tampa industrial is disciplined and consistent. Loan-to-value typically ranges from 55 to 65 percent on standard industrial and warehouse product, with credit-tenant NNN structures occasionally reaching 70 percent where the lease term and tenant credit justify it. These are not leverage-maximizing executions. Life companies are pricing for permanence and credit quality, not for aggressive advance rates. Sponsors entering this execution with expectations calibrated to bank bridge or debt fund leverage will need to reset their assumptions.

Fixed-rate terms run 10 to 25 years, with the sweet spot for most Tampa industrial deals falling in the 10 to 15 year range for multi-tenant light industrial and the 15 to 25 year range for single-tenant NNN distribution facilities with long remaining lease terms. In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury in the range of 4.25 to 4.35 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Tampa industrial are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the comparable Treasury, depending on asset class, tenancy, and sponsor profile. Class A logistics and distribution facilities with credit tenants are seeing spread compression at the tighter end of that range, making life company execution genuinely competitive on an all-in cost basis relative to other fixed-rate permanent sources.

DSCR requirements generally fall in the 1.25x to 1.35x range on underwritten cash flow, with underwriters applying market vacancy assumptions and normalized expense loads rather than in-place numbers. Non-recourse structure is standard, with carve-outs for standard bad-boy acts. Full-term interest-only is available for trophy distribution product with credit tenancy, which meaningfully changes the cash-on-cash profile for sponsors structuring long holds. Prepayment is yield maintenance or a declining schedule, consistent with the long-duration nature of the capital.

Typical Tampa Life Company Industrial Deal Profile

The Tampa industrial transactions that close through our direct correspondent program share a consistent profile. Loan sizes range from $10 million to $75 million, with a preference for transactions at $15 million and above where the economics of permanent capital placement are most efficient. Properties are typically 2010 vintage or newer, Class A or strong Class B, with 28-foot or greater clear heights, ESFR sprinkler systems, and modern truck court configurations. Single-tenant net lease distribution facilities along the I-4 and I-75 corridors represent the highest-demand product for life company capital, though well-leased multi-tenant light industrial parks in Hillsborough County with strong rollover coverage also fit the program.

Occupancy at closing is expected to be 90 percent or better on a stabilized basis, with lease terms providing adequate coverage through the loan term or meaningful WALT for multi-tenant product. Sponsor profiles that fit this execution are experienced institutional owners or private operators with demonstrated industrial management history, clean financial statements, and sufficient liquidity post-closing. Timeline from LOI to closing for a direct correspondent transaction in this market runs 45 to 75 days for a clean deal, compared to 75 to 120 or more days for an indirect placement through the brokered channel.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Tampa

For industrial assets, life company financing does not compete with agency execution, which is limited to multifamily. The relevant comparisons for Tampa industrial are CMBS, bank balance sheet, and debt funds.

CMBS can offer higher leverage (up to 70 to 75 percent in some structures) and may be more flexible on secondary markets or shorter lease terms, but CMBS execution comes with securitization risk, servicer-driven inflexibility during the loan term, and spreads that are often wider than life company pricing on stabilized Class A product. For sponsors who intend a long hold and want the cleanest fixed-rate execution available, life company typically wins on pricing and structure for qualifying assets.

Bank balance sheet lending in Tampa industrial is active, particularly for relationships, but banks are pricing floating-rate and shorter fixed terms. Three to five year fixed periods with adjustments, or floating over SOFR, are common. For sponsors who want 15 or 20 years of rate certainty with a non-recourse structure, bank balance sheet is not the right capital source regardless of pricing at origination.

Debt funds are the right answer for value-add, lease-up, or higher-leverage needs that life companies will not touch. If a Tampa industrial asset is 70 percent occupied with two years of lease-up ahead of it, a debt fund bridge execution is appropriate. Once the asset is stabilized and the business plan is executed, that is precisely when a permanent takeout through a direct correspondent life company relationship makes sense.
