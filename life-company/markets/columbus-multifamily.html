Life Company Multifamily Lending in Columbus: A Market Worth Understanding

Columbus has moved from a secondary consideration to a primary target for life insurance company capital over the past several years, and the drivers are structural rather than cyclical. Ohio State University anchors a permanent base of renter demand across a broad income spectrum. The metro's employment base is genuinely diversified across healthcare, education, financial services, and technology, which matters to life company underwriters whose credit committees are looking decades forward, not quarters. No rent control exists at the state or local level, which is not a minor footnote for lenders pricing 15 and 25 year fixed-rate money. Columbus multifamily fundamentals rank among the strongest in the Midwest by absorption, rent growth trajectory, and new supply digestion.

The Intel Ohio mega-campus in New Albany is reshaping the eastern corridor of the metro in ways that are only beginning to surface in underwriting conversations. Intel's commitment of more than $20 billion to central Ohio is drawing semiconductor supply chain industrial users and a significant wave of highly compensated workers who will form the tenant base for Class A multifamily in New Albany, Westerville, and adjacent submarkets. Life companies are paying attention to this because their mandate is long-duration capital deployment into markets with identifiable economic tailwinds. The New Albany corridor now appears on shortlists alongside established submarkets like Short North, German Village, the Arena District, and Dublin, which have long commanded life company interest for their walkability, income demographics, and low vacancy rates.

That said, life companies are selective in Columbus as they are everywhere. They are not providing bridge money or funding lease-up. The assets that attract correspondent capital in this market are stabilized, professionally managed properties with demonstrated occupancy history, quality sponsorship, and a clear long-term hold rationale. Class A and Class B assets in core and established suburban submarkets are the target. Class C, workforce housing, and transitional assets generally do not fit the credit box regardless of how strong Columbus fundamentals look in the aggregate.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Columbus Multifamily Deals

Life company underwriting for Columbus multifamily centers on long-term credit quality, not short-term yield maximization. Loan-to-value requirements typically fall in the 55 to 65 percent range for most assets in this market, though premier sponsors with demonstrated track records in gateway and primary markets can access up to 70 percent LTV on select transactions. Debt service coverage ratios are generally required at 1.25x or better on an in-place basis, with underwriters stress-testing vacancy and expense assumptions over the life of the loan rather than simply using trailing 12-month actuals.

Fixed-rate terms run from 10 to 25 years, which distinguishes life company execution from virtually every other permanent capital source. In the current 2026 rate environment with the 10-year Treasury around 4.3 percent, life company spreads for stabilized Columbus multifamily are generally ranging from 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury, depending on asset quality, sponsorship strength, LTV, and term. That pricing lands life company rates 10 to 30 basis points inside comparable agency execution for the right deals, which is a meaningful difference on a $15 million or $30 million loan held for 15 years. Interest-only periods ranging from partial-term to full-term are available for strong sponsorship profiles, and the non-recourse structure is standard, with carve-outs limited to fraud, environmental liability, and voluntary bankruptcy. Amortization schedules run 25 to 30 years for loans that are not full-term interest-only.

Occupancy requirements are firm. Life companies in this market want to see sustained physical occupancy above 90 percent with economic occupancy to match. Assets in lease-up, those relying on concessions to hold occupancy, or those with pending capital programs that will temporarily displace tenants are not life company candidates until stabilization is complete and documented.

The Direct Correspondent Advantage

Commercial Lending Solutions is a direct correspondent lender for life insurance company financing. This distinction has real operational meaning that borrowers should understand before they engage any capital markets intermediary on a life company execution.

When a broker submits a deal to a life company's home office, the loan enters a queue. An underwriter the borrower has never met and may never speak to reviews the package according to that institution's current appetite, allocation position, and internal credit culture. If questions arise, they travel through the broker and back again. If the deal needs to be restructured, that conversation happens at arm's length, on the life company's timeline. Approval, modification, and commitment all depend on decisions made by people who have no direct relationship with the sponsor and no ownership of the outcome. This process routinely takes 75 to 120 days or longer, and it is not uncommon for deals to shift in structure or pricing after the initial submission.

As a direct correspondent lender, Commercial Lending Solutions underwrites, commits to, and closes life company loans in our own name. Our underwriter is the underwriter. Our credit decision is the credit decision. Borrowers have a single point of contact who controls the process from initial sizing through closing. When a Columbus sponsor brings us a stabilized Short North or New Albany asset, we are not forwarding that package to a home office for a stranger to review. We are underwriting it directly, which means faster term sheets, more transparent structuring conversations, and closing timelines that typically run 45 to 75 days rather than the 75 to 120 plus days common in the indirect submission model. For a borrower trying to close a refinance or execute on a rate lock strategy, that difference is not abstract.

The deals that close through our direct correspondent life company program in Columbus share a recognizable profile. Loan sizes typically run from $8 million to $50 million, with our program accommodating transactions from $5 million at the low end and up to $75 million for exceptional assets and sponsorship. Sponsors are typically experienced multifamily operators with meaningful equity in the deal and a demonstrable long-term hold strategy. Life companies are funding 15 and 20 year money. They want borrowers who intend to own the asset for most or all of that term, not sponsors looking for a two-year rate fix before a disposition. Occupancy at application should be 92 percent or above on an economic basis for a minimum of 90 days. Leases should reflect market rents without material concession exposure. Timeline from signed term sheet to closing runs 45 to 75 days under our direct correspondent process, assuming timely delivery of third-party reports and borrower documentation.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Columbus

For stabilized Columbus multifamily with a long hold period and a loan size above $8 million, life company execution is usually the most competitive permanent capital option available. Agency lenders through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are strong competitors and should always be quoted in parallel, but life company rates are typically inside agency for comparable stabilized assets in the current spread environment, and life company terms extend to 25 years versus agency maximums of 30 years on a more rigid amortization schedule. Sponsors who want full-term interest-only on a 20-year fixed will generally find life company execution more accommodating than agency.

CMBS can compete on proceeds at 65 to 70 percent LTV but carries less favorable prepayment mechanics, a more complex securitization process, and servicer relationships that many long-term holders find frustrating over time. Bank balance sheet loans and regional lender programs are appropriate for shorter hold periods, value-add business plans, and situations where recourse is acceptable in exchange for flexibility. Debt funds serve transitional assets, lease-up, and bridge scenarios where life company or agency execution is not yet available. When a Columbus sponsor is acquiring a value-add asset in Eastland or funding a ground-up development near Rickenbacker, a debt fund or construction lender is the right first conversation. When that same sponsor refinances the stabilized completed asset three years later, a life company correspondent loan is likely the most competitive exit.
