Life Company Net Lease Lending in Chicago, IL

Chicago has long been one of the most consequential life company markets in the Midwest, and net lease retail is a category where that capital remains consistently active. Life insurance companies have financed single-tenant NNN assets for decades because the product structure is nearly purpose-built for their balance sheets: long lease terms, predictable cash flows, and investment-grade tenants that carry their own credit ratings into the underwrite. In Chicago and its suburban collar counties, that dynamic plays out across a deep inventory of institutional-quality net lease assets anchored by tenants like Walgreens, Dollar General, AutoZone, and Starbucks. These properties trade in volume throughout the metro, and long-term permanent financing from life company capital is consistently among the most aggressive executions available for qualified deals.

Life companies operating in the Chicago market have grown more selective on certain asset classes in recent years. Illinois rent control debates and persistently high property tax burdens have made some investors cautious on multifamily, and life companies reflect that caution. But net lease retail occupies a different risk profile entirely. A corporate-guaranteed absolute NNN lease with 12 years of term remaining on a freestanding Walgreens in Naperville or a Dollar General on a hard corner in Schaumburg does not carry the same policy or tax exposure that makes multifamily underwriting complicated in Cook County. Life companies recognize that distinction, and suburban NNN assets in the collar counties, including Oak Brook, Rosemont, and the broader I-88 and I-90 corridors, continue to see strong life company appetite.

The geography matters for this program. Life companies are drawn to assets with strong daytime population density, established retail corridors, and tenants with long operating histories at their specific locations. Chicago's suburban markets deliver that profile consistently. A freestanding AutoZone on a major arterial in a built-out suburb with strong traffic counts and 10-plus years of lease term remaining is exactly the kind of collateral that fits a life company's 20-year liability horizon. That alignment between asset profile and lender need is what makes this market work, and what makes correspondent life company capital the right starting point for net lease sponsors who intend to hold.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Chicago Net Lease Deals

Life company underwriting for net lease assets in Chicago follows a consistent framework, and sponsors who understand it will structure cleaner deals from the outset. Leverage is typically capped at 60 to 70 percent loan-to-value, with the upper range reserved for investment-grade tenants carrying long remaining lease terms. A non-investment-grade tenant or a lease with less than 10 years remaining will underwrite closer to 55 to 60 percent LTV. Debt service coverage requirements generally sit at 1.25x or better on an interest-only basis, which for a full-term IO structure on an investment-grade credit tenant is usually not the binding constraint. Rate is.

In the current rate environment, with the 10-year Treasury operating in the 4.25 to 4.40 percent range heading into 2026, life company spreads for stabilized NNN assets with investment-grade tenants are running approximately 125 to 175 basis points over the corresponding Treasury. That puts all-in fixed rates in a range that remains materially attractive relative to floating-rate alternatives, particularly for sponsors who are underwriting a long hold and want to eliminate refinance risk. For credit tenants with long leases and clean absolute NNN structures, life company pricing is among the tightest available in the permanent debt market today.

Beyond LTV and pricing, life companies scrutinize lease structure carefully. Absolute NNN is the preferred form. Any landlord obligation, even a modest one, introduces ambiguity that complicates long-term underwriting. Lease term must extend through or beyond the loan maturity, which for a 15 or 20-year fixed instrument means 15 to 20 years of remaining term at close. Sponsor quality matters as well, though it matters less for net lease than for other asset classes precisely because the tenant credit is doing much of the work. Non-recourse is standard for investment-grade tenants with appropriate LTV, and full-term interest-only is a realistic outcome for the strongest deals in this market.

Typical Chicago Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A realistic life company net lease deal in the Chicago metro generally starts at $2 million for a single-tenant asset, with a preferred floor of $5 million for maximum execution quality. Most deals in this market fall between $4 million and $20 million, reflecting freestanding single-tenant retail on suburban arterials in Cook, DuPage, Lake, and Will counties. The typical asset is a 2000s or newer vintage single-tenant building in good physical condition with a corporate or corporate-guaranteed lease, 10 to 20 years of original term, and an absolute NNN structure. Occupancy is 100 percent by definition for single-tenant product.

Sponsor profile for life company net lease capital tends toward institutional owners, private equity platforms, and high-net-worth private investors who are buying or holding for long-term income rather than value-add execution. Life companies do not finance lease-up risk or vacant repositioning plays. The asset must be stabilized and performing. Timeline from a signed term sheet to closing for a correspondent transaction typically runs 45 to 75 days, compared to 75 to 120 or more days for deals routed through indirect channels where home office approval and third-party review cycles add time at every stage.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Chicago

For net lease assets in Chicago, life company financing competes primarily with CMBS, bank balance sheet, and to a lesser extent debt funds. Agency financing is not relevant for single-tenant retail. CMBS is the most common alternative and has real advantages for sponsors who need higher leverage, shorter decision timelines on smaller deals, or more flexibility on lease term. CMBS will routinely go to 70 to 75 percent LTV and accommodates a broader range of tenant credits. However, CMBS carries yield maintenance or defeasance prepayment with less structural flexibility, and the securitized execution means no relationship lender to negotiate with if circumstances change.

Bank balance sheet financing is appropriate for shorter hold periods, value-add transitions, or deals with lease rollover in the near term where a life company's long-term fixed structure is not a good match. Banks offer more flexibility on prepayment and term length but carry floating-rate or shorter fixed-rate risk that is inconsistent with a 15 or 20-year hold strategy. Debt funds fill in at higher leverage points and for non-investment-grade tenants, but at meaningfully higher cost of capital and typically on floating-rate terms.

Life company correspondent financing wins decisively when the sponsor is holding long, the tenant is investment-grade, the lease is absolute NNN with strong remaining term, and the priority is locking in fixed-rate non-recourse debt at the tightest spread available in the permanent market. For that profile in Chicago's suburban net lease market, no other execution is more competitive.
