Life Company Net Lease Lending in Orlando: What the Capital Wants to Own

Orlando has moved well past its identity as a single-industry tourism market. The I-4 corridor now anchors a diversified regional economy built around healthcare, technology, defense simulation, and logistics, and that diversification has not gone unnoticed by institutional capital. Life insurance companies have been steadily increasing their Orlando allocations over the past several years, drawn by Florida's population inflows, the absence of rent control, and a suburban retail base that continues to serve one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the country. For net lease assets specifically, Orlando offers a compelling combination of credit tenant density and long-term lease structures that align well with life company liability matching requirements.

The submarkets generating the most life company interest on the net lease side are the high-traffic suburban corridors surrounding Lake Nona, Lake Mary, Altamonte Springs, and the Kissimmee and Osceola County growth zones to the south. These are not tertiary markets with occupancy risk. They are established or rapidly maturing suburban nodes with strong daytime population counts, income demographics, and tenant credit profiles that life companies have consistently underwrote at their best pricing tiers. A Walgreens, AutoZone, or Dollar General on a hard corner in Lake Mary or a Starbucks drive-through adjacent to a Lake Nona medical campus represents exactly the kind of single-tenant, long-lease, predictable-NOI asset that life companies have financed for decades.

It is worth being specific about where life companies in Orlando are more selective. They are not chasing secondary credit tenants on short remaining lease terms in fringe locations. The program is built for investment-grade tenants, absolute NNN structures, and lease terms that extend well into the next decade. Properties with below-investment-grade tenants, near-term lease expirations, or locations without demonstrable trade area demand will find a different capital source more appropriate. For the right asset profile, however, Orlando is actively in favor with life company allocations, and execution here is genuinely competitive.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Orlando Net Lease Deals

Life companies underwriting net lease assets in Orlando are not running a checklist in isolation. They are making a long-duration credit decision, and the underwriting reflects that. LTV expectations for this market run 60 to 70 percent for most stabilized NNN assets, with the upper range of 75 percent reserved for investment-grade tenants on long remaining lease terms. Sponsors expecting agency-level leverage in the 75 to 80 percent range should understand that life company capital is priced for quality and structure, not leverage maximization.

On the rate side, with the 10-year Treasury holding in the 4.25 to 4.35 percent range entering 2026, life company spreads for well-located Orlando net lease assets with investment-grade tenants are landing in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the benchmark. That produces all-in fixed rates that remain among the most competitive permanent financing available in the market today for the right asset class. DSCR requirements typically fall in the 1.20 to 1.30 range on IO debt service, and for full-term interest-only structures, which are standard for investment-grade credit tenants, the coverage calculation is straightforward given the absolute NNN pass-through structure. Amortization is often not required at all for the strongest credit profiles.

Term structures run 10 to 25 years, and the best executions match the loan term closely to the remaining lease term. A 15-year Walgreens with 14 years remaining is a natural fit for a 12 to 15 year fixed-rate life company structure with yield maintenance or a declining prepayment schedule. Life companies are not interested in refinancing risk at maturity, and they structure accordingly. Non-recourse is standard. Carve-outs follow standard bad-boy conventions, but there is no personal guarantee requirement on the primary debt service obligation for qualifying assets and sponsors.

Typical Orlando Life Company Net Lease Deal Profile

A representative life company net lease transaction in the Orlando market looks something like this: a single-tenant retail property in the $3M to $20M range, built between 2005 and 2020, occupied by an investment-grade tenant on an absolute NNN lease with 10 to 20 years of remaining term. The property sits on a high-traffic suburban corridor in a submarket like Lake Nona, Lake Mary, Altamonte Springs, or along a primary Kissimmee artery with demonstrated trade area density. The tenant is a national credit retailer, pharmacy, quick-service restaurant, or essential service operator with a lease that requires zero landlord involvement in operations.

The sponsor profile for this capital is an experienced owner with a demonstrated hold history, a clean balance sheet, and no immediate plans to dispose of the asset. Life company capital is not construction financing, value-add bridge debt, or a short-term hold solution. The sponsor is typically acquiring a stabilized asset for long-term hold or refinancing an existing position at maturity. Loan sizes from $2M are considered, with $5M or above preferred for portfolio submissions. The timeline from signed LOI to funded loan runs 45 to 75 days when structured and submitted correctly through our direct correspondent process.

Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Orlando

For single-tenant NNN retail, life company financing is the dominant permanent execution when the asset and sponsor profile qualify. It is worth understanding where the competition sits. CMBS offers higher leverage at times and broader credit tenant flexibility, but comes with securitization complexity, servicing transfer risk, and less flexible prepayment structures. For a long-hold sponsor who never plans to sell before maturity, CMBS servicer risk alone is a meaningful consideration. Bank balance sheet lending offers relationship flexibility and sometimes faster closing on smaller deals, but terms are typically 5 to 7 years with recourse requirements and floating or shorter fixed periods that introduce refinancing risk.

Debt funds can close quickly and go to higher leverage, but their cost of capital reflects that flexibility. For a 65 percent LTV, non-recourse, 15-year fixed-rate hold on an investment-grade NNN asset, a debt fund is simply not competitive on pricing. Agency lending (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) is not applicable to single-tenant net lease retail. Life company financing wins decisively when the hold period is long, the asset is stabilized, the tenant is investment-grade, and the sponsor does not need aggressive leverage. For value-add, lease-up, or shorter-term business plans, other capital sources are more appropriate.
