Life Company Self Storage Lending in Savannah, GA

Savannah has undergone one of the most dramatic economic transformations of any secondary market in the country over the past decade, and life insurance companies have taken notice. The Port of Savannah, the largest single container terminal in North America, anchors a logistics ecosystem that now includes major Amazon distribution infrastructure, the Hyundai Metaplant America electric vehicle assembly facility in nearby Ellabell, and a rapidly expanding EV supply chain corridor stretching through Effingham County and into Rincon. That industrial and population growth has created exactly the kind of dense, working-population demand drivers that life companies look for when evaluating self storage allocations in secondary markets.

Within the Savannah MSA, the submarkets drawing the most institutional attention for self storage are Pooler, Garden City, and the I-16 industrial corridor. Pooler in particular has become a high-demand pocket, driven by warehouse and logistics workers, new residential construction, and the support population servicing the Hyundai Metaplant supply chain. Richmond Hill and Rincon are also generating interest as residential expansion pushes deeper into Bryan and Effingham counties. Life companies targeting self storage in Savannah are not looking at the historic downtown core for new storage supply. They want climate-controlled, institutionally operated facilities in the suburban infill locations where population growth is actually happening.

Georgia's regulatory environment is a meaningful backdrop to the life company calculus here. With no rent control at the state level and a landlord-favorable legal framework, life companies can underwrite self storage cash flows in Savannah with a relatively clean set of assumptions about long-term income trajectory. That regulatory clarity, combined with the port-driven population growth, makes Savannah one of the more attractive secondary markets in the Southeast for life company self storage capital in 2026.

What Life Companies Are Looking For in Savannah Self Storage Deals

Life insurance companies are not flexible capital. They are permanent, disciplined capital, and the underwriting criteria for self storage in Savannah reflects that. The baseline for a life company-eligible deal in this market starts with stabilized occupancy, typically 85 percent or better on an economic basis, sustained over a meaningful trailing period. A facility that just crossed 85 percent occupancy in the past 60 days is not a stabilized asset in the life company framework. They want seasoned income, ideally 12 to 24 months of demonstrated performance at or near stabilization.

On leverage, life companies are generally comfortable in the 55 to 65 percent loan-to-value range for stabilized self storage, with the lower end of that range reserved for larger facilities, climate-controlled assets, and deals in the most supply-constrained locations. Debt service coverage requirements typically sit at 1.30x or better, with some lenders pushing toward 1.35x in markets where new supply risk is a factor. In Savannah's growth submarkets like Pooler and Garden City, supply pipeline analysis will be part of the underwriting conversation. Sponsors should expect life company underwriters to pull permitting data and competitive supply studies as part of standard due diligence.

On pricing, life company spreads for stabilized self storage in 2026 are generally running in the 125 to 175 basis point range over the 10-year Treasury, which has been trading around 4.30 percent. All-in fixed rates for the best facilities in the best locations are therefore in the mid-to-upper 5 percent range, with weaker deals or more supply-exposed submarkets pricing wider. Terms run 10 to 20 years fixed, with 25 to 30 year amortization. Prepayment is structured as yield maintenance or a declining schedule. Non-recourse is standard, with the customary carve-outs for bad-act provisions. Sponsors running climate-controlled, institutional-quality facilities in Savannah's core growth corridors are going to see the most competitive execution from life company capital relative to other permanent sources.

Typical Savannah Life Company Self Storage Deal Profile

A representative life company self storage deal in the Savannah market today looks something like this: a climate-controlled, Class A facility in Pooler or along the I-16 logistics corridor, built within the last 10 to 15 years, operating at or above 87 percent economic occupancy with at least 18 months of stabilized performance. Life Company versus Other Permanent Options in Savannah Self Storage

Life company financing wins on rate and structure for the right deal profile, but it is not the right tool for every situation. CMBS can go higher leverage, often 65 to 70 percent LTV, and the securitization market is generally less prescriptive about asset class concentrations. For sponsors who need more proceeds than a 55 to 65 percent life company advance provides, CMBS is the more likely execution. The tradeoff is less flexible prepayment and defeasance rather than yield maintenance, which matters for sponsors who anticipate a sale or refinance event mid-term.

Bank balance sheet lenders are active in Savannah self storage at smaller loan sizes, generally under $5M, and can offer more flexible structures for transitional or lease-up assets. But bank pricing on permanent fixed-rate self storage is rarely competitive with life company all-in rates for stabilized deals, and recourse requirements at community and regional banks are a meaningful distinction from the non-recourse standard that life companies provide. Debt funds fill the gap on value-add and lease-up deals where life companies simply will not go, pricing higher but offering execution certainty for assets that are not yet at the stabilization threshold.

Agency financing, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, does not apply to self storage. That is a multifamily-only product. Life company capital is, in practice, the premier permanent financing option for stabilized, Class A self storage in Savannah for sponsors with long hold horizons and no near-term exit pressure.
