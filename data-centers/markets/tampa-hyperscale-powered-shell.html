Hyperscale and Powered Shell Financing in Tampa

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at CLS CRE. Tampa, FL data centers market.

How Hyperscale and Powered Shell Financing Works in Tampa

Tampa's data center market has moved from an emerging story to an actionable investment thesis over the past two years. The metro's combination of strong enterprise demand from financial services and healthcare sectors, dual-path fiber infrastructure, and proximity to submarine cable landing stations serving the Southeast corridor has positioned it as a credible alternative to Miami for hyperscale operators evaluating Florida deployments. Build-to-suit and powered shell projects targeting tenants like Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud are now part of active development conversations across the market, particularly in suburban corridors with access to utility-grade substation infrastructure and land capable of accommodating 100-plus megawatt campuses.

Within the Tampa metro, hyperscale-oriented development activity concentrates in submarkets where large land parcels, power delivery capacity, and connectivity converge. The Brandon and Temple Terrace corridors have drawn attention from developers pursuing campus-scale facilities given their proximity to transmission infrastructure and lower land basis relative to the urban core. Westshore and the I-275 technology corridor remain relevant for powered shell projects with enterprise and hybrid cloud tenants, while St. Petersburg and Clearwater offer alternative positioning for operators seeking diverse routing paths. The key constraint across all Tampa submarkets is power. Florida Power and Light and TECO interconnection timelines are a material variable in site feasibility, and developers pursuing hyperscale here need utility coordination moving in parallel with land control.

The capital markets thesis for Tampa hyperscale is built on the same fundamentals driving the national market: long-term NNN leases to investment-grade tenants with massive power purchase commitments that produce highly predictable, bond-like cash flows. The AI infrastructure buildout has accelerated hyperscale absorption nationally, and Tampa benefits as operators diversify away from supply-constrained primary markets. Lenders who understand the credit quality of an AWS or Azure lease on a purpose-built facility underwrite this product differently than conventional industrial or office. The lease is the collateral story, and Tampa's market momentum provides the demand layer that supports presale lease execution before a shovel goes in the ground.

Lender Appetite and Capital Stack for Tampa Hyperscale and Powered Shell

The most competitive capital sources for Tampa hyperscale and powered shell follow a clear pattern dictated by asset stabilization. For ground-up construction, national bank syndicates and specialty data center construction funds dominate. These lenders are comfortable underwriting to a presigned hyperscale lease with an investment-grade tenant, typically sizing construction loans in the 55 to 65 percent loan-to-cost range. With SOFR currently around 3.6 percent, all-in construction pricing for pre-leased hyperscale runs roughly SOFR plus 200 to 350 basis points, translating to a total rate range in the high fives to low sevens depending on deal complexity, sponsor track record, and lease structure. For deals without a presale lease, proceeds compress and lender appetite narrows substantially.

For stabilized hyperscale facilities with executed NNN leases to investment-grade tenants, life insurance companies become the most competitive permanent lenders. Life companies price to the credit of the tenant rather than the real estate cycle, and a long-term AWS or Azure lease structures cleanly into a life co permanent loan at spreads of roughly 125 to 175 basis points over the 10-year Treasury. With the 10-year currently around 4.3 percent, that positions stabilized hyperscale permanent debt in the high fives to mid-sixes on an all-in basis. Life company LTV on this product ranges from 55 to 65 percent with full-term interest only available for the strongest credit tenants and lease structures. CMBS is active for stabilized single-tenant hyperscale as well, pricing somewhat wider than life companies but offering higher leverage in the 60 to 70 percent range for borrowers willing to accept defeasance or yield maintenance prepayment structures. Mezzanine debt and preferred equity are available from debt funds and specialty finance platforms to close leverage gaps on larger capital stacks, particularly during the construction and lease-up phase.

Tampa-specific lender commentary reflects cautious optimism. Debt funds and Florida-focused regional banks are the most active participants in the market today, drawn by growth fundamentals and willing to price in Florida-specific risk at spreads that compensate accordingly. Life companies are beginning to engage selectively on stabilized credit-tenanted assets but remain attentive to hurricane and flood zone exposure in site selection.

Underwriting Criteria That Matter in Tampa

Lenders underwriting Tampa hyperscale focus first on the lease and the tenant. An executed NNN lease with a creditworthy hyperscale operator carrying a 10-to-20-year term and contracted power purchase commitments in the 50 to 500 megawatt range is the foundation of every capital markets conversation. Without a signed lease or at minimum a letter of intent from an investment-grade tenant, construction financing options narrow to a small group of speculative capital providers, and permanent takeout lenders will not engage.

Site-level underwriting in Tampa carries additional weight relative to primary data center markets. Lenders require detailed flood zone analysis, storm surge modeling, and wind load specifications. FEMA flood map designation is a first-pass filter. Facilities in high-risk flood zones face significant underwriting friction regardless of lease quality. Structural specifications including raised critical infrastructure, hardened envelopes, and backup power redundancy must be documented. Life companies in particular will require these specifications to be addressed comprehensively in the offering package before issuing a term sheet.

Power infrastructure documentation is equally critical. Confirmed utility substation access, interconnection agreements, and timeline certainty around power delivery are scrutinized as heavily as the lease itself. A hyperscale facility without contracted power delivery is a speculative land position, not a financeable development. Lenders also evaluate fiber diversity, water access for cooling systems, and construction cost validation. With shell construction running $150 to $300 per square foot and mechanical and power infrastructure adding $500 to $1,500 per square foot depending on power density, independent cost validation from a qualified data center construction consultant is standard in any institutional capital process.

Typical Deal Profile and Timeline

A realistic Tampa hyperscale financing engagement involves a sponsor with demonstrated data center development experience, a site under control in a submarket with confirmed power availability, and an active tenant relationship with a hyperscale or enterprise cloud operator. Deal sizes typically start at $100 million for single-building powered shell projects and scale into the hundreds of millions for campus developments with phased power delivery. Lenders want to see sponsors who have delivered comparable product, as hyperscale construction is operationally complex and lender due diligence will scrutinize the development team's track record with equal rigor as the tenant credit.

Timeline from executed term sheet through construction loan closing typically runs 90 to 150 days for pre-leased hyperscale with an experienced sponsor and clean site. The critical path items are environmental and geotechnical diligence, utility confirmation, lease review, and lender-required third-party reports including cost review, appraisal, and environmental assessments. Sponsors who arrive at the financing process with these components already advanced close faster and with better execution. Permanent loan refinancing at stabilization typically closes within 60 to 90 days of lease commencement with a life company or CMBS lender already engaged during construction.

Common Execution Pitfalls Specific to Tampa

The most common execution failure in Tampa hyperscale deals is underestimating utility timeline risk. Sponsors frequently control sites with strong demand drivers and active tenant interest but have not initiated formal interconnection requests with Florida Power and Light or TECO. Hyperscale power delivery timelines in Florida can extend two to four years from initial application to energization. Lenders will not credit uncommitted power capacity, and tenant LOIs often include power delivery milestones as closing conditions. Starting utility coordination late creates deal risk that financing cannot solve.

A second pitfall is insufficient attention to Florida hazard underwriting at the site selection stage. Developers who acquire land based on price or location without conducting detailed flood zone and storm surge analysis before lender engagement frequently encounter insurability problems and structural cost overruns that renegotiate the capital stack. Life companies and institutional construction lenders will not accept elevated flood risk without significant mitigation documentation, and some sites in the Tampa Bay geography are simply not viable for hyperscale financing regardless of demand fundamentals.

Third, sponsors occasionally approach lenders with tenant interest letters rather than executed leases and expect construction financing to proceed on the same timeline as a pre-leased deal. The gap between a letter of intent and a signed hyperscale lease is substantial in terms of lender confidence, and most institutional construction lenders will not issue a firm commitment without an executed lease. Timing the financing process to the lease negotiation timeline, not ahead of it, is essential.

Finally, Tampa's relative market immaturity compared to primary data center markets like Northern Virginia or Phoenix means that comparable transaction data supporting appraisal conclusions is thin. Sponsors who engage appraisers without hyperscale-specific data center experience routinely produce appraisal reports that lenders reject or require significant revision. Engaging a qualified data center appraiser with demonstrated Florida market experience at the outset of the financing process avoids costly delays at the term sheet stage.
