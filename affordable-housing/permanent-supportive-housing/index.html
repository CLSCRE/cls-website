Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) Financing

Permanent supportive housing routinely layers six or more capital sources, NPLH, HHAP, Section 8 project-based vouchers, 9% LIHTC equity, and in Los Angeles, Proposition HHH bond proceeds, making it one of the most complex affordable capital stacks to close. It's also one of the strongest-scoring categories in most state 9% LIHTC competitive rounds, because homeless and special-needs set-asides carry heavy scoring weight.

The Capital Stack

Most Permanent Supportive Housing deals layer several sources rather than a single loan:

  • Construction loan (CDFI, community development bank, or HUD 221(d)(4) for larger deals)
  • NPLH (No Place Like Home) capital: $30,000 to $60,000 per unit for qualified permanent supportive housing
  • HHAP: Local homeless housing assistance and prevention funds from city or county
  • Proposition HHH (Los Angeles only): City bond proceeds for permanent supportive housing
  • 9% LIHTC equity (strong PSH projects score well in most state HFA rounds)
  • Section 8 project-based vouchers (HUD VASH or CoC-sponsored) as permanent operating subsidy
  • Sponsor equity and deferred developer fee

Six sources means six timelines to coordinate

NPLH, HHAP, and LIHTC each run on independent application and award calendars, and a project-based voucher commitment from a PHA or Continuum of Care adds a fourth. The construction lender needs enough certainty on all of them to close, which means the real skill on PSH deals is sequencing commitments so the weakest link doesn't hold up the whole closing.

Services capacity is underwritten, not just the real estate

Most PSH funding sources require the operator to demonstrate genuine supportive services capacity, often with LAHSA or county behavioral health approval, before capital commits. Sponsors who haven't lined up a credible services partner early lose months later trying to satisfy this requirement after the real estate underwriting is otherwise done.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does NPLH provide per unit?

$30,000 to $60,000 per qualifying unit statewide in California, funded from a $2B program, layered against the rest of the capital stack rather than covering the deal on its own.

Is Proposition HHH still active?

It's LA-specific and winding down as of 2026; sponsors should confirm current fund availability before underwriting to it as a certain source.

Why does PSH score well in 9% LIHTC rounds?

Most state HFA scoring systems award significant points for homeless and special-needs set-asides, which PSH projects satisfy by design, giving them a real competitive edge over standard family or senior affordable applications in the same round.

What population does PSH typically serve?

Chronically homeless individuals, people with serious mental illness or substance use disorders, veterans through HUD-VASH vouchers, and transition-age youth, depending on the specific funding source and local priorities.
