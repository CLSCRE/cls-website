HUD 221(d)(4) Multifamily Construction-to-Permanent Financing

HUD 221(d)(4) is the most favorable long-term capital available for affordable and workforce multifamily construction when the sponsor can absorb the timeline: up to 90% loan-to-cost on qualifying affordable deals, a 40-year fully amortizing fixed rate, and non-recourse except for standard carve-outs. The tradeoff is 12 to 18 months from application to construction closing and mandatory Davis-Bacon prevailing wage, which rules it out for anything time-sensitive.

The Capital Stack

Most HUD 221(d)(4) deals layer several sources rather than a single loan:

  • HUD 221(d)(4) first mortgage (FHA-insured, non-recourse, construction-to-perm)
  • 4% or 9% LIHTC investor equity where affordable set-asides qualify
  • Tax-exempt bond financing (often the same lender as HUD MAP lender on single-close structures)
  • State soft debt: MHP, AHSC, NPLH where eligible
  • Local soft debt: city housing trust funds, HOME, CDBG
  • Sponsor equity and deferred developer fee

The MAP lender relationship is the whole game

HUD 221(d)(4) only closes through an FHA-approved MAP (Multifamily Accelerated Processing) lender, and not every MAP lender has the same appetite, speed, or track record with HUD's regional office review. Picking the right MAP lender for a specific market and deal profile matters more on this program than on almost any other affordable capital source, because the lender's HUD relationship directly affects how long the application actually takes.

Where 221(d)(4) pairs with LIHTC and bonds

On affordable deals, the HUD first mortgage typically layers underneath 4% or 9% LIHTC equity, and on larger deals the same MAP lender can often also serve as the tax-exempt bond issuer's construction lender in a single-close structure. That single-close option is one of the more valuable, and underused, structuring choices on $20M+ affordable deals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does 221(d)(4) actually take from application to closing?

Typically 12 to 18 months to construction closing, which is why it's the wrong tool for a deal with a tight site control or entitlement expiration timeline.

Is Davis-Bacon prevailing wage really mandatory?

Yes, on every HUD-insured construction project without exception. Build it into the earliest budget, not after a GC bid comes back higher than the market-rate comparable would suggest.

What loan-to-cost can an affordable deal actually get?

Up to 90% LTC when 50% or more of the units are affordable at 80% AMI or below, versus 87.5% for straight market-rate deals, a meaningful difference in required sponsor equity.

Can 221(d)(4) close alongside LIHTC and tax-exempt bonds?

Yes, this is one of the most common affordable structures at the larger end of the deal-size spectrum, and the same MAP lender can sometimes serve both roles in a single-close structure.
