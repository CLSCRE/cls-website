9% LIHTC in West Palm Beach

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at CLS CRE. West Palm Beach, FL.

How 9% LIHTC Works in West Palm Beach

The 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit remains the most powerful financing tool available for affordable multifamily development in West Palm Beach, but it demands a level of preparation and local fluency that separates competitive applications from the rest of the field. Florida Housing Finance Corporation administers the competitive 9% credit through annual scoring rounds, and the Southeast Florida region consistently draws some of the most competitive application pools in the state. Sponsors working in West Palm Beach are navigating a layered regulatory environment: Florida Housing controls allocation, the City of West Palm Beach Community Services Department administers HOME and CDBG entitlement dollars that often serve as critical gap financing, and Palm Beach County Housing and Economic Sustainability runs a parallel program with its own substantial affordable housing resources. Assembling the right local relationships across all three of these channels is not optional. It is a prerequisite.

The sponsor profile that actually closes 9% deals in West Palm Beach typically combines experienced affordable development capacity with demonstrated local community ties. Florida Housing's scoring criteria reward local government support letters, proximity to services, and site readiness, and Palm Beach County's independent HOME entitlement program gives well-connected sponsors a real edge in closing the funding gap that even strong credit equity cannot fill. The Housing Authority of the City of West Palm Beach administers project-based vouchers, and a committed project-based voucher HAP contract can materially strengthen both the scoring profile and permanent debt underwriting. Sponsors who treat the WPBHA relationship as an afterthought routinely leave points and long-term operating stability on the table.

The Capital Stack in West Palm Beach

A 9% LIHTC deal in West Palm Beach will typically see approximately 70% of total development cost covered by tax credit equity placed by a syndicator or direct investor. That leaves roughly 30% to be assembled from a construction loan, permanent debt, soft debt, and sponsor equity including deferred developer fee. The construction loan is usually provided by a mission-focused CDFI, a community bank with an affordable housing platform, or a larger regional lender meeting CRA obligations. Permanent debt on a 9% deal is materially smaller than what you would see on a 4% bond deal precisely because the credit equity is larger, and lenders size the permanent loan to what supportable income can cover at conservative debt service coverage.

Soft debt is where West Palm Beach sponsors have genuine competitive options. Florida Housing's State Apartment Incentive Loan program and Sadowski Housing Trust Fund dollars are available to qualifying 9% applications and can contribute meaningful subordinate debt. At the county level, Palm Beach County Housing and Economic Sustainability administers HOME entitlement independently of the city, and both the county and the City of West Palm Beach Community Services Department have awarded gap financing to 9% deals in the region. CDBG dollars occasionally layer in for projects serving the lowest income tiers or those with community facility components. Sponsors with projects in submarkets such as Northwood Hills, Pleasant City, Coleman Park, or Mangonia Park should engage both city and county programs early, since the soft debt commitments required for a competitive Florida Housing application have their own lead times that are easy to underestimate.

One dynamic worth flagging: the competitiveness of Florida Housing's 9% rounds has continued to intensify. Sponsors who cannot achieve a winning score in one round should understand that a failed application cycle consumes time, costs predevelopment capital, and may trigger site control extension negotiations. Some sponsors in this market pivot to the 4% credit and bond cap pathway when 9% scoring prospects are uncertain, accepting a larger permanent loan and smaller equity contribution in exchange for a non-competitive process.

Active Lender Types for West Palm Beach Affordable Deals

The construction lending market for 9% deals in West Palm Beach is anchored by CDFIs with national affordable housing mandates and community banks carrying active CRA commitments in the South Florida market. CDFIs in this space are often willing to take on complexity, including deals with layered soft debt, local government subordinate lenders, and HAP contracts, that conventional bank credit departments find difficult to underwrite. Community banks with dedicated affordable platforms are active here as well, particularly on deals with simpler capital stacks.

On the permanent debt side, agency lenders are the primary execution path at stabilization. Freddie Mac's Targeted Affordable Housing product and Fannie Mae's Multifamily Affordable Housing program both offer terms specifically designed for LIHTC properties, including favorable treatment of rental assistance contracts and extended amortization. Life insurance companies with affordable housing allocations are present in this market for deals that meet their hold criteria, though their appetite in Southeast Florida can be selective. HUD's 223(f) and 221(d)(4) programs remain relevant for deals that can absorb the processing timelines, and a 223(f) refinance post-stabilization is a legitimate exit strategy for some permanent lenders who bridge to agency. For projects with project-based vouchers issued by WPBHA, permanent lenders will underwrite the HAP contract carefully, and sponsors should anticipate detailed questions about contract term and renewal assumptions.

Typical Deal Profile and Timeline

A realistic 9% LIHTC transaction in West Palm Beach falls in the range of roughly $10 million to $25 million in total development cost, with unit counts frequently in the 60-to-100 unit range given land costs and density constraints in the urban core and adjacent submarkets. Site control should be secured well before the Florida Housing application window, ideally with option terms long enough to survive one failed allocation round without losing the site.

A reasonable timeline from site control to stabilization runs approximately 36 to 48 months under favorable conditions. Florida Housing's scoring round, award notification, tax credit reservation, investor closing, construction start, construction completion, lease-up, and cost certification with IRS Form 8609 issuance all have their own sequencing requirements. Sponsors new to this timeline consistently underestimate the gap between allocation and financial closing, which typically runs six to twelve months and requires active lender and investor engagement throughout. Lenders and investors expect sponsors to demonstrate prior completed LIHTC projects, a construction contingency in the range of 8 to 12 percent, and a development team with active Florida contractor relationships capable of delivering on the guaranteed maximum price contract.

Common Execution Pitfalls in West Palm Beach

First, local soft debt timing is a consistent problem. Both the City of West Palm Beach and Palm Beach County run their own application and award cycles for HOME and other gap financing. These cycles do not automatically align with Florida Housing's scoring round calendar. Sponsors who begin soft debt conversations after the Florida Housing application window opens are frequently unable to obtain commitment letters in time to score the local government contribution points they were counting on.

Second, prevailing wage and Davis-Bacon exposure deserves early attention. Deals incorporating federal HOME or CDBG funds trigger Davis-Bacon wage requirements on construction. In a market where construction labor costs are already elevated, underestimating the hard cost impact of Davis-Bacon compliance can create budget gaps that soft debt cannot absorb without renegotiating the entire stack.

Third, site control in West Palm Beach's active affordable submarkets, particularly Northwood Hills and Coleman Park, has become more complicated as land values respond to broader market pressures. Sellers in these areas are increasingly aware of the LIHTC premium, and option terms that made sense two years ago are being renegotiated at closing. Sponsors should build option extension fees and potential purchase price escalators into their predevelopment budgets.

Fourth, zoning and entitlement timelines through the City of West Palm Beach are frequently underestimated on infill sites. Conditional use approvals, variances, and neighborhood compatibility reviews can add six to twelve months to a predevelopment schedule, and a delayed entitlement directly threatens the construction start timeline that Florida Housing and the tax credit investor require to maintain reservation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 9% LIHTC financing typically look like in West Palm Beach?

In West Palm Beach, 9% lihtc deals typically range from $8M to $25M total development cost and assemble a stack that includes construction loan (bank, cdfi, or mission-focused lender), 9% lihtc investor equity (~70% of tdc), permanent loan (smaller than 4% deals because credit equity is larger), layered with local soft debt from administering agencies including west palm beach community services gap financing and related programs.

Which lenders close 9% lihtc deals in West Palm Beach?

Active capital sources in West Palm Beach include mission-focused CDFIs, community banks with affordable platforms, life insurance companies with affordable allocations, agency lenders (Fannie Mae MAH / Freddie Mac TAH) on the permanent take-out, and HUD 221(d)(4) for larger construction-to-permanent transactions. The specific lender that fits best depends on deal size, sponsor profile, and capital stack complexity.

How does the Florida Housing Finance Corporation (Florida Housing) allocate LIHTC in West Palm Beach?

Florida Housing Finance Corporation (Florida Housing) administers both the competitive 9% LIHTC allocation rounds and the non-competitive 4% credit pathway for West Palm Beach and the rest of FL. Scoring criteria, set-aside categories, and geographic preferences vary by funding cycle. For 9% deals, understanding how this HFA weights location, income targeting, and sponsor capacity is essential before committing to a specific application round. For 4% LIHTC, the key gating factor is private activity bond cap allocation through the state bond authority.

How long does a 9% lihtc deal typically take to close in West Palm Beach?

From site control through construction close, 9% lihtc deals in West Palm Beach typically take 18 to 30 months depending on program selection, entitlement pathway, allocation round timing for competitive sources, and sponsor capacity to run multiple application cycles in parallel. Construction itself adds another 18 to 30 months, with stabilization and permanent conversion following.
