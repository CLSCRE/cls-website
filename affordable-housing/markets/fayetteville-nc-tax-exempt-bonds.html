Tax-Exempt Bonds in Fayetteville

By Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker at CLS CRE. Fayetteville, NC.

How Tax-Exempt Bonds Work in Fayetteville

Tax-exempt bond financing for affordable multifamily in Fayetteville runs through the North Carolina Housing Finance Agency, which controls both the private activity bond cap allocation and the 4% Low Income Housing Tax Credit awards that automatically accompany qualifying bond deals. Unlike the competitive 9% LIHTC round, the 4% credit is non-competitive by design, but the bond cap itself is a finite annual resource allocated by NCHFA on a rolling application basis. Sponsors targeting Fayetteville need to understand that the program is state-administered, not locally issued at the city level, which means the primary relationship for bond financing runs to NCHFA in Raleigh rather than to City of Fayetteville Community Development or Cumberland County. The city and county remain relevant as soft debt sources and permitting authorities, but they are not issuers here.

Fayetteville's affordable housing market carries a demand profile that distinguishes it from most mid-sized North Carolina cities. Fort Liberty, formerly Fort Bragg, is the largest military installation in the United States by population, and it generates consistent, treaty-bound demand for workforce and affordable housing serving active duty families, veterans, and support workers who cannot afford market-rate product in the surrounding area. This demographic creates a strong case for project-based voucher attachment through the Fayetteville Metropolitan Housing Authority, HUD-VASH vouchers serving veteran households, and rental assistance structures that institutional investors and bond purchasers can underwrite with confidence. Sponsors who understand how to document that demand story in a bond application and LIHTC pro forma tend to advance more quickly through NCHFA review than those presenting generic affordability narratives.

The typical sponsor closing a tax-exempt bond deal in Fayetteville is an experienced affordable developer with prior LIHTC and bond closings on record, a strong development team including bond counsel, a tax credit syndicator relationship already in place, and the predevelopment capitalization to carry the project through a six-to-twelve month application and structuring process before construction financing closes. First-time developers occasionally participate in this market but typically do so in joint venture with experienced co-sponsors whose track records satisfy NCHFA underwriting requirements.

The Capital Stack in Fayetteville

A tax-exempt bond deal in Fayetteville assembles its capital stack in layers, starting with the bond issuance itself. During construction, the tax-exempt bonds serve as the primary construction financing vehicle, often structured as variable-rate demand obligations with credit enhancement from a letter of credit provided by a bank lender. At stabilization, the bonds either convert to a permanent fixed-rate structure or are refunded into permanent debt, frequently through an agency execution. The 4% LIHTC equity raised from a syndicator occupies the next significant layer, typically covering a meaningful share of total development cost depending on applicable fraction and credit pricing. Below equity, sponsors layer in soft debt from available state and local sources.

At the state level, NCHFA administers several soft debt and subsidy programs that can layer into bond deals, including its Housing Finance Fund and, where applicable, federal HOME funds distributed through the state. At the local level, the City of Fayetteville Community Development administers HOME and CDBG entitlement, and Cumberland County administers its own HOME entitlement separately, meaning a project with strong community benefit arguments can potentially access soft debt from two distinct local sources. The Fayetteville Metropolitan Housing Authority's project-based voucher program is not direct capital, but PBV commitments meaningfully improve permanent debt sizing and investor pricing, functioning as a credit enhancement that affects every other layer of the stack. HUD-VASH vouchers add a similar benefit for projects serving veterans.

Because bond cap allocation in North Carolina is non-competitive in the sense that it does not score against other projects in a single round, the primary constraint is timing and capacity within NCHFA's annual bond cap volume. Sponsors should not assume that non-competitive means easy or fast. The 4% LIHTC application still requires a full underwriting submission to NCHFA, and the agency applies its own threshold and underwriting criteria. Projects that score well on NCHFA's housing needs criteria, demonstrate site readiness, and arrive with complete development teams close faster and on better terms.

Active Lender Types for Fayetteville Affordable Deals

The lender ecosystem for tax-exempt bond deals in Fayetteville reflects the national affordable housing capital markets, with certain lender types more actively deploying in this market than others. Mission-focused CDFIs with national affordable housing platforms are often the most flexible construction lenders for bond deals in secondary markets like Fayetteville, particularly for projects with layered soft debt or PBV commitments that require a lender willing to hold complexity in the capital stack. Community banks with dedicated affordable housing or CRA-motivated lending teams are active here as well, and some provide both the construction letter of credit and construction debt in a single relationship, which simplifies execution.

For permanent financing, agency executions through Fannie Mae Multifamily Affordable Housing and Freddie Mac's Tax-Exempt Loan and Targeted Affordable Housing programs are the most common permanent loan takeouts for stabilized bond deals in this market. Both agencies have appetite for income-restricted properties in markets with demonstrable demand, and a Fort Liberty-adjacent project with PBV or HUD-VASH coverage is a credible story under agency underwriting criteria. Life insurance companies with affordable housing allocations participate selectively in North Carolina, typically on larger or higher-quality assets, and are less consistently active in the Fayetteville submarket specifically. HUD's 221(d)(4) and 223(f) programs are available and competitive on cost, but their timelines require sponsors to plan accordingly and are generally a better fit when agency or CDFI execution timelines are a constraint.

Typical Deal Profile and Timeline

A realistic tax-exempt bond deal in Fayetteville today involves total development costs in the range of $20 million to $50 million, reflecting land costs, hard construction costs that have elevated across the region, and soft costs associated with a full bond and LIHTC closing. Unit counts typically range from 80 to 200 units, with income targeting at 60% AMI or below, often with a tiered set-aside that accommodates PBV or HUD-VASH overlay. Projects on the lower end of that range need to be scrutinized carefully against issuance costs, since the practical floor for bond financing economics sits near $15 million in total development cost.

Timeline from site control to construction close typically runs 18 to 24 months in North Carolina, accounting for NCHFA application and review, bond counsel engagement, tax credit syndication closing, soft debt applications to the city and county, and permitting. Construction periods for multifamily of this scale run 18 to 24 months. Stabilization and permanent loan conversion or agency takeout close roughly 90 to 120 days after stabilization. A sponsor entering today with site control and a complete predevelopment team should model a 48-to-54-month horizon from site control to permanent loan close. Lenders and investors expect sponsors to present audited financials, prior project performance documentation, and evidence of local government relationships before formal applications are submitted.

Common Execution Pitfalls in Fayetteville

The first pitfall is underestimating NCHFA's application completeness requirements. NCHFA applies rigorous threshold review to bond and 4% LIHTC applications, and incomplete submissions are returned rather than held. Sponsors who arrive without finalized bond counsel engagement, a committed syndicator term sheet, or completed environmental and site assessments routinely lose months of runway. In a program where bond cap is allocated on a rolling basis, that delay has real cost.

The second is failing to sequence the two local soft debt applications correctly. The City of Fayetteville Community Development and Cumberland County HOME programs operate on separate application calendars and have distinct threshold requirements. Sponsors targeting both sources need to engage both agencies early, since awards from each are conditional on project viability showings that take time to document, and neither agency will hold funds indefinitely while the NCHFA process advances.

The third pitfall is site control in the Murchison Road corridor and East Fayetteville submarkets, where historically challenged land ownership, title issues, and environmental assessments have extended predevelopment timelines. Sponsors should commission Phase I assessments and title review before engaging NCHFA, not after, particularly in those submarkets where redevelopment activity has been inconsistent and parcel histories are complex.

The fourth is prevailing wage exposure. Projects financed with federal funds, including HOME, are subject to Davis-Bacon wage requirements, and layering multiple federal sources can trigger compliance obligations that materially affect hard cost budgets. Sponsors who build pro formas without accounting for prevailing wage on federally assisted components routinely encounter budget shortfalls late in predevelopment, at exactly the moment when soft debt commitments should be locking in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Tax-Exempt Bonds financing typically look like in Fayetteville?

In Fayetteville, tax-exempt bonds deals typically range from $15M to $100M+ total development cost and assemble a stack that includes tax-exempt bond issuance (construction phase), 4% lihtc investor equity, permanent bond issuance or conversion to permanent debt at stabilization, layered with local soft debt from administering agencies including fayetteville community development gap financing and related programs.

Which lenders close tax-exempt bonds deals in Fayetteville?

Active capital sources in Fayetteville include mission-focused CDFIs, community banks with affordable platforms, life insurance companies with affordable allocations, agency lenders (Fannie Mae MAH / Freddie Mac TAH) on the permanent take-out, and HUD 221(d)(4) for larger construction-to-permanent transactions. The specific lender that fits best depends on deal size, sponsor profile, and capital stack complexity.

How does the North Carolina Housing Finance Agency (NCHFA) allocate LIHTC in Fayetteville?

North Carolina Housing Finance Agency (NCHFA) administers both the competitive 9% LIHTC allocation rounds and the non-competitive 4% credit pathway for Fayetteville and the rest of NC. Scoring criteria, set-aside categories, and geographic preferences vary by funding cycle. For 9% deals, understanding how this HFA weights location, income targeting, and sponsor capacity is essential before committing to a specific application round. For 4% LIHTC, the key gating factor is private activity bond cap allocation through the state bond authority.

How long does a tax-exempt bonds deal typically take to close in Fayetteville?

From site control through construction close, tax-exempt bonds deals in Fayetteville typically take 18 to 30 months depending on program selection, entitlement pathway, allocation round timing for competitive sources, and sponsor capacity to run multiple application cycles in parallel. Construction itself adds another 18 to 30 months, with stabilization and permanent conversion following.
