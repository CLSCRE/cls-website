9% Competitive LIHTC Financing

The 9% credit delivers roughly 70% of total development cost as equity, more than twice the 4% credit's share, which is why sponsors chase it for smaller deals where a deep equity contribution makes the difference between penciling and not. The tradeoff is a competitive scoring round through the state housing finance agency, multiple rounds per year, a winning threshold that varies by region and set-aside, and real risk of needing more than one application cycle before allocation.

The Capital Stack

Most 9% LIHTC deals layer several sources rather than a single loan:

  • Construction loan (bank, CDFI, or mission-focused lender)
  • 9% LIHTC investor equity (~70% of TDC)
  • Permanent loan (smaller than 4% deals because credit equity is larger)
  • State soft debt: MHP, AHSC, HHAP, NPLH for qualifying profiles
  • Local soft debt where needed to close the gap
  • Sponsor equity and deferred developer fee

What actually wins a 9% scoring round

State HFAs score applications on a published point system, typically rewarding readiness (site control, entitlements in hand), leverage of other funding sources, and special-needs or homeless set-asides. Permanent supportive housing projects tend to score especially well because of the homeless and special-needs points layered on top of the base affordability scoring. We model the scoring profile before a sponsor commits capital to an application, not after a losing round.

Sizing the deal around the equity, not the debt

Because 9% equity covers so much of total development cost, the permanent loan on these deals is meaningfully smaller than on a comparable market-rate or 4%-financed project. That changes which lenders are actually competitive: smaller permanent loan amounts often favor a community bank or CDFI over a conduit or agency lender who needs loan size to justify the underwriting lift.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many scoring rounds does it typically take to win an allocation?

It varies widely by state and region, but sponsors should budget for the possibility of two application rounds, not one, especially in high-demand metro regions where set-aside categories are oversubscribed.

Does the 9% credit require prevailing wage?

Yes, along with every other LIHTC-financed pathway. Budget it into the pro forma from the first application, since the scoring round itself often happens well before final construction pricing.

What set-asides tend to score best?

Permanent supportive housing and special-needs set-asides typically carry the most scoring points in most state HFA systems, followed by projects that layer multiple non-competitive funding sources onto the base LIHTC application.

Is 9% credit equity harder to place than 4%?

Not usually, once you've won the allocation. Most active LIHTC investors buy both 4% and 9% credits; the real friction on 9% deals is winning the scoring round, not finding an equity buyer afterward.
