Affordable Housing Financing Guide

Workforce Housing and NOAH Preservation Financing

Workforce and NOAH (naturally occurring affordable housing) preservation deals serve households at 60-120% of area median income without necessarily needing government subsidy, which means they can close faster than a LIHTC transaction using conventional debt, bridge loans, agency financing, and mezzanine capital. It's also the largest segment of the affordable housing universe by unit count, far more units nationally sit in this band than in deeply subsidized LIHTC housing.

The Capital Stack

Most Workforce & NOAH Preservation deals layer several sources rather than a single loan:

  • Acquisition or rehab bridge loan (bank, CDFI, or private lender)
  • Permanent agency debt (Freddie Mac TEL, Fannie Mae MTEB, or conventional permanent mortgage)
  • 4% LIHTC investor equity (where income restrictions are accepted in exchange for below-market equity)
  • Mezzanine debt or preferred equity for gap coverage
  • State soft debt where workforce income limits qualify
  • Local inclusionary fund or housing trust proceeds where available

Preservation is a race against conversion

NOAH properties, typically 1960 to 1990 vintage multifamily, are naturally affordable today because they're older and haven't been renovated, not because of any regulatory restriction. That means they're also the properties most at risk of being bought and converted to market-rate or luxury rehab. The financing decision on a NOAH acquisition is often a race against a competing buyer with exactly that plan.

The subsidy-optional structure changes the lender pool

Because these deals don't require government subsidy to close, the lender pool is much wider than LIHTC financing, banks, agency lenders through Freddie Mac TAH and TEL or Fannie Mae MTEB, mezzanine funds, and private bridge lenders all compete for NOAH acquisitions. Sponsors willing to accept a 4% LIHTC regulatory agreement in exchange for below-market equity narrow that pool but gain access to cheaper capital, a tradeoff worth modeling on every deal rather than assuming.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a NOAH deal require government subsidy to close?

No, that's the core distinction from LIHTC financing. Most NOAH acquisitions close on conventional or agency debt plus sponsor equity, without any subsidy layer, which is also why they typically close faster.

What agency programs specifically support NOAH?

Freddie Mac's Targeted Affordable Housing and Tax-Exempt Loan programs and Fannie Mae's MTEB execution are built specifically for this segment, offering better pricing than conventional bank debt in exchange for an affordability regulatory agreement.

Can a NOAH deal still use LIHTC?

Yes, optionally. A sponsor can accept 4% LIHTC equity in exchange for a 55-year rent restriction at 60% AMI on the LIHTC-eligible units, trading some rent upside for below-market capital.

What AMI range does workforce housing typically serve?

60% to 120% of area median income, the band between deeply subsidized affordable housing and unrestricted market-rate rents.
