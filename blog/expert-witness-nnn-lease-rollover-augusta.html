Expert Witness: NNN Lease Rollover Upside and Financing Impact in Augusta, GA

By Trevor Damyan· June 25, 2026 ·Below-Market NNN Lease and Rollover Upside Analysis

The Augusta Market Context

Augusta, Georgia occupies a distinct position in the Southeast commercial real estate landscape. It is not Atlanta, and understanding that distinction matters enormously when analyzing financing feasibility for a single-tenant NNN asset. Augusta's economy anchors on the medical corridor along Washington Road and the surrounding Summerville and West Augusta submarkets, the cyber and intelligence defense cluster concentrated at Fort Eisenhower (formerly Fort Gordon), and a regional retail and distribution base serving a trade area that extends well into South Carolina. That economic mix produces a commercial real estate market with characteristics lenders evaluate differently than they would in a Tier 1 gateway city.

For single-tenant NNN properties specifically, Augusta presents a bifurcated picture. Credit-tenant NNN deals backed by investment-grade obligors, national QSR operators, or auto parts retailers have historically traded at cap rates in the 5.75% to 7.25% range depending on lease term remaining, tenant credit, and rent-to-sales ratios. However, single-tenant assets occupied by regional or local tenants, or national tenants whose credit has softened, trade considerably wider, often 7.5% to 9.0% or beyond when lease term is short or in-place rents sit materially below or above market. That cap rate spread directly feeds into lender underwriting and is central to any dispute involving a below-market lease and its financing implications.

Retail vacancy in the Augusta MSA has remained relatively stable in the 5% to 8% range for well-located freestanding retail, but that aggregate figure masks submarket divergence. The Gordon Highway corridor and portions of Fort Gordon's adjacent commercial zone have experienced tenant churn as the base's mission expansion reshapes demand patterns, while the Wheeler Road and Washington Road corridors have maintained stronger occupancy. Industrial fundamentals in Augusta have tightened considerably, with functional warehouse and flex product below 100,000 square feet seeing vacancy compress toward 4% to 6%, supported by regional distribution activity and construction logistics tied to the Savannah port expansion's inland reach. These fundamentals matter because they define what a lender believes a vacant single-tenant box can realistically re-lease for, and at what timeframe, when they stress-test rollover scenarios.

The Engagement

Counsel for a party to a commercial real estate financing dispute in Augusta retained Commercial Lending Solutions to provide a Rule 26 expert report analyzing the financing implications of a below-market NNN lease structure on a freestanding single-tenant commercial property. The asset was a freestanding retail building of approximately 8,000 to 12,000 square feet situated in a primary Augusta commercial corridor, occupied by a single tenant under a triple-net lease with a remaining term of under four years at the time of the dispute.

The core questions presented to CLS CRE were as follows. First, how do institutional and regional lenders underwrite a NNN asset whose in-place rent is demonstrably below current market, and does that below-market position help or hurt loan sizing? Second, does the existence of rollover upside, meaning the prospect of re-leasing at higher market rents upon lease expiration, provide any present financing benefit that a lender would recognize in sizing a loan today? Third, given the in-place income stream and the remaining lease term, what is the maximum supportable loan amount under market-standard DSCR and LTV constraints, and does that amount satisfy or fall short of the financing needs at issue in the dispute? Fourth, does the lease structure, specifically the combination of below-market rent, short remaining term, and tenant credit profile, place the asset outside the credit box of certain lender categories entirely?

These questions required a detailed underwriting analysis grounded in actual lender market conditions in Augusta and the broader Southeast, not a theoretical academic exercise. Our methodology is the same methodology a credit officer at a regional bank or a CMBS conduit would apply when reviewing a loan submission on this asset.

The Financial Analysis

The income analysis began with the in-place NNN rent as reported in the lease documentation. For an asset of this type and size in Augusta, a plausible in-place rent of approximately $18.00 per square foot per year on a 10,000 square foot building produces gross potential income of $180,000 annually. Under a true triple-net structure, the landlord's expense exposure is limited to management overhead and reserves, so effective NOI before debt service and capital reserves is approximately $171,000 to $174,000 per year after a 3% to 4% management fee. For purposes of lender underwriting, most institutional lenders will apply a vacancy and credit loss haircut even on a NNN lease with a single occupying tenant, typically 5% for a tenant without investment-grade credit, reducing effective gross income to approximately $171,000 before expenses, and producing a lender-underwritten NOI of approximately $163,000 to $167,000.

At market rents for comparable freestanding retail in Augusta's primary corridors, which currently range from $22.00 to $26.00 per square foot NNN depending on location and configuration, the same 10,000 square foot building would support a market NOI of approximately $209,000 to $247,000 after the same underwriting haircuts. That gap between in-place and market NOI is the quantified below-market deficit, and it is the central variable in the loan sizing analysis.

Lenders underwriting a NNN single-tenant retail asset in Augusta in the current rate environment are working with 30-year amortization schedules and interest rates ranging from approximately 6.75% to 7.75% depending on loan program, term, recourse structure, and lender category. For this analysis, I applied a 7.25% note rate on a 25-year amortization schedule, which is a conservative but market-representative assumption for a community bank or regional bank term loan on an Augusta NNN asset with a sub-investment-grade tenant. That produces an annual debt service constant of approximately 8.65 cents per dollar of loan balance.

The market-standard DSCR for NNN single-tenant retail in the community and regional bank category is 1.20x to 1.25x. Some lenders require 1.30x for single-tenant assets due to concentration risk. Applying the stricter 1.25x DSCR to lender-underwritten NOI of $165,000 yields maximum supportable annual debt service of $132,000. Dividing $132,000 by the 8.65% annual constant produces a maximum supportable loan of approximately $1,526,000.

LTV constraints impose a parallel ceiling. For a NNN single-tenant retail asset in Augusta with a tenant of non-investment-grade credit and a sub-four-year remaining lease term, institutional lenders apply LTV limits of 65% to 70% of the appraised value of the fee simple interest based on in-place income. If the property appraises at $2,200,000 on a going-in cap rate of approximately 7.5% applied to in-place NOI, the 65% LTV ceiling produces a maximum loan of $1,430,000. The DSCR constraint and the LTV constraint converge in the $1.43 million to $1.53 million range, confirming the loan sizing ceiling.

The critical finding relevant to the dispute: the rollover upside, the higher market rent achievable after the existing tenant vacates or the lease renews, does not move either ceiling today. Lenders do not capitalize future speculative income when sizing a loan on an occupied NNN asset. The in-place lease is the only income a lender will underwrite, and future upside is neither a credit enhancement nor a loan sizing tool under any standard commercial mortgage underwriting protocol I am aware of in the current market.

The Complicating Factors

Remaining Lease Term Below the Loan Term. The most significant structural complicating factor in this scenario is the mismatch between remaining lease term and requested loan term. Most commercial lenders offering five-year or seven-year loan terms on NNN assets require that the lease extend, inclusive of renewal options that the lender can demonstrate are likely to be exercised, at least co-terminous with or beyond the loan maturity date. When the remaining lease term is under four years and the borrower seeks a five-year or longer loan, the lender is effectively underwriting a value-add or re-tenanting scenario for the back end of the loan, which they are not equipped to do within a stabilized NNN underwriting framework. Lenders handle this in one of three ways: they decline the loan, they reduce the loan term to match the lease term, or they require a significant interest reserve and cash flow sweep mechanism to accumulate re-tenanting reserves. None of these outcomes is favorable to the borrower, and they represent a meaningful constraint on refinancing optionality.

Tenant Credit Classification. Single-tenant NNN lending is intensely credit-stratified. Investment-grade tenants, defined by lenders as entities with a Moody's or S&P rating of Baa3/BBB- or better, access a materially wider lender universe including CMBS, life insurance companies, and agency-adjacent programs. Non-investment-grade or unrated tenants restrict the lender universe to community banks, regional banks, credit unions, and certain private debt funds. In Augusta, the institutional depth for non-investment-grade NNN loans is meaningful but not unlimited. The asset cannot access the full capital markets execution available to a Walgreens or Dollar General, and the pricing and structure reflect that. This matters in the dispute context because financing feasibility cannot be evaluated without specifying which tenant credit tier applies.

Below-Market Rent as a Double-Edged Complicating Factor. Below-market rent is sometimes presented by borrowers or their counsel as a positive attribute, on the theory that the tenant is locked into a favorable lease and therefore unlikely to vacate. Lenders do not underwrite that narrative. A below-market lease actually increases lender concern about lease expiration because a rational tenant whose rent is below market has no economic incentive to vacate early, but the lender must still stress-test the vacancy scenario at lease expiration. More importantly, a below-market rent produces a lower NOI today, which directly reduces the DSCR-supportable loan amount, regardless of the theoretical upside at rollover. The existence of below-market rent therefore simultaneously tightens the current loan sizing ceiling and heightens lender concern about re-tenanting risk if the lease does not renew.

Appraisal Methodology and Its Interaction with Lender Underwriting. An appraiser may conclude that the property has a market value above the income-approach value based on in-place rents, relying on a hypothetical as-market or as-stabilized value. Some appraisals will present both an as-is value and an as-stabilized value. Lenders do not use the as-stabilized value for loan sizing on an occupied property. They use the as-is, in-place income value for DSCR and LTV constraints. This distinction is frequently misunderstood in dispute contexts, where parties may conflate an appraiser's as-stabilized conclusion with a lender's willingness to lend against that value.

The Augusta Lender Market

Augusta supports a reasonably active commercial lending community for single-tenant NNN assets, but the relevant lender universe for a below-market lease scenario with short remaining term and a non-investment-grade tenant is narrower than it might appear. Here is how I categorize the active lenders and what each requires for this asset type.

Community and Regional Banks. Augusta and the surrounding CSRA (Central Savannah River Area) are served by a combination of local community banks headquartered in Augusta or Aiken, and regional banks based in Atlanta, Charlotte, and Greenville with Augusta market teams. These lenders are the primary execution path for this type of asset. They typically require personal recourse on loans under $5 million, want a minimum 1.20x to 1.25x DSCR on in-place income, cap LTV at 65% to 70%, and will require lease assignments and subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment agreements. Pricing ranges from approximately SOFR plus 200 to 275 basis points on floating structures, or fixed rates in the 6.75% to 7.50% range for 3-to-5 year terms. Relationship history and deposit accounts materially affect pricing at this category of lender.

CMBS Conduit Lenders. CMBS is not a practical execution path for this scenario. CMBS requires investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenant credit for single-tenant assets, and the minimum loan size for CMBS on NNN retail is typically $3 million to $5 million with preferred minimums higher. The short remaining lease term would disqualify this asset from most CMBS conduit credit boxes regardless of tenant credit. CMBS lenders require lease terms extending at least to loan maturity as a hard underwriting requirement in most programs.

Life Insurance Companies. Life company lenders offer the best pricing in the NNN space but are the most restrictive on credit quality and lease structure. Investment-grade tenant, 10-plus years of remaining lease term, and core or core-plus locations are baseline requirements. This asset category does not qualify.

Private Debt Funds and Bridge Lenders. If the asset is being repositioned or the borrower is bridging to a re-tenanting event, private debt funds operating in the Southeast would consider this asset. Pricing is materially higher, typically 9% to 11% current pay or higher, with 1-to-3 year terms, interest reserves, and origination fees of 1% to 2%. These lenders will lend on value-add upside scenarios but they price the risk accordingly. Their presence in Augusta is more limited than in Atlanta, but regional bridge programs do reach this market.

What Attorneys Handling Augusta CRE Disputes Need to Know

A commercial real estate financing expert is not a substitute for an appraiser, and an appraiser is not a substitute for a CRE financing expert. The distinction matters in litigation preparation. An appraiser quantifies value. A CRE financing expert quantifies what the capital markets will actually do with that value, which is a different and often more consequential question when the dispute turns on financing feasibility, loan sizing, or whether a party could or could not have refinanced or closed a transaction.

Attorneys should consider retaining a CRE financing expert when the dispute involves any of the following: a failed financing contingency where the question is whether the failure was the borrower's fault or a market impossibility; a breach of contract claim where the allegedly breached obligation required financing that may or may not have been obtainable; a lender liability claim where the lender's underwriting decision is alleged to have been commercially unreasonable; a bankruptcy plan confirmation where a lender is challenging the feasibility of a reorganization plan that depends on refinancing; or a partnership or JV dispute where one party claims the other prevented or enabled a refinancing event.

A qualified CRE financing expert must have active, current market experience as a mortgage broker, lender, or capital markets professional. A retired banker whose market knowledge is five or more years old cannot reliably testify about current lender credit boxes, current DSCR standards, or current pricing spreads. At CLS CRE, our qualifications derive from active deal activity across approximately $1 billion in transaction volume, more than 1,000 lender relationships across 50 states, and ongoing engagement with lenders at the credit level, not just at the origination level. That is what allows us to say with specificity what a lender would or would not have done with a particular asset in a particular market at a particular time.

Before retaining a CRE financing expert for an Augusta matter, counsel should gather the following: the complete lease document including all amendments; any rent commencement letters or estoppels; the most recent rent roll; any appraisals of the property; any prior loan documents, term sheets, or commitment letters; any lender correspondence including denial letters or credit memos; operating statements for at least the prior two years; and any letters of intent, purchase agreements, or financing commitments relevant to the disputed transaction. The more complete this record, the more defensible and specific the expert's opinions will be.

At trial or in a plan confirmation hearing, CRE financing expert testimony is most effective when it is grounded in specific underwriting arithmetic, not generalities. The expert should be able to walk a judge or jury through the actual loan sizing calculation, explain why a specific lender category would or would not have approved the loan, and distinguish between what the market could support and what the parties may have hoped or expected it would support.

Key Takeaways

  • Below-market in-place rent reduces loan sizing today, regardless of future rollover upside. Lenders underwrite in-place income only. Speculative future rents at lease rollover provide no present financing benefit under standard commercial mortgage underwriting protocols.
  • Rollover upside is a valuation concept, not a financing concept. An appraiser may recognize as-stabilized value based on market rents; a lender will not
