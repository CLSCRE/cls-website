$9,000,000 Affordable Multifamily (Non-Profit GP / Welfare Exemption), Glendale / Pasadena / Long Beach, CA

By Trevor Damyan · April 21, 2026 · Permanent Loan

When a non-profit sponsor needed $9 million in permanent financing for their 50-unit affordable housing portfolio across Glendale, Pasadena, and Long Beach, the deal looked straightforward on the surface. The properties were stabilized, cash flowing, and serving low-income tenants under long-term affordability restrictions. The complexity emerged in the details: maximizing proceeds by crediting California's welfare exemption across three different jurisdictions, each with their own interpretation of how the exemption applies.

The Deal

The borrower needed permanent financing to refinance existing debt on three affordable multifamily properties totaling approximately 50 units across Los Angeles County. The sponsor had structured ownership through a 501(c)(3) non-profit general partner specifically to qualify for California's welfare exemption, which can reduce property taxes by 50% or more on qualifying affordable housing.

The properties were performing well operationally, with stable occupancy and rents restricted under regulatory agreements. However, the sponsor needed to maximize loan proceeds to fund additional affordable housing development, which meant finding a lender that would underwrite the deal based on the reduced property tax burden the welfare exemption provided.

The Challenge

While the welfare exemption playbook is well-established in Long Beach, extending it into Glendale and Pasadena introduced jurisdictional complexity. Each city's assessor office handles welfare exemption applications differently, with varying timelines, documentation requirements, and interpretations of qualifying ownership structures.

More critically, most lenders either don't understand the welfare exemption or won't credit the tax savings in their underwriting. Banks typically underwrite affordable housing deals at current property tax levels, ignoring potential exemption benefits. This conservative approach protects the lender if the exemption isn't approved or gets revoked, but it leaves money on the table for borrowers with legitimate exemption structures.

The sponsor had received several quotes from regional banks that ignored the welfare exemption entirely, resulting in lower loan proceeds based on debt service coverage ratios calculated with full property tax burdens. They needed a lender sophisticated enough to model the exemption benefits while still maintaining appropriate underwriting standards.

The Solution

We identified a national life insurance company that had previously underwritten welfare exemption benefits on California affordable housing deals. Their underwriting team understood the exemption mechanics and was comfortable crediting the tax savings in their debt service coverage analysis.

The key was presenting a comprehensive exemption analysis for each jurisdiction. We worked with the sponsor's tax consultant to model the exemption-adjusted property taxes for all three properties, accounting for each city's specific exemption policies. For the Long Beach property, we had clear precedent. For Glendale and Pasadena, we documented the applicable statutes and provided comparable exemption approvals from similar non-profit affordable housing deals.

We structured the financing as a single loan secured by all three properties, allowing the lender to underwrite the portfolio's blended debt service coverage ratio. The life company was comfortable with a 75% loan-to-value ratio based on current appraised values, with debt service coverage calculated using the welfare exemption-adjusted property tax projections.

The Outcome

The life insurance company funded a $9 million permanent loan at a fixed rate in the mid-4% range, with a 30-year amortization and 10-year term. The exemption-adjusted underwriting delivered approximately $800,000 in additional proceeds compared to the best bank quote that didn't credit the welfare exemption.

The financing closed within 60 days, with the lender accepting current exemption applications as satisfactory evidence of qualification. The borrower used the additional proceeds to acquire land for their next affordable housing development, directly expanding their mission-driven housing program.

The deal demonstrated how specialized lender knowledge can unlock significant value in affordable housing transactions. While many lenders view welfare exemptions as too complex or risky to underwrite, life insurance companies with affordable housing experience often have the analytical framework to properly credit these benefits. The key is matching the right borrower story with the right capital source, then presenting the exemption analysis in terms the underwriter can confidently rely on.

For non-profit affordable housing sponsors, this transaction highlighted the importance of structuring ownership correctly from the outset and working with brokers who understand both the exemption mechanics and which lenders will credit them in their underwriting.
