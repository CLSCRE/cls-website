Case Study

$33,000,000 ED1 Workforce Housing (88 Units) in West Adams, Los Angeles, CA

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Construction Loan

The Deal

Commercial Lending Solutions recently closed a $33 million construction financing package for an 88-unit workforce housing development in West Adams, Los Angeles. The project falls under California's ED1 program (Executive Directive 1), which mandates that state agencies make excess land available for affordable housing development at below-market prices.

This development targets moderate-income workers earning 50-80% of Area Median Income (AMI), specifically teachers, nurses, and city employees who face increasing displacement pressure in LA's rapidly gentrifying neighborhoods. The 24-month construction timeline positions the project to deliver much-needed housing in West Adams, one of Los Angeles' fastest-appreciating areas with strong Metro connectivity.

The Challenge

Workforce housing occupies an awkward position in affordable housing finance. Unlike deeply affordable projects targeting 30-50% AMI residents, workforce housing at 50-80% AMI generates higher rental income but qualifies for significantly less subsidized financing. This creates a persistent financing gap that kills many otherwise viable projects.

The higher income restrictions mean fewer grant dollars, reduced soft loan availability, and less favorable terms from Community Development Financial Institutions. However, the rental income still falls well short of market-rate projects, limiting conventional debt capacity. Many lenders view this income band as a "tweener" that doesn't fit cleanly into either their affordable housing or conventional multifamily boxes.

Adding complexity, the West Adams location presents both opportunity and risk. While transit access and neighborhood momentum support long-term value creation, construction costs in hot LA submarkets continue escalating, and the local entitlement process remains unpredictable despite ED1's streamlined approach.

The Solution

We structured a capital stack that maximized the project's debt capacity while minimizing expensive equity gaps. The foundation consisted of 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits combined with tax-exempt bond financing, creating the base layer of subsidized capital.

The key insight was recognizing that 50-80% AMI rents could support a larger conventional first mortgage than typical affordable deals. We modeled the cash flows to demonstrate a debt service coverage ratio that met conventional standards, despite the income restrictions. This allowed us to layer in substantial conventional debt above the tax-exempt bond proceeds.

For construction financing, we identified a regional bank with strong CRA motivations and affordable housing experience. Rather than competing solely on rate, we emphasized the deal's solid fundamentals: the higher debt coverage ratios, the stable rent roll backed by working professionals, and the strong West Adams submarket dynamics. The bank provided construction terms competitive with CDFI pricing while offering the execution certainty that tax credit deals require.

The ED1 designation proved crucial for feasibility. California's Executive Directive 1 requires state agencies to inventory excess property and make suitable parcels available for affordable housing development. This dramatically reduced land basis, creating room in the project budget for the higher construction costs that workforce housing commands relative to deeply subsidized projects.

The Outcome

The financing closed at terms that work for all stakeholders. The developer achieved a capital stack with manageable debt service and sufficient equity returns to justify the execution risk. The construction lender received appropriate risk-adjusted pricing with strong sponsor guarantees and a proven general contractor relationship.

More importantly, this deal demonstrates a replicable model for workforce housing in expensive California markets. By rightsizing the debt component and leveraging programs like ED1, developers can make the numbers work for the crucial 50-80% AMI demographic without relying on increasingly scarce deep subsidy dollars.

The West Adams location positions tenants near major employment centers while maintaining relative affordability compared to westside alternatives. Transit access via multiple Metro lines reduces transportation costs, effectively increasing housing affordability for the target demographic.

Construction is proceeding on schedule with completion targeted for late 2025. Pre-leasing interest from the target demographic remains strong, validating the market demand for quality workforce housing in gentrifying neighborhoods. The project establishes a template that other developers are already exploring for similar sites across Los Angeles County.

For lenders, this transaction illustrates how workforce housing can offer attractive risk-adjusted returns while fulfilling CRA objectives. The moderate income restrictions provide more predictable cash flows than deeply affordable projects while maintaining the social impact that drives institutional investment in the affordable housing sector.
