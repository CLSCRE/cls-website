NNN Financing in Sacramento: 2026 Guide for Net Lease Investors

# Net Lease (NNN) Financing in Sacramento and the Central Valley in 2026

The Sacramento NNN Market in 2026

Sacramento stands as the largest net lease market in the Central Valley, driven by the state capital's stable employment base, growing population, and spillover demand from Bay Area investors and remote workers seeking affordability. As California's largest employer, state government provides a recession-resistant workforce that anchors demand for quick-service restaurants (QSR), pharmacy, and convenience retail across the metro area.

Current market conditions reflect strong investor appetite across multiple property types. QSR properties trade at cap rates between 4.75 and 5.50 percent, with pharmacy assets yielding 5.00 to 5.75 percent. Auto parts and dollar store properties command wider spreads at 5.50 to 6.75 percent, reflecting their defensive retail profile. These cap rate ranges remain attractive compared to coastal markets, positioning Sacramento as a compelling alternative for yield-focused investors.

Active tenant types define the Sacramento market. McDonald's, In-N-Out Burger, Starbucks, and Habit Burger (a California regional QSR chain) drive QSR demand. Pharmacy investors target CVS and Walgreens locations, while dollar store and auto parts investors focus on Dollar General and AutoZone. A critical note for investors: In-N-Out Burger, owned by the Snyder family, retains nearly all its locations and presents very limited NNN investment opportunities in the market.

Sacramento's appeal extends beyond government workers. Tech talent migrating from the Bay Area through remote work arrangements has accelerated population growth and consumer spending. This demographic shift supports both QSR growth and pharmacy expansion, particularly in affluent suburbs.

Sacramento as a Bay Area 1031 Exchange Destination

Sacramento has emerged as one of the top 1031 exchange destinations for Bay Area sellers, offering a compelling cap rate arbitrage opportunity. Investors selling apartments or commercial property in San Francisco, Oakland, or the greater Bay Area can exchange proceeds into Sacramento NNN properties yielding 5.00 to 5.75 percent, compared to cap rates of just 3.50 to 4.25 percent in the Bay Area. This 125 to 200 basis point yield advantage, combined with lower acquisition prices, attracts institutional and individual Bay Area capital consistently.

The bank program has become the dominant financing tool for Bay Area 1031 buyers targeting Sacramento NNN. These programs offer speed and simplicity, delivering 25 to 35 day closes essential for meeting 1031 timelines. For larger acquisitions exceeding $5 million, life company and CMBS programs provide non-recourse alternatives. The combination of price advantage, yield pickup, and accessible financing has made Sacramento the preferred 1031 destination for Northern California sellers.

Lender Programs for Sacramento NNN

Multiple lending programs actively finance Sacramento NNN acquisitions, accommodating transactions across all size ranges and credit profiles.

  • Bank Program: The dominant tool for Sacramento NNN financing ranges from $750,000 to $8 million. Terms typically include CMT plus 190 to 260 basis points, 5-year terms with 25-year amortization, and recourse structure. This program excels for Bay Area 1031 exchanges and 1099 individual investors targeting fast closings.
  • CMBS Conduit: For acquisitions from $5 million to $50 million and above, CMBS conduits offer fixed rates, non-recourse structure, 10-year terms, and 30-year amortization. These programs accommodate larger institutional portfolios and provide balance sheet relief.
  • Life Company Lenders: Non-recourse programs starting at $5 million feature fixed rates, 10-year terms, and 30-year amortization. Life companies maintain strong appetite for California NNN assets with investment-grade tenants, particularly QSR and pharmacy.
  • California Regional Banks and Credit Unions: Active at $1 million to $5 million, these lenders offer recourse financing with familiarity and speed in California NNN structures, often delivering faster credit decisions than national programs.

Sacramento Suburban NNN Markets

Sacramento's metro area encompasses several distinct suburban markets, each with unique demographics, tenant types, and cap rate profiles.

Elk Grove represents Sacramento's fastest-growing suburb, with diverse demographics and strong QSR demand. New construction activity remains active, attracting national QSR chains and creating pipeline opportunities for investors seeking growth exposure at relatively tight cap rates.

Roseville and Rocklin occupy the affluent northeast corridor in Placer County. Anchored by the Galleria mall and surrounding retail development, these communities attract premium QSR and pharmacy operators. Cap rates of 4.75 to 5.25 percent for QSR reflect their strong demographic and credit fundamentals.

Folsom combines tech company employment with state government workers, producing high household income and substantial purchasing power. The capital market has tightened accordingly, with limited sub-5.00 percent cap rate opportunities.

Citrus Heights and Rancho Cordova serve middle-income east county populations. These markets support dollar store and auto parts retail at wider cap rates of 5.75 to 6.75 percent, appealing to yield-focused investors.

West Sacramento and Davis benefit from state government concentration and UC Davis affiliation. Pharmacy and QSR remain active, with Davis featuring the unique dynamic of student housing and academic-adjacent retail.

Central Valley NNN: Stockton, Modesto, and Fresno

Beyond Sacramento, the Central Valley presents distinct investment opportunities and risk profiles. Stockton, Modesto, and Fresno trade at cap rates of 5.75 to 7.25 percent, significantly wider than Sacramento metro properties. These cities' economies remain tied to agricultural production and commodity cycles, introducing regional economic exposure distinct from Sacramento's government anchor.

Dollar store NNN properties dominate Central Valley investment activity, with Dollar General and Family Dollar locations across small and mid-sized communities. Lenders actively support dollar store acquisitions throughout the region, viewing these properties as defensive retail with consistent cash flow.

Turlock and Merced present emerging growth markets, with UC Merced campus expansion driving enrollment-adjacent demand. These communities offer intermediate cap rate opportunities and demographic tailwinds distinct from mature San Francisco Bay Area markets.

Sacramento NNN Outlook 2026

Looking forward to 2026, several factors support continued Sacramento NNN investor demand. Bay Area tech migration through remote work arrangements will persist, driving population growth and consumer spending. State capital stability provides recession buffers absent in purely private sector markets. Lender competition remains robust across all program types and loan sizes, maintaining attractive pricing and terms.

Best opportunities align with growth corridors and demographic strength: Elk Grove and Roseville for QSR expansion, downtown-adjacent Midtown neighborhoods for pharmacy, and Central Valley dollar store locations for yield investors comfortable with agricultural economic exposure.
