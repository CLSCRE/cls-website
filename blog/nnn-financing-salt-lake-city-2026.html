NNN Financing in Salt Lake City: 2026 Guide for Net Lease Investors

# Net Lease (NNN) Financing in Salt Lake City and the Wasatch Front: 2026 Market Overview

The Salt Lake City NNN Market in 2026

Salt Lake City and the greater Wasatch Front metro area represent the dominant net lease market in the Mountain West. With a population exceeding 1.3 million and robust job growth driven by the technology sector, the region has emerged as a premier destination for NNN investors seeking yield with demographic tailwinds.

Current cap rates reflect a competitive but still attractive investment landscape. Quick-service restaurant (QSR) properties trade between 5.0 and 5.75%, while pharmacy assets command rates of 5.25 to 6.00%. Auto and dollar store properties occupy the wider end of the spectrum at 5.75 to 7.0%. These cap rates remain substantially wider than comparable coastal properties, making the market appealing for yield-focused investors.

A unique demographic advantage underpins the Salt Lake City NNN market: the large LDS Church population drives exceptionally high family formation rates. This secular tailwind creates outsized demand for childcare facilities, pharmacies, quick-service restaurants, and dollar stores. Simultaneously, the so-called "Silicon Slopes" phenomenon -- anchored by major technology employers including Adobe, eBay, Goldman Sachs' tech hub, Qualtrics, and Ancestry -- has attracted high-income households and fueled suburban expansion.

Active tenant rosters reflect national credit quality mixed with Utah-based operators. McDonald's, Chick-fil-A, and the Utah-based Cafe Rio dominate the QSR landscape. Pharmacy tenants include CVS and Walgreens. Convenience stores are led by the Utah-based Maverick brand. Dollar General and AutoZone anchor the value retail segment. This combination of national credits and successful regional operators provides NNN buyers with both stability and local economic connection.

Geographically, Salt Lake City enjoys an isolated position as the capital of the Mountain West with no meaningful competing NNN markets in Nevada or California nearby. This insularity, combined with strong population growth, has sustained investor demand and limited speculative overbuilding.

Utah NNN Lease Structure and Legal Considerations

Utah commercial real estate law follows national NNN lease conventions with no unusual provisions that complicate underwriting or increase landlord risk. The state adheres to straightforward landlord-tenant law principles similar to most jurisdictions, with no idiosyncratic regulations affecting net lease structures.

Standard lease terms in the Salt Lake City market typically include initial terms of 10 to 15 years with absolute NNN obligations -- tenant responsibility for property taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance. These structures mirror national standards and pose no legal surprises for lenders or investors familiar with net lease transactions elsewhere in the United States.

Utah does not impose unusual environmental disclosure requirements that would affect NNN underwriting, nor are there hidden liabilities that net lease investors should anticipate. Additionally, the state has no rent control or tenant-favorable regulations that constrain commercial NNN economics. This regulatory simplicity makes Utah a straightforward jurisdiction for net lease lending and investing.

Lender Programs for Salt Lake City NNN

Multiple lender channels serve the Salt Lake City NNN market with competitive programs tailored to different borrower profiles and transaction sizes.

  • Bank Programs: National banks with dedicated net lease divisions offer loans from $750,000 to $8 million at floating-rate pricing (CMT plus 190 to 260 basis points), with 5-year terms and 25-year amortization schedules. These programs remain recourse to the borrower and appeal to investors seeking rapid execution and lower documentation burdens.
  • CMBS Conduits: Life insurance companies and CMBS platforms provide competitive fixed-rate financing from $5 million to $50 million and above. Terms typically include 10-year maturities with 30-year amortization and non-recourse structures. These programs cater to larger acquisitions and borrowers prioritizing balance sheet protection.
  • Life Company Lenders: Dedicated net lease life companies maintain strong appetite for Utah assets, particularly QSR and pharmacy properties. Programs begin at $5 million, feature 10-year fixed-rate terms, 30-year amortization, and non-recourse execution.
  • Utah Community Banks and Credit Unions: Local institutions remain exceptionally active in the $750,000 to $4 million range. Recourse to the borrower is standard, but community banks offer local market knowledge and faster decision-making. Several LDS Church-affiliated institutions add meaningful capital supply to the market.

Wasatch Front NNN Submarkets

The Wasatch Front comprises multiple distinct submarkets, each with unique demographics and investment characteristics.

  • Salt Lake City and Sugar House: Urban infill QSR and pharmacy assets trade at 5.0 to 5.5% cap rates. These properties appeal to investors seeking location prestige and walkability.
  • South Jordan, Riverton, and Herriman: The fast-growing southwest valley represents the current frontier for suburban NNN development. New-construction assets price at 5.0 to 5.75%, attracting value-add and development-stage investors.
  • Murray and Draper: Mid-valley properties reflect established QSR and pharmacy demand with moderate growth rates.
  • Provo and Orem (Utah County): The BYU-adjacent region experiences very high population density relative to its size. Dollar store and QSR demand remains robust, with pricing competitive to the broader Wasatch Front.
  • Ogden and Weber County: The northern metro offers wider cap rates (5.5 to 6.5%) and represents a more affordable entry point for yield-focused investors.

California 1031 Exchange Buyers in Utah

Utah has emerged as a dominant destination for California 1031 exchange buyers. California's 13.3% combined state and federal capital gains tax, paired with elevated property values, creates compelling economics for California-based investors to exchange multifamily apartments and commercial properties into Utah NNN assets.

Bank programs offering sub-$5 million loans with 25- to 35-day closing timelines dominate the California 1031 buyer segment. The speed of execution and lower recourse burdens appeal to California investors executing time-sensitive exchanges.

Life company programs with non-recourse structures and 10-year fixed rates attract larger California 1031 buyers at $5 million and above, who prioritize balance sheet protection and long-term passive income.

Salt Lake City NNN Outlook 2026

The Salt Lake City and Wasatch Front NNN market remains among the strongest in the Mountain West for long-term investment. Population and job growth, a capital-friendly regulatory environment, and the technology sector's continued expansion position the region favorably through 2026 and beyond.

While cap rates have compressed from prior-year levels, they remain wider than comparable coastal properties, sustaining yield advantage for investors seeking passive income. The greatest opportunities exist in the South Jordan and Herriman growth corridor and Provo/Orem for yield-focused investors.
