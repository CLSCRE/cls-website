NNN Financing in Seattle: 2026 Guide for Net Lease Investors

The Seattle NNN Market: Supply Constraint as a Feature

Net lease (NNN) real estate in Seattle represents one of the most competitive and supply-constrained markets in the United States outside of California. For investors seeking triple-net retail properties, the Emerald City and surrounding King County present a paradox: exceptional tenant quality and strong underlying economics, paired with limited availability and compressed cap rates that reflect fierce institutional demand.

The Seattle NNN market is characterized by structural supply constraints that benefit landlords and long-term holders. Land costs in King County rank among the highest outside of California, with commercial pad sites in prime retail corridors commanding $50 to $150+ per square foot of ground area. Entitlement complexity -- driven by environmental reviews, traffic impact analyses, and local land-use regulations -- extends development timelines by 18 to 36 months. Density requirements and height restrictions further limit the number of viable pad site opportunities. As a result, most new NNN inventory in the Seattle area originates from sale-leaseback transactions of existing retail properties or redevelopment of underperforming sites rather than ground-up new construction.

Current cap rates in the Seattle NNN market reflect this scarcity premium. Quick-service restaurants (QSR) trade between 4.75% and 5.5%, pharmacy properties range from 5.0% to 5.75%, and auto service centers are priced between 5.5% and 6.5%. These rates are 50 to 150 basis points tighter than national averages, signaling strong investor appetite and limited deal flow.

Seattle's tenant roster is anchored by operators headquartered in or closely tied to the region. Starbucks, founded and headquartered in Seattle, commands special attention from local and regional investors who view a Starbucks NNN property as a hometown brand connection and a tangible link to the company's success. McDonald's, Chick-fil-A, CVS, Walgreens, AutoZone, and Jiffy Lube also maintain strong presence and expansion plans across the Seattle metropolitan area. The concentration of major tech employers, including Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing, and Starbucks headquarters, drives a high-income consumer base that supports premium retail real estate values.

High-Cost Market Dynamics

Seattle's high-cost real estate environment extends into the NNN sector, creating a unique buyer profile and lending landscape. Land prices in King County are among the nation's steepest, and this reality compresses cap rates across all property types. Investors accepting sub-5.75% yields on pharmacy or auto service properties are making a deliberate choice to accept below-national-average returns in exchange for supply certainty, tenant credit quality, and geographic diversification.

Out-of-state capital flows heavily into Seattle NNN. California investors, in particular, view Washington's lack of state income tax and higher cap rates (relative to California) as an attractive arbitrage. The comparison is especially compelling for 1031 exchange buyers exiting high-basis California multifamily properties; a Seattle NNN acquisition at 5.25% can exceed California net lease yields while offering the same tax-free state income benefit for Washington-domiciled investors.

Minimum loan sizes in Seattle NNN transactions have crept upward. Most active lenders target deal sizes of $3 million or above, with limited appetite for properties below $2 million. This threshold reflects the fixed costs of underwriting, appraisal, and legal work; smaller deals do not generate sufficient fee income to justify the effort. Accordingly, mom-and-pop investors and owner-operators seeking to finance single pad sites often face higher all-in costs or are steered toward portfolio acquisitions to meet lender minimums.

Lender Programs for Seattle NNN

The lending landscape for Seattle NNN properties is robust and tiered by loan size and tenant credit quality. For loans between $1.5 million and $5 million, traditional bank programs dominate. A national bank with a dedicated net lease division offers 5-year fixed terms with recourse requirements, pricing typically in the 6.5% to 7.75% range (depending on tenant and property quality). These programs favor investment-grade tenants and properties in prime retail corridors.

Loans ranging from $5 million to $15 million increasingly flow through CMBS conduits. Conduit lenders offer longer amortization (20 to 30 years), fixed or floating rate options, and non-recourse structures that appeal to larger investors and institutional buyers. Conduit programs are especially active for Starbucks, CVS, and Walgreens NNN properties in the $7 million to $12 million range.

Life insurance companies are active lenders for loans $7 million and above in Washington State. Washington ranks among their preferred underwriting states due to favorable demographics, strong employment, and the absence of state income tax. Life insurance lenders typically offer competitive rates, flexible terms (5 to 10 years), and a willingness to work with borrowers on customized structures such as cross-collateralization or multiple-property portfolios.

Washington-based regional and community banks also participate in smaller NNN deals. Puget Sound-area banks with deep local market knowledge often finance $2 million to $6 million properties with relationship pricing and faster underwriting timelines than national competitors. These institutions are particularly receptive to local operators and mom-and-pop investors with established banking relationships.

Washington State-Specific Factors

Several state-level characteristics influence NNN financing and investment in Washington. The absence of state income tax is a primary attraction, allowing investors to retain more after-tax cash flow and appreciation compared to high-tax states. For out-of-state buyers considering a Washington 1031 exchange, the tax-neutral environment is a significant factor.

The Business and Occupation (B&O) tax, a gross-receipts-based tax levied on Washington businesses, affects retail operators' profitability. In a NNN structure, however, most B&O tax obligations pass through to the tenant, mitigating landlord exposure. Sophisticated investors and lenders account for B&O liability in underwriting pro formas, but the tax is rarely a deal-breaker for strong tenants.

The employment base in Washington supports strong suburban retail demand. Amazon, Microsoft, and Boeing each employ tens of thousands across the region, with satellite offices and contractor networks extending into Eastside communities like Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland, and Issaquah. This employment concentration creates robust demand for convenience retail, quick-service restaurants, and auto service facilities. Properties on the Eastside command cap rates 25 to 50 basis points tighter than Seattle proper, reflecting the wealth and income levels of commuters and residents.

1031 Exchange Buyers in Seattle

Seattle is a magnet for 1031 exchange capital, both from in-state and out-of-state sources. In-state exchanges often involve apartment owners in Seattle suburbs (Shoreline, Tacoma, Renton, Kent) selling small multifamily properties at high valuations and reinvesting proceeds into NNN properties. These local investors prize NNN for its passive income, single-tenant simplicity, and lower management burden compared to multifamily.

Out-of-state California investors are frequent participants. A typical transaction involves the sale of a San Francisco or Los Angeles fourplex or small apartment building at a very high price, with proceeds rolled into a Seattle NNN portfolio. The buyer benefits from Washington's lack of state income tax, higher cap rates than available in California, and a stable tenant base. For a California-based investor, the arbitrage between California multifamily prices and Seattle NNN yields can justify the geographic relocation of capital.

Seattle and Pacific Northwest NNN Outlook for 2026

The 2026 Seattle NNN market is stabilizing after tech sector uncertainties of 2023 to 2025. While Amazon and Microsoft experienced modest layoffs, employment in the region remains resilient and growing. Consumer spending, supported by high household incomes, continues to underpin retail fundamentals. Cap rates may compress slightly as confidence returns, though limited new supply will continue to support landlord economics.

Secondary markets in the South Sound region, including Tacoma, Puyallup, and Lakewood, offer higher cap rates (5.75% to 6.5% for QSR) and faster population growth. These markets attract value-add investors and out-of-state capital seeking better yields than Seattle proper.

The best near-term opportunities lie in suburban Eastside communities such as Issaquah, Sammamish, and Maple Valley, where new retail pad sites are coming to market and demographic tailwinds support long-term tenant demand.
