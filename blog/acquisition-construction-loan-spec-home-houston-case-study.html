Case Study: $636K Combined Acquisition and Construction Loan for a Houston Spec Home

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $635,600 combined lot-acquisition and construction loan through a private construction lender for a repeat spec-builder client in Houston, Texas. The loan funded both the $252,662 purchase price and the full $442,600 construction budget in a single 12-month facility at 9.25% fixed interest-only, priced tighter than the sponsor's prior construction loan with the same lender a few months earlier.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal came from the same Cedar City, Utah-based builder client we had just financed a spec build for in Houston's Near Northside a few months earlier. This time, the project was a new lot purchase in Houston, Texas (TX) paired with the ground-up construction budget, needing a single facility from a private construction lender that could close on the acquisition and fund the build without the borrower arranging separate purchase and construction loans back to back. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in construction and bridge financing for spec-home builders and investors across Texas and nationwide.

Structuring an acquisition and a construction budget into one loan is common in the spec-building world, but it only works when the lender is comfortable underwriting a purchase price, a full construction line, and an interest reserve together as a single capital stack, rather than treating the lot purchase and the build as two separate credit decisions.

What Made It Hard

  • Combining two funding needs into one closing. The loan had to fund the $252,662 lot purchase and the $442,600 construction budget simultaneously, plus an interest reserve, without the deal collapsing into two separate, harder-to-coordinate loans.
  • Sizing the equity requirement correctly. Against $748,884.88 in total project cost, the sponsor needed to bring roughly $112,390 in cash, and getting that split right between debt and equity mattered for both loan approval and the sponsor's own capital planning.
  • A short acceptance window on the letter of interest, again just two days from issuance, requiring quick borrower turnaround to keep the underwriting clock on schedule.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed this loan with a private construction lender, LLC, the same private construction lender from the sponsor's earlier Houston project, closing the letter of interest on August 19, 2025:

  • $635,600 total loan, combining the $252,662 lot purchase price and the $442,600 construction budget into a single first-mortgage facility
  • $42,416.80 interest reserve funded at closing to cover monthly interest during the build
  • 9.25% fixed rate, interest-only, meaningfully tighter than the 9.85% rate on the sponsor's prior construction loan with the same lender a few months before, a rate improvement consistent with an established repayment relationship
  • 12-month term with up to two extensions available at the lender's discretion
  • A single construction draw (versus a two-draw structure on the sponsor's earlier, larger project), each carrying a $500 construction administration fee, appropriate for a smaller build
  • Underwriting caps of 70% loan-to-after-repair-value and 85% loan-to-cost, with the sponsor required to demonstrate $100,000 in confirmed liquidity via recent bank statements before underwriting proceeded
  • $112,389.88 in borrower equity against $748,884.88 in total project cost, with the balance of settlement costs, origination points, underwriting, appraisal, and title/escrow fees layered into the closing
  • The same dual guaranty structure as the sponsor's prior deal: a personal repayment guaranty from the sponsor, plus completion guaranties from both the sponsor and a second individual

Net cash required from the borrower at closing came to roughly $140,272, reflecting the equity contribution plus closing costs after the loan proceeds were applied to the purchase and initial funding.

"Once a builder has proven he can execute and repay on the first deal, the second one should be easier and cheaper. That is exactly what happened here, the rate came down and the structure got cleaner."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

The rate improvement between this deal and the sponsor's prior construction loan is the clearest evidence of why a repeat lending relationship pays off in this asset class: 9.25% versus 9.85% on a nearly identical loan structure a few months apart, with the same lender, the same guarantors, and the same broker relationship. Construction lenders price meaningfully on demonstrated performance, and closing the first deal cleanly set up better terms on the second.

Combining the acquisition and construction into one facility, rather than a separate purchase loan followed by a construction refinance, also saved the sponsor a second round of closing costs and underwriting friction on a project this size, letting the capital stack come together in a single, coordinated closing.

Is This Structure Right for Your Deal?

If you are purchasing a lot and building on it, financing both pieces in a single facility can save real time and money compared to sequencing separate loans. We arrange construction loans and bridge financing from a network of more than 1,000 capital sources nationwide.
