July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $1,386,000 refinance through Logix Federal Credit Union for a commercial property in Santa Ana, California, paying off an existing note with United Business Bank. The loan closed at 6.875% fixed for five years, amortized over 30 years, with a compensating balance program that discounted the rate 50 basis points in exchange for maintaining a minimum deposit relationship, and a reserve holdback tied to extending the property's existing leases.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance brought us a Santa Ana, California mixed-use commercial property owner looking to replace an existing note with United Business Bank on better long-term terms. The ownership group was open to a relationship-based lender if the pricing made sense, which opened the door to a credit union structure most conventional bank shoppers never see quoted.

Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in structuring relationship-priced and credit union financing for commercial property owners across California, drawing on a background in structured finance at CBRE and Marcus and Millichap Capital Corporation to match deals like this one with the right kind of lender.

Santa Ana's commercial corridors mix retail, office, and food-service tenants across a range of building types, and a lender comfortable underwriting a property with existing leases in place, rather than requiring full vacancy or a fresh-start valuation, was the right fit for a straightforward payoff refinance.

What Made It Hard

  • Near-term lease rollover risk. With existing leases not yet extended to a minimum three-year term, the lender's underwriting required a real reserve mechanism rather than simply accepting the current rent roll at face value.
  • Structuring a relationship-priced rate. Getting the full 50 basis point discount required committing to a real, ongoing minimum deposit balance, a genuine operational commitment beyond simply signing loan documents.
  • Meeting dual ongoing covenants. The loan required both a minimum 1.20x debt service coverage ratio and a separate minimum 80% occupancy requirement to be maintained throughout the full term, two distinct triggers the borrower had to manage simultaneously.
"This one worked because the borrower had the liquidity to actually support a deposit relationship, so instead of forcing a standard bank quote we went and found the credit union willing to price that trade explicitly. Handling the lease reserve upfront, instead of letting it turn into a fight at the closing table, is what kept the file moving."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed this loan with Logix Federal Credit Union's commercial lending team, with the commitment letter signed August 21, 2024:

  • $1,386,000 used entirely to pay off the existing United Business Bank note, secured by a first priority lien on the property plus an assignment of rents and a security interest in personal property
  • 6.875% fixed for five years, amortized over 30 years with an estimated monthly payment of $9,105.03
  • A minimum 1.20x debt service coverage ratio and a separate minimum 80% occupancy requirement, both required to be maintained throughout the loan term
  • A 6-month payment reserve holdback at closing if the property's current leases were not extended to a minimum three-year term beforehand, released once a satisfactory lease extension or comparable new lease was signed
  • A compensating balance program: the quoted rate reflected a 50 basis point relationship discount, contingent on maintaining a minimum average daily deposit balance of 10% of the loan amount, $138,600, across the borrower's and guarantor's related accounts for the life of the loan
  • A non-compliance rate mechanism: if the deposit balance requirement was not cured within 30 days of a shortfall, the loan converted to a higher non-compliance rate for the remainder of the term, with a second violation triggering that rate immediately with no cure period
  • A 0.50% loan fee ($6,930), deemed fully earned and non-refundable at closing, plus reimbursement of the credit union's underwriting, appraisal, and title expenses
  • A personal guaranty from the managing member, with any obligations owed by the borrower to the guarantor subordinated to the credit union's position

The refinance closed within the commitment letter's acceptance window, retiring the prior bank note and giving the ownership group a fixed-rate facility with a meaningfully lower rate in exchange for the deposit relationship.

Why We Won This One

The relationship-pricing structure is the piece that made this refinance genuinely better than a standard bank quote. Committing to a real, ongoing deposit balance in exchange for a 50 basis point discount only makes sense when the borrower actually has the liquidity to support it without straining operations, and matching this deal to a credit union willing to structure that trade explicitly, with a defined non-compliance mechanism rather than vague discretion, gave the borrower clarity on exactly what maintaining the discount required.

Handling the lease-extension reserve proactively, rather than letting it become a point of last-minute renegotiation, also kept the closing on schedule. Knowing upfront that a holdback would apply unless leases were extended gave the borrower the choice to either secure those extensions before closing or accept the reserve, rather than being surprised by it during underwriting.

If you are refinancing a leased commercial property and have the liquidity to support a deposit relationship, credit union relationship pricing can meaningfully beat a standard bank quote.
