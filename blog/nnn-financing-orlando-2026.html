NNN Financing in Orlando: 2026 Guide for Net Lease Investors

# Net Lease (NNN) Financing in Orlando and Central Florida: 2026 Market Guide

The Orlando NNN Market in 2026

Orlando and the surrounding Orange County metro area rank among Florida's most active net lease markets. The region's combination of strong population growth, a robust tourism economy, and consistent demand for quick-service restaurants (QSR), convenience retail, and essential services creates a steady flow of NNN investment opportunities for both institutional buyers and 1031 exchange investors.

Current cap rates in the Orlando metro reflect the market's stability and investor demand. QSR properties, particularly those occupied by national tenants like McDonald's and Chick-fil-A, trade between 4.75% and 5.50%. Pharmacy anchored properties (CVS, Walgreens) command slightly higher yields at 5.25% to 6.00%, while auto service and dollar store properties range from 5.50% to 6.75%. These cap rate ranges apply to properties with investment-grade or strong private credit tenants and absolute triple net lease structures.

Several tenant types dominate the Orlando NNN landscape. McDonald's remains the most visible QSR tenant, with franchises distributed across the I-4 corridor and suburban areas. Chick-fil-A has expanded significantly in Central Florida, particularly in affluent residential neighborhoods. Wawa, the Delaware-based convenience chain, has undertaken substantial growth in the region with absolute NNN leases backed by strong private credit. Starbucks, both standalone and co-tenanted, appears frequently. For essential services, CVS and Walgreens pharmacies anchor many mixed-use and pad retail centers. Dollar General and Family Dollar continue expanding into secondary and tertiary markets throughout Osceola, Brevard, and Volusia counties. AutoZone and similar auto service retailers remain active in suburban locations.

The tourism economy, anchored by Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando, and SeaWorld, creates exceptionally high density of retail and QSR real estate along the I-4 corridor and in the immediate metro. However, as discussed in later sections, lenders do not underwrite NNN properties to tourism-dependent revenue, since tenant rent obligations remain flat regardless of traffic or economic cycles.

Florida Property Insurance: What Lenders Require

Property insurance in Orlando and inland Central Florida is materially cheaper than in coastal markets like Tampa, Miami, or Jacksonville. This cost advantage is a meaningful benefit to property buyers and should be factored into underwriting. However, lenders increasingly scrutinize insurance assumptions, and borrowers should avoid the dangerous assumption that insurance premiums remain flat over a 10-year loan term.

Florida property insurance has experienced significant volatility since 2022. Rates have climbed statewide, and insurers have withdrawn from the market or reduced capacity. Even inland Orlando properties face rising premiums. Lenders now commonly require three years of actual insurance loss history and impose reserve requirements to protect against future increases. Some lenders require annual insurance cost escalation assumptions of 3% to 5%, particularly on longer-term CMBS loans.

For net lease properties, the tenant typically pays insurance as part of the triple net obligation. However, lenders still underwrite insurance costs to ensure the property would cash flow under worst-case scenarios. If a property is untenanted or in transition, the borrower bears the insurance cost directly. Lenders also verify that the property has adequate liability and property coverage limits and that the lender is named as loss payee.

Buyers considering 1031 exchanges into Orlando NNN properties should explicitly underwrite insurance costs separate from other operating expenses. Insurance represents one of the few variables that can shift dramatically year to year, independent of rent growth or tenant performance.

Lender Programs for Orlando NNN

CLS CRE works with multiple lender platforms to deliver competitive financing for Orlando and Central Florida NNN acquisitions across all property sizes and buyer profiles.

  • Bank programs: A dedicated national bank net lease division offers loans from $750,000 to $8 million. These loans carry rates at CMT plus 190 to 260 basis points, with five-year terms and 25-year amortization. These loans are typically recourse, meaning the borrower carries personal liability. Bank programs close quickly (45 to 60 days) and require strong sponsor credit and reserves.
  • CMBS conduits: Life company and insurance-backed CMBS platforms lend $5 million and above, with no upper limit for large portfolios. These loans feature fixed rates, non-recourse structure, 10-year terms, and 30-year amortization. CMBS conduits are ideal for 1031 exchange buyers, portfolio acquisitions, and borrowers seeking long-term rate certainty and liability insulation. Conduit underwriting is rigorous but transparent, and execution is reliable for investment-grade tenants.
  • Life company lenders: Dedicated to net lease, life companies deploy capital at $5 million and above with non-recourse structure, 10-year fixed rates, and 30-year amortization. Life companies show particular appetite for McDonald's, Chick-fil-A, CVS, and Walgreens properties. Their underwriting is conservative but fair, and they rarely impose surprises late in the process.
  • Local and regional Florida banks: Community and regional banks active in Florida remain engaged in the $1 million to $5 million space. These lenders typically require recourse, but they offer faster underwriting and greater flexibility on tenant diversification. Local banks often close in 30 to 40 days and value relationship banking.

I-4 Corridor and Suburban Orlando Markets

The I-4 spine connecting Orlando to Tampa represents one of Florida's highest-density retail and QSR corridors. Prime pad sites in this corridor command cap rates of 4.75% to 5.25%, reflecting the consistent traffic and affluent demographic reach along the highway.

Lake Nona, a master-planned medical and technology hub south of downtown Orlando, has emerged as a strong market for pharmacy and QSR NNN. The area attracts high-income medical professionals and hospital workers, supporting strong per-capita sales volumes and tenant credit quality.

Kissimmee and Osceola County benefit from tourism adjacency and domestic migration. Dollar store and value retail tenants dominate the area, with cap rates ranging from 6.00% to 6.75%. These properties appeal to value-oriented investors and 1031 exchange buyers seeking higher yields.

Sanford, Lake Mary, and Oviedo represent the northeastern suburban growth corridor. These areas attract white-collar office workers, biotech employees, and young families seeking affordable housing and shorter commutes. QSR and convenience retail demand is strong and growing in these submarkets.

Tourism Economy and NNN Underwriting

While the tourism economy drives enormous retail density in metro Orlando, it does not directly affect NNN lease underwriting. Net lease properties generate flat rent payments regardless of tenant sales volume or traffic patterns. A McDonald's franchise near Disney World and a McDonald's in suburban Sanford pay identical rent to their landlords, even though the Disney-area unit likely generates substantially higher sales.

Tourism economy matters indirectly through franchisee credit and operator health. Lenders examine franchise disclosure documents (FDDs) for sub-$5 million QSR transactions to understand system-wide sales performance and individual unit economics. A franchisee operating multiple units in high-traffic areas may have stronger credit and lower default risk than one operating in slower secondary markets.

Corporate-guaranteed leases, including McDonald's LLC, Walgreens, CVS, and Chick-fil-A, are not affected by tourism economics. These tenants' rent obligations are backed by strong corporate credit, and lenders treat these leases as institutional-quality credits regardless of location.

Orlando NNN Outlook for 2026

Orlando's NNN market enters 2026 with strong fundamentals. Domestic migration from high-tax states in the Northeast, Midwest, and California continues to drive population growth. Employment growth in healthcare, technology, and hospitality remains robust. These factors support continued demand for QSR, convenience, and essential services real estate.

Cap rate compression has occurred since 2021, and modest further compression is expected in 2026 as more capital chases limited inventory of investment-grade tenants. The best opportunities for investors lie in suburban growth corridors (Lake Nona, Oviedo, Sanford) where cap rates remain slightly higher and tenant demand is accelerating. 1031 exchange buyers from California and the Northeast continue to seek Florida properties, providing steady demand for NNN products.
