Commercial Lending Solutions Market Perspective: August 2026

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

August 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions breaks down the August 2026 CRE capital markets landscape: where rates are trading, which lenders are deploying, what property types are clearing, and what sponsors need to know heading into Q4 budget season.

August arrived with more clarity than most market participants expected heading into the summer. The 10-year Treasury has been trading in a relatively tight band between 4.25% and 4.55% for the better part of six weeks, a level of stability the broader CRE lending community has quietly welcomed after the volatility that defined Q1. The Federal Reserve held at its July FOMC meeting, keeping the federal funds target range at 4.00% to 4.25%, and the language in the accompanying statement tilted incrementally dovish, acknowledging that inflation is "continuing to move toward target" without committing to a timeline. Futures markets are now pricing two cuts by year-end 2026, with the first expected in November. That's not a sprint to lower rates, but it is a directional signal that sponsors can begin to build around.

For the CRE market specifically, the convergence of a stable Treasury curve, recovering transaction volume, and lenders sitting on meaningful undeployed allocations has created an environment where well-structured deals are clearing with real execution certainty. This is not a return to 2021 underwriting; lenders are still disciplined on in-place debt service coverage, they want to see actual rent rolls rather than pro forma projections, and leverage tolerances remain conservative relative to where they were at the cycle peak. But the bid-ask gap that paralyzed the market throughout much of 2024 and early 2025 has meaningfully compressed. Price discovery is happening again, and volume is recovering. According to MSCI Real Assets data, Q2 2026 transaction volume was up approximately 18% year-over-year nationally, with gateway markets outperforming secondary markets for the first time since 2022.

The headline challenge for August is origination competition on the right side of the risk curve. High-quality multifamily, industrial, and grocery-anchored retail in primary markets are seeing four to six credible lender term sheets within two weeks of launch. Sponsors who have not updated their lender relationships in the last 12 months may be surprised by how much has shifted, both in terms of who is active and what program terms look like. Below, Commercial Lending Solutions breaks down the current environment capital markets layer by layer.

Rate Environment and Treasury Curve

The 10-year Treasury opened August at 4.38% and has been remarkably range-bound compared to the 80-basis-point swings we saw in the back half of 2025. The 2-year Treasury sits around 4.10%, producing a mildly positive curve that has not been consistently present since early 2022. For CRE borrowers, a positively sloped curve matters for two reasons: it improves the math on fixed-rate agency and life company executions relative to floating alternatives, and it signals that the market expects rate normalization rather than emergency cuts, which is a healthier backdrop for asset valuation.

SOFR, the benchmark underlying most floating-rate construction and bridge debt, is currently printing at approximately 4.18% on a 30-day term basis. With debt fund spreads running 250 to 375 basis points over SOFR depending on leverage and property type, borrowers on floating-rate bridge loans are still looking at all-in rates in the high 6% to mid-7% range. That is workable on value-add and construction deals where yield-on-cost underwrites to 6.50% or better at stabilization, but it continues to create pressure on deals that were capitalized in 2023 and 2024 on the assumption of rate cuts arriving earlier and more aggressively than they did. Extension negotiations and maturity modifications are still a significant part of the mid-market lending conversation.

For fixed-rate borrowers, the current environment is genuinely attractive on a relative basis. Life companies are pricing 10-year fixed-rate loans in the 5.75% to 6.25% range on quality assets. CMBS conduit executions on 10-year paper are running roughly 30 to 50 basis points wider than life company depending on the deal, reflecting the structural differences in prepayment flexibility and the defeasance cost embedded in conduit loans. Sponsors with assets that do not require significant capital flexibility over the hold period should be taking a hard look at locking in fixed-rate debt right now.

Lender Program Appetite

Life Insurance Companies

Life companies are among the most active allocators in the current market and, arguably, the most attractive execution for the right deal. Allocation windows that were effectively closed in Q4 2025 have reopened, with most major platforms reporting meaningful deployment capacity through the end of 2026. Minimum loan sizes generally start at $5 million, with most platforms' sweet spot running $15 million to $75 million. Property type preferences are concentrated in multifamily, industrial, and Class A office with strong tenancy, with select platforms showing renewed appetite for grocery-anchored retail. Loan-to-value tolerances are running 55% to 65% on stabilized assets, and DSCR minimums are firm at 1.25x on most programs. Spreads over the applicable Treasury are in the 140 to 200 basis point range for core assets, producing all-in rates that are highly competitive for long-duration capital. Prepayment flexibility is limited but manageable for sponsors with a clear hold horizon.

CMBS Conduit

Conduit volume has recovered meaningfully in 2026, with the first half of the year tracking well above 2025 issuance pace. The pipeline of deals clearing securitization has improved B-piece buyer appetite, which had been the constraint on conduit lending through much of 2024. Minimum loan sizes are $10 million, with most conduit programs most efficient between $15 million and $100 million per loan. Leverage is available to 65% to 70% LTV on core property types, and interest-only periods are available for well-covered stabilized assets. The conduit execution works well for borrowers who want non-recourse, fixed-rate debt and can live with the prepayment structure. Retail, multifamily, and industrial are the most commonly priced property types at the moment. Office underwriting remains highly deal-specific, with concentrated single-tenant leases to investment-grade credits being the primary path to conduit execution on office collateral.

Agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac)

Both agencies remain the dominant execution for conventional and affordable multifamily, though allocation dynamics at Freddie Mac have tightened slightly heading into the second half of 2026. Fannie Mae's DUS program is active across conventional, affordable, and manufactured housing, with 10-year fixed rates in the 5.50% to 5.90% range depending on tier and affordability restrictions. Freddie Mac's Optigo network is competitive on value-add and lease-up deals through the TAH and lease-up supplemental products. Green financing addendum remains a meaningful rate reducer for assets that qualify, often improving the rate by 10 to 25 basis points. Minimum loan size on both platforms is $1 million for select small-balance programs, with conventional execution most efficient above $5 million. Discussions around GSE conservatorship reform have continued to circulate in Washington but have not yet produced concrete structural changes that would affect borrower execution in the near term.

Banks and Credit Unions

Regional and community banks remain selectively active, but the credit quality bar has not moved lower. Most bank platforms are still managing CRE concentration ratios in response to regulatory guidance from the OCC and FDIC, and construction lending continues to face the most scrutiny. Where banks are active, they tend to favor relationships, in-market collateral, and deals with meaningful sponsor equity. Construction financing in primary markets is available for strong sponsors at 60% to 65% loan-to-cost, with recourse and completion guarantees required across the board. Credit unions have emerged as a meaningful alternative for multifamily in the $1 million to $10 million range, particularly in Western markets, with pricing that can be quite competitive for well-documented stabilized deals. Portfolio lenders generally want 10% to 20% global recourse depending on the relationship and loan size.

Debt Funds and Specialty Finance

Debt funds are actively deploying into bridge and construction with minimum loan sizes starting at $5 million and most programs preferring $10 million and above for new construction. The competitive gap between debt fund pricing and bank pricing has compressed modestly as banks have pulled back, making some debt fund executions surprisingly competitive on a net cost basis when you factor in certainty of close and speed. Preferred equity and mezzanine capital is available from $5 million upward for sponsors who need to fill gap positions in their capital stack, with coupon rates running 10% to 14% depending on position in the stack and risk profile. Specialty finance platforms are particularly active in construction-to-permanent executions for multifamily in markets where agency takeout is highly probable, essentially competing with the bank construction market at sharper pricing for deals with demonstrable exit visibility.

Property Type Commentary

Multifamily

Multifamily remains the deepest, most liquid capital market in commercial real estate. Stabilized conventional assets in primary and major secondary markets are experiencing genuine lender competition. The rent growth story has moderated nationally, with coastal markets stabilizing after supply-driven softness in 2025, but fundamental demand indicators remain constructive. New supply completions peaked nationally in late 2025 and delivery pipelines have contracted, which will support vacancy and rent performance heading into 2027. Affordable and workforce housing continues to attract premium pricing from mission-driven lenders, life companies with social mandate mandates, and agency execution. Value-add deals require genuine business plan credibility; lenders are stress-testing renovation timelines and re-leasing assumptions carefully.

Industrial

Industrial cap rates have stabilized in the 5.0% to 5.75% range nationally, with infill last-mile assets in Southern California, Northern New Jersey, and the Inland Empire trading at the tighter end. Vacancy has ticked up modestly from historic lows as the post-pandemic e-commerce wave was absorbed, but net absorption remains positive in most primary markets. Lenders are comfortable with industrial collateral up to 65% LTV at current spreads. Sale-leaseback industrial transactions have been active this summer, providing acquisition financing opportunities for buyers willing to underwrite single-tenant credit exposure.

Office

The stratification between Class A trophy and everything else has never been sharper. Class A, amenity-rich, sub-100,000-square-foot buildings with in-place leases to creditworthy tenants in live-work-play locations are financeable, though the lender universe is smaller than it was. Class B and suburban office continues to face significant headwinds, and lenders across all channels have either priced the risk out of reach or declined to quote entirely. Conversion opportunities, particularly residential adaptive reuse, are generating capital markets interest in the right jurisdictions, though entitlement complexity in California continues to lengthen timelines.

Retail

Grocery-anchored and necessity-based retail is the clear capital market darling within the retail sector. Occupancy rates at well-located community and neighborhood centers have improved materially, and lender appetite has followed. Single-tenant net lease backed by investment-grade credits continues to price tightly with life companies and CMBS. Lifestyle and power centers are receiving financing, but with more conservative LTV assumptions and closer scrutiny of anchor lease expiration schedules. Unanchored strip retail in secondary and tertiary markets remains difficult to finance through institutional channels.

Hospitality

Hotel financing has improved in 2026, driven by continued RevPAR growth in leisure destinations and urban core markets that completed their post-pandemic recovery. Branded select-service and extended-stay assets with strong flags are the most financeable, with debt funds and select CMBS platforms leading the market. Full-service hotel financing remains relationship-driven and is concentrated in a narrower lender set. Minimum loan sizes for hospitality bridge and permanent financing through institutional channels typically start at $5 million, with most institutional appetite above $10 million.

Specialty: Self-Storage, Data Centers, Medical Office, Senior Living

Self-storage fundamentals have normalized after peak demand years, but well-located urban infill assets are still generating strong lender interest. Data center financing has become a specialized institutional market of its own, with lenders underwriting power access and lease structure as primary credit variables. Medical office and healthcare real estate continues to benefit from demographic demand tailwinds and is commanding life company and agency interest where the real estate quality supports it. Senior living, particularly assisted living and memory care, is experiencing a capital availability improvement relative to 2024 as occupancy recovery across the sector has been sustained for multiple consecutive quarters.

Regulatory and Policy Watch

California continues to be the most consequential regulatory environment for CRE capital deployment in the country. Several policy items are directly affecting deal timelines and capital structure decisions in the market Commercial Lending Solutions serves most actively.

Los Angeles Measure ULA, the so-called Mansion Tax, continues to impose a 4% transfer tax on properties transferring above $5 million and a 5.5% tax on transfers above $10 million. The tax has materially affected transaction velocity on larger assets in the city and continues to factor directly into hold-versus-sell analyses for owners of qualifying assets. Court challenges to the measure have not produced a resolution that borrowers can rely on, and sponsors should be modeling the tax into any disposition calculus on LA city assets.

AB 2011 and SB 9 remain important tools for multifamily developers in California, enabling ministerial approval pathways on commercially zoned and single-family zoned sites respectively. The practical utilization rate of both programs is still lower than legislative intent would suggest, partly due to local implementation inconsistency and partly due to the financial feasibility challenge created by construction costs in the current environment. Executive Directive 1 (ED1) in the City of Los Angeles, which streamlines 100% affordable residential projects, continues to generate significant pipeline activity, and Commercial Lending Solutions has seen multiple ED1-related financing requests move through our desk in recent months.

At the federal level, FHA multifamily programs, particularly the 221(d)(4) construction-to-permanent product, remain a powerful tool for affordable and workforce housing developers despite processing timelines that can run 12 to 18 months. GSE reform chatter has picked up in Washington through the summer, with several legislative proposals in early committee stages, but no structural change to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac operations appears imminent for 2026 transaction purposes. Agency allocations for the full year remain intact.

What We Are Seeing on Active Deals

Several observations from Commercial Lending Solutions' current active pipeline are worth sharing in generalized form, as they reflect broader market conditions rather than isolated circumstances.

On a garden-style multifamily acquisition in the San Fernando Valley at roughly $18 million, we are seeing six to eight term sheets from agency, life company, and bank lenders, with the tightest pricing coming from life company on a 10-year fixed execution. The borrower had initially assumed a floating-rate bridge execution would be necessary given light value-add components; agency flexibility on a modest renovation escrow structure made fixed-rate permanent execution viable from day one.

On a $35 million industrial refinance in the Inland Empire, the sponsor is taking proceeds off a significant equity gain from basis established in 2019. Conduit execution is leading the field, with several CMBS platforms eager to underwrite the long-term lease to a creditworthy tenant. Life company quotes came in slightly tighter but with prepayment constraints that do not work for the sponsor's business plan.

A construction request for a mixed-use multifamily project in Downtown Los Angeles is illustrating the ongoing complexity of that submarket. Debt fund and bank quotes are available, but the gap between loan-to-cost available and total project cost is requiring a preferred equity solution in the $7 million range. We are actively working the stack to close that gap without materially impairing the sponsor's return profile.

In Phoenix, a $22 million refinance of a Class B multifamily asset with stabilized occupancy is drawing competitive interest from regional bank, agency, and one life company. The Phoenix market's supply deliveries have been elevated, and lenders are applying modest discounts to rental rate assumptions relative to current in-place rents; something that sponsors in oversupplied Sun Belt markets should anticipate and build into loan proceeds expectations.

Month Ahead Outlook

First, watch the September FOMC meeting closely. Fed language has been evolving toward a November cut, but any material softening in August employment data or CPI could accelerate that timeline. A confirmed rate cut signal before Labor Day would be a meaningful positive catalyst for CRE transaction volume and loan request flow heading into Q4. Sponsors with assets ready to transact should be positioned with lender relationships warm and materials current so they can move quickly if the window sharpens.

Second, CMBS issuance volume is on pace for a strong second half. The conduit market is competitive and liquid right now, and that window can narrow quickly if credit spreads widen on broader volatility. Sponsors with larger non-recourse, fixed-rate needs above $10 million should be actively exploring conduit execution in August and September rather than waiting for Q4, when deal flow typically compresses and lender attention becomes scarcer.

Third, the California legislative session closes in mid-September, and several bills affecting ADU rights, inclusionary housing percentages, and construction defect liability are in final stages. Sponsors with projects in permitting in California should be monitoring the outcome of that session, as the regulatory environment for residential development continues to evolve in ways that have direct capital structure implications.

Takeaways for Sponsors and Borrowers

  • The fixed-rate window is real and worth capturing now. Life company and conduit executions are producing all-in rates that represent genuine long-term value relative to where rates could go in either direction. Sponsors with stable, income-producing assets should not defer rate lock decisions without a specific reason.
  • Lender appetite is highly bifurcated by asset quality. The difference in execution between a well-documented, stabilized asset and a thin-margin value-add story is larger than it has been in years. Invest in your deal materials; lenders are reading every line of the rent roll and the operating statement.
  • Construction financing requires a clear takeout narrative. Whether the permanent exit is agency, conduit, life company, or a sale, lenders providing construction capital want a specific and underwritten answer to that question before they commit. Deals that cannot articulate the exit are losing execution certainty and paying for it in rate.
  • Sun Belt multifamily sponsors should expect lenders to discount current rental rates by 5% to 10% in markets with elevated supply. Phoenix, Austin, Dallas, and Charlotte are the markets where this haircut is most consistently applied right now. Build it into your proceeds expectations before going to market.
  • The California policy environment rewards sponsors who understand the tools. ED1, AB 2011, and the welfare exemption for affordable projects are genuine advantages for developers who can structure into them. Projects that access these entitlement pathways are attracting capital on terms that pencil for affordable and mixed-income development in markets where market-rate construction cannot.
  • Mezz and preferred equity are available for well-structured gap positions. If your senior leverage does not meet your capital need, a $5 million to $15 million preferred equity position from a specialty finance platform is often more efficient than renegotiating senior terms. Commercial Lending Solutions structures these hybrid stack solutions regularly.
