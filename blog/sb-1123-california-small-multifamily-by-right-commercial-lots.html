SB 1123 Explained: By-Right Multifamily Approval on Commercial Lots Near Transit

By Trevor Damyan May 15, 2026

California's SB 1123 is now in effect, and it quietly eliminates the single biggest obstacle small developers face: discretionary approval. If you hold a commercially zoned lot near transit in Los Angeles or anywhere in California, you may be sitting on a by-right multifamily opportunity with no hearings, no CEQA review, and a ministerial approval process that moves in weeks, not years. Here is what you need to know to act on it.

What SB 1123 Actually Does

Signed into law in September 2024 and effective January 1, 2025, SB 1123 grants ministerial, by-right approval to qualifying small multifamily residential projects. That means the local planning department must approve a qualifying project administratively, without any public hearing, planning commission vote, or discretionary review of any kind.

CEQA review is also eliminated for qualifying projects. That is not a minor footnote. CEQA has historically been the most commonly weaponized tool used to delay or kill infill development in California, often by competing developers or neighborhood opposition groups with no legitimate environmental grievance. SB 1123 removes that lever entirely for projects that meet the statute's criteria.

The practical result: a developer who would previously have spent 18 to 36 months navigating entitlements, hearings, and environmental review can now receive approval administratively, in a matter of weeks, and move directly into permitting and construction financing.

Which Projects Qualify Under SB 1123

The statute sets four core eligibility criteria. All four must be satisfied for a project to receive ministerial approval.

  • Zoning: The parcel must be commercially zoned, or residentially zoned in a manner that allows multi-unit housing. Vacant commercial lots along retail corridors are a primary target for this law.
  • Unit Count: The project must propose between 2 and 10 residential units. Projects above 10 units do not qualify and remain subject to standard discretionary review.
  • Transit Proximity: The site must be located within one-half mile of a major transit stop or a high-quality transit corridor. In Los Angeles, this captures a substantial portion of the urban core, including neighborhoods like Van Nuys, Koreatown, West Adams, and Highland Park, among many others.
  • No Protected Demolition: The project cannot involve the demolition of affordable housing, rent-controlled units, or currently occupied housing. Vacant commercial structures and surface parking lots are generally unaffected by this restriction.

If your site checks all four boxes, you have a by-right project. That distinction has enormous value in your capital stack.

How to Underwrite an SB 1123 Deal

The underwriting calculus on a by-right project is fundamentally different from a discretionary project, and lenders know it. When entitlement risk is removed from the equation, the remaining risks are construction execution, market absorption, and exit. That is a profile most construction lenders are comfortable with, particularly on small infill deals in supply-constrained urban markets.

Start your underwriting with the following inputs specific to SB 1123 projects:

  • Land basis: Commercially zoned lots that have not yet been repriced for residential use often carry favorable basis relative to their as-completed multifamily value. That spread is where your equity return lives.
  • Unit mix and size: SB 1123 does not mandate affordable set-asides, though layering in local density bonus incentives on top of this statute may unlock additional units or height in some jurisdictions. Model your unit mix against current rent comparables in the submarket.
  • Hard cost assumptions: Small infill projects in the 2 to 10 unit range typically run between $200 and $350 per square foot in construction costs depending on finish level and site conditions. Confirm your contractor budget against current subcontractor pricing before going to a lender.
  • Timeline compression: The elimination of discretionary review and CEQA materially shortens your pre-construction timeline. Model 60 to 120 days from land close to construction start rather than the 18 to 36 months a discretionary project would require.
  • Exit strategy: These projects exit via either a refinance into a permanent loan or an outright sale. In transit-adjacent urban infill locations, both paths are viable. Know which one your lender is underwriting to before you close your construction loan.

Financing an SB 1123 Project: Bridge, Construction, and Permanent

The capital stack on a small by-right multifamily project typically involves two to three financing events depending on the developer's land position and timeline.

Bridge or Land Loan: If you are acquiring a commercial lot to execute an SB 1123 project, a private bridge lender or a debt fund will typically advance 60 to 70 percent of the land purchase price. These loans are short term, usually 12 to 18 months, and are designed to carry you through the ministerial approval and permitting phase. Because SB 1123 eliminates entitlement risk, some lenders are willing to underwrite a higher loan-to-cost on the land position than they would on a discretionary project.

Construction Loan: Once permits are in hand, a regional bank or a private construction lender will advance funds against the construction budget, typically at 65 to 75 percent of total project cost. The elimination of CEQA and discretionary review meaningfully improves a lender's comfort with a fast-track approval, and experienced construction lenders familiar with SB 1123 projects can close in 30 days on a well-structured deal. Draws are tied to construction milestones and inspected by the lender's inspector.

Permanent Financing: At stabilization, typically defined as 90 percent occupancy for 90 days, the project is eligible for a permanent loan from a regional bank, a credit union, or in some cases a life insurance company for larger loan amounts. Debt service coverage and loan-to-value at stabilization drive the permanent loan sizing. Model your permanent debt conservatively using today's cap rates rather than projecting compression.

Approval Timeline Under SB 1123

One of the most important underwriting variables on any development deal is time, because time is money in a leveraged real estate transaction. Here is a realistic timeline for an SB 1123 project from site identification to construction start:

  • Weeks 1 to 4: Site due diligence, zoning confirmation, transit proximity verification, and preliminary design.
  • Weeks 4 to 8: Submission of ministerial approval application to the local planning department.
  • Weeks 8 to 12: Administrative review and approval. The statute requires a ministerial determination, which most jurisdictions are processing within this window for compliant applications.
  • Weeks 12 to 20: Building permit application and plan check. This remains the primary remaining timeline variable, as building departments are not subject to the same ministerial mandate.
  • Week 20 onward: Construction start, assuming financing is in place.

Compare that to a discretionary project, where the entitlement phase alone can consume two to three years. That compression is real, it is bankable, and it is why SB 1123 sites are beginning to attract investor attention from groups that previously avoided small California infill deals.

The CLS CRE Perspective

We work with developers across the California capital markets, and the deals that are getting done right now share a common thread: reduced entitlement risk. SB 1123 creates a category of project that lenders can underwrite with confidence because the political and procedural risk has been substantially removed by statute.

That said, execution still matters. A by-right approval does not guarantee a well-structured deal. Lenders will still scrutinize your construction budget, your contractor track record, your cost basis, and your exit assumptions. The projects that close financing quickly are the ones where the developer has done the work to present a clean, lender-ready package before the first call.
