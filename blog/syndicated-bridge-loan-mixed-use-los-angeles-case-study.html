Case Study: $2.685M Syndicated Bridge Loan for Two Los Angeles Properties

By Trevor Damyan July 28, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $2,685,000 syndicated loan against two properties in Los Angeles, California, combining a $2,255,000 senior participation from a private lending fund with a $430,000 direct co-lending position to fully fund the borrower's request.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal brought us a borrower who needed a combined $2,685,000 financed against two properties in the Los Angeles area at once. Rather than sizing the request to whatever a single lender's capacity or risk appetite could absorb, the goal was to get the full amount funded through one coordinated closing.

Getting a private lending fund and a direct co-lender to close together against two properties in a single escrow meant coordinating two separate capital sources onto one closing timeline rather than sequencing two independent loans.

Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in bridge loans and syndicated private money financing for owners of multiple properties across Los Angeles, CA and the surrounding Southern California market.

What Made It Hard

  • Syndicating the loan across two capital sources. With a $2,255,000 senior participation and a $430,000 co-lending piece needed to reach the full request, both pieces had to close simultaneously through the same escrow rather than as two separate transactions.
  • Financing two properties under one coordinated closing. Cross-collateralizing the borrower's equity across two separate Los Angeles properties added coordination on title, escrow, and lien positioning across both assets at once.
  • Moving on private-money timing. With the loan structured as private financing rather than a bank underwrite, the deal had to move through escrow and fund on a compressed timeline typical of private capital.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE #02244836), placed the syndicated loan combining a private lending fund's senior position with a direct co-lending participation, closing through escrow in early 2024:

  • $2,255,000 funded as the senior participation from the private lending fund
  • $430,000 funded as a direct co-lending participation to complete the full request
  • $2,685,000 total, secured against two properties in Los Angeles, closed through a single coordinated escrow
  • Broker fee paid directly at the close of escrow, wired from loan proceeds per the standard broker demand process

The loan closed with both capital sources funding together, giving the borrower the full amount requested against both properties in one transaction rather than piecing together separate financings.

"No single lender had to stretch on this one. Splitting it between a senior fund and a co-lender let both sides stay inside their comfort zone, and the borrower still got the full amount in one closing."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

Structuring the loan as a syndicated participation, rather than trying to force one lender to fund the entire request, is what let this deal close at the size the borrower actually needed. Coordinating both capital sources onto the same escrow timeline, instead of sequencing two separate closings, kept the transaction moving on private-money speed rather than stretching it out.

Cross-collateralizing both properties under a single loan gave the lending group comfortable collateral coverage across the combined request, which is what made a syndicated structure workable in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Syndicating a loan lets a larger private fund take the majority position while a second co-lender fills the remaining balance, allowing a full loan amount to close even when a single fund's allocation limits or risk appetite cap its participation below the total request. This loan combined a $2,255,000 senior participation with a $430,000 co-lending position to reach the full $2,685,000 request.
Combining two properties under a single loan lets a borrower cross-collateralize their equity across both assets, often unlocking more proceeds or better pricing than financing each property separately would allow. This loan closed against two Los Angeles properties simultaneously.
A broker demand is the formal invoice a mortgage broker submits to escrow instructing the fee due for arranging the loan to be wired directly from loan proceeds at closing, ensuring the broker is paid concurrently with funding. This transaction closed through escrow with the broker's fee wired directly at the close of escrow.
Private lenders underwrite primarily against real estate equity and a clear payoff plan rather than a full institutional credit and cash flow review, letting them close complex or multi-property requests in a fraction of the time a bank would need. This syndicated loan closed through escrow within about two weeks of the broker demand being issued.
