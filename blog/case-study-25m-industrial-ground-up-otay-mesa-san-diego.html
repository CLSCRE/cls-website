Case Study

$25,000,000 Ground-Up Industrial (175,000 SF) in Otay Mesa, San Diego, CA

By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Construction Loan

The Deal

Our client, an experienced industrial developer, secured $25 million in construction financing for a 175,000 square foot speculative industrial warehouse in Otay Mesa, San Diego. The ground-up development targeted the high-demand cross-border logistics market, capitalizing on Otay Mesa's strategic position as the primary industrial corridor serving US-Mexico trade flows.

The capital stack consisted of a single construction loan at 55% loan-to-cost, requiring $20.5 million in developer equity. The financing structure included an 18-month construction period with a 12-month lease-up extension, providing adequate runway for both construction completion and tenant stabilization in a speculative development scenario.

The Challenge

Speculative industrial construction financing presents significantly higher risk than build-to-suit transactions. Without pre-committed tenants, lenders face dual completion and lease-up risk, making many institutions reluctant to finance spec industrial projects regardless of market fundamentals.

The 175,000 square foot building size created additional complexity. While large enough to attract institutional tenants requiring substantial warehouse space, the building could also subdivide into smaller blocks for multiple tenants, creating flexibility but uncertainty around ultimate tenant mix and lease rates.

Despite Otay Mesa's sub-2% industrial vacancy rate and consistent demand from cross-border logistics operators, many lenders lacked market knowledge to properly underwrite the location's unique dynamics. The border-adjacent industrial market operates differently from traditional inland distribution centers, requiring lender familiarity with cross-border trade patterns and tenant profiles.

The Solution

We targeted national banks with active industrial lending programs and specific experience in border market dynamics. Rather than approaching construction lenders who viewed the deal as generic spec industrial, we focused on institutions that understood Otay Mesa's role in US-Mexico supply chain logistics.

The winning lender recognized that Otay Mesa's proximity to the Otay Mesa Port of Entry creates structural demand advantages that mitigate typical spec development risks. Cross-border logistics companies require warehouse space within specific proximity to border crossings, limiting their location options and creating inelastic demand.

We structured the deal with conservative leverage at 55% LTC, providing substantial equity cushion while maintaining attractive returns for the developer. The 30-month total loan term (18 months construction plus 12-month mini-perm) aligned with realistic construction timelines while providing adequate lease-up runway.

The financing included standard construction loan mechanics: monthly interest payments during construction, third-party cost monitoring, and completion guarantees from the developer principals. Interest rate was fixed for the construction period with a floating rate during the lease-up extension.

The Outcome

Construction proceeded on schedule with no significant delays or cost overruns. The developer's market thesis proved correct as leasing activity began during the construction phase, with multiple cross-border logistics tenants expressing interest before building completion.

By construction completion, the building achieved 60% pre-leasing across two tenants: a major freight forwarder took 75,000 square feet and a third-party logistics provider committed to 30,000 square feet. Both tenants specifically required Otay Mesa locations due to their cross-border operations.

The successful pre-leasing validated both the developer's market knowledge and our financing strategy. By partnering with a lender that understood Otay Mesa's unique market dynamics rather than treating it as generic spec industrial, we secured financing terms that allowed the project to move forward profitably.

The remaining 70,000 square feet of space continued attracting interest from additional cross-border logistics companies, positioning the project for full stabilization well within the loan's lease-up period. The transaction demonstrated how proper lender selection and market positioning can overcome the inherent challenges in speculative industrial construction financing.
