Childcare and Daycare NNN Financing: Underwriting, Cap Rates, and Lender Programs 2026

Childcare NNN Financing in 2026: A Primer for CRE Investors and 1031 Exchange Buyers

The childcare sector has emerged as one of the most resilient and attractive net lease investment categories in commercial real estate. Unlike traditional retail or quick-service restaurant properties, childcare centers offer long lease terms, demographic tailwinds driven by millennial parents entering their peak earning years, and a mission-critical service model that performs through economic cycles. For 1031 exchange buyers and real estate investors seeking stable, passive income with institutional-grade financing, childcare NNN properties represent a compelling opportunity in 2026.

This article provides a comprehensive overview of childcare net lease investing, financing programs available in 2026, underwriting criteria, and how childcare stacks up against other net lease property types.

What Makes Childcare NNN Unique

Childcare centers are single-tenant net lease investments typically located in suburban markets on 0.5 to 1.5 acre sites. Most properties range from 6,000 to 12,000 square feet and are either purpose-built for childcare operations or thoughtfully converted from existing structures. What sets childcare apart from other net lease categories is the fundamental, non-discretionary nature of the service.

Dual-income households depend on reliable childcare to maintain employment. Unlike a restaurant or retail tenant that can be replaced if the operator fails, childcare is deeply embedded in family logistics and community infrastructure. This structural demand driver translates into lower closure risk during economic downturns and higher tenant retention compared to retail or hospitality tenants.

Lease terms for childcare NNN properties typically range from 15 to 20 years on initial periods, with renewal options extending into year 30 or beyond. Most leases are structured as absolute NNN, meaning the tenant (operator) pays property taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance. Some leases are modified gross, where the landlord absorbs roof and major HVAC replacement; lenders discount modified gross leases accordingly.

The major operators dominating this sector include:

  • KinderCare Learning Companies: The largest publicly traded childcare operator, with thousands of centers nationwide and a mix of company-operated and franchised locations.
  • Bright Horizons Family Solutions (NYSE: BFAM): Focuses on corporate-sponsored childcare and employer-partnered centers; institutional-grade credit and high visibility.
  • Learning Experience: Private equity backed, rapidly expanding franchise model; strong growth trajectory.
  • Primrose Schools: Franchise system with premium positioning; regional and local presence across U.S.
  • La Petite Academy: Regional operator with a smaller footprint; typically stronger local market presence than national recognition.

Childcare Cap Rates by Operator Tier

Cap rate compression has accelerated in the childcare sector, driven by strong investor demand and limited institutional supply. In 2026, pricing varies significantly by operator tier and market conditions.

Tier 1 Operators (Bright Horizons, KinderCare): Properties leased to these blue-chip operators trade at 5.25 to 6.00% cap rates in suburban markets. New construction childcare for Tier 1 operators in strong suburban submarkets commands the tightest cap rates (5.25 to 5.75%). These operators benefit from institutional investor recognition, investment-grade or near-investment-grade credit, and predictable operating models.

Tier 2 Operators (Learning Experience, Primrose Schools): Cap rates range from 6.00 to 6.75%. These operators are well-capitalized and growing rapidly but lack the public market profile of Tier 1. Lenders underwrite corporate credit more carefully and may apply tighter debt service coverage ratios.

Tier 3 Operators (Regional and Local Chains): Cap rates typically fall between 6.50 and 7.50%. These smaller operators may be privately held, have limited operating history, or serve a single state or region. Lenders require personal guaranties from ownership and scrutinize balance sheets closely.

Urban vs. Suburban Positioning: Corporate-campus childcare operated by Bright Horizons at major employer sites (Google, Amazon, hospitals, universities) may trade differently than suburban standalone centers due to the unique corporate guarantee and strategic value to the employer. However, most childcare NNN transactions involve suburban standalone properties, which are more liquid and easier to finance.

Lender Programs for Childcare NNN

The childcare sector has attracted significant lender interest, expanding the menu of financing options available in 2026. Below is a summary of the major lending programs currently active:

Bank Dedicated Net Lease Programs: National banks with dedicated net lease divisions offer programs for childcare NNN from $750,000 to $8 million. Terms typically include floating rates (CMT plus 190 to 260 basis points), 5-year terms, 25-year amortization, and recourse. These programs are available for Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators with strong lease terms and typically require minimum debt service coverage ratios of 1.25x.

CMBS Conduit Lending: Commercial mortgage-backed securities conduits actively finance childcare NNN properties from $5 million to $50 million and larger. CMBS loans are fixed-rate, 10-year terms, 30-year amortization, and non-recourse. CMBS is ideal for portfolio financing (5+ unit childcare portfolios) and large 1031 exchanges where investors seek predictable long-term debt service.

Life Company Lenders: Insurance company lenders offer non-recourse financing for childcare NNN at $5 million and above, typically 10-year fixed rate, 30-year amortization. Life companies are conservative underwriters and prefer Bright Horizons or KinderCare with 12 or more years remaining on the lease term. Loan sizing and pricing may be tighter than bank or CMBS programs.

Community and Regional Banks: Local and regional banks are actively financing childcare NNN in the $1 million to $4 million range, particularly within their Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) footprint. These lenders often offer favorable rates and faster approval timelines compared to national programs, though loans are typically recourse.

SBA 7(a) Financing: The SBA loan program is available for owner-operators of childcare businesses seeking to finance both real estate and working capital. SBA financing is not typical for passive NNN investors and requires the borrower to maintain an active operational role.

Underwriting Childcare: Lease Abstract, Enrollment, and Licensing

Lenders evaluate childcare NNN properties through a lens that balances real estate fundamentals with operational metrics unique to the childcare industry. Key underwriting considerations include:

Lease Abstract Review: The lease document is paramount. Lenders examine whether the lease is absolute NNN (tenant pays all property costs) or modified gross (landlord absorbs roof, major HVAC). Absolute NNN leases command higher leverage and tighter pricing. Renewal option structures, rental escalators, and tenant improvement responsibility are carefully reviewed. Leases with 10 or more years remaining strongly preferred; below 7 years significantly limits lender appetite.

Licensed Capacity and Enrollment: Childcare properties are licensed by state authorities for a maximum number of children (typically 75 to 150 infants, toddlers, and preschoolers depending on facility size). Lenders prefer to see enrollment at 75% or higher of licensed capacity. Properties operating below 60% enrollment may face lender hesitation or reduced loan amounts.

State Licensing and Regulatory Compliance: Childcare is heavily regulated at the state level. Lenders review licensing history, any violations, staff-to-child ratios, facility inspections, and background check compliance. A facility with a history of licensing violations or operating under a consent order carries elevated risk. Clean licensing history is a prerequisite for institutional financing.

Building Age and Condition: Older buildings (pre-2000) may have layout inefficiencies for modern childcare operations (limited bathrooms, poor sight lines, outdoor play areas not compliant with current standards). Properties built within the last 15 years or recently renovated are preferred. Phase I and Phase II environmental assessments are standard, particularly for conversions from other uses.

Operating History: Properties with 3 or more years of operator P&L statements and tax returns are underwritten more favorably. New operators entering an existing facility require stronger credit support (personal guaranty, letter of credit) or reduced leverage.

Corporate-Campus vs. Standalone Childcare NNN

Two distinct childcare NNN market segments exist: corporate-campus childcare and standalone suburban centers.

Corporate-Campus Childcare: Bright Horizons is the dominant player, operating on-site childcare centers at major employers such as Amazon, Google, hospitals, and universities. These properties are often leased directly from the employer or through a long-term ground lease. Corporate-campus childcare NNN transactions are rare but highly valued; the employer guarantee in addition to the operator's lease covenant significantly reduces credit risk. These transactions may command cap rates at the tighter end of the spectrum (5.25 to 5.50%).

Standalone Suburban Childcare: Properties located on dedicated land in suburban markets with KinderCare, Learning Experience, Primrose Schools, or other independent operators represent the majority of NNN childcare transactions. These properties are more common, easier to finance, and offer more liquidity to investors. Cap rates typically range from 5.75% to 6.75% for Tier 1 and Tier 2 operators.

Single-Location Regional Operators: Smaller regional or local chains may operate a single high-performing center. Lenders require personal guaranties from ownership, 3+ years of operating financial statements, and corporate tax returns. These transactions are often overlooked by large investors but can offer attractive yields (6.50% to 7.00%) for investors comfortable with unrated operator credit.

Childcare NNN vs. QSR and Pharmacy: Investment Comparison

How does childcare NNN stack up against other net lease staples such as quick-service restaurants and pharmacies?

Advantages vs. QSR: Childcare leases typically run 15 to 20 years versus 10 to 15 for QSR, providing longer cash flow visibility. Childcare locations are in suburban residential markets with lower foot traffic risk compared to quick-service restaurants. Purpose-built childcare facilities have specialized layouts not easily adaptable to other uses; however, this also reduces versatility. Childcare is mission-critical, not discretionary, resulting in lower dark risk than QSR during economic recessions.

Disadvantages vs. QSR: Childcare operators have lower brand recognition than major QSR chains (McDonald's, Subway, Chipotle). The buyer pool for childcare NNN is smaller, reducing liquidity. Childcare properties are specialized; conversion to another use is costly and complicated.

Advantages vs. Pharmacy: Childcare enrollment is growing as millennial parents enter peak earning years; demographic tailwinds are favorable. Childcare leases are often newer and longer (15 to 20 years) than pharmacy leases, which may have shorter remaining terms due to consolidation in the pharmacy sector.

Disadvantages vs. Pharmacy: Walgreens and CVS have BBB or higher credit ratings; most childcare operators are unrated or below investment-grade. Pharmacy real estate has deeper institutional liquidity and lower cap rates. Pharmacy has longer operating history as a real estate asset class with more established underwriting standards.

Best Use Case for Childcare NNN: Childcare NNN is most attractive for 1031 exchange buyers seeking long-term passive income (15 to 20 year lease terms) in suburban markets with growing families and dual-income households.
