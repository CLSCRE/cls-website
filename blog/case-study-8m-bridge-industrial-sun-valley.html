$8M Industrial Acquisition in Sun Valley, Los Angeles, CA

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan·April 05, 2026·Bridge Loan

The Deal

A seasoned real estate investment group identified a compelling value-add opportunity in Sun Valley, Los Angeles. The 40,000 square foot multi-tenant industrial property was generating below-market rents across all tenant spaces, with every lease operating on month-to-month terms. The acquisition price of $8 million represented significant upside potential, as market analysis indicated rents could be increased by approximately 30% once tenants were secured on longer-term agreements. The investment strategy was straightforward but required precise execution. The buyers planned to acquire the property using bridge financing, then immediately begin negotiations with existing tenants to secure five-year lease commitments at current market rates. Once stabilized with long-term leases in place, the property would qualify for permanent financing at more favorable terms, allowing the investors to refinance out of the higher-cost bridge loan. The Sun Valley submarket had been experiencing steady industrial demand, driven by its proximity to major transportation corridors and the ongoing expansion of e-commerce fulfillment operations throughout the San Fernando Valley. The property's strategic location and the potential for immediate income enhancement made it an attractive acquisition target.

The Challenge

Traditional bank financing was not viable for this transaction due to several factors that created complexity for conventional lenders. The month-to-month lease structure meant the property lacked the income stability that permanent lenders typically require for acquisition financing. Banks viewed the tenant arrangements as creating unacceptable cash flow risk, even though the tenants had been operating in the space for multiple years. The compressed timeline presented additional challenges. The purchase contract required a quick close to beat competing offers, leaving insufficient time for the lengthy underwriting process associated with traditional commercial mortgages. The sellers had received multiple offers and were prioritizing buyers who could demonstrate proof of funds and execute rapidly. Furthermore, the business plan relied on implementing rent increases immediately after closing, which created a transitional period of uncertainty that traditional lenders were unwilling to underwrite. The gap between current income and projected stabilized income represented exactly the type of value-add scenario where bridge financing becomes essential. The borrowers needed a lending solution that could accommodate the property's current lease structure while recognizing the realistic potential for income enhancement based on market conditions and the tenants' established operations at the location.

The Solution

Commercial Lending Solutions structured an $8 million bridge loan through a specialized debt fund that understood the value-add industrial market. The financing was structured at 70% loan-to-value ratio, providing the borrowers with sufficient leverage while maintaining conservative underwriting standards for the lender. The interest rate was priced at SOFR plus 300 basis points, reflecting the bridge loan's temporary nature and the property's transitional income profile. This rate structure offered predictable payments during the stabilization period while remaining competitive within the current market environment. The 18-month term provided adequate runway for the borrowers to execute their business plan without feeling rushed through the tenant negotiation process. This timeframe allowed for systematic lease renewals with each tenant, ensuring that rent increases could be implemented thoughtfully rather than forcing tenants into untenable situations that might result in vacancies. The debt fund's underwriting focused on the borrowers' track record with similar industrial properties and the realistic potential for rent growth based on comparable properties in the submarket. Rather than relying solely on existing cash flow, the lender evaluated the business plan's feasibility and the borrowers' capability to execute the value-add strategy. The loan structure included interest-only payments during the bridge period, maximizing the borrowers' cash flow during the lease-up phase and providing flexibility to reinvest in tenant improvements or other property enhancements as needed.

The Outcome

The bridge financing enabled a successful acquisition that closed within 30 days of contract execution. The borrowers immediately began implementing their business plan, approaching each tenant with market-rate lease proposals backed by comprehensive market analysis demonstrating the reasonableness of the proposed increases. Within six months, 85% of the tenants had signed five-year lease agreements at the targeted rental rates, representing the projected 30% increase over previous month-to-month rates. The remaining tenants were in active lease negotiations, with completion expected before the one-year anniversary of the acquisition. The stabilized lease structure transformed the property's financing profile, making it eligible for permanent financing at rates significantly below the bridge loan cost. The borrowers initiated the refinancing process at month 12, positioning themselves to complete the permanent financing well within the 18-month bridge term. The successful execution of this value-add strategy demonstrated how bridge financing can unlock opportunities that traditional lending cannot accommodate, providing investors with the flexibility needed to capitalize on market inefficiencies in the industrial real estate sector.
