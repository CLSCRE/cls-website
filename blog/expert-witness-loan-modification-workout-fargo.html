Expert Witness: Commercial Loan Modification Standards in Fargo, ND

By Trevor Damyan· June 25, 2026 ·Loan Modification and Workout Dispute

The Fargo Market Context

Fargo, North Dakota sits at the intersection of regional agricultural commerce, a growing technology and healthcare employment base, and one of the most stable multifamily markets in the northern plains. For a loan modification and workout dispute, that stability cuts both ways. On one hand, Fargo's commercial real estate fundamentals have remained relatively resilient through rate cycles that decimated coastal markets. On the other hand, lenders operating in Fargo often apply workout standards developed for larger metros, and when those standards are misapplied to a market with Fargo's specific demand drivers and income characteristics, disputes arise.

The Fargo-Moorhead metro has seen consistent population and employment growth anchored by North Dakota State University, Sanford Health, Essentia Health, and a technology sector that has drawn remote and regional talent throughout the post-pandemic period. Industrial vacancy in the metro has run in the low single digits, reflecting sustained demand from agricultural supply chain users, last-mile distribution, and manufacturing support tenants. Multifamily fundamentals have been strong by any reasonable measure: effective rents have increased steadily, vacancy has remained well below national averages, and new supply, while active, has been absorbed at a pace consistent with continued rent growth. Retail has bifurcated, with well-located neighborhood and necessity-anchored product outperforming, while secondary strip and power center assets have faced the same structural headwinds present nationally. Office in Fargo reflects a smaller-market dynamic where locally-anchored tenants, professional services, and medical office uses have held firmer than in major coastal CBDs, but suburban office leasing velocity remains measured.

When a workout or modification dispute emerges in this market, the lender's conduct must be evaluated against Fargo's actual conditions, not against proxy data from Minneapolis or national aggregates. A lender who applies a generic workout framework without accounting for Fargo's specific cap rate environment, its limited institutional transaction volume, and its reliance on regional and community banking relationships is not meeting the standard of care that a sophisticated commercial mortgage lender should apply. That disconnect is often where our expert analysis begins.

The Engagement

Commercial Lending Solutions was retained by litigation counsel representing a borrower in a dispute involving a commercial real estate loan modification and subsequent enforcement action. The underlying asset was a mixed-use income-producing property in the Fargo submarket, combining retail and multifamily components. The borrower had sought a loan modification following a period of income disruption caused by tenant turnover and a temporary revenue gap during re-tenanting. The lender had initiated a workout process, entered into preliminary discussions, and ultimately imposed modification terms that the borrower contended were commercially unreasonable given the property's stabilized cash flow trajectory and the prevailing lender market conditions at the time of the modification.

Counsel retained CLS CRE to address four core questions. First, whether the lender's conduct during the modification process conformed to industry standards for commercial real estate workouts, including the adequacy of communication, the completeness of underwriting review, and the reasonableness of the timeline imposed. Second, whether the modification terms offered, specifically the interest rate adjustment, the amortization change, and the reserve requirements imposed, were commercially reasonable given the property's cash flow and the Fargo market at the time. Third, what a qualified lender operating in the Fargo market at that time would have offered a similarly situated borrower with comparable collateral. Fourth, whether the lender's acceleration and enforcement action was consistent with industry practice given the state of the modification discussions at the time enforcement was initiated.

These are precisely the questions that fall outside the scope of a real estate appraisal. They require a practitioner who understands how lenders actually underwrite distressed and transitional assets, how workout decisions are made inside a credit department, and how the Fargo lending market would have responded to this specific borrower profile at this specific moment in the credit cycle.

The Financial Analysis

The core analytical task in a modification dispute is reconstructing the property's debt capacity at the time of the modification, then comparing that debt capacity to the terms the lender imposed. If the lender's modification terms required the borrower to carry a debt load that exceeded what any reasonable underwriting standard would support given the property's cash flow, the terms were commercially unreasonable regardless of what the original loan documents permitted.

For this engagement, we began with a stabilized net operating income analysis. The property's trailing twelve-month NOI at the time of the modification request reflected the disruption period, but forward-looking NOI based on executed leases, historical occupancy, and Fargo submarket rent data presented a materially different picture. We calculated two NOI scenarios: a trailing NOI reflecting the distressed period, and a stabilized NOI based on in-place leases plus market-rate assumptions for the vacant component. The stabilized NOI, which is the correct basis for a workout analysis on an asset with a credible re-tenanting path, was approximately $310,000 annually.

Applying the market-standard debt service coverage ratio threshold of 1.25x, which is the underwriting floor used by the overwhelming majority of regional banks, credit unions, and CMBS conduits for mixed-use assets in secondary markets, the supportable annual debt service was approximately $248,000. At the then-prevailing modification rate the lender imposed, which was a floating rate of prime plus 200 basis points during the workout period, amortized over a 20-year schedule rather than the original 25-year schedule, the modified loan's annual debt service on the outstanding balance of approximately $2.8 million was approximately $285,000. That figure exceeded the 1.25x DSCR threshold the lender's own credit policy required. In simple terms, the lender imposed modification terms that their own underwriting standards would have rejected as inadequately covered at origination.

From an LTV perspective, the analysis was equally instructive. Using a capitalization rate consistent with Fargo mixed-use transactions at that time, approximately 6.75% to 7.25% applied to stabilized NOI, the property's value ranged from approximately $4.3 million to $4.6 million. The outstanding loan balance of $2.8 million represented an LTV of 61% to 65% against stabilized value. Regional and community lenders in Fargo will routinely extend modification accommodations to borrowers with LTVs in that range because the collateral coverage is adequate and the credit risk is manageable. A lender who refuses reasonable modification terms when LTV is below 65% and the debt service gap is attributable to a temporary income disruption, not a structural impairment, is not acting in accordance with how lenders in the Fargo market actually conduct workouts.

The maximum supportable loan at 1.25x DSCR on stabilized NOI, at a market-rate restructured interest rate of 7.00% fixed on a 25-year amortization, was approximately $2.95 million. The existing balance was below that threshold. This analysis demonstrated that the property could have supported its existing debt at market-rate modification terms, and that the lender's decision to impose more punitive terms was not driven by underwriting necessity.

The Complicating Factors

Transitional Occupancy and the Trailing vs. Stabilized NOI Debate. The most significant complicating factor in this dispute was the lender's insistence on using trailing twelve-month NOI rather than stabilized NOI in evaluating the modification request. In a market like Fargo, where the tenant universe for retail and mixed-use space is thinner than in a major metro, re-tenanting timelines can run six to eighteen months even when market conditions are favorable. A lender who evaluates a modification request exclusively on trailing income during a re-tenanting gap is, in effect, treating a recoverable vacancy event as a permanent impairment. Industry practice, reflected in guidance from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation on troubled debt restructurings and loan modifications, requires lenders to consider the borrower's capacity to perform under modified terms over the loan's remaining term, not merely at the point of modification. A trailing-only analysis in a transitional Fargo asset fails that standard.

Reserve Requirements as a Disguised Paydown Demand. The modification terms included a requirement that the borrower fund a cash reserve equal to six months of debt service, approximately $142,500, within thirty days of modification execution. For a borrower whose liquidity had been stressed by the income gap that precipitated the modification request, this reserve demand was functionally equivalent to a partial paydown demand. In standard workout practice, reserve requirements of this magnitude imposed on a transitional asset are appropriate only when the debt coverage is genuinely below threshold or when there is documented credit concern beyond the immediate vacancy event. When collateral coverage is adequate and the income gap is demonstrably temporary, a reserve of this size imposed on a thirty-day timeline is inconsistent with how regional lenders in Fargo and comparable markets handle modification requests from otherwise creditworthy borrowers.

Shortened Amortization and Its Cash Flow Impact. The lender's modification terms also shortened the remaining amortization schedule from 25 years to 20 years, increasing the principal component of each payment and compressing DSCR further. Amortization acceleration in a workout context is a legitimate tool when the lender has genuine concerns about long-term collateral coverage, but it must be applied in proportion to the actual risk. When the collateral LTV is 61% to 65% on stabilized value and the income disruption is temporary, shortening amortization increases borrower distress without meaningfully improving lender protection. It is a punitive term, not a protective one, and it is inconsistent with the workout practices of regional lenders operating in markets like Fargo.

Timeline of Enforcement Relative to Modification Discussions. A procedurally significant factor was the lender's acceleration notice, which was issued while modification discussions were actively ongoing and before the borrower had been given a final written rejection of the modification proposal. In standard commercial real estate workout practice, a lender who has engaged a borrower in substantive modification discussions, including exchanging term sheets and requesting financial documentation, is expected to complete that process before initiating enforcement. Acceleration during active workout negotiations, absent a material deterioration in the borrower's position or collateral, departs from industry norms and raises questions about good faith that are squarely within the scope of expert testimony.

The Fargo Lender Market

Understanding who lends in Fargo, and on what terms, is essential to evaluating whether a lender's modification conduct was commercially reasonable. The Fargo market is served by a layered lending community with distinct characteristics at each tier.

Regional community banks and credit unions dominate the commercial mortgage landscape in Fargo for mixed-use, multifamily, and smaller commercial assets. These institutions hold loans on balance sheet, maintain active workout departments, and have strong familiarity with local market conditions. Their workout culture tends to be relationship-oriented, meaning they will typically extend accommodations to borrowers with demonstrated equity and a credible stabilization plan before pursuing enforcement. Their credit boxes generally require 1.20x to 1.25x DSCR on stabilized income, LTV below 75% to 80% for most asset types, and borrower liquidity consistent with carrying reserves without lender imposition. For mixed-use assets in the $2 million to $5 million range, community lenders are the primary source of workout flexibility in this market.

Regional banks with North Dakota and upper Midwest footprints represent the next tier, and they bring slightly more standardized credit policy to workout decisions. These lenders are more likely to apply written workout policy uniformly, but they are also accountable to OCC and Federal Reserve guidance on modified loans, which requires a documented analysis of repayment capacity under modified terms. Their modification terms for transitional assets with adequate collateral coverage tend to be more structured but also more transparent about what thresholds must be met.

CMBS conduit and life company loans, while less prevalent in Fargo than in larger metros, do appear on stabilized multifamily and anchored retail assets above $5 million. Workouts on securitized loans are handled by special servicers operating under pooling and servicing agreement constraints, and the standard of care for a special servicer differs meaningfully from that of a portfolio lender. Special servicers are obligated to act in the best interest of the certificate holders, not merely the borrower or even the senior lender, and their workout decisions are subject to a different analytical framework than what applies to a community bank modifying a loan it originated and holds.

When we opine that a particular modification term was outside market norms for a Fargo borrower in a given year, we are drawing on transaction-level knowledge of what lenders in this market were actually doing, not on theoretical standards.

What Attorneys Handling Fargo CRE Disputes Need to Know

When to Retain a CRE Financing Expert. If your case involves a lender's conduct during a modification, forbearance, or workout process, a real estate appraiser cannot answer the most important questions. Appraisers establish value. CRE financing experts establish what a qualified lender would have done with that value, what modification terms were commercially supportable given the cash flow, and whether the lender's decision-making process met the standard of care applicable to commercial mortgage workouts. These are distinct disciplines, and conflating them produces incomplete expert testimony.

Questions the Expert Can Answer That an Appraiser Cannot. A qualified CRE financing expert can opine on: whether the DSCR and LTV analysis the lender performed was consistent with industry underwriting standards; whether the modification terms imposed were within the range of what comparable lenders would have offered; whether the lender's timeline for processing the modification request was reasonable; whether the lender's reserve and amortization requirements were proportionate to the actual credit risk; and whether enforcement was initiated prematurely relative to the state of the modification discussions. These opinions require active market participation, not retrospective valuation methodology.

What Qualifies a CRE Financing Expert. Relevant qualifications include active participation in commercial mortgage origination, workout, or capital markets functions; demonstrated familiarity with the specific asset class and market at issue; experience providing Rule 26 disclosures and surviving Daubert challenges; and the ability to explain complex financing mechanics clearly to a judge or jury without losing technical credibility. A retired banker who has not stayed current with lending practices, lender market conditions, and underwriting evolution over the past several years will struggle to establish the contemporaneous market knowledge that makes expert testimony persuasive.

What Counsel Should Gather Before the Engagement. Before retaining our firm, counsel should compile the complete modification request file, including all borrower-submitted financial packages, all lender correspondence, the term sheet or terms conveyed during negotiations, and any internal lender communications produced in discovery. Rent rolls, historical operating statements, and the original loan underwriting package are essential. Any written credit committee decisions or loan review committee notes that address the modification decision are particularly valuable because they allow the expert to evaluate the lender's internal reasoning against the standard of care.

How Expert Testimony Is Used. In a breach of contract or lender liability trial, CRE financing expert testimony typically addresses what a reasonable lender in the same market, facing the same collateral and borrower profile, would have done. In a bankruptcy plan confirmation proceeding involving a disputed modification or cram-down analysis, the expert may opine on whether the proposed plan terms satisfy the feasibility standard from a financing market perspective. In either context, the expert's role is to translate complex capital markets mechanics into a framework the fact-finder can apply to the specific conduct at issue.

Key Takeaways

  • Stabilized NOI is the correct underwriting basis for a modification on a transitionally vacant asset. A lender who evaluates modification capacity exclusively on trailing distressed income, when executed leases or credible market absorption data support a forward-looking income figure, is not applying industry-standard workout underwriting.
  • The 1.25x DSCR threshold is the industry benchmark for commercial mortgage workouts. Modification terms that produce a DSCR below this threshold under the lender's own stabilized income assumptions are internally inconsistent with standard credit policy and are analytically indefensible.
  • LTV below 65% on stabilized value is a meaningful indicator of adequate collateral protection. Lenders in the Fargo market routinely extend modification accommodations to borrowers at this LTV level when the income disruption is temporary and the borrower's equity cushion is preserved. Enforcement in this collateral position requires justification beyond the temporary cash flow gap.
  • Reserve requirements and amortization acceleration must be proportionate to actual credit risk.
