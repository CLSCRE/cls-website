CRE Capital Markets Report: Treasury Yields and Lending Trends, June 22, 2026

By Trevor Damyan June 22, 2026

Every two weeks, CLS CRE publishes a Capital Markets Report to give commercial real estate borrowers, investors, and brokers a clear read on where the debt markets actually stand. The June 22, 2026 edition focuses on the US Treasury yield curve and what its month-over-month movement means for pricing, deal structure, and lender appetite across property types. If you are actively pursuing a commercial mortgage, bridge loan, or refinance this summer, this is the rate environment you are working inside.

US Treasury Yield Curve: Month-Over-Month Comparison

The centerpiece of this report is a direct comparison of the Treasury yield curve between late May 2026 and mid-June 2026. The curve continues to reflect a market navigating competing pressures: residual inflation caution from the Federal Reserve on one side, and slowing economic growth signals on the other.

The short end of the curve, anchored by the 2-year Treasury, has held relatively steady as the Fed has signaled a patient stance on rate cuts through the second half of 2026. The longer end, particularly the 10-year Treasury, has seen modest fluctuation as bond investors weigh fiscal supply concerns against the possibility of a softer growth trajectory into year-end. For commercial mortgage borrowers, the 10-year Treasury remains the most consequential benchmark, directly influencing fixed-rate pricing on agency loans, CMBS executions, and life company debt.

What the month-over-month comparison reveals is not a dramatic shift but a continued recalibration. The yield curve has steepened slightly at the long end, which has a dual effect: it makes long-term fixed-rate debt marginally more expensive, but it also signals that the rate environment is stabilizing rather than repricing sharply in either direction. For deals that have been waiting on the sidelines for a clear entry point, this kind of stability is often the green light capital markets participants need.

What the Yield Curve Means for Commercial Mortgage Pricing Right Now

Lenders price commercial real estate debt as a spread over a corresponding Treasury or index benchmark. With the 10-year Treasury holding in its current range, all-in fixed rates on permanent financing have remained relatively consistent entering the summer. Spreads from life insurance companies have tightened modestly on high-quality, stabilized assets, reflecting strong demand for investment-grade commercial real estate paper. Regional bank pricing on shorter-term floating-rate construction and bridge loans continues to track the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, which remains sensitive to any shift in Fed policy expectations.

The practical implication for borrowers: lenders are open, pricing is knowable, and execution risk has decreased compared to the volatility windows of 2023 and 2024. The deals getting done right now are the ones where sponsors came in with clean underwriting, realistic valuation assumptions, and a clear business plan. The lenders that passed on deals eighteen months ago due to uncertainty are now actively competing for the same assets.

Bridge Lending Activity: Demand Remains Elevated

Short-term bridge capital has continued to see strong demand in June 2026. Transitional assets, including value-add multifamily, light industrial conversions, and mixed-use repositioning plays, are attracting capital from private bridge lenders at leverage levels that reflect a more confident credit environment than twelve months ago. Loan-to-cost ratios on bridge executions have crept back toward 70 to 75 percent on the right deals, and interest reserve structures are being underwritten with more flexibility.

One consistent theme across recent bridge transactions: lenders are scrutinizing the exit strategy more carefully than the entry. A compelling story about how and when permanent financing or a sale gets executed is weighted heavily in credit decisions. Borrowers who can demonstrate a clear path to stabilization, with realistic rent growth assumptions and a defined timeline, are finding lenders willing to move quickly and competitively.

Permanent Financing: Life Companies and CMBS Lead the Conversation

On the permanent debt side, life insurance companies remain among the most competitive executions for stabilized industrial, multifamily, and well-anchored retail assets. Their appetite for 10- and 12-year term paper with strong amortization profiles has not diminished, and their willingness to hold loans in portfolio means they can move efficiently without CMBS execution risk or market timing concerns.

CMBS executions have been active in June as well, particularly for larger loan sizes where balance sheet lenders hit concentration limits. Conduit spreads have tightened from the wider levels seen in early 2025, making CMBS a viable option for sponsors who need non-recourse execution, interest-only periods, or higher leverage on cash-flowing assets. Borrowers considering CMBS should plan for a 60- to 90-day timeline and should work with a broker who has active relationships across multiple conduit shelves to ensure competitive spread pricing.

What to Watch Heading Into Q3 2026

The second half of 2026 will be shaped by three variables: Federal Reserve policy communication, Treasury supply dynamics, and the pace of commercial real estate transaction volume. If the Fed signals any acceleration in rate relief, the 10-year Treasury could rally and fixed-rate mortgage pricing would follow. If fiscal supply continues to pressure the long end of the curve, spreads may need to absorb some of that movement to keep all-in rates competitive.

Transaction volume has been building gradually, and lenders want to deploy capital. That combination of motivated capital and improving deal flow is a constructive setup for borrowers who are ready to move in Q3. The window between now and Labor Day is historically when well-prepared sponsors can execute efficiently before year-end urgency creates competition for lender bandwidth.
