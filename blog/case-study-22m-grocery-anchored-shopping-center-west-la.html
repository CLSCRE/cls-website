$22,000,000 Grocery-Anchored Shopping Center in West Los Angeles, CA

By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Permanent Loan

Mitigating Re-Tenanting Risk on a $22M West LA Grocery-Anchored Deal

Grocery-anchored retail represents the gold standard of retail lending, offering predictable cash flows and resilient tenant bases that weather economic cycles. But even the strongest retail categories can present underwriting challenges when lease terms create uncertainty in the capital stack.

Commercial Lending Solutions recently closed a $22 million acquisition loan for a grocery-anchored shopping center in West Los Angeles, where the anchor tenant's upcoming lease renewal created hesitation among several lenders despite the property's otherwise strong fundamentals.

Property Overview and Challenge

The 45,000-square-foot shopping center sits at a major intersection in West Los Angeles, anchored by a regional grocery chain with eight inline tenants occupying the balance of the space. At 92% occupancy, the property generated strong cash flow with a diverse tenant mix including restaurants, personal services, and retail.

The complication: the grocery anchor's lease expires in three years, creating what lenders perceived as re-tenanting risk in a 10-year loan structure. Several banks and CMBS lenders expressed concern about the anchor potentially vacating mid-loan term, despite the grocer's 20-plus year operating history at the location.

The borrower, a family trust with a long-term hold strategy, had acquired the property from an estate sale and sought non-recourse financing to complete the transaction.

Market Positioning Strategy

Rather than accept shorter loan terms that matched the lease expiration, we positioned the deal around three core strengths that mitigated re-tenanting risk:

Location irreplaceability: The property sits at the intersection of two major arterials in West Los Angeles, surrounded by dense residential neighborhoods with household incomes exceeding $120,000. This intersection sees over 45,000 vehicles daily, making it among the most valuable grocery locations in the market.

Below-market anchor rent: Our rent analysis showed the grocery tenant paying approximately $18 per square foot, while comparable grocery spaces in West LA were leasing between $28-35 per square foot. This $400,000 annual savings provided strong economic incentive for lease renewal.

Operating history and investment: The anchor tenant had operated at this location for over two decades, building customer loyalty and making significant improvements to the space. Their capital investment in refrigeration, flooring, and fixtures created additional lease renewal motivation.

Lender Feedback and Execution

Regional banks offered terms but required lease extension or personal guarantees through the renewal period. CMBS lenders provided quotes but with higher rates reflecting the perceived risk. Credit unions expressed interest but couldn't reach the loan size required.

Life insurance companies proved most receptive to our risk mitigation narrative. Their longer investment horizons and portfolio approach to retail assets allowed them to look beyond the three-year lease concern.

A national life company ultimately provided the winning execution: 10-year fixed rate financing at 60% LTV with 30-year amortization, structured as non-recourse debt. The rate came in at 5.25%, reflecting current life company pricing for grocery-anchored retail.

The lender required a property condition assessment and environmental Phase I, but waived requirements for lease extension or additional guarantees that other lenders had requested.

Closing and Risk Factors

The transaction closed within 45 days of application, meeting the borrower's acquisition timeline. Total loan proceeds of $22 million provided 60% of the $36.7 million purchase price, requiring $14.7 million in equity from the borrower.

The life company's underwriting ultimately aligned with our positioning: they viewed the below-market rent and irreplaceable location as lease renewal drivers that outweighed the timing risk. Their portfolio included similar grocery properties where anchor tenants had renewed despite initial market concerns.

Market Implications

This transaction demonstrates how proper positioning can overcome perceived risks in retail lending. While grocery-anchored properties command premium pricing from lenders, lease timing issues require narrative development that addresses lender concerns with market data.

The successful execution also highlights life companies' continued appetite for well-located grocery properties, even with lease considerations that concern other lender types. Their patient capital approach allows for risk assessment that extends beyond immediate lease terms.

For borrowers facing similar lease timing issues, the key lies in demonstrating economic incentives for renewal rather than simply highlighting tenant operating history. Below-market rents provide quantifiable motivation that resonates with lenders' underwriting models.
