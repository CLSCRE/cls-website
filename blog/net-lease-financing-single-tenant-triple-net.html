Net Lease Financing: Single-Tenant Triple-Net Underwriting

By Trevor Damyan · April 20, 2026

Net Lease: The Most Financeable Commercial Real Estate

Single-tenant triple-net lease commercial real estate represents one of the most financeable asset classes in the capital markets today. When underwritten properly with strong tenant credit and favorable lease terms, net lease properties attract aggressive competition from life insurance companies, CMBS lenders, specialty debt funds, and banks. The appeal is straightforward: predictable cash flows, minimal landlord responsibilities, and credit-driven underwriting that resembles bond analysis more than traditional real estate financing.

At CLS Commercial Real Estate, we've arranged financing for major net lease portfolios including a $19.1 million pharmacy portfolio and a $43.4 million build-to-suit transaction. These deals showcase the capital markets' appetite for well-structured net lease investments when tenant credit and lease terms align with lender criteria.

Understanding Net Lease Structures

The net lease universe encompasses several variations, each defining the allocation of operating expenses between landlord and tenant. Triple-net (NNN) leases require tenants to pay property taxes, insurance, and all maintenance expenses, leaving landlords with minimal ongoing obligations beyond debt service and potential structural repairs. Double-net (NN) structures split certain expenses, with tenants typically covering taxes and insurance while landlords retain some maintenance responsibilities.

Absolute triple-net leases represent the most landlord-friendly structure, transferring virtually every expense to the tenant, including structural repairs and capital improvements. These arrangements are particularly common with investment-grade corporate tenants who accept comprehensive expense obligations in exchange for favorable rental rates and lease terms.

The expense allocation directly impacts financing terms. Lenders prefer NNN and absolute NNN structures because they minimize landlord risk and create more predictable net operating income streams. Properties with significant landlord expense obligations require higher debt service coverage ratios and may face more conservative loan-to-value parameters.

Tenant Categories and Credit Profiles

Net lease financing success depends heavily on tenant creditworthiness, which the capital markets segment into distinct tiers. Investment-grade corporate tenants with formal credit ratings from Moody's or S&P command the tightest spreads and most favorable terms. These include publicly traded pharmacy chains, national restaurant brands, and major retail operators with balance sheet strength sufficient to support their lease obligations.

National non-rated operators represent the second tier, encompassing established brands without formal credit ratings but with strong operating histories and market positions. Regional pharmacy chains, successful restaurant concepts, and specialized operators like car wash chains or urgent care providers often fall into this category. Lenders conduct detailed credit analysis on these tenants, examining financial statements, operating trends, and industry positioning.

Franchisee-operated locations present additional complexity, as the individual franchise owner's financial strength becomes critical alongside the brand recognition. Many lenders require personal guarantees from franchisee operators, particularly for smaller operators or newer franchise relationships. The underlying franchise agreement terms, including renewal options and territorial protections, also influence lender comfort.

Tenant industry concentration affects portfolio financing strategies. Retail tenants span pharmacy chains, quick-service restaurants, dollar stores, and automotive services. Industrial users include logistics operators, light manufacturing, and distribution facilities. Medical tenants encompass urgent care centers, dental practices, dialysis centers, and specialized medical services. Each sector carries distinct risk profiles that lenders evaluate differently based on recession resistance, lease renewal likelihood, and operational stability.

Active Lender Categories

Life insurance companies dominate investment-grade net lease financing, offering the most competitive rates and terms for properties with strong tenant credit. Their long-term liability matching makes 15 to 25-year net lease investments particularly attractive. Life companies typically require minimum loan sizes of $5 million to $10 million and prefer lease terms exceeding 10 years with corporate guarantees or investment-grade tenant credit.

CMBS execution works effectively for net lease portfolios and individual properties exceeding $5 million. The conduit market appreciates the predictable cash flows and simplified underwriting that net lease properties provide. Portfolio transactions often achieve better execution in CMBS than individual property sales, as the diversification and scale appeal to rating agencies and investors.

Specialty net lease debt funds have emerged as significant capital sources, particularly for non-rated tenants and shorter-term lease structures that don't fit traditional life company parameters. These funds often provide faster execution and more flexible underwriting but typically at higher cost of capital than life companies for comparable credit quality.

Banks remain active in the $3 million to $15 million net lease space, particularly for local or regional relationships. Community and regional banks often provide competitive terms for smaller net lease transactions, especially when existing banking relationships exist or when local market knowledge creates comfort with specific tenant or location dynamics.

Critical Underwriting Factors

Lease term duration significantly impacts financing availability and terms. Lenders strongly prefer initial lease terms or remaining terms exceeding 10 years, with 15 to 20-year terms considered optimal. Shorter lease terms require higher debt service coverage ratios and may limit loan-to-value ratios, as lenders discount cash flows approaching lease expiration.

Rent escalation provisions protect lenders against inflation and provide cash flow growth over the loan term. Fixed annual increases of 1% to 3% are common, while Consumer Price Index escalations offer inflation protection but create cash flow uncertainty that some lenders discount. Percentage rent provisions in retail leases can provide upside but are typically ignored in underwriting base case scenarios.

Landlord expense obligations directly impact net operating income and debt service coverage calculations. Properties where landlords retain roof and structure responsibilities require reserves for capital expenditures, reducing available cash flow for debt service. Absolute net leases eliminate these concerns but are primarily available with investment-grade tenants.

Location quality influences both financing terms and lease renewal probability. Properties in primary markets or strong demographic areas support higher leverage and better rates. Conversely, secondary or tertiary market locations may face loan-to-value restrictions regardless of tenant credit quality, as lenders consider re-tenanting prospects in adverse scenarios.

Current Market Conditions and Pricing

The 2026 interest rate environment has created opportunities for well-positioned net lease borrowers, particularly those with investment-grade tenant credit. Prime net lease properties with strong tenant credit are typically pricing 150 to 225 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, depending on tenant quality, lease term, and transaction size. This represents a normalization from the elevated spreads experienced during recent market volatility.

Life insurance companies are providing the most aggressive pricing for investment-grade net lease properties, often quoting sub-200 basis point spreads over Treasuries for optimal tenant credit and lease structures. CMBS markets have shown renewed appetite for net lease collateral, particularly in portfolio transactions where diversification benefits are clear.

Loan-to-value ratios for strong net lease properties typically range from 70% to 80%, with investment-grade tenant credit supporting the higher end of this range. Debt service coverage requirements generally fall between 1.20x and 1.35x, depending on tenant credit quality and lease term remaining.

Deal Structure Considerations

Prepayment provisions vary significantly across lender types and market conditions. Life insurance companies typically require yield maintenance or defeasance for the majority of the loan term, protecting their long-term investment returns. Banks may offer more flexible prepayment terms but often at higher base rates to compensate for refinancing risk.

Loan terms generally align with lease terms, with 10 to 25-year amortization periods common. Some lenders offer interest-only periods for stabilized properties with strong tenant credit, particularly in portfolio transactions or build-to-suit scenarios where initial cash flow optimization is important.

Recourse provisions depend primarily on tenant credit quality and loan size. Investment-grade tenant credit often supports non-recourse financing, while franchisee operators or non-rated tenants may require limited or full recourse guarantees. Environmental and standard carve-out guarantees are typical regardless of tenant credit.

Portfolio vs. Individual Property Strategies

Portfolio financing often achieves better execution than individual property transactions through diversification benefits and transaction efficiency. Lenders can underwrite tenant credit once across multiple locations, reducing due diligence costs and timeline. Geographic diversification within portfolios also mitigates location-specific risks that might impact individual property underwriting.

Minimum portfolio sizes vary by lender type, with life companies typically requiring $15 million to $25 million aggregate transactions, while specialty lenders may consider smaller portfolio sizes. Mixed-tenant portfolios require careful structuring to ensure compatibility across tenant credit profiles and lease terms.

Individual property financing remains viable for larger assets or unique credit situations where portfolio assembly isn't practical. Properties exceeding $10 million with strong tenant credit can often achieve financing terms comparable to portfolio execution, particularly when lease terms and tenant credit align with lender preferences.

The net lease financing market continues to offer attractive capital solutions for properly structured transactions. Success requires understanding the distinct preferences across lender types and matching tenant credit profiles with appropriate capital sources. As interest rates stabilize, net lease properties with strong tenant credit and favorable lease terms remain among the most financeable assets in commercial real estate.
