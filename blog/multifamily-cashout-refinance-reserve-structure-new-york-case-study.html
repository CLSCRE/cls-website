Case Study: $16.5M Multifamily Cash-Out Refinance with a Layered Reserve Structure in New York

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $16,500,000 cash-out refinance through Axos Bank for a multifamily unit portfolio in New York, New York. The loan closed at 5.00% fixed for the first five years of a 10-year term, amortized over 30 years, with a $1,600,000 payment reserve and a separate restricted deposit account equal to 5% of the loan amount, both held with the lender for the life of the loan.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance brought us an ownership group holding a substantial multifamily co-op unit portfolio in New York, NY. The ownership wanted to pull significant cash out of the portfolio while managing the specific regulatory and reserve requirements that come with financing residential units in a market shaped by rent regulation history. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in multifamily cash-out refinancing and permanent financing for investors across New York and nationwide.

New York, New York multifamily lending carries underwriting considerations that don't come up in most other markets, particularly around rent regulation status once units turn over, and finding a bank willing to size a large cash-out refinance against that backdrop required careful structuring around reserves and legal confirmations rather than a standard multifamily underwrite.

What Made It Hard

  • Layering two separate cash reserves on top of the loan itself. Beyond the standard debt service, the structure required both a $1,600,000 payment reserve and a separate 5% restricted deposit account, each with its own replenishment and reduction mechanics, adding real complexity to how the ownership managed liquidity around the refinance.
  • Confirming rent regulation status on a co-op portfolio. The lender required a legal opinion confirming the collateral units were not subject to New York rent regulations once vacated, a market-specific diligence item that materially affects how a lender values future income potential as units turn over.
  • Structuring a hybrid fixed-to-adjustable rate against a 10-year term. With the rate fixed for five years before converting to a 6-month LIBOR-indexed adjustable rate with semi-annual resets, the ownership had to plan around both the known initial period and a capped but variable second half of the term.
"Two stacked reserves and a rent regulation legal opinion is a lot to manage on one closing. Getting the ownership group to see those requirements as capital planning, not just underwriting boxes to check, is what kept the actual net proceeds matching what we modeled at application."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the cash-out refinance with Axos Bank, with the signed letter of interest dated October 4, 2021:

  • $16,500,000, or 45% loan-to-value as determined by the lender, whichever was more restrictive, structured as a cash-out refinance
  • An initial rate of 5.00% fixed for 60 months, converting after the fixed period to the 6-month LIBOR index plus a 3.75% margin, with a 5.00% floor and semi-annual adjustments capped at 1.00% per adjustment and 6.00% over the life of the loan
  • A 10-year term amortized over 30 years, with a declining prepayment premium of 5% in year one down to 1% in year five, and no premium thereafter
  • Full recourse to all managing members and any member holding 25% or more ownership interest in the borrowing entities
  • An equity pledge agreement requiring the borrower to pledge membership interests in the borrowing entity as additional collateral alongside the mortgage
  • A $1,600,000 payment reserve established at closing in a dedicated deposit account, required to be replenished back to 18 months of principal and interest coverage each time the balance fell to 3 months of coverage remaining
  • A restricted deposit account equal to 5% of the loan amount ($825,000), held for the life of the loan and reducing to half that amount ($412,500) once the unpaid principal balance amortized down to 50% of the original loan amount
  • A dedicated operating deposit account with the lender, with loan proceeds deposited directly into that account at closing and automatic ACH payments enrolled for debt service
  • A required legal opinion confirming the collateral units were not subject to New York rent regulations once vacated, plus DHCR registration and a complete rent roll with move-in dates provided prior to appraisal
  • Partial release provisions allowing individual units to be released from the collateral pool at 130% of their allocated loan amount plus a 1% paydown fee, with allocated amounts set at final underwriting

The refinance closed with both reserve accounts fully funded and the equity pledge, rent regulation opinion, and other market-specific conditions satisfied, delivering substantial cash-out proceeds to the ownership group against a portfolio structured for long-term hold.

Why We Won This One

Structuring the layered reserve requirements clearly upfront, rather than negotiating them piecemeal during underwriting, is what kept this large cash-out refinance on track. A $1,600,000 payment reserve and a separate $825,000 restricted deposit represent meaningful capital set aside outside the loan proceeds themselves, and getting the ownership group aligned early on exactly how much liquidity the deal actually required avoided a mismatch between expected and actual net proceeds.

Getting ahead of the rent regulation legal opinion also mattered on a New York co-op portfolio. Confirming that status directly, rather than letting it surface as a late-stage underwriting condition, kept a market-specific diligence item from becoming the deal's critical path this close to closing.

Frequently Asked Questions

A payment reserve account is a lender-held deposit funded at closing that serves as a backstop for future loan payments, separate from the borrower's regular operating account. This refinance required a $1,600,000 payment reserve at closing, with the borrower obligated to replenish it back to 18 months of principal and interest coverage each time it fell to 3 months of coverage remaining.
An equity pledge agreement gives the lender a security interest in the ownership interests of the borrowing entity itself, in addition to the mortgage on the real estate, providing an additional layer of collateral and control if the borrower defaults. This loan required the borrower to pledge its membership interests in the borrowing entity as collateral alongside the mortgage.
A restricted deposit account is a separate, fixed-percentage holdback tied to the original loan amount that remains in place for the life of the loan (though it may reduce as the balance amortizes), distinct from a payment reserve that specifically backstops monthly debt service. This loan required a restricted deposit account of 5% of the loan amount, reducing to half that amount once the unpaid principal balance fell to 50% of the original amount.
In markets with rent stabilization or rent control laws, a lender wants assurance that units aren't subject to regulations limiting rent increases or tenant turnover, since that materially affects the property's income potential once vacated. This loan required the lender to review and approve a legal opinion confirming the collateral units were not subject to local rent regulations once vacated.
