Case Study: $686K Ground-Up SFR Construction Loan in Houston's Near Northside

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $686,200 ground-up single-family construction loan through Genesis Capital for a spec-build project in Houston, Texas. The loan closed at 9.85% fixed, interest-only, over a 12-month term with up to two extensions, structured with a $461,700 construction holdback released in draws, a $49,399.72 interest reserve, and separate repayment and completion guaranties from the sponsor and a second individual.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, California. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in ground-up construction financing for spec builders and investors nationwide. This deal came from a builder client based in Cedar City, Utah, financing a ground-up single-family spec home in Houston, Texas (TX), specifically in the Near Northside, a neighborhood just north of downtown that has seen steady infill redevelopment activity. The project needed a private construction lender comfortable funding an out-of-state sponsor building on a single lot, rather than requiring the scale of a multi-home development pipeline to get attention.

Near Northside sits close enough to downtown Houston to attract infill spec buyers, but it is still a market where financing a single ground-up build takes a lender genuinely built for small-balance construction rather than a bank looking for a larger, portfolio-scale relationship.

What Made It Hard

  • A single-lot spec build for an out-of-state sponsor. With the ownership entity based in Cedar City, Utah and the property in Houston, the loan needed a lender willing to underwrite a first-time relationship on a single spec home rather than a repeat borrower with an established local track record.
  • Structuring the draw schedule and reserve correctly. Getting the construction holdback, draw count, and interest reserve sized right up front mattered, too few draws or too small a reserve would have left the sponsor exposed mid-build.
  • A tight execution window. The letter of interest carried its own short acceptance deadline, just two days from issuance, requiring the borrower to move quickly from term sheet to signed acceptance to keep the underwriting timeline on track.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the loan with Genesis Capital, LLC, a private construction lender built specifically for spec and rehab building, closing the letter of interest on June 13, 2025:

  • $686,200 total loan secured by a first mortgage on the Houston property, structured as a draw construction loan
  • $461,700 construction holdback, released in up to two draws at a $500 fee per draw, funding the actual build-out as work progresses
  • $49,399.72 interest reserve funded at closing, covering monthly interest payments during construction so the sponsor did not need separate carry capital
  • 9.85% fixed rate, interest-only, with monthly payments due in arrears starting the first of the second month after closing
  • 12-month term with up to two extensions available at the lender's discretion if the build needed more time
  • Underwriting caps of 70% loan-to-after-repair-value (based on the lower of the lender's internal valuation or the appraisal) and 85% loan-to-cost, keeping real sponsor equity in the project at both ends
  • 1.0% origination fee ($6,862) plus standard third-party costs, appraisal, underwriting, title, and escrow, totaling roughly $12,646 in estimated settlement costs
  • Dual guaranty structure: the sponsor signed both a personal repayment guaranty and a completion guaranty, while a second individual signed a completion guaranty covering the construction obligation specifically

With financing available at closing (after settlement costs) of roughly $175,100, the sponsor had working capital in hand at the start of the build, with the remaining construction budget released through the draw schedule as the home progressed.

Why We Won This One

Small-balance, single-lot spec construction loans are exactly the kind of deal that a lot of construction lenders quietly deprioritize in favor of larger, repeat-borrower relationships. Matching this project with a lender whose entire underwriting model is built around exactly this size and structure, rather than treating a first-time, single-home sponsor as an afterthought, is what got favorable leverage and a workable draw schedule in place quickly.

The other piece that mattered was structuring the interest reserve and draw count correctly from the start. Sizing the reserve to actually cover the construction period, rather than the bare minimum, meant the sponsor was not exposed to a cash flow gap mid-build, a detail that is easy to underprice in a fast-moving term sheet negotiation.

Frequently Asked Questions

A draw loan disburses in stages as construction milestones are completed rather than all at once. This $686,200 loan was structured with a $461,700 construction holdback released in draws (capped at two draws, $500 fee each) plus an upfront interest reserve to cover payments during the build.
An interest reserve is a portion of loan proceeds set aside at closing specifically to cover monthly interest payments during construction, when the property is not generating income. This loan funded a $49,399.72 interest reserve so the borrower did not need separate cash flow to service the debt while building.
LTARV (loan-to-after-repair-value) caps the loan against the property's value once finished; LTC (loan-to-cost) caps it against total project cost. This loan was capped at 70% LTARV, based on the lower of the lender's internal valuation or the appraisal, and 85% LTC, keeping meaningful sponsor equity in the deal at both ends.
A repayment guaranty makes a guarantor personally liable for the debt itself, while a completion guaranty specifically obligates a guarantor to see the construction project through to completion. This loan required a repayment guaranty from the sponsor and completion guaranties from both the sponsor and a second individual, splitting financial and construction-performance risk across two people.
Because this was a single-property, single-sponsor spec build rather than a multi-lot development, the lender was able to move from a signed letter of interest to underwriting quickly, with a 12-month construction term and up to two available extensions if the build needed more time.
