Case Study

$18M Value-Add Multifamily (52 Units) in Highland Park, Los Angeles, CA

By Trevor Damyan·April 05, 2026·Bridge Loan

The Deal

A seasoned real estate investment group approached Commercial Lending Solutions seeking bridge financing for an $18 million acquisition of a 52-unit multifamily property in Highland Park, Los Angeles. The value-add opportunity presented significant upside potential through strategic renovations and improved property management, despite operating at 85% occupancy at the time of purchase. The Highland Park submarket had experienced substantial gentrification over the preceding decade, with young professionals and families drawn to the area's proximity to downtown Los Angeles, historic charm, and relatively affordable housing options compared to neighboring markets. The subject property, built in the 1960s, featured spacious one and two-bedroom units with original hardwood floors and period architectural details that appealed to the area's evolving demographic profile. The borrower's investment thesis centered on methodically upgrading vacant units with modern amenities, improved lighting, updated kitchens and bathrooms, and enhanced common areas to justify market-rate rents upon re-tenanting. Their track record included successful execution of similar value-add strategies across multiple Los Angeles submarkets over the previous fifteen years.

The Challenge

The primary obstacle facing this transaction involved navigating Los Angeles' Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO), which significantly constrains renovation activities and rent increases for occupied units. Under RSO regulations, landlords cannot renovate occupied units or increase rents beyond prescribed annual limits, typically ranging from 3% to 8% depending on inflation metrics. This regulatory framework limited the borrower's value creation strategy to vacancy decontrol opportunities only, meaning renovations could occur solely when tenants voluntarily vacated units. The restriction created uncertainty around renovation timelines and cash flow projections, as tenant turnover rates directly impacted the borrower's ability to execute their business plan. Traditional construction and bridge lenders expressed hesitancy about the deal structure due to unfamiliarity with RSO complexities and concerns about extended lease-up periods. Several institutional lenders withdrew from consideration after their underwriting teams struggled to model cash flows under vacancy-dependent renovation scenarios. The borrower required a lender who understood rent-stabilized multifamily dynamics and could structure financing terms accommodating the inherent timeline uncertainties. Speed of execution was critical, as the borrower faced a competitive acquisition environment with multiple qualified buyers pursuing the asset.

The Solution

Commercial Lending Solutions leveraged its extensive capital markets network to identify a specialized debt fund with deep expertise in Los Angeles rent-stabilized multifamily properties. This lender had previously financed similar value-add projects under RSO constraints and demonstrated sophisticated understanding of vacancy decontrol economics. The debt fund structured a 24-month bridge loan at 75% loan-to-value, providing $13.5 million in acquisition financing. The interest rate of SOFR plus 350 basis points reflected the lender's comfort with both the sponsor's track record and the Highland Park submarket fundamentals. The relatively conservative leverage allowed for adequate equity cushion while the borrower executed renovations over the loan term. Loan terms included flexibility for the borrower to request a 12-month extension if renovation progress warranted additional time for lease-up activities. The lender agreed to release renovation holdbacks based on completed unit upgrades rather than occupancy milestones, recognizing that tenant turnover timing remained outside the borrower's direct control. The debt fund's underwriting approach incorporated conservative turnover assumptions while giving credit for Highland Park's strong rental demand and the borrower's proven ability to attract quality tenants through targeted marketing and competitive unit finishes.

The Outcome

The transaction closed within 45 days of initial application, enabling the borrower to secure the acquisition against competing offers. The expedited timeline proved crucial in the borrower's successful bid, as the seller prioritized certainty of execution over marginal price differences between offers. Within the first twelve months, the borrower successfully renovated and re-leased eighteen units as they became vacant through natural tenant turnover. Average rent increases of 35% were achieved on renovated units, validating the initial underwriting assumptions about Highland Park's rental market strength. Property occupancy improved to 96% as the borrower's enhanced management practices reduced tenant friction and improved resident satisfaction scores. Common area improvements, including updated landscaping and security systems, contributed to the property's appeal among prospective renters. The borrower refinanced the bridge loan after eighteen months with a permanent Freddie Mac loan, capturing significant equity appreciation as the property's stabilized value exceeded original projections. The successful execution strengthened the borrower's relationship with both the bridge lender and Commercial Lending Solutions for future investment opportunities. This transaction demonstrated the importance of matching specialized lending sources with complex regulatory environments, where traditional lenders lack the expertise to properly evaluate risk and structure appropriate terms.
