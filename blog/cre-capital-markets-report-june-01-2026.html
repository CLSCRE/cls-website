CRE Capital Markets Report: Treasury Yields, Lending Conditions, and Deal Flow for June 2026

By Trevor Damyan June 01, 2026

Where the Treasury Yield Curve Stands Heading Into June 2026

The month-over-month comparison of the US Treasury yield curve entering June 2026 tells a nuanced story for commercial real estate borrowers and lenders. Short-term rates remain elevated relative to historical norms, while the intermediate and long end of the curve has shown modest movement that is reshaping how capital is being priced across every major asset class.

For CRE sponsors and investors, the shape of the yield curve is not an abstract economic indicator. It is the foundation on which lenders build their spread requirements, set their reserve assumptions, and decide which deals pencil. When the curve flattens or inverts, bridge lenders and banks price to protect themselves. When the curve steepens, permanent financing through life companies and CMBS becomes more competitive. Right now, we are navigating a curve that has partially re-steepened from its prior inverted posture, and that shift is creating real opportunities for borrowers who are positioned to move.

What the Month-over-Month Yield Movement Means for Loan Pricing

The 5-year and 10-year Treasury benchmarks are the two reference points that matter most for the majority of commercial mortgage executions. Conduit lenders index to the 10-year. Many bank and credit union portfolio lenders key off the 5-year for fixed-rate terms. Even floating-rate bridge debt, which prices off SOFR, is indirectly influenced by Treasury movement because it affects cap costs and overall debt service coverage calculations.

Over the past 30 days, the yield curve has shown a pattern that favors borrowers seeking five-to-seven-year fixed-rate financing on stabilized assets. Life insurance companies and CMBS shops have been active, and spreads have held relatively tight for deals with strong in-place cash flow, low leverage, and quality sponsorship. That combination is not always easy to assemble, but when it is present, lenders are competing for the paper.

On the short end, the Fed has held its policy rate steady, and market expectations reflected in futures pricing suggest the first cut remains a 2026 event but is not imminent. This means floating-rate borrowers are still carrying meaningful debt service burdens, and many are actively looking at fixed-rate refinance options or interest rate cap replacement strategies as existing caps approach expiration.

Bridge Lending Activity and Transitional Deal Flow

Despite the higher-rate environment, transitional asset lending has remained active. A private bridge lender recently closed on a multifamily value-add acquisition in the Mid-Atlantic at a floating rate inside 300 basis points over the index, reflecting strong competition among non-bank lenders for quality sponsors with clear business plans. A regional bank closed on a mixed-use refinance in the Southeast at a fixed rate in the high sixes, blending a shorter fixed period with a competitive amortization schedule to keep debt service manageable for the borrower.

These are not outlier transactions. They reflect a market where capital is available but selective. Lenders are underwriting hard to replacement cost, trailing cash flow, and exit assumptions. Deals that rely on aggressive rent growth projections or optimistic cap rate compression are getting pushed back. Deals that show real basis, real income, and realistic exit strategies are getting funded.

Permanent Capital: Life Companies and CMBS Holding Steady

The permanent lending market has been one of the more positive stories in commercial real estate finance through the first half of 2026. A life insurance company recently provided long-term fixed-rate financing on a net lease industrial asset in the Midwest at terms that would not have been available six months ago. CMBS volume has picked up from its 2023 and 2024 lows, with conduit spreads tightening as investor appetite for structured credit has improved.

For borrowers with assets that qualify, the window for competitive permanent financing is open. Spreads on 10-year conduit execution have compressed, and life company allocations remain active through mid-year. Sponsors who have been waiting on the sidelines for rates to fall significantly before locking in long-term debt may find that the more practical move is executing now while lender appetite is strong and spreads are working in their favor.

Key Takeaways for CRE Investors and Sponsors in June 2026

The capital markets environment as we enter June 2026 can be summarized in a few direct observations. First, money is available across the capital stack for deals that are structured correctly. Second, the partial re-steepening of the Treasury yield curve has improved execution options for intermediate and long-term fixed-rate borrowers. Third, bridge lenders remain competitive but are underwriting with discipline, and sponsors who bring clean deal packages are seeing the best terms. Fourth, CMBS and life company executions are pricing well for qualifying assets, and mid-year allocations are not unlimited.

For sponsors with near-term maturities, refinancing decisions made in the next 60 to 90 days will define performance for the next several years. Waiting for a dramatic rate cut that may not arrive on the timeline many are projecting is a risk management decision as much as a financial one. The deals getting done right now are being done by sponsors who understand that execution quality and lender relationships matter as much as the rate environment itself.
