Medical Office Building (MOB) Financing: Healthcare Real Estate Lending

By Trevor Damyan · April 20, 2026

The Medical Office Exception: Why MOB Financing Remains Strong in 2026

While traditional office real estate continues wrestling with structural headwinds, medical office buildings stand as the clear exception. In 2026, MOB financing remains one of the most attractive segments in commercial real estate, with aggressive lender competition driving spreads tighter and terms more favorable than most asset classes.

The fundamentals supporting medical office are straightforward: healthcare demand grows consistently, tenants rarely relocate, and demographic trends create sustained occupancy pressure. Unlike corporate office tenants who discovered remote work flexibility, medical practices remain anchored to their physical locations. Patient care requires in-person visits, specialized equipment, and regulatory compliance that makes tenant mobility extremely limited.

At CLS CRE, we've closed medical office transactions across multiple markets, including Los Angeles and other major metros, and consistently see lender appetite that outpaces most commercial real estate sectors. The financing landscape for quality MOB assets in 2026 offers borrowers meaningful options across the capital stack.

Understanding MOB Property Types

Medical office buildings aren't monolithic. Lenders evaluate different MOB sub-types with distinct underwriting approaches, and understanding these categories is critical for positioning financing requests effectively.

Hospital-anchored properties represent the gold standard for MOB financing. These assets feature health system tenancy, typically on long-term leases with institutional credit quality. When the hospital system guarantees lease obligations, underwriting becomes straightforward. Lenders view these as quasi-healthcare bonds with real estate collateral.

On-campus medical office buildings without hospital anchors occupy middle ground. Location proximity to hospital campuses provides referral flow and operational synergies, but tenant credit analysis becomes more granular. Independent physician groups and specialty practices require individual credit evaluation, though campus location generally supports stable occupancy.

Off-campus MOB assets depend heavily on tenant mix and market positioning. Successful off-campus properties typically serve established medical communities with strong physician relationships and patient loyalty. Lenders focus intensively on lease rollover risk and market competition analysis for these assets.

Ambulatory surgery centers represent specialized MOB financing, often featuring single-tenant or limited tenant configurations. ASC financing requires understanding regulatory environments, reimbursement trends, and operator track records. Lenders with healthcare specialty desks actively pursue quality ASC opportunities.

Life science buildings blur lines between medical office and laboratory space. These properties command premium rents but require specialized HVAC, power, and infrastructure investments. Financing often resembles hybrid MOB and industrial underwriting approaches.

Active Lender Categories

The MOB financing ecosystem in 2026 features diverse capital sources, each with distinct appetites and execution capabilities.

Life insurance companies with dedicated healthcare lending desks dominate the quality MOB space. These lenders understand healthcare real estate nuances and price accordingly. Life companies typically offer 10-year terms with moderate leverage and competitive pricing for stabilized assets. Their underwriting sophistication allows evaluation of complex tenant mixes and lease structures.

Specialty healthcare REITs have expanded beyond acquisition into direct lending. These organizations combine real estate expertise with healthcare industry knowledge, creating informed capital sources for MOB transactions. REIT lenders often provide flexible terms and faster execution than traditional institutional sources.

CMBS execution works effectively for larger, stabilized MOB assets with credit tenancy. Conduit lenders appreciate healthcare tenant stickiness and consistent cash flows. CMBS pricing remains competitive for assets meeting standard parameters, though healthcare-specific lease provisions require experienced legal review.

Regional and community banks actively finance MOB construction and owner-user transactions. Bank construction lending for MOB development often features competitive terms, particularly when physician groups provide pre-leasing commitments. Owner-user financing through banks frequently offers relationship pricing advantages.

Agency financing remains limited but available through specialized programs. Certain Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac initiatives accommodate medical office components within mixed-use developments, though pure MOB assets rarely qualify for agency execution.

Tenant Credit Analysis

MOB underwriting hinges on tenant credit evaluation, requiring lenders to assess healthcare-specific factors beyond traditional commercial lease analysis.

Health system tenants provide institutional credit quality, but lenders examine system financial health, merger activity, and strategic priorities. Large health systems occasionally consolidate facilities or relocate services, creating lease rollover risk even with strong credit profiles. Hospital affiliation agreements between health systems and physician tenants add complexity but generally strengthen credit analysis.

Independent physician groups require detailed financial review. Lenders evaluate practice revenue diversification, reimbursement mix, and physician demographics. Practices heavily dependent on specific insurance contracts or government reimbursement face additional scrutiny. Successful physician group underwriting requires understanding medical specialties and their respective revenue stability characteristics.

National healthcare operators, including dialysis centers, urgent care chains, and specialty service providers, offer corporate credit quality with healthcare sector exposure. These tenants typically feature standardized lease terms and expansion potential, though regulatory changes can impact entire portfolio performance.

Multi-tenant MOB assets require portfolio-level analysis. Lenders examine tenant diversification, lease expiration schedules, and market replacement prospects. Successful multi-tenant underwriting identifies properties with sustainable competitive advantages and stable tenant demand characteristics.

Critical Underwriting Factors

MOB underwriting extends beyond standard commercial real estate analysis to incorporate healthcare-specific considerations.

Lease structure analysis focuses on rent escalation mechanisms, tenant improvement allowances, and renewal options. Healthcare tenants typically negotiate CPI-based escalations rather than fixed increases, providing inflation protection. Tenant improvement amortization through rental premiums represents common MOB lease structuring, requiring careful cash flow analysis.

Capital expenditure requirements for MOB assets often exceed typical office buildings. Medical equipment installation, specialized infrastructure, and regulatory compliance modifications create ongoing capital needs. Lenders factor maintenance and improvement costs into debt service coverage calculations.

Location analysis emphasizes accessibility, parking availability, and proximity to referral sources. Medical office tenants require convenient patient access, often making ground-floor space more valuable than upper floors. Adequate parking ratios are critical, as medical appointments generate predictable visitor traffic.

Regulatory compliance affects MOB operations and financing. Healthcare facilities must meet ADA requirements, medical waste handling protocols, and various safety standards. Lenders review compliance history and factor ongoing regulatory costs into underwriting analysis.

Market Dynamics and Execution

The 2026 MOB financing market reflects broader healthcare real estate trends while maintaining distinct characteristics.

Pricing for quality MOB assets generally tracks investment-grade commercial real estate, with healthcare sector premiums creating spread compression versus traditional office properties. Lenders recognize MOB stability advantages and price accordingly. Competition among healthcare-focused lenders drives aggressive terms for premium assets.

Leverage levels vary by property type and tenant profile. Hospital-anchored assets often achieve 75% to 80% loan-to-value financing, while off-campus properties typically see 70% to 75% leverage limits. Construction financing for pre-leased MOB development can reach 80% of cost for qualified borrowers.

Due diligence timelines accommodate healthcare-specific requirements. Environmental assessments focus on medical waste handling and potential contamination issues. Legal review emphasizes healthcare regulations and specialized lease provisions. Title work examines easements and access rights critical for medical operations.

The outlook for MOB financing remains positive through 2026 and beyond. Demographic trends support sustained healthcare demand growth. Technology advances complement rather than replace in-person medical care for most services. Lender familiarity with healthcare real estate continues expanding, creating more sophisticated capital sources.

For borrowers and brokers navigating MOB financing, success requires understanding the healthcare-specific factors that differentiate medical office from traditional commercial real estate. Quality assets with stable tenancy continue attracting competitive financing terms, while lenders maintain selective underwriting standards that reward healthcare expertise and market knowledge.
