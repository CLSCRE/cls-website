The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, California (CA). Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in bridge loans and conversion financing for healthcare and hospitality assets across California. This deal came to us mid-project: a family investor group, operating through a living trust and an individual guarantor, had already committed to converting a former hotel property in San Bernardino into a licensed assisted living facility. The existing capital on the property was a $2,400,000 note from a community bank, originally put in place in 2020 and extended into late 2025 with a monthly interest reserve requirement attached, a structure built for a stabilized asset, not a mid-conversion one.

The conversion itself was real and underway, but the property sat in the awkward middle ground that most institutional lenders avoid entirely: no longer a hotel, not yet a licensed, income-producing assisted living operation. The borrower needed a private bridge lender willing to underwrite to what the property would be worth once the conversion was finished and licensed, not what a hotel on that site would fetch today.

What Made It Hard

  • A change-of-use story mid-conversion. Banks and most institutional bridge lenders will not fund against a going-concern value for a use that does not exist on the property yet, they want to see the license and the operating history first.
  • An existing note built for a different property life stage. The 2020 bank loan carried a variable rate tied to Wall Street Journal Prime, monthly payment reserve top-up requirements, and a maturity extension that assumed the hotel use continuing, not a licensed care facility being built out.
  • Sizing the capital stack for what comes next. The loan could not simply maximize proceeds today, it needed enough headroom left in the value to support a subordinate takeout facility once the assisted living operation stabilized and qualified for permanent financing.
"Banks will not lend against a use that does not exist on the property yet. The going-concern appraisal is what let a private lender see the finished, licensed operation instead of a half-converted hotel, and that is what got this funded."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the loan with Stonecrest Financial, a Santa Clara-based private lender that originates and services loans funded directly by its own network of private investors, the kind of lender built specifically to underwrite a going-concern valuation on a conversion project rather than a stabilized-asset appraisal. The transaction was structured and closed in April 2025 through Commonwealth Land Title Company in Los Angeles:

  • $6,800,000 in first position, retiring the existing $2,400,000 bank note in full and funding the balance of the conversion
  • Priced against an $11,850,000 going-concern appraisal of the completed, licensed assisted living operation, not a bare real estate value
  • 57.38% total loan-to-value with zero senior encumbrances ahead of the new loan and $5,050,000 of protective equity behind it, the cushion that let a private lender move at this size and speed
  • 10.99% fixed, interest-only for a 12-month term, monthly payments of $62,276.67, with a balloon payment of $6,862,276.67 due at maturity
  • Deliberately not maxed out: the sizing left roughly $5,900,000 of anticipated headroom in the capital stack, built in from day one for a subordinate facility once the property stabilized and could qualify for permanent, lower-cost financing behind the bridge
  • Estimated borrower-side closing costs of $221,639.76, disclosed and itemized up front as part of the private-money lending process

The loan closed and the prior bank note was retired in the same transaction, giving the borrower a single, purpose-built facility to carry the conversion through to stabilization.

Why We Won This One

The going-concern appraisal is the mechanism that made this financeable at all. A standard as-is appraisal on a half-converted hotel would have supported a fraction of what this property is worth once it operates as a licensed assisted living facility. Matching the borrower to a lender that underwrites to the finished, operating value, rather than the awkward in-between state the property is actually in today, is what turned a stalled bank relationship into a fully funded conversion loan.

Equally important was resisting the urge to size the bridge to the maximum the going-concern value would support. Leaving real headroom in the capital stack meant the borrower has a credible, pre-planned path to a lower-cost permanent facility once the operation stabilizes, instead of a bridge loan that has to be refinanced by another expensive bridge loan.
