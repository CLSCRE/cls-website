The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this acquisition brought us a Southern California sponsor under contract to purchase a 106,572-square-foot single-tenant industrial building in Vista, California occupied entirely by a specialty manufacturing tenant on a triple-net lease. At $29,500,000, this was a large enough acquisition that conventional bank financing wasn't the natural fit, a life insurance company balance sheet lender, comfortable underwriting long-duration, fixed-rate industrial debt, was. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in permanent and life insurance company financing for industrial and net-lease investors across California and nationwide.

North San Diego County's industrial corridor around Vista, CA has drawn steady tenant demand from manufacturing and light-industrial users, and a single-tenant building carries a specific kind of underwriting risk: the entire loan's performance rides on one lease. Structuring around that concentration, rather than treating it as a simple multi-tenant deal, shaped everything about how this financing came together.

What Made It Hard

  • Single-tenant concentration risk. With one tenant occupying 100% of the building, the lender's underwriting and guaranty structure both had to account for what happens if that lease isn't renewed, rather than diversifying across a typical multi-tenant rent roll.
  • A tight 21-day document deadline to protect the rate lock. The Treasury-indexed rate was locked at application, but the full underwriting checklist, tenant estoppels, financials, organizational documents, appraisal engagement, had to reach the lender within 21 days or the lock would expire and the rate would float with the market.
  • Structuring a guaranty that could actually shrink. Rather than a flat guaranty for the life of the loan, the deal required negotiating specific, lender-acceptable conditions, a five-year lease extension at a defined minimum rent, before the guarantor's exposure would step down.
"A stepping guaranty only works if the lender will commit to the exact conditions in writing before you close. That is what turned a standard single-tenant risk into a real incentive for the sponsor."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (DRE #02244836), placed this acquisition financing with Symetra Life Insurance Company, with the signed loan application dated October 9, 2023 and closing in late November 2023:

  • $16,250,000 to fund the acquisition of the Vista industrial property, purchased for $29,500,000 with a $13,250,000 cash down payment, secured by a first lien deed of trust
  • An interest rate locked at 2.25% over the 5-year U.S. Treasury Yield, with a floor of 6.80%, fixed for a full five-year term, amortized over 30 years
  • A maximum 55% loan-to-value against a required appraised value of no less than $29,500,000
  • A stepping personal guaranty: an initial 50% guaranty of the unpaid principal and accrued interest, reducing to 25% once the tenant's lease was extended a minimum of five additional years at a minimum base rent of $17.15 per square foot NNN, with no uncured defaults in the interim
  • A yield-maintenance prepayment structure: the greater of 1% of the outstanding balance or the present value of remaining scheduled payments discounted at a comparable Treasury yield, waived entirely in the final 90 days of the term
  • A 2% good faith deposit at application, refundable at closing, with a 0.1% processing fee ($16,250) deducted from that deposit and fully earned upon the lender's receipt of the application
  • A 75-day commitment expiration window from rate lock, with the underwriting checklist due within 21 days to avoid the lock expiring and the rate re-floating with the index
  • Single-asset special purpose entity status required of the borrowing entity, with a due-on-transfer restriction on more than 49% of ownership interests and OFAC screening on any party acquiring a 25% or greater stake
  • Existing lease terms verified: the tenant's two suites (44,630 SF and 61,942 SF) under NNN leases running through June 2027 at a combined $1,882,884 in annual base rent, supported by tenant estoppel certificates and an SNDA

The acquisition closed with the full underwriting package submitted inside the 21-day window, protecting the locked rate and giving the sponsor a long-duration, fixed-rate facility on a single-tenant industrial asset.

Why We Won This One

Negotiating the stepping guaranty structure is what separated this financing from a standard fixed-rate acquisition loan. Life insurance lenders are typically conservative about guaranty exposure on single-tenant assets, and getting one comfortable committing in writing to a specific, achievable path, extend the lease five years at a defined minimum rent, to cut the sponsor's personal exposure in half gave the borrower a real incentive and a real payoff for strengthening the asset's income profile after closing.

Managing the 21-day underwriting checklist against the rate lock clock also mattered here. On a loan this size, with tenant estoppels, an MAI appraisal, organizational documents, and full financial packages all due on a hard deadline, missing that window would have meant re-pricing off a moving Treasury yield. Keeping every workstream, appraisal, title, tenant coordination, and borrower financials, moving in parallel rather than sequentially is what kept the locked rate intact through closing.
