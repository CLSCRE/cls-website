Case Study

$20,000,000 Density Bonus Multifamily (65 Units) in North Park, San Diego, CA

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Construction Loan

The Deal

Commercial Lending Solutions recently closed a $20 million construction loan for a 65-unit multifamily project in North Park, San Diego. The developer leveraged California's Density Bonus Law to achieve a 35% density increase by designating 15% of units as affordable housing at 50% Area Median Income (AMI).

The project capitalizes on North Park's status as one of San Diego's most desirable rental markets, with strong fundamentals driven by its walkable urban village character, proximity to Balboa Park, and established transit connectivity. The site was originally zoned for 48 units but qualified for density bonus provisions under California Government Code Section 65915, allowing the additional 17 units.

Loan structure included 65% loan-to-cost ratio with a 24-month construction term. Interest reserves were funded from loan proceeds, providing the sponsor with predictable carrying costs during the construction phase. The project represents a sophisticated approach to maximizing development potential while addressing California's affordable housing mandates.

The Challenge

While density bonus projects have become increasingly common in California markets, this deal presented specific underwriting complexities that concerned several potential lenders. The primary issue centered on parking ratios: the density bonus allowed 65 units on the site, but achieving maximum density required invoking a parking reduction concession under the law.

The reduced parking ratio of 0.8 spaces per unit, down from the standard 1.5 spaces typically required, created lender hesitation despite the project's transit-oriented location. Many construction lenders lack familiarity with density bonus mechanics and the parking concessions available under the statute. Some viewed the reduced parking as a potential lease-up risk or future resale concern.

Additionally, the affordable housing component required ongoing compliance monitoring and rent restrictions on the designated units, adding operational complexity that needed to be properly structured in the loan documentation. The 55-year affordability covenant needed careful consideration in terms of exit strategy and potential refinancing scenarios.

The Solution

We identified a regional bank with significant experience in California density bonus projects and deep market knowledge of San Diego's urban core neighborhoods. The lender understood that North Park's demographics and transit accessibility supported the reduced parking model, particularly given the neighborhood's walkability scores and proximity to the Mid-City transit corridor.

The bank's underwriting team had previously financed similar density bonus projects and recognized that the parking reduction was a legislative concession, not a planning deficiency. They analyzed comparable projects in urban San Diego submarkets and confirmed that reduced parking ratios had not negatively impacted absorption or rental rates in similar transit-oriented developments.

Loan documentation included specific provisions addressing the affordable housing compliance requirements, with clear parameters for ongoing monitoring and reporting. The lender structured appropriate reserves for compliance costs and established protocols for any future refinancing that would need to preserve the affordable housing restrictions.

The construction loan included standard disbursement controls with third-party inspections, cost-to-complete analysis, and completion guarantees from the sponsor. Interest rate was fixed for the construction term, eliminating rate risk during the 24-month build period.

The Outcome

The loan closed within 45 days of application, allowing the developer to commence construction on schedule. The density bonus structure enabled the project to achieve significantly higher returns per square foot of land while contributing to San Diego's affordable housing inventory.

The reduced parking ratio proved to be a non-issue during lease-up, with the project achieving 95% occupancy within four months of completion. North Park's urban amenities and transit connectivity validated the lender's analysis that parking demand would be lower than suburban multifamily projects.

The successful execution demonstrates the importance of matching density bonus deals with lenders who understand California's evolving housing legislation and can properly evaluate the risk-reward profile of these transactions. Regional banks with local market knowledge often provide more sophisticated analysis than national construction lenders unfamiliar with state density bonus programs.

This transaction highlights the growing sophistication of California's multifamily development market, where regulatory incentives are being leveraged to create economically viable projects that address housing affordability while generating appropriate returns for developers and lenders. The project is now stabilized and the sponsor is evaluating permanent financing options for the mini-perm conversion.
