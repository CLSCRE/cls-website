Industrial Debt Fund Construction Pricing (Week of May 25, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·May 25, 2026

Construction Capital Recalibration Creates Opportunities

Industrial construction financing has undergone a fundamental shift as traditional bank lenders continue pulling back from ground-up development exposure. What emerged through Q1 2026 is a bifurcated market where debt funds have stepped into the void with increasingly competitive pricing, particularly for sponsors with proven industrial track records. The spread compression we're seeing reflects both the maturation of the debt fund space and genuine competition for quality industrial construction deals.

Current debt fund construction pricing for industrial projects typically ranges from SOFR plus 450 to 650 basis points, depending on deal structure and sponsor strength. This represents a meaningful tightening from the SOFR plus 600 to 800 basis points we observed through much of 2025. The compression stems partly from improved market confidence in industrial fundamentals, but more significantly from the sheer volume of construction-focused debt capital competing for deals as bank lending capacity remains constrained.

Ground-Up vs. Conversion Dynamics

The pricing differential between ground-up construction and adaptive reuse projects has narrowed considerably. Where conversion projects traditionally commanded 50 to 100 basis points of pricing advantage due to lower perceived risk, that gap has compressed to roughly 25 to 75 basis points in the current environment. This convergence reflects debt funds' growing comfort with industrial construction risk, particularly in established logistics corridors where absorption remains robust.

Spec buildouts continue commanding premium pricing, typically running 75 to 125 basis points above pre-leased construction deals. However, even spec pricing has become more palatable as debt funds recognize the velocity advantages of delivering completed product in markets with constrained supply. The key differentiator remains sponsor experience: developers with proven spec leasing track records in their target markets can often negotiate pricing that approaches pre-leased levels.

Structure Evolution Beyond Rate

The most significant development isn't just pricing compression but structural evolution in debt fund construction loans. Loan-to-cost ratios have crept higher, with best-in-class sponsors now accessing 80% to 85% LTC on strong deals, compared to the 70% to 80% range that dominated 2025. Extension options have become more standardized, typically offering 12-month extensions at predetermined spreads rather than the punitive pricing adjustments we saw in earlier vintages.

Interest reserve structures have also improved markedly. Many debt funds now offer full interest carry through stabilization plus six months, recognizing that industrial lease-up timelines remain extended despite strong underlying demand. This structural enhancement often proves more valuable than marginal rate improvements, particularly for sponsors managing multiple projects simultaneously.

Personal guaranty requirements have similarly moderated. While completion and bad acts guaranties remain standard, the prevalence of full recourse structures has diminished for established sponsors with substantial balance sheet strength. Non-recourse construction debt, while still premium-priced, has become genuinely accessible rather than theoretical.

Positioning for the Next Development Cycle

For sponsors evaluating 2026 development starts, the current environment presents a compelling opportunity to lock favorable construction terms before the next wave of bank re-entry potentially reshuffles market dynamics. The debt fund construction market has reached sufficient scale and sophistication to support meaningful deal flow, but hasn't yet become commoditized enough to sacrifice structural flexibility.

The optimal approach involves initiating lender conversations during the entitlement phase rather than waiting for shovel-ready status. Debt funds increasingly value early relationship development and can provide valuable market perspective during the design and entitlement process. This contrasts sharply with traditional bank construction lending, where engagement typically began only after full entitlements were secured.

Geographic diversification in lender outreach remains critical. While national debt funds dominate the headlines, regional and specialty funds often provide superior execution for deals in their core markets. The key is matching deal characteristics with lender preference rather than simply chasing the lowest advertised rate.

Looking ahead through 2026, expect continued evolution in construction debt availability and terms as more institutional capital targets the industrial development space. The sponsors best positioned to capitalize will be those who establish lending relationships now, before increased competition drives up basis competition costs and tightens structural terms.
