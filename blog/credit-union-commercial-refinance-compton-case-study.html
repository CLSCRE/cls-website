Case Study: $3.66M Credit Union Refinance for a Commercial Building in Compton

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $3,662,000 refinance for a Los Angeles-based ownership group's commercial building on El Segundo Boulevard in Compton, California. The loan closed with a Southern California credit union's commercial lending desk, secured by a first deed of trust with standard institutional covenants on insurance, transfers of ownership, and use restrictions, including an explicit prohibition on cannabis-related tenants.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance came from a Los Angeles-based ownership group with a commercial building on El Segundo Boulevard in Compton, California (CA), a well-established commercial and industrial corridor in the South Bay submarket. The ownership needed a straightforward refinance from an institutional credit union lender comfortable with the property's location and use, priced and documented the way a credit union commercial desk typically structures a first-position loan. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in commercial refinance placements with credit unions and banks for owners across California and nationwide.

El Segundo Boulevard runs through one of the more active industrial and commercial corridors in southern Los Angeles County, and a lender with a dedicated commercial lending team, rather than a generalist branch underwriter, was the right fit to move the refinance through cleanly.

What Made It Hard

  • Institutional documentation discipline. The loan closed on a full GoDocs-prepared commercial loan package, loan agreement, promissory note, and deed of trust with assignment of rents, security agreement, and fixture filing, requiring the borrowing entity's paperwork to line up cleanly with credit union underwriting standards.
  • Use and transfer restrictions to navigate. The loan agreement carried standard but strict covenants: no cannabis-related use of the property whatsoever, and lender consent required before any meaningful transfer of ownership interest in the borrowing entity, both of which needed to be understood and accepted well before closing.
  • Keeping title clean throughout the term. The loan required any mechanic's or materialman's lien to be resolved within 60 days of filing, a real operational obligation for an active commercial property with ongoing maintenance or improvement work.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the refinance with a Southern California credit union's commercial lending division, closing on October 29, 2025:

  • $3,662,000 secured by a first deed of trust, assignment of rents, security agreement, and fixture filing against the Compton property
  • Full institutional loan documentation: a GoDocs-prepared loan agreement, promissory note, and deed of trust package under Loan No. 1524105788
  • An explicit prohibition on cannabis-related uses, cultivation, sale, or production of marijuana or marijuana-based products or merchandise, standard for a credit union operating under federal deposit insurance rules
  • Ownership transfer restrictions requiring lender consent for changes in the legal or beneficial ownership of the borrowing entity, including any single transferor's cumulative transfer of 5% or more of equity within a rolling 12-month period
  • A 60-day cure period for any mechanic's, materialman's, or judgment lien against the property, keeping the collateral's title position clean throughout the loan
  • Standard insurance covenants: property insurance plus commercial general liability, workers' compensation, and any other coverage the lender required from time to time, with the lender named as an additional insured on liability policies

The refinance closed cleanly against the Compton property, giving the ownership group an institutional, credit-union-backed loan documented to the standards a conventional commercial lender expects.

"Credit unions can be some of the best-priced lenders out there, but only if you match the deal to one that actually has a commercial desk. Getting that fit right upfront is what kept this one on schedule."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

Matching this refinance to a credit union with an actual dedicated commercial lending desk, rather than treating the loan as a generic small-business request routed through a retail branch, is what kept the underwriting and documentation process moving on a straightforward timeline. Institutional lenders like this one bring real underwriting discipline, seen in the transfer restrictions and lien-cure requirements built into the loan agreement, but that same discipline also means a well-prepared, complete application moves through cleanly.

Getting ahead of the use restrictions and ownership covenants before closing, rather than discovering them during underwriting, kept this refinance on schedule and avoided the kind of last-minute renegotiation that can happen when a borrower's actual plans for the property or its ownership structure don't match what the lender's standard loan agreement assumes.
