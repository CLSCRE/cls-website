Commercial Lending Solutions Market Perspective: September 2026

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

September 2026
Quick answer: September 2026 finds CRE capital markets at an inflection point: spreads are compressing selectively, lender allocations are shifting ahead of year-end, and borrowers who understand where each capital source actually sits today have a meaningful execution advantage.

September 2026 arrives with more clarity than the market has seen in roughly 18 months. The Federal Reserve has now held the federal funds rate steady across three consecutive meetings after executing two 25-basis-point cuts in the first quarter of 2026, landing the target range at 4.00 to 4.25 percent. The 10-Year Treasury has settled into a 4.20 to 4.50 percent trading band that, while still elevated by pre-2022 standards, is exhibiting a consistency that lenders and borrowers can actually underwrite around. That stability is doing something that rate cuts alone could not: it is restoring conviction. Deal volume in August 2026 posted its third consecutive month of sequential gains, and pipeline conversations at Commercial Lending Solutions have accelerated noticeably since Labor Day.

What is driving the improved tone is less about cheap money and more about a recalibration of expectations on both the equity and debt sides of the capital stack. Buyers have largely accepted that 2021-era cap rates are not returning, and sellers in several major asset classes have moved toward pricing that produces executable transactions. Life companies are deploying with urgency as they enter the final quarter of their allocation cycles. CMBS conduit volume is tracking toward its highest annual total since 2021. And a cohort of well-capitalized debt funds, many of which raised new vehicles in 2024 and 2025, are actively competing for senior bridge positions that bank retrenchment left behind.

The risks have not disappeared. Office remains structurally impaired in most markets. Floating-rate borrowers who layered on bridge debt in 2022 and 2023 are still working through maturity pressure, and while extend-and-pretend has given way to more pragmatic resolutions, the pipeline of distressed dispositions has not fully cleared. California-specific headwinds, from Measure ULA to shifting agency postures on rent-controlled stock, continue to complicate underwriting in the state's largest markets. But the overall posture of capital markets in September 2026 is decidedly more constructive than this time last year, and sponsors who are prepared to move with complete documentation and realistic pricing are finding real execution.

Rate Environment and Treasury Curve

The 10-Year Treasury opened September 2026 at approximately 4.31 percent after briefly touching 4.48 percent in mid-August on stronger-than-expected jobs data before retreating. The 2-Year is currently trading near 4.05 percent, producing a modestly positive yield curve of approximately 26 basis points. That uninversion, which began in earnest in late Q1 2026, has improved the fundamental economics of fixed-rate commercial lending. Lenders who were reluctant to commit to long-dated fixed spreads under an inverted curve are now more willing to quote aggressively.

SOFR term rates (1-month and 3-month) are hovering in the 4.10 to 4.20 percent range, reflecting the Fed's current pause posture. Floating-rate all-in costs for stabilized transitional assets are running 7.25 to 8.50 percent depending on leverage, property type, and sponsor quality. For borrowers still carrying pre-2024 floating rate bridge debt with rate caps expiring, September is a decision window: rate cap renewal premiums remain elevated but have eased modestly, and refinancing into fixed-rate permanent debt is now mathematically achievable on assets that have achieved stabilization.

The Fed's forward guidance has been deliberately cautious. Chair messaging through August has emphasized that two additional cuts remain possible in Q4 2026 only if core PCE continues its gradual descent toward the 2.5 percent range, where it currently sits. Markets are pricing in roughly 40 basis points of additional easing by year-end, though that is not fully baked into fixed-rate mortgage pricing. Lenders are generally quoting off current Treasuries rather than anticipating further moves, which means borrowers who lock today are not giving away much by acting ahead of potential Fed action.

Lender Program Appetite

Life Insurance Companies

Life companies are the most active, most competitive fixed-rate source in the market heading into Q4. Most major platforms are 60 to 75 percent through their 2026 allocation targets, which creates urgency to deploy on quality deals before year-end caps force them to the sideline. Spreads on 10-year fixed-rate permanent loans are landing between 135 and 165 basis points over comparable Treasuries for stabilized multifamily and industrial, producing all-in rates in the 5.65 to 6.15 percent range at 55 to 65 percent LTV. Mixed-use and anchored retail with credit tenancy are also finding receptive audiences. Minimum loan size at most life company platforms sits at $5 million, with $10 to $15 million as the sweet spot for competitive terms. Prepayment structures remain make-whole or declining percentage, so borrowers with anticipated refinance events within five years should weigh other capital sources carefully.

CMBS Conduit

CMBS conduit is executing well. Spreads on AAA-rated new issue paper have tightened approximately 18 basis points over the past 60 days, and that improvement is flowing through to borrower pricing. 10-year fixed conduit paper is available in the 6.00 to 6.75 percent range for qualifying properties at 55 to 70 percent LTV, with minimum loan sizes at $10 million for most originators. Conduit works best for retail, hospitality, and mixed-use where life company appetite is more selective. Borrowers should plan for 60 to 75 days from application to close, and underwriting is rigorous on cash flow haircuts: expect management fee grossups, replacement reserves, and TI/LC reserves on any office or retail with near-term lease rollover. Single-asset single-borrower structures remain available for larger transactions above $50 million with better pricing than conduit pools.

Agency (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac)

Both agencies are active, though their respective postures diverge at the margins. Fannie Mae is leaning into affordable and green-certified product, where pricing at the DUS level can reach sub-5.50 percent all-in on 10-year terms for qualifying projects. Freddie Mac's Small Balance Loan program is competitive for deals in the $1 million to $7.5 million range on stabilized conventional multifamily, with index-based floating options also available. Both agencies are scrutinizing rent-controlled assets in California more carefully than they were 18 months ago, with particular attention to operating expense trends on older stock in Los Angeles and San Francisco. Loan-to-value maximums on rent-stabilized California product have tightened at several DUS lenders to 65 percent versus the 75 percent available on comparable uncontrolled assets in other states.

Banks and Credit Unions

Regional and community banks remain a bifurcated story. Well-capitalized institutions with manageable CRE concentration ratios are competing for relationship-based construction lending, stabilized multifamily in suburban markets, and owner-occupied commercial deals. Recourse is nearly universal. Community development financial institutions and credit unions are active on owner-occupied deals and smaller multifamily below $5 million in specific geographies. The institutions that are constrained are sitting out selectively rather than across the board: declining acquisition financing on value-add office, retail in non-core markets, and construction in markets with softening fundamentals. For borrowers seeking bank debt in September, relationship, deposit correlation, and a clean operating history are the three most important factors driving willingness to quote.

Debt Funds and Specialty Lenders

Bridge debt funds raised in 2024 and 2025 are actively seeking deployment, and competition within this category has compressed spreads modestly relative to Q1 2026. Senior bridge from institutional debt funds on multifamily value-add is currently pricing at SOFR plus 275 to 375 basis points at 70 to 75 percent loan-to-cost, with 24-month initial terms and extension options. Mezzanine and preferred equity for the right deals starts at $5 million and is pricing at 11 to 14 percent depending on attachment point, property type, and sponsor track record. Construction-to-perm from non-bank lenders remains available but requires demonstrated pre-leasing or presales milestones in most cases. Construction lenders want to see 20 to 25 percent equity in the deal and are underwriting exit values conservatively.

Property Type Commentary

Multifamily

Multifamily continues to anchor institutional capital appetite, even as rent growth in several oversupplied sunbelt markets has flatlined through mid-2026. Southern California fundamentals are more stable: Los Angeles vacancy for market-rate product is running approximately 4.8 percent, and effective rent growth has returned to the low single digits. New supply completions in LA and the Inland Empire are slowing as the 2021 to 2023 construction pipeline delivers, which positions the next 18 to 24 months favorably for existing owners. Agency debt is the preferred execution for stabilized assets. Value-add with meaningful renovation scope is finding competitive bridge capital. Ground-up construction underwriting requires strong absorption modeling and a clear permanent loan exit.

Industrial

Industrial remains the most liquid asset class for permanent debt. Life companies and CMBS are competing aggressively for single-tenant net-lease industrial, and cap rate compression has resumed modestly at sub-5.5 percent for institutional quality product in the Inland Empire and Southern California. Last-mile and infill industrial in gateway markets continues to attract the tightest spreads. Vacancy has ticked up from cycle lows, with the Inland Empire approaching 6 percent, but net absorption is still positive and rent levels are holding. For construction financing on speculative industrial, lenders want to see strong broker pre-marketing activity and borrower contribution above 20 percent of total cost.

Office

Office remains the most constrained sector for capital formation with few exceptions. Trophy Class A product in Beverly Hills, Century City, and specific West LA submarkets is finding life company interest at conservative leverage, generally 50 to 55 percent LTV on buildings with 80 percent-plus occupancy and weighted average lease terms exceeding five years. Everything else is facing a narrow lender universe of special situation debt funds pricing at 400 to 600 basis points over SOFR with aggressive recourse and reserve structures. Suburban and commodity Class B office has almost no institutional bid for new originations.

Retail

Grocery-anchored and necessity-based retail is performing well from a leasing and debt markets perspective. CMBS and life companies are both writing grocery-anchored transactions. Non-anchored strip retail with strong occupancy and below-market rents is also finding conduit execution. Power centers with exposure to softgoods retail require more credit analysis. Experiential and food-and-beverage-anchored product is drawing investor attention in high-barrier coastal markets. Malls remain almost entirely outside the institutional lender appetite except for the top tier of dominant regional centers with demonstrable traffic and sales performance.

Hospitality

Select-service hotels in drive-to leisure markets and urban markets with strong group demand are executing well in CMBS. RevPAR trends through mid-2026 are positive in Southern California coastal markets, with ADR holding above $180 in Los Angeles coastal submarkets. Full-service and luxury product requires specialized lenders: CMBS is available but relationship-driven hotel lenders often provide better execution on flagged full-service product. Hospitality construction financing requires significant preopening reserves and lender familiarity with the brand and franchise structure.

Specialty: Self-Storage, Data Centers, Medical Office, Senior Living

Self-storage fundamentals have softened in markets that saw outsized supply additions, but infill urban product in Southern California is still attracting life company and agency interest on a case-by-case basis. Data center financing is active but specialized: lenders want long-term hyperscaler commitments and proven operator track records. Medical office buildings with health system credit tenancy are among the tightest-spread assets in the life company universe right now, routinely pricing inside multifamily. Senior living is seeing renewed lender interest as occupancy recovery has been consistent through 2025 and 2026, but underwriting remains conservative on leverage and HUD remains the long-term financing vehicle of choice for skilled nursing and assisted living.

Regulatory and Policy Watch

In California, the operational impact of Executive Directive 1 (ED1) continues to evolve. While the streamlined approvals pathway for 100 percent affordable projects has functioned as designed for ministerial review in most jurisdictions, a small number of city councils have sought to layer back discretionary conditions, and litigation risk on those projects is not fully resolved. AB 2011, which allows by-right residential development along commercial corridors, is generating more feasibility studies than actual groundbreakings, largely because construction cost and affordable set-aside requirements compress pro formas at current debt and equity costs. Lenders are underwriting AB 2011 projects with the same rigor as discretionary entitlements.

Measure ULA, Los Angeles's additional transfer tax on transactions above $5 million (4 percent) and above $10 million (5.5 percent), remains a persistent underwriting factor. Buyers are either pricing it into acquisition models, negotiating seller credits, or structuring transactions to mitigate exposure through entity transfers where legally defensible. Capital markets professionals underwriting Los Angeles assets need to run a hard ULA line-item calculation on every transaction projection.

At the federal level, GSE reform conversation has picked up modestly in Washington, though no legislation is considered imminent. The more immediate concern is the trajectory of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac annual volume caps, which were adjusted earlier in 2026. Agency DUS lenders are managing carefully to avoid allocation exhaustion before year-end, which may compress agency execution availability in Q4. FHA multifamily and healthcare programs continue to be a long-duration financing option for the right deals, with 35-year amortization available on stabilized multifamily, though timeline (typically 12 to 18 months) limits applicability to patient sponsors.

What We Are Seeing on Active Deals

Commercial Lending Solutions is currently working across a diverse pipeline that reflects both the opportunities and the friction points described above. A few observations without identifying details:

On a 96-unit multifamily value-add acquisition in the San Gabriel Valley, we are running a parallel process between agency small balance and a debt fund bridge product. The agency execution offers better rate but requires 90-day stabilization documentation the seller cannot provide at close; the debt fund gets the deal done with a 12-month stabilization window and an agency takeout structure already mapped. This kind of parallel process is increasingly common and should be standard practice on any acquisition with a lease-up story.

A $22 million net-lease industrial refinance in the Inland Empire is in final credit on a 10-year life company term loan at a spread inside 150 basis points. The deal pencils for the borrower because the existing floating rate debt originated in late 2022 has become expensive relative to current fixed-rate alternatives, and the property's five-year lease term with a creditworthy tenant satisfies the life company's weighted average lease term requirements.

A mixed-use ground floor retail and upper-floor office asset in West Hollywood is illustrating exactly why the conduit market requires careful asset selection: the office component is performing but the lender is stress-testing lease rollover conservatively, and the retail component is 100 percent occupied but with shorter-term leases. The deal is executable in conduit but at lower proceeds than the borrower initially modeled. A mezzanine layer is being considered to bridge the gap.

We are also tracking several note purchase opportunities on office assets where the original lender is moving toward resolution. These are attracting interest from high-yield and special situation funds, and several have turned into operational real estate acquisitions for well-capitalized buyers willing to take on the repositioning risk.

Month Ahead Outlook

First, watch the September 17 FOMC meeting closely. Markets are pricing roughly a 35 percent probability of a 25-basis-point cut. If the Fed moves, expect a brief Treasury rally followed by spread normalization; the net effect on all-in mortgage pricing is likely modest but the psychological impact on transaction volume could be meaningful heading into Q4.

Second, life company allocation windows are closing. Most major platforms will reach 85 to 90 percent of annual allocation by mid-October, and many will stop accepting new applications for funded-in-2026 deals by early November. For borrowers who want life company execution, the window to submit complete applications is September and early October, not after Thanksgiving.

Third, we expect increased distressed asset transaction activity in Q4 as lenders and special servicers who have been extending maturities push toward resolution before year-end balance sheet reporting. Well-capitalized buyers with predefined underwriting criteria and capital committed should be ready to move on short timelines. Some of the most interesting risk-adjusted opportunities in the current market are sitting in note sale and deed-in-lieu processes that have not yet hit formal marketing channels.

Takeaways for Sponsors and Borrowers

  • The fixed-rate refinancing window is real and it is open right now. Borrowers carrying floating-rate debt on stabilized assets should model a fixed-rate exit with current life company and agency terms; the case for locking in has not been stronger since early 2022.
  • Life company applications submitted after mid-October face meaningful execution risk for year-end funding. Move sooner than you think you need to.
  • Do not underestimate Measure ULA on Los Angeles acquisitions or dispositions. Model it at the outset, not as an afterthought during due diligence.
  • Parallel process two capital sources on any acquisition with a lease-up or value-add component. Agency and bridge are not mutually exclusive; running them simultaneously protects execution certainty.
  • Mezzanine and preferred equity above $5 million are available at rates that are elevated but manageable if the senior debt is appropriately sized and the business plan is credible. Stacked capital structures are working for the right deals.
  • Complete documentation packages close faster and price better. Lenders who are competing for quality deals are rewarding borrowers who arrive with clean rent rolls, trailing 12 operating statements, current inspection reports, and a clear business plan narrative.
