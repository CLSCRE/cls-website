Commercial Real Estate Capital Markets Report: May 11, 2026 Treasury Yields and Deal Activity

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

May 11, 2026

Every two weeks we pull the data that actually moves commercial mortgage pricing and synthesize what it means for borrowers trying to close deals. This is the May 11, 2026 edition of the CLS CRE Capital Markets Report. Here is what we are watching across the Treasury curve, the lending landscape, and active deal flow.

US Treasury Yield Curve: Month-Over-Month Comparison

The most important benchmark for fixed-rate commercial real estate debt is the US Treasury yield curve, and the month-over-month picture heading into mid-May 2026 tells a nuanced story. Rather than a uniform shift up or down across maturities, the curve has continued to steepen modestly at the intermediate tenors that matter most for CRE pricing: the 5-year and 10-year.

Why does this matter for deals? Permanent agency debt, life company loans, and CMBS execution are all indexed off the 10-year Treasury. When the 10-year moves even 15 to 20 basis points in a single month, a borrower who was underwriting a 6.50% all-in rate two weeks ago may be looking at 6.65% or higher today. That gap compresses debt service coverage ratios, sometimes enough to reduce loan proceeds or require additional equity. Sponsors who lock their rate at application rather than waiting for closing protect themselves from that slippage.

The short end of the curve, anchored by Federal Reserve policy, has remained relatively stable compared to the belly and long end. That dynamic continues to make floating-rate bridge debt comparatively attractive for value-add and transitional assets where a borrower needs 24 to 36 months of runway before stabilization.

What Lenders Are Pricing Right Now

Across the lender conversations and term sheets we are actively working in May 2026, a few themes stand out.

Life insurance companies remain the most competitive execution for stabilized, well-leased assets in primary and strong secondary markets. Spreads from this lender cohort have held firm in the low-to-mid 150s over the 10-year Treasury for five-year fixed terms, with modest step-ups for seven- and ten-year paper. Leverage caps at 60 to 65 percent loan-to-value remain the norm, and prepayment structures are typically yield maintenance or a declining schedule. If your asset qualifies, life company debt is the tightest all-in cost of capital available in the market right now.

Regional and community banks are selectively active on shorter-term balance sheet loans, particularly for sponsors with existing deposit relationships. These lenders are pricing in the mid-to-high 6 percent range on 3 to 5 year fixed terms, often with a 25-year amortization. Credit appetite varies considerably by geography and asset type, with industrial and anchored retail getting better looks than unanchored retail or office.

Private bridge lenders continue to fill the gap for transitional assets, lease-up plays, and situations where speed or certainty of execution outweighs cost. Bridge rates in May 2026 are generally ranging from SOFR plus 300 to SOFR plus 450 depending on sponsorship, asset quality, and exit strategy clarity. A lender willing to close in 30 days on a value-add multifamily acquisition with a credible business plan is worth a lot more than a theoretical permanent loan that takes 90 days to close on an asset that is not yet stabilized.

Deal Activity and Transaction Flow

Transaction volume in April and early May has been meaningfully better than the same period in 2025. Sellers and buyers are finding more common ground on pricing as rate expectations have stabilized, and the bid-ask spread that froze deal flow through much of 2024 has narrowed across several product types.

Multifamily continues to lead volume by a wide margin. Industrial remains well-bid. Retail transactions are happening but require a clear credit story at the tenant level. Office is transacting selectively, primarily in high-barrier coastal markets and for assets with strong in-place occupancy, and even then at meaningful discounts to replacement cost.

Refinance activity has picked up as well. Borrowers with loans originated in 2021 and 2022 that are approaching maturity are working through their options now rather than waiting for rates to fall further. In many cases the math on a bridge-to-perm structure, or a short-term extension followed by a permanent takeout, pencils better today than it did six months ago because net operating income has grown while values have partially recovered from their 2023 and 2024 lows.

What This Means for Your Next Deal

The capital markets environment in May 2026 rewards preparation and speed. Lenders are open for business but they are selective, and the gap between a well-prepared loan package and a disorganized one shows up directly in rate, leverage, and certainty of close. Knowing which lenders are actively quoting your asset type, in your market, at your basis, is the difference between a smooth close and a frustrating process.
