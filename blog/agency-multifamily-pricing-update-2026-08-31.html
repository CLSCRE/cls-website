Fannie Mae DUS and Freddie Mac Optigo Pricing Update (Week of August 31, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·August 31, 2026

Agency Multifamily Pricing: Where Things Stand Heading Into Fall

As we move through the final stretch of summer 2026, agency multifamily pricing across both Fannie Mae DUS and Freddie Mac Optigo platforms is telling a story worth paying close attention to. Spreads have been firming in the mid-to-upper range of where they've traded over the past two quarters, with benchmark Treasury yields holding in a range that continues to push all-in coupons higher than many sponsors had modeled going into the year. For borrowers sitting on floating-rate bridge debt with near-term maturities, the urgency to engage agency execution is real, and the pricing environment rewards sponsors who come to the table prepared.

On the Fannie side, DUS fixed-rate spreads for standard conventional deals are trading in a range that reflects continued caution from lenders around rent growth assumptions and valuation support in secondary and tertiary markets. Primary markets with demonstrated occupancy stability are pricing tighter, while assets in markets showing concession pressure or softening absorption are seeing spread premiums that can move the needle meaningfully on debt coverage. Freddie Optigo conventional pricing is running largely in parallel, with competitive tension between the two agencies still present but somewhat compressed compared to the frothier execution environment of 2021 and early 2022.

Green Advantage and TAH Premiums: Still Worth the Work

One of the more consistent value levers available to sponsors right now is the spread reduction available through Fannie Mae's Green Advantage program and Freddie Mac's comparable green certification pathway. For deals that can support third-party energy and water benchmarking, the pricing benefit remains in a range that meaningfully offsets the cost and time associated with the certification process. Sponsors who have embedded green building standards into their construction or rehabilitation scope are seeing this translate to lower all-in coupons relative to standard execution, and in the current rate environment that differential matters more than it did when absolute rates were lower.

Targeted Affordable Housing, or TAH, executions through both agencies continue to carry a notable pricing premium for deals with income restrictions and regulatory agreements in place. If your asset has a recorded Land Use Restrictive Agreement, HAP contract, or other affordability covenant, make sure your capital markets advisor is running full parallel analyses across both agency platforms. The TAH lane is not a niche product. It is a structurally advantaged execution that can result in proceeds and pricing that outperform market-rate alternatives on eligible assets by a meaningful margin. Sponsors coming out of LIHTC deals or acquiring Section 8-assisted properties should be building TAH pricing into their initial underwriting, not treating it as an afterthought.

SBL Pricing in the $1M to $7.5M Deal Zone

The Small Balance Loan programs from both Fannie and Freddie remain one of the most underutilized execution paths for sponsors active in the $1 million to $7.5 million loan range. Freddie Mac SBL in particular has continued to show competitive spread performance relative to conventional bank execution, and streamlined documentation requirements make it a viable option for borrowers who want agency quality of execution without the overhead of a full-size DUS deal.

Pricing in the SBL range is currently trading in a zone that is modestly wider than large-loan agency execution, which is expected given the relative loan size and portfolio concentration dynamics. However, the fixed-rate certainty and non-recourse structure available through SBL continue to make it attractive compared to community bank or credit union alternatives in this size band, particularly as smaller balance sheet lenders have been selective about new commitments. For sponsors with smaller garden-style communities, workforce housing assets, or individual properties within a larger portfolio that fall in this size bracket, SBL deserves a serious look in the current stack.

One dynamic worth watching: SBL volume across both agencies has been building through mid-year, and that pipeline activity can influence lender bandwidth and rate lock availability. Sponsors who are targeting a Q4 close should be initiating conversations and moving through application now rather than waiting for deal conditions to be perfect.

Actionable Takeaways for Sponsors Planning Fall Closings

The agencies are open and active, but pricing rewards preparation. Sponsors who can demonstrate strong trailing occupancy, clean expense histories, and a credible rent roll will access tighter spread execution. Green certifications and affordability overlays are not just impact plays. They are yield optimization tools in the current environment. And for smaller balance deals, the SBL market is functioning well and offers non-recourse fixed-rate execution that the broader market is not consistently matching.
