Life Insurance Company Commercial Real Estate Loans: Rates, Requirements, and How to Access Them

By Trevor Damyan· June 25, 2026 ·Commercial Lending Solutions

Why Life Insurance Company Loans Are the Gold Standard for Permanent Commercial Financing

In 25 years of commercial mortgage brokerage, the single most underutilized capital source I encounter is life insurance company lending. Investors who qualify routinely leave 50 to 100 basis points on the table because they default to their bank or a CMBS conduit. On a $10 million loan over 10 years, that spread difference is $500,000 to $1,000,000 in excess interest. The reason most borrowers never access this capital is simple: life company lenders are relationship-driven, selective, and not visible in the way banks and CMBS originators are. You have to know where the door is.

This guide explains exactly how life insurance company commercial real estate lending works, what it takes to qualify, what the loan looks like structurally, and how CLS CRE's direct correspondent relationship with Symetra Life Insurance Company gives our clients a fundamentally different path to this capital than shopping a package through a broker.

How Life Company Lending Differs From Every Other Capital Source

Portfolio Lending: No Securitization Risk

Life insurance companies make commercial real estate loans to hold on their own balance sheet and match against their long-term policy liabilities. They are not securitizing, not syndicating, not packaging into a CMBS trust. This single structural fact changes everything about how the loan is underwritten, priced, and serviced.

Because a life company holds the loan for its life, it underwrites the asset the way a long-term owner would: conservatively, with genuine attention to tenancy quality, lease structure, market depth, and property management. They are not optimizing for AAA tranche execution the way a CMBS conduit does. They are optimizing for 30 years of reliable cash flow into their investment portfolio.

The practical consequences of portfolio lending:

  • More flexibility on loan structure: partial releases, substitutions, interest-only periods, and non-standard property configurations are negotiable on a case-by-case basis
  • Relationship-based decisions: the loan officer who reviews your deal has authority; there is no faceless committee reviewing a securitization pool
  • Consistent servicing: you know who services your loan for the life of the term
  • No call risk: CMBS borrowers have experienced servicer complications during refinance, maturity extensions, and modifications; life company borrowers do not have a bondholder class to satisfy

Rate Advantage: The Real Numbers

Life company loans have historically priced 25 to 100 basis points below CMBS on equivalent assets. In mid-2026, a well-leased Class A office or multifamily deal in a primary market can clear life company pricing in the 5.75% to 6.50% range on a 10-year fixed term, depending on LTV, DSCR, and property type. The equivalent CMBS execution for the same asset is typically 50 to 75 basis points higher. Bank debt on a 5-year fixed is higher still, carries refinancing risk at maturity, and comes with personal recourse in most cases.

The compounding effect matters: a borrower who places a 10-year life company loan avoids one full refinancing cycle compared to a 5-year bank loan, eliminating closing costs, rate reset risk, and lender re-approval at a potentially worse point in the cycle.

Non-Recourse as a Standard Feature

Life company loans are non-recourse by default, with standard carve-outs for fraud, environmental indemnification, and voluntary bankruptcy filing. Unlike bank lending, where personal guaranty is nearly universal on commercial loans under $20 million, life company lending does not tie up the borrower's personal balance sheet. For high-net-worth operators with multiple assets, this is a meaningful structural advantage.

Asset Quality Requirements: What Life Companies Actually Want

Life insurance companies are the most selective commercial real estate lenders in the market. They do not chase yield. They prioritize credit quality, asset stability, and market depth above all else. Understanding their requirements in detail is the difference between a qualifying deal and a wasted application.

Occupancy and Tenancy Standards

Life companies require stabilized assets. Typical minimum occupancy thresholds:

  • Multifamily: 90% physical occupancy for the trailing 12 months
  • Office / Industrial / Retail: 90% economic occupancy with creditworthy tenants on leases with 5+ years of weighted average lease term (WALT) remaining
  • Tenant credit: Investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenants are strongly preferred; high concentrations in a single tenant above 25-30% of revenue will require additional credit support

What disqualifies an asset immediately: vacancy above 10-15%, value-add business plans, lease-up assumptions in the underwriting, or tenants on leases expiring within 24 months of closing. If your asset is not yet stabilized, the right path is a bridge loan first. Close the lease-up, season the occupancy for 12 months, then transition to permanent life company financing.

Market Requirements

Life companies lend in primary and strong secondary markets. The definition of "primary" has expanded in recent years to include Sun Belt metros like Phoenix, Austin, Nashville, and Charlotte alongside the legacy gateways. What they avoid: tertiary markets with populations under 150,000 to 200,000, markets with single-employer dependency, and markets with demonstrated negative absorption trends in the relevant property type.

Underwriting Parameters: The Real Thresholds

Life company underwriting is more conservative on LTV than banks or CMBS but compensates with better rates and longer terms. These are the thresholds you need to underwrite to before presenting a deal:

The Rise of Debt Yield as a Primary Constraint

In the current rate environment, debt yield has become as important as DSCR in life company underwriting. Debt yield is calculated as NOI divided by loan amount. At a 7.5% minimum debt yield, a property generating $750,000 in NOI can support a maximum loan of $10,000,000, regardless of what the appraised value supports.

This matters because in markets where cap rates have compressed below 5%, LTV and DSCR calculations can theoretically support higher loan amounts than debt yield will allow. Sophisticated borrowers should run all three tests (LTV, DSCR, and debt yield) before approaching any lender. The binding constraint determines your actual loan sizing.

Rate Structure and Pricing Mechanics

Fixed Rate for the Full Term

Life company loans are fixed rate for the entire loan term, whether that is 10, 15, 20, 25, or 30 years. This is a structural feature, not an option. When you close a life company loan at 6.00%, you will pay 6.00% in year 28 if you still hold the asset. This certainty has compounding value for long-term hold strategies that is difficult to quantify but very real.

Treasury Spread Pricing

Life company rates are priced as a spread above the corresponding US Treasury yield. A 10-year life company loan is priced off the 10-year Treasury; a 20-year loan is priced off the 20-year Treasury. The spread reflects the life company's required yield premium over risk-free rates, factoring in credit risk, illiquidity premium, and loan servicing costs.

In mid-2026, typical life company spreads are running:

  • Multifamily (Class A, primary market): 130 to 175 basis points over 10-year Treasury
  • Industrial (long-term NNN, credit tenant): 140 to 190 basis points over corresponding Treasury
  • Office (Class A, primary market): 175 to 250 basis points over 10-year Treasury, reflecting current sector uncertainty
  • Retail (grocery-anchored): 160 to 210 basis points over 10-year Treasury

With the 10-year Treasury in the 4.25% to 4.50% range as of mid-2026, all-in rates for the strongest multifamily and industrial deals are clearing in the 5.60% to 6.25% range. Equivalent CMBS pricing for comparable assets is typically 50 to 75 basis points higher.

Rate Lock Timing

Rate lock happens at commitment, not at application. This means there is a period between application submission and commitment issuance (typically 30 to 60 days) during which the rate floats. Borrowers closing in a rising rate environment need to account for this. Some life companies offer forward rate locks for an additional spread premium, typically 10 to 20 basis points per month of forward lock. This is negotiable and deal-dependent.

Prepayment: The Real Constraint and How to Think About It

Life company loans are not flexible debt. The prepayment structures are the trade-off for the long-term fixed rate, and borrowers who do not understand this going in make expensive mistakes.

Yield Maintenance

The most common prepayment structure on life company loans is yield maintenance. The prepayment premium is calculated as the present value of the remaining interest payments, discounted at the current Treasury yield for the remaining loan term. In practical terms, if you locked a 6.00% rate and Treasuries have since fallen to 4.00%, you would owe the present value of the 2.00% rate differential across the remaining loan term. On a $10 million loan with 8 years remaining, this could be a prepayment penalty of $1.5 million or more.

Defeasance

Some life company loans, particularly those originated through CMBS-style execution or with agency-adjacent terms, use defeasance. Rather than paying a cash penalty, the borrower substitutes a portfolio of US Treasuries for the collateral property, maintaining the lender's cash flow stream. Defeasance is administratively complex and typically costs $50,000 to $150,000 in legal and structuring fees on top of the Treasury purchase.

Strategy: Match Asset Hold Period to Loan Term

The correct use of a life company loan is on a stabilized, long-term hold asset. If there is any meaningful probability you will sell within 5 to 7 years, the prepayment penalty erodes the rate advantage. For a 30-year hold multifamily, an anchored retail center, or a build-to-core industrial property, the life company loan is the right permanent vehicle. For assets with near-term disposition potential, bank debt or CMBS with step-down prepayment or open periods is more appropriate.

I always ask clients to be honest about their hold intentions before recommending life company financing. The rate savings are real, but they require commitment.

Minimum Loan Sizes and Closing Timeline

Life companies focus on institutional loan sizes. Symetra and most other life company lenders have a practical minimum of $5 million, with the best execution available on loans of $10 million and above. Below $5 million, the operational overhead of life company underwriting and servicing is not economical, and other lenders (CMBS, regional banks, credit unions) will offer more competitive execution.

Closing timeline is longer than CMBS or bank debt. Expect 60 to 90 days from complete application to closing. Life companies conduct thorough third-party due diligence: full FIRREA appraisal, Phase I environmental, property condition report, and title review. The underwriting process is deliberate. Borrowers who need to close in 30 to 45 days should use bridge financing and refinance into life company debt after stabilization.

How to Prepare a Life Company Loan Application

Life company applications require complete, institutional-quality packages. A thin submission creates delay and erodes credibility. The baseline documentation package:

  • Rent roll: Current, signed leases for all tenants; tenant contact and credit information for any tenant above 10% of revenue
  • Operating statements: 3 years of income and expense history plus current year-to-date actuals
  • Property management agreements
  • Schedule of current financing: All existing debt on the property being refinanced
  • Borrower financial statements: 2 years personal and entity tax returns, current personal financial statement, liquidity verification
  • Property description: Year built, renovation history, site plan, survey, photos
  • Market overview: Vacancy rates, comparable rents, absorption data for the submarket

For deals submitted through CLS CRE's correspondent relationship, I review this package before it goes into formal underwriting. A preliminary assessment call typically takes 20 to 30 minutes and can identify disqualifying issues before you invest in the third-party reports.

Who Should Pursue Life Company Financing

The ideal life company borrower:

  • Owns a stabilized Class A or strong Class B asset in a primary or secondary market
  • Has 90%+ occupancy with creditworthy tenants on long-term leases
  • Needs a loan of $5 million or more
  • Plans to hold the asset for 10 years or longer
  • Wants non-recourse, long-term fixed rate debt and can accept conservative LTV
  • Has equity in the asset to support 55-65% LTV underwriting

If your asset is not yet stabilized (if you are in lease-up, completing a renovation, or running below 90% occupancy), the right sequence is a bridge loan to stabilize the asset, then a refinance into life company permanent debt.
