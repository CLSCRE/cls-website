TCAC Allocation Round Insights for 9% LIHTC Applicants (Week of August 31, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·August 31, 2026

Reading the Room: What the Latest TCAC 9% Round Is Telling the Market

Each TCAC allocation round functions as a pressure test, revealing where the pipeline is thickening, where set-aside buckets are over-subscribed, and which project characteristics are separating funded deals from the waitlist. The most recent 9% round delivered a set of signals that any developer planning a 2026 or early 2027 application should be studying carefully. At CLS CRE, we track these dynamics closely because capital structure decisions made in predevelopment are directly shaped by how competitive the scoring environment looks at the moment of application.

The headline observation from this round is familiar but worth restating with force: demand for 9% credits continues to substantially outpace available volume across most geographic set-asides. Application counts relative to available credit authority remained elevated, and the spread between the lowest funded score and the highest unfunded score in several set-asides was narrow enough that single-point scoring decisions became decisive. That is not a forgiving margin, and it demands a level of pre-application diligence that many teams still underestimate.

Scoring Trends and Set-Aside Pressure Points

Competitive scoring pressure was not uniform across the state. Rural and small development set-asides continued to attract a concentrated field of experienced operators, which kept those pools competitive despite their structural protections. At-risk preservation applicants faced similar dynamics, with site control timing and rehabilitation scope documentation emerging as recurring differentiators during TCAC review.

On the general pool side, geographic tie-breaker mechanics played a larger role in final outcomes than in some prior rounds, reinforcing how important it is for applicants to understand not just their raw score but where they are likely to land within their county or region relative to competitors who may be pursuing the same set-aside category.

Basis limits and eligible basis calculations also generated friction for a meaningful share of applicants. Projects in higher-cost markets are bumping against difficult cost certification thresholds, and the tension between land and construction cost inflation on one side and basis cap constraints on the other is compressing developer fee projections in ways that can affect scoring under several TCAC point categories. Teams that have not stress-tested their cost assumptions against current hard cost indices and recent prevailing wage escalation are entering the round with a structural disadvantage.

Readiness documentation remained a consistent differentiator. Applications that arrived with local approvals substantially in place, environmental clearances advanced, and utility commitments documented outperformed those relying on post-award timelines to resolve preconditions. TCAC's scoring framework rewards readiness, and lenders evaluating bridge and construction exposure feel the same way.

Capital Structure Considerations Going Into the Next Round

From a capital markets perspective, what we observe on the debt side mirrors the scoring dynamics. Lenders across the spectrum, including mission-driven CDFIs, specialty affordable debt funds, and agency-aligned construction lenders, are applying sharper underwriting scrutiny to projects with unresolved site control, soft-money gaps that depend on County or City approvals not yet in hand, and operating proformas that assume rent levels or operating cost structures inconsistent with current market data.

For applicants targeting the next round, the practical implication is that financing conversations should begin well before the application window opens. A lender's preliminary term sheet or letter of interest is increasingly functioning as a signal of project viability during internal scoring review, and developers who arrive at application without a credible debt story are carrying unnecessary risk into an already competitive pool.

Equity pricing assumptions also warrant a fresh look. Tax credit pricing has moved within a range that reflects both investor appetite and broader interest rate conditions, and deals underwritten to equity pricing assumptions that are even modestly stale can produce pro forma gaps that complicate both TCAC review and lender confidence. Developers should be pressure-testing equity assumptions with active syndicators or investor-direct partners, not relying on pricing from closings that are twelve or more months old.

What to Do Before the Next Round Opens

The developers who consistently perform well across TCAC allocation rounds share a few common disciplines. They advance local entitlements and environmental work on an aggressive timeline relative to the application deadline. They model their scoring position across multiple set-aside scenarios rather than assuming a single path to allocation. They engage capital partners early enough to surface structure issues before those issues become disqualifying. And they build enough schedule cushion to respond substantively if TCAC issues a request for additional information during review.

For teams currently in predevelopment or moving through entitlement, this is the window to pressure-test your deal against the dynamics this round has surfaced. A project that is six to nine months from application eligibility should already be in active capital structure conversations.
