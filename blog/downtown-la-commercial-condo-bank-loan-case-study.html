Case Study: $1.325M Bank Loan for a Commercial Condo in Downtown Los Angeles

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $1,325,000 bank loan through HAB Bank for a commercial condo unit in downtown Los Angeles. The loan closed at 7.25% fixed for the first five years (floating at prime with a matching floor for years six through ten), amortized over 25 years, capped at 63% loan-to-value, with a step-down prepayment penalty and a required deposit banking relationship.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal brought us a downtown Los Angeles commercial condo unit needing a straightforward, fixed-rate bank loan. The ownership group had already been through this financing process with us on other commercial condo holdings, and this unit needed its own independent underwriting sized to its own appraised value and income.

Downtown Los Angeles' commercial condo market gives owners flexibility to hold and finance individual units separately, which matters when a bank's loan-to-value and debt service requirements are specific to each unit's own characteristics rather than a blanket portfolio structure.

Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in placing bank financing for commercial condo owners and investors across Los Angeles and the wider California market, and on this deal a bank was the right fit for a straightforward, fixed-rate structure sized to a single unit.

What Made It Hard

  • A conservative loan-to-value ceiling. At a maximum 63% loan-to-value, the loan had to be sized carefully against an independent appraisal of the unit, leaving real equity behind the bank's position.
  • Full institutional documentation on a relatively small loan. Title insurance, survey, borrower's counsel opinion, corporate resolutions, and ongoing annual financial reporting requirements all applied at the same level of rigor as a much larger commercial loan.
  • A required banking relationship. The lender conditioned the loan on the borrower maintaining its primary deposit account with HAB Bank and routing all rental and business income through it, a real operational commitment beyond just the mortgage itself.
"What made this one work was refusing to treat it like a scaled down version of a bigger deal. We sized it against its own appraisal and its own income from day one, so there was nothing to renegotiate once the bank got deep into underwriting."
— Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (DRE #02244836), placed this loan with HAB Bank, with the commitment letter issued June 28, 2024:

  • $1,325,000 secured by a first deed of trust, assignment of rents and leases, and a UCC-1 filing against the condo unit
  • 7.25% fixed for the first five years, then floating at the prime rate for years six through ten, subject to a 7.25% floor
  • 25-year amortization with monthly payments of $9,577.19, structured as 59 payments followed by a balloon at the 60th month unless the 5-year renewal option is exercised
  • A minimum 1.25x debt service coverage ratio throughout the loan, with the bank retaining discretion to raise the rate rather than call a default if coverage fell short
  • 63% maximum loan-to-value based on an independent appraisal satisfactory to the lender
  • A step-down prepayment penalty: 3% in year one, 2% in year two, 1% in year three, and no penalty in years four and five, waived entirely if refinanced with HAB Bank
  • A $9,937.50 non-refundable commitment fee, deemed earned upon issuance of the commitment regardless of whether the loan closed
  • A required demand deposit relationship, with the borrower obligated to maintain its primary account at HAB Bank and deposit all rental income and business proceeds there
  • A personal guaranty from the principal(s)/beneficial owner(s), plus a 15-percentage-point default rate increase if any loan document obligation was breached

The loan closed inside the commitment window, giving the ownership group a fixed-rate facility on this specific unit structured around its own value and income, independent of any other holdings.

Why We Won This One

Underwriting each commercial condo unit on its own merits, rather than trying to force a blanket facility across multiple holdings, is what let this loan close cleanly at bank pricing. The conservative 63% loan-to-value ceiling meant sizing the request carefully against the unit's actual appraised value from the outset, avoiding a renegotiation later in underwriting.

Working through the deposit relationship requirement and annual reporting obligations up front, rather than treating them as afterthoughts, kept the closing on schedule within the commitment's expiration window, a real constraint on bank commitment letters that expire if the loan doesn't close in time.
