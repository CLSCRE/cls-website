Industrial Build-to-Suit Credit Tenant Update (Week of July 06, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·July 06, 2026

Build-to-Suit Activity Finds Its Footing in Mid-2026

After roughly eighteen months of compressed deal flow driven by rate volatility and tenant hesitation on long-term commitments, the industrial build-to-suit market for investment-grade logistics tenants is showing meaningful signs of recovery heading into the second half of 2026. Forward pipelines at several major third-party logistics operators, e-commerce fulfillment platforms, and cold-chain distributors have reopened in earnest, and developers who maintained entitlement-ready sites through the downturn are now finding themselves in a materially stronger negotiating position than they were twelve months ago.

The key dynamic right now is selectivity on both sides of the table. Tenants with investment-grade credit profiles are pursuing larger, purpose-built footprints in infill and last-mile corridors where existing supply has been absorbed, while stepping back from speculative interior markets where vacancy is still working through the system. For developers, that selectivity is actually welcome news. It concentrates activity around projects where the capital stack can be structured cleanly from day one, rather than deals requiring heroic assumptions about stabilization timing.

Forward Commitment Pricing: Where the Market Is Today

Permanent lender appetite for forward commitments on credit-tenant industrial projects has improved noticeably from the tight conditions of late 2024 and early 2025. Life insurance companies, which pulled back significantly on forward exposure during the rate reset, have re-entered the conversation for deals featuring long initial lease terms (generally fifteen years or better), investment-grade tenancy, and Class A construction specifications in supply-constrained markets.

Forward commitment spreads for qualifying projects are currently running wider than comparable in-place permanent loans by a range that reflects the extended construction timeline and rate lock risk, though that premium has narrowed relative to where it was during peak uncertainty. Developers should expect the all-in forward rate on a well-structured credit-tenant deal to sit in a range that still requires disciplined underwriting of development cost, particularly on land basis and hard cost escalation assumptions. Specialty debt funds have also been active in the forward commitment space for deals that fall outside the core life company box, including projects with shorter initial terms or in secondary logistics corridors, though pricing reflects the incremental risk profile accordingly.

One structural nuance worth flagging for development teams: forward commitment fees and rate lock mechanics have become more varied across the lender universe in 2026 than they were in the prior cycle. Some lenders are offering floating-to-fixed conversion structures at closing as an alternative to full rate locks at commitment issuance. Others are pricing longer rate lock windows at a meaningful premium. Understanding which structure best aligns with your projected completion timeline and cost certainty is an underwriting conversation that needs to happen early in the capitalization process, not at the construction loan closing.

Permanent Take-Out Conditions: What Lenders Are Underwriting

On the take-out side, the conditions lenders are imposing at commitment issuance have become more explicit and more consequential than they were during the looser underwriting environment of 2021 through 2023. Lenders are scrutinizing tenant credit quality with renewed discipline. Investment-grade tenants with strong parent guarantees and publicly reported balance sheets are clearing underwriting at leverage levels that remain competitive. Tenants in the investment-grade spectrum but closer to the crossover threshold are being underwritten more conservatively, with lenders applying haircuts to lease credit assumptions and, in some cases, requiring additional structural protections at the loan level.

Lease form and lease commencement mechanics have also come into sharper focus. Lenders want clarity on when the permanent loan obligation triggers relative to substantial completion, certificate of occupancy, and tenant acceptance. Gaps or ambiguity in those definitions have caused friction at commitment funding in several deals this cycle. Development teams should be coordinating lease form review with their permanent lender early, particularly on any provisions that give the tenant discretion over acceptance timing or buildout completion standards.

Cap rate assumptions embedded in forward commitments are also being stress-tested more rigorously. Lenders are not simply accepting developer pro formas at face value on exit value. They are running their own sensitivity analysis on where the credit-tenant industrial cap rate range could move between commitment issuance and loan origination, and sizing loan proceeds accordingly. Developers who built their equity return models on aggressive cap rate assumptions during the compression era may find that the available permanent loan proceeds at take-out are tighter than anticipated.

Actionable Takeaways for Developers Planning Q3 and Q4 Deals

For development teams with credit-tenant build-to-suit projects in predevelopment or entitlement right now, three priorities stand out. First, begin permanent lender conversations as early as the lease letter of intent stage. Forward commitment appetite is real but not unlimited, and lenders are being selective. Second, pressure-test your development cost underwriting against a range of hard cost outcomes. Forward commitments will be sized against stress scenarios, not best-case projections. Third, align your lease form with the permanent lender's take-out requirements before you finalize the tenant deal. Misalignment there is expensive and slow to fix.

The market is open for well-structured credit-tenant industrial transactions. The window favors developers who approach capitalization with discipline and preparation rather than those relying on market momentum to paper over underwriting gaps.
