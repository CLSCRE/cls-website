Case Study: $11M Cross-Collateralized Bridge Loan for a Two-Building R&D Portfolio in Sunnyvale

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged an $11,000,000 cross-collateralized bridge loan through a California private debt fund secured by first trust deeds on two leased research-and-development buildings in Sunnyvale, California. The loan closed at 7.90% fixed interest-only over a 12-month term with per-property partial reconveyance release pricing, no prepayment penalty, and no personal guaranty, consolidating and upsizing debt on one of the two buildings that the same lender had financed separately a year earlier.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal came from a repeat client we had placed a $4,500,000 loan for a year earlier on a single leased R&D building in Sunnyvale, California (CA). This time, the sponsor wanted to bring a second Sunnyvale R&D building into the capital structure alongside the first, consolidating both properties under one larger, more efficiently priced facility with a private bridge lender rather than carrying two separate loans. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in cross-collateralized bridge financing for R&D and industrial portfolios for investors across California and nationwide.

Sunnyvale sits at the center of Silicon Valley's R&D and light-industrial corridor, and combining two leased buildings in the same submarket under one loan gave the lender a larger, more diversified collateral pool to underwrite, exactly the kind of structure that supports a bigger loan at a better rate than either property could achieve alone.

What Made It Hard

  • Structuring a true cross-collateralized facility. Combining two separate properties under a single loan meant negotiating first trust deeds on both, plus an allocated loan amount and release price for each, rather than simply refinancing one property and leaving the other untouched.
  • Building in exit flexibility for either asset. The sponsor needed the ability to sell or refinance either building independently down the road without being forced to pay off the entire $11,000,000 loan, requiring a real partial reconveyance mechanism rather than an all-or-nothing payoff structure.
  • Scaling up with the same lender relationship. Moving from a $4.5 million single-asset loan to an $11 million two-asset facility with the same private lender required renegotiating pricing, fees, and structure at a materially larger scale.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), returned to a California private debt fund, the same Los Angeles-based private lender from the sponsor's earlier Sunnyvale loan, on a conditional offer dated November 12, 2025 and accepted on November 18, 2025:

  • $11,000,000 total loan secured by first trust deeds against two Sunnyvale properties, cross-collateralized under a single facility
  • 7.90% fixed rate, interest-only, with monthly payments of $72,416.67 drawn automatically from the borrower's account, a lower rate than the sponsor's earlier $4.5 million single-property loan at 8.40%
  • 12-month term, with an option to extend up to 12 additional months at 8.40% for one additional origination point
  • Partial reconveyance pricing set individually per property: $9,750,000 to release one building and $6,750,000 to release the other, each set at 150% of that property's allocated share of the loan, giving the sponsor a clear, pre-negotiated path to sell or refinance either asset independently
  • No prepayment penalty and no personal guaranty, matching the structure of the sponsor's earlier loan and again priced entirely on the strength of the real estate and its leases
  • Junior financing permitted, subject to the lender's consent, preserving flexibility to layer in additional capital later
  • 1.00 origination point to the lender ($110,000) and a 1.00 point broker fee, plus a nonrefundable $2,000 evaluation and processing fee due at acceptance
  • Closing within five days of the lender's receipt of a complete loan package, consistent with the lender's private balance-sheet underwriting speed

The loan closed cleanly, giving the sponsor a single, larger facility across both Sunnyvale buildings with a lower blended rate and a defined mechanism to release either property independently as their plans for the portfolio evolved.

"Two good buildings underwritten together are worth more to a lender than the same two buildings underwritten separately. That is the whole case for consolidating, and it showed up directly in the rate."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

The rate improvement between the sponsor's two loans with the same lender, from 8.40% on a single $4.5 million property loan to 7.90% on an $11 million two-property facility, shows what a larger, cross-collateralized pool of quality collateral can do to pricing with a private lender. Consolidating two strong assets under one loan let a California private debt fund underwrite more capital more efficiently, and passed real savings back to the sponsor.

Getting the partial reconveyance pricing negotiated correctly up front was just as important as the headline rate. Setting a clear, formulaic release price for each property, rather than leaving that negotiation for a future refinance or sale, gave the sponsor certainty about their exit options on either asset from day one.

Is This Structure Right for Your Deal?

If you own multiple leased commercial properties and want to consolidate financing for better pricing while preserving the flexibility to sell or refinance any one of them independently, a cross-collateralized bridge loan can be the right structure. Cross-collateralization means a single loan is secured by first trust deeds on more than one property, with the combined collateral supporting the full loan amount. This $11,000,000 loan was secured by first trust deeds on two separate Sunnyvale R&D buildings rather than financing each one separately.
A partial reconveyance lets the borrower sell or refinance one property out of a multi-property loan by paying a specified release price, without having to pay off the entire loan. This loan set release prices of $9,750,000 for one building and $6,750,000 for the other, each priced at 150% of that property's allocated share of the loan.
Consolidating separate collateral into one loan can streamline servicing, potentially improve pricing through greater diversification for the lender, and simplify the ownership group's overall debt structure, exactly what happened here as this $11,000,000 facility replaced financing that had previously existed at the property level.
