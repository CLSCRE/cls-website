Hard Money to Conventional: The Referral Partnership Every Private Lender Should Have

By Trevor Damyan·June 25, 2026·Commercial Lending Solutions

How Hard Money Lenders Can Turn Refinances Into a Referral Engine

I am Trevor Damyan, a commercial mortgage broker at CLS CRE in Los Angeles. Over the past decade placing commercial real estate loans from $500,000 to $50 million across all 50 states, some of my most productive referral relationships have not come from other brokers or attorneys. They have come from hard money lenders, private capital providers, and trust deed investors who realized something counterintuitive: referring your borrower to a conventional lender does not lose you a client. It locks them in for life.

This article is written for the private lending community: hard money shops, trust deed investors, mortgage fund managers, and bridge capital providers. If you lend at 9 to 13 percent on commercial real estate and your borrowers eventually stabilize their properties, this referral dynamic matters to you. Here is how it works and why it benefits both firms.

Why Hard Money Lenders Are Uniquely Positioned for CRE Financing Referrals

Private lenders occupy a critical inflection point in the commercial real estate lifecycle. You fund the deal that institutional capital will not touch: the value-add play with deferred maintenance, the 68 percent occupied multifamily, the ground-up construction project, the time-sensitive acquisition where a bank would take 90 days to close. Your capital makes the deal happen.

But your capital is expensive by design. That is the bargain. And the borrower knows, at the moment of closing, that the exit is a refinance into cheaper conventional debt once the business plan executes. The question is not whether they refinance. The question is who places that refinance and whether you stay in the relationship when it happens.

If you refer the takeout, you stay in the deal. Your borrower calls you first on the next acquisition because you handled the full cycle professionally. Your capital is freed up to deploy into the next bridge opportunity. And when your referral partner places a 10-year, 6.5 percent permanent loan on a property your borrower owns free-and-clear of your note, everyone wins.

Private lenders who systematize this referral behavior compound their deal flow. Private lenders who ignore it watch their borrowers disappear into the conventional lending market and never call back.

The Five Financing Scenarios Hard Money Lenders Face Most Often

1. The Borrower Has Stabilized and Is Ready for a Conventional Takeout

This is the most common scenario and the most natural referral. Your borrower closed a bridge loan 18 months ago on a 60-unit multifamily in Phoenix. Occupancy is now 94 percent. NOI has increased. The business plan worked. Your loan matures in six months and they need to refinance.

At this point they have multiple paths: agency financing through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac (if the property is 5+ units and qualifies), a bank balance sheet loan, a CMBS permanent loan, or a life insurance company fixed-rate note. Each path has different thresholds, documentation requirements, and pricing. A conventional commercial mortgage broker navigates all of them simultaneously and brings back the best execution.

Your job at this moment is to make the introduction early, before the borrower starts calling around on their own. If you introduce them to a broker you trust, that broker will keep you informed, protect your payoff timeline, and potentially earn you a referral fee depending on your state's licensing structure. More importantly, your borrower will remember that you handled the full cycle, not just the origination.

At CLS CRE, we regularly handle these takeouts. We have direct access to life insurance companies, CMBS conduits, regional banks, and agency platforms. We can often close within 45 to 75 days from application, which protects your maturity date.

2. The Private Lender Is at LTV Limit and the Borrower Needs Additional Capital

You lend at 65 percent of as-is value. Your borrower needs 75 percent to complete the acquisition or fund a capital improvement program. You are at your box limit. The deal is real, the borrower is creditworthy, and there is a gap.

In this situation a commercial mortgage broker can often place the piece you cannot hold. That might be a mezzanine loan sitting between your first deed of trust and the borrower's equity. It might be a preferred equity structure. It might be a senior first mortgage at a lower rate that displaces your position entirely and sizes larger because the debt service coverage math works at conventional rates.

When you bring a broker into this scenario early, before you commit and structure your piece, the whole capital stack can be designed more efficiently. We have placed mezzanine tranches alongside hard money firsts for mixed-use, multifamily, and industrial deals. The intercreditor agreement governs the relationship between positions, and we handle the documentation on the conventional side.

The practical benefit to you: the deal closes, your borrower succeeds, and you did not have to stretch beyond your underwriting standards to make it happen.

3. You Have a Performing Loan You Want to Sell or Refinance Out of Your Portfolio

This comes up in mortgage funds and trust deed pools more than it does in single-loan structures. You originated a performing first trust deed three years ago. The property has appreciated. The loan has amortized. And your fund now wants to redeploy that capital into higher-yield opportunities, or return capital to investors who are requesting liquidity.

A commercial mortgage broker can find the takeout lender to refinance the property and pay off your note at par. In some cases, where the note is performing and seasoned, we can also connect you with note buyers or secondary market participants who purchase performing CRE loans.

The borrower may or may not know the note sale is happening, depending on your loan documents. But if the borrower is refinancing into conventional debt, they are aware, and you control the timing by making the introduction early. We have handled payoffs on private notes ranging from $800,000 to $22 million. The process is routine for a commercial mortgage broker with the right capital market relationships.

4. The Borrower Came to You but Actually Qualifies for Agency or Bank Financing

This one requires honesty, and the hard money lenders who practice it build the strongest long-term reputations.

Sometimes a borrower walks in with a deal that looks like hard money but is actually bankable at a fraction of your rate. Maybe they have a 78 percent occupied 12-unit multifamily in a primary market. Maybe it is a stabilized net lease retail strip with a national tenant on a long-term lease. Maybe it is a small-balance multifamily that qualifies for Freddie Mac Small Balance or a bank CRA product.

If you fund a hard money loan on a deal that qualifies for conventional financing at 6.5 percent and charge 11 percent, your borrower will eventually figure that out. They will refinance, and they will not come back. If you refer them to a commercial mortgage broker who closes the conventional deal and tells the borrower "your hard money lender sent me," that borrower comes back to you for the next deal that actually needs bridge capital.

We screen deals quickly. Send us the rent roll, trailing 12 months of income and expenses, and a property description. Within 48 hours we can tell you whether the deal is conventionally financeable and at what approximate rate and terms. That call costs nothing. It earns you a relationship.

5. The Deal Is Outside Your Product Box: Too Large, Wrong Property Type, or Needs Non-Recourse

Hard money lenders are specialists. Most have a core product: residential bridge to $5 million, or commercial bridge to $10 million, or a specific property type focus. When a deal comes in outside that box, you have three choices: decline and lose the relationship, stretch into underwriting you are not set up for, or refer to a specialist and keep the relationship.

The third path is almost always the best business decision. A $30 million office repositioning in Dallas is outside most hard money shops' capacity. A hotel acquisition in Nashville requires hospitality underwriting expertise most private lenders do not carry. A ground-up industrial development requiring construction-to-perm financing needs a lender who can hold both phases.

A commercial mortgage broker with national capital markets access handles all of these. We work with CMBS conduits, life insurance companies, debt funds, construction lenders, and bank balance sheets. A referral from you keeps your borrower from shopping around blindly and builds equity in the relationship. When they do their next deal that fits your box, they remember who helped them close the one that did not.

What Lenders Actually Require: Technical Thresholds by Financing Type

Knowing the conventional financing requirements helps you identify which of your borrowers are approaching the refinance threshold. Here are the actual thresholds by product:

Agency Multifamily (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac)

  • Minimum units: 5 units for Freddie Mac Small Balance; 5 units for Fannie Mae DUS
  • Minimum occupancy: 90 percent physical occupancy for 90 days prior to rate lock
  • DSCR: Minimum 1.25x at the agency stress rate (often 200 to 250 basis points above the note rate)
  • LTV: Up to 80 percent on acquisition; 75 percent on cash-out refinance
  • Borrower credit: Minimum 680 FICO for most programs; 660 for some small balance
  • Non-recourse: Available with standard carve-outs
  • Loan size: Freddie Mac Small Balance: $1 million to $7.5 million; DUS: $5 million and up

CMBS Permanent Loans

  • Minimum loan size: $3 million to $5 million (most conduits); some platforms down to $2 million
  • DSCR: Minimum 1.25x; often 1.30x for hospitality and retail
  • LTV: Up to 75 percent; 65 to 70 percent preferred for non-core property types
  • Non-recourse: Standard
  • Fixed rate term: 5, 7, or 10 years with 25 to 30 year amortization
  • Prepayment: Defeasance or yield maintenance; no clean exit before term

Life Insurance Company Fixed-Rate

  • Property type preference: Stabilized multifamily, industrial, anchored retail, Class A office
  • Minimum loan size: $5 million; most life cos prefer $10 million and up
  • LTV: 55 to 65 percent; conservative by design
  • DSCR: 1.30x to 1.35x at the actual note rate
  • Non-recourse: Standard
  • Pricing advantage: Life co rates are often 15 to 40 basis points below CMBS for qualifying assets

Bank Balance Sheet

  • Recourse: Typically full recourse; some partial recourse on larger relationships
  • DSCR: 1.20x to 1.25x
  • LTV: Up to 75 percent; often 65 to 70 percent on commercial
  • Term: 3 to 5 year fixed with 20 to 25 year amortization; sometimes 10-year
  • Flexibility: More underwriter discretion on mixed-use, transitional tenancy, and sponsor experience

How the Referral Process Works

The mechanics are simple. Complexity is not required here.

When you identify a borrower who is approaching a refinance, a gap in your capital stack, or a deal outside your product, you make a single email introduction. Something like: "Trevor, meet [borrower first name]. They have a 24-unit multifamily in Sacramento that is now 95 percent occupied and they need to refinance my bridge loan by September. I want to make sure they get placed with someone I trust."

From that point, we take it entirely. We collect the financials, pull the rent roll, run the DSCR math, identify the right capital source, present term sheets, negotiate terms, open escrow, and close. You receive a payoff demand when it is time. Your capital comes back clean.

We keep you informed at every milestone. You know when the loan goes to credit, when an appraisal is ordered, and when the closing is scheduled. We are not trying to build a direct relationship with your borrower at your expense. We are working as an extension of your platform for the piece of the deal you do not do.

On referral compensation: in California and most states, a referral fee from a licensed mortgage broker to another licensed professional is permissible under RESPA commercial exemptions. We handle this through a referral agreement. Reach out to discuss your specific situation and licensing status before assuming a fee structure applies.

Questions Hard Money Lenders Should Ask Before Referring to a Commercial Mortgage Broker

Not all commercial mortgage brokers are built the same. Before you stake your borrower relationship on a referral, ask these questions:

  • Do you have direct access to life insurance companies, or only through correspondent lenders? Direct access means faster decisions and lower intermediary cost. A broker who can call a life co underwriter directly is different from one routing through a third-party correspondent.
  • What is your average time from application to close on a permanent loan? If your bridge note matures in 90 days, a broker who takes 120 days to close does not solve your problem. Ask for specific recent examples.
  • Can you handle both the placement and the payoff coordination? Some brokers hand off the file once they collect a fee. You want someone who stays through closing and coordinates with your servicing department on the payoff demand.
  • What deal sizes do you actually close, not just market to? A broker who says they do $5 million to $50 million but whose average closed deal is $2.5 million may not have the relationships to close a $20 million CMBS loan efficiently.
  • Do you refer deals you cannot close, and to whom? A broker who admits their limits and maintains a network to handle overflow is more trustworthy than one who takes every file and delivers slowly or not at all.
  • How do you protect confidentiality in the capital markets process? Your borrower's financial information goes to multiple lenders during a competitive process. What protocols does the broker use to limit exposure and manage the process cleanly?

At CLS CRE, I am happy to answer all of these directly. Our track record is built on roughly $1 billion in closed transaction volume, relationships with 1,000+ lenders across the capital stack, and a core practice focused on commercial real estate from $1 million and up.

Key Takeaways

  • The borrower who refinances is not a lost client. If you handle the transition professionally, they come back to you for the next bridge loan. The referral is the retention tool, not a concession.
  • Identifying the refinance window early is a competitive advantage. Private lenders who monitor their portfolio for stabilization milestones and introduce the takeout broker proactively close on their own timeline, not the borrower's crisis timeline.
  • Your capital stack gaps are solvable. If you are at your LTV limit, a commercial mortgage broker can often place the piece you cannot hold without disrupting your position or your underwriting discipline.
  • Honesty about deal fit builds the strongest referral reputation. Telling a borrower their deal qualifies for conventional financing instead of funding it at your rate is a long-term relationship investment. Borrowers remember who looked out for them.
  • The conventional financing thresholds are learnable. Knowing that Fannie Mae requires 90 percent occupancy for 90 days, or that CMBS minimum loan sizes start at $3 million, lets you identify qualifying borrowers before they figure it out on their own.
  • The mechanics of the referral are simple. One email introduction is all it takes. A good commercial mortgage broker handles the rest and keeps you informed without requiring ongoing management from you.
  • Ask the right qualifying questions before referring. Direct lender access, realistic timelines, and willingness to admit limits are the signals that distinguish a broker who will protect your relationship from one who will not.
  • The best referral relationships are mutual. When a conventional borrower needs bridge capital for their next acquisition, a commercial mortgage broker with integrity sends that deal back to you. The relationship compounds in both directions.
