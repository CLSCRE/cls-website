Case Study: $8.5M Office Cash-Out Refinance with a Mini-Perm Bank Loan in Sherman Oaks

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged an $8,500,000 cash-out refinance through a bank for an office property in Sherman Oaks, California, paying off an existing credit union loan. The mini-perm loan closed at 3.85% fixed for the first seven years of a 10-year term, amortized over 25 years, with a rate floor equal to the starting rate and no prepayment penalty for the life of the loan.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance brought us an ownership group looking to cash out equity from a Sherman Oaks office property currently financed by a credit union. The ownership wanted a bank willing to structure meaningful cash-out proceeds against the property's value while offering genuinely long-term fixed-rate certainty, not just a short-term bridge. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in bank mini-perm and permanent financing for office and other commercial properties for owners and investors across California and nationwide.

Sherman Oaks, CA sits along the Ventura Boulevard office corridor, an established submarket for owner and investor-held commercial office space, and finding a bank willing to underwrite to a full cash-out structure, rather than a conservative rate-and-term-only refinance, took the right kind of relationship-oriented commercial lender.

What Made It Hard

  • Structuring genuine cash-out proceeds on a 55% LTV cap. With the loan amount capped at the lesser of a flat dollar ceiling, 55% of appraised value, or a 1.25x debt service coverage ratio, sizing the refinance to deliver meaningful cash out while satisfying all three constraints simultaneously required precise coordination with the appraisal and underwriting process.
  • A long-duration rate structure spanning two distinct pricing periods. With the rate fixed for seven years before resetting to a Treasury-indexed rate for years eight through ten, the borrower needed to plan around both a known initial period and a floor-protected but otherwise uncertain later period.
  • A full underwriting stack tied to the payoff of an existing credit union loan. Retiring the existing lender's position while satisfying a new bank's full underwriting requirements, tax returns, leases, rent roll, SNDA, and tenant estoppels, required careful sequencing to close on schedule.
"This deal came down to proving which of the three sizing constraints actually governed the loan amount, not just assuming it was the flat dollar cap. Once we had that confirmed, getting a bank to waive prepayment penalty on a ten year fixed loan for a deposit relationship was the real win for this ownership group."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the cash-out refinance with a bank, with the signed proposal letter dated March 10, 2022:

  • $8,500,000 used to pay off the existing credit union loan and deliver cash-out proceeds, secured by an ALTA-insured first deed of trust and assignment of rents
  • The loan amount capped at the lesser of $8,500,000, 55% of the appraisal's as-is market value, or the amount supporting a minimum 1.25x debt service coverage ratio, whichever proved most restrictive
  • 3.85% fixed for the first seven years, calculated on an Actual/360 basis, then resetting for years eight through ten to the prevailing 10-year Treasury rate plus a 2.50% margin, with a 3.85% floor applying for the full loan term
  • A 25-year amortization schedule, producing an estimated monthly principal and interest payment of approximately $44,426.82 at the initial rate, with a 10-year maximum maturity
  • No prepayment penalty for the life of the loan, with the bank instead encouraging the borrower to bring operating accounts associated with the property over to the bank
  • No loan fee, with only a nominal ~$500 loan documentation fee, plus a 1/10% plus $500 loan processing, document, and escrow fee at closing
  • A 60-day rate lock upon delivery of the signed proposal letter, secured by a $7,185 payment covering the environmental survey and appraisal costs
  • Full underwriting conditions: three years of federal tax returns for borrower and guarantors, fully executed leases with all modifications, a current rent roll, an SNDA and tenant estoppel certificate, and three years of property profit and loss statements
  • A Hazardous Substance Questionnaire and EDR Environmental Risk Review, with the bank reserving the right to require a full Phase I Environmental Site Assessment at its discretion

The cash-out refinance closed with the existing credit union loan retired and the ownership group holding a fixed-rate facility with no prepayment exposure for the full ten-year term.

Why We Won This One

Getting the loan sized correctly against all three constraints, the dollar cap, the 55% LTV ceiling, and the 1.25x DSCR requirement, is what determined how much genuine cash-out proceeds the ownership group could actually pull from the refinance. Rather than assuming the flat dollar cap was the binding constraint, confirming which of the three limits actually governed the final loan amount protected the borrower from a last-minute surprise in proceeds.

Negotiating a no-prepayment-penalty structure on a long-duration fixed loan also mattered here. Most seven-to-ten-year fixed-rate commercial loans carry some form of prepayment protection for the lender; getting a bank comfortable waiving that entirely, in exchange for the deposit relationship, gave the ownership group full flexibility to refinance or sell without penalty at any point.

A mini-perm loan bridges the gap between short-term financing and a full 25-30 year permanent loan, typically running 5 to 10 years with a fixed rate for an initial period before resetting. This loan carried a 10-year maturity with the rate fixed for the first seven years, then resetting to the prevailing 10-year Treasury rate plus a fixed margin for years eight through ten.
Some banks forgo a prepayment penalty to compete for a full banking relationship, encouraging the borrower to bring deposit accounts to the bank rather than locking in prepayment fee revenue. This loan carried no prepayment penalty for its full 10-year term, with the bank instead encouraging the borrower to move operating accounts associated with the collateral property to the bank.
The maximum loan amount is typically the lesser of a stated dollar cap, a percentage of the appraised value, and the amount supported by a minimum debt service coverage ratio, whichever constraint is most restrictive.
