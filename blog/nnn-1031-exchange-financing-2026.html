NNN 1031 Exchange Financing in 2026: How to Close in 45 Days

Single-tenant NNN properties are the most common 1031 exchange replacement property in the country. The appeal is obvious: passive income, corporate credit, and long leases with no landlord responsibilities. The challenge is financing one within the IRS timeline. Here is what you need to know in 2026.

Why NNN Is the #1 Choice for 1031 Exchange Buyers

Every year, thousands of investors sell actively managed properties: apartments, strip centers, self-storage: and exchange into single-tenant NNN assets. The motivation is consistent: they want to eliminate management burden, lock in a long-term corporate lease, and hold a passive income stream with predictable cash flows. For investors who have spent 20 or 30 years managing properties, a NNN lease from a national retailer, restaurant, or healthcare operator is the exit they have been working toward.

NNN also makes sense from a financing perspective. Lenders love investment-grade NNN tenants. The corporate guarantees, long lease terms, and absolute triple net obligations (tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance) produce the cleanest underwriting profile in commercial real estate. Clean deals close fast: which matters enormously when you are working against an IRS deadline.

The 1031 Exchange Timeline: Where Financing Fits

The IRS rules for a 1031 exchange are specific. Once you close the sale of the relinquished property:

  • 45 days: You must identify one or more replacement properties in writing
  • 180 days: You must close on the replacement property (or properties)

The 45 and 180 day counts run simultaneously: the clock does not reset after identification. If you identify a NNN property on day 30, you have 150 days remaining to close. Most NNN acquisitions involve 30 to 60 days of due diligence and lender closing requirements. That leaves little room for a slow lender process.

The practical close window for most 1031 exchange buyers is between days 45 and 120. Buyers who identify properties late in the window: on day 40 or 45: face the tightest financing timelines. Engaging a lender on day 1 of the identification period is the most important thing you can do to protect the exchange.

What Lenders Need Upfront to Move Fast

Speed is a function of preparation. Lenders cannot approve and close a NNN acquisition without a complete submission package. Have these ready at the time you engage your broker:

  • Lease abstract: Summarizes tenant name, lease commencement, expiration, options, rent, escalations, and guarantor. If the seller does not provide one, the lender will require preparation as part of the process: this takes time. Request it from the seller immediately.
  • Rent roll: Confirms current rent in place, any abatements, and lease status.
  • Title commitment: Order immediately upon signing the purchase agreement. Title takes 5 to 10 days and must be reviewed by both lender and borrower counsel before closing.
  • Phase I environmental: Required by virtually every lender. Takes 10 to 20 days to complete. Order on day one of due diligence, not week two.
  • Financial statements: Lender will need 2 years of personal financial statements, tax returns, and a real estate schedule from the borrower.
  • Purchase agreement: Fully executed, including any addenda and the due diligence period terms.

Common Mistakes That Kill 1031 NNN Deals

These are the situations I see derail 1031 NNN exchanges most often:

  • Waiting too long to engage a lender. Some buyers want to have their purchase agreement fully negotiated before talking to a lender. By the time they start the financing process, they have 90 days left to close. Order your Phase I and contact your broker the same week you go under contract.
  • Assuming debt can be assumed. Most NNN leases are on properties with existing financing. Seller debt assumption is rarely available or desirable: new financing is standard. Confirm the financing approach on day one, not week three.
  • Lease issues that surface late in due diligence. Dark clauses (tenant right to go dark without triggering default), co-tenancy provisions, unusual landlord obligations, or below-market rent can disqualify the deal from lender programs. A lender will review the full lease before committing. Have your attorney review it first.
  • Financing contingency tied to CMBS when timeline requires bank. CMBS is an excellent execution at $5M and above, but it takes 60 to 75 days. A buyer with 55 days left to close should not be pursuing CMBS financing. Know your timeline and match the capital source to it.
  • Inadequate reserves. Bank STNL programs typically require borrower liquidity equal to 6 to 12 months of debt service in post-close reserves. Confirm reserve requirements before signing the purchase agreement, not after.

The Right NNN Acquisitions for 1031 Exchange Financing

Not all NNN properties are equal from a lender's perspective. The deals that finance fastest and at the best terms share these characteristics:

  • Investment-grade tenant with national presence (500-plus locations preferred)
  • 15 or more years of remaining lease term (or 10 or more with strong renewal structure)
  • Absolute NNN: no landlord expense obligations
  • Rent escalations at least every 5 years (10% bumps or CPI-tied)
  • Freestanding building on a pad site (avoids inline retail complications)
  • Phase I clear with no environmental concerns
  • Cap rate that supports acceptable DSCR at the target leverage (check this early)
