Case Study: $3.5M Community Bank Refinance for a Commercial Property in West Des Moines, Iowa

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $3,500,000 refinance for a Modesto, California-based ownership group's commercial property in West Des Moines, Iowa. The loan closed with a community bank headquartered in Clive, Iowa, at a 7.00% fixed rate, amortized over 25 years with a 5-year balloon maturity, and a declining prepayment penalty that steps down from 3% to 2% to 1% over the first three years and disappears entirely after that.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, California. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in community bank placements for out-of-state commercial property owners nationwide. This refinance connected an ownership group based in Modesto, California with the community bank already anchored in the market where their asset actually sits: West Des Moines, Iowa (IA). The property, a commercial parcel in Polk County, needed a straightforward refinance, but "straightforward" for an out-of-state owner still means finding a lender who actually knows the local market rather than treating the loan as an exception.

West Des Moines is one of the stronger commercial submarkets in the Des Moines metro, and a lender headquartered a few miles away in Clive, Iowa was positioned to underwrite the property with real local knowledge of comparable sales, tenant demand, and construction costs, exactly what an out-of-state ownership group needs on the other side of the table.

What Made It Hard

  • An owner far from the asset. With ownership based in Modesto, California and the property in Iowa, the loan needed a lender comfortable underwriting an absentee owner rather than requiring local, hands-on management.
  • Balancing rate against penalty exposure. A 7.00% fixed rate with a declining prepayment penalty meant structuring the deal so the borrower had real flexibility to sell, refinance, or pay down the loan after the early years without being boxed in by a flat penalty for the full term.
  • A demand-note structure to work around. Community bank commercial notes are frequently written payable on demand as a contractual safety valve, and the deal had to be priced and structured with the understanding that the bank's actual practice would follow the stated amortization and maturity terms.
"The instinct with an out-of-state owner is to bring the loan back to a lender near the client. The better move is usually the opposite, find the bank that already knows the property's own backyard."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the loan directly with an Iowa community bank in Clive, Iowa, the same community bank with local knowledge of the West Des Moines submarket, closing on February 24, 2025:

  • $3,500,000 secured by a first mortgage on the Polk County, Iowa property
  • 7.00% fixed for the full loan term, calculated on a 365/360 basis
  • 25-year amortization (300 monthly payments of $24,957.67), keeping monthly debt service manageable relative to a shorter-term note
  • 5-year maturity (February 24, 2030), with the full remaining balance, estimated at roughly $3.22 million, due at that point
  • A step-down prepayment penalty: 3% of any amount prepaid in year one, 2% in year two, 1% in year three, and no penalty at all after three years or if the loan is paid off through a property sale or a refinance back with an Iowa community bank itself
  • An 18.00% default rate and a technically demand-payable note, standard community bank protections that do not affect pricing or servicing so long as the loan performs

The refinance closed cleanly, giving the ownership group a fixed-rate loan on their Iowa asset without needing to manage the relationship through a California-based lender unfamiliar with the property's market.

Why We Won This One

The core insight here was resisting the instinct to bring the loan back to a California lender just because the ownership group is based there. A community bank with boots on the ground in West Des Moines already understands the collateral, the comparable sales, and the tenant base far better than an out-of-market lender would, and that local knowledge translated into a straightforward, fairly priced refinance rather than a longer underwriting fight.

The declining prepayment structure also mattered to the outcome. Locking in a flat penalty for the full term would have limited the ownership group's options if a sale or better refinance opportunity came along; negotiating a schedule that steps down to zero after three years, with a same-bank refinance carve-out from day one, gave the borrower real optionality without giving up rate.
