The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance brought together two industrial properties on opposite ends of the Southern California logistics map: a 105,000-square-foot site in Vernon, in the heart of the Los Angeles industrial core, and a 323,651-square-foot site roughly 70 miles east in Perris, deep in the Inland Empire's warehouse and distribution corridor. The ownership group, controlled by the same sponsors across both assets, needed a single refinance that could underwrite both properties together rather than as two disconnected loans, and it ultimately closed as a $28,000,000 non-recourse CMBS conduit refinance placed with a CMBS conduit lender.

Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in structuring institutional, non-recourse CMBS conduit financing for industrial property owners across California and nationwide, drawing on a background in structured finance at CBRE and Marcus and Millichap Capital Corporation. Vernon and Perris sit at opposite ends of the same industrial supply chain that runs through Southern California, and combining both properties under one institutional lender's underwriting let the sponsors size the loan against the full combined portfolio rather than each property's standalone limitations.

What Made It Hard

  • Underwriting two non-adjacent properties as one credit. Vernon and Perris are different submarkets entirely, and getting a single lender to size, price, and structure one loan against both properties required real coordination on valuation, cash flow, and reserve requirements across the combined portfolio.
  • A lease condition that had to close before the loan could. The lender's underwriting was expressly conditioned on a named tenant executing a 10-year, triple-net lease for 14,500 square feet of industrial outdoor storage space and actually being in occupancy, open, and paying full unabated rent before the loan would fund, a real timing dependency between leasing and closing.
  • Full institutional CMBS-style structure. Between the lockbox cash management, tax and insurance escrows, replacement and rollover reserves, and defeasance-based prepayment, this loan carried the full weight of conduit-style documentation that had to be negotiated and closed correctly.
"What made this one interesting was getting a single lender comfortable underwriting two properties that aren't even in the same submarket as one credit, while a lease condition still had to close on schedule alongside it. That takes real coordination, not just paperwork."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed this loan with 3650 Real Estate Investment Trust 2 LLC, a CMBS conduit lender, closing in early 2024:

  • $28,000,000 secured by a first mortgage across both the Vernon and Perris industrial properties
  • A floating rate of 335 basis points over the greater of the 5-year SOFR swap rate or a 3.30% floor, giving the lender downside rate protection while still tracking the broader rate environment
  • 5-year term, full-term interest-only, underwritten to a minimum 1.20x debt service coverage ratio and a 9.25% minimum debt yield against $2,621,000 in underwritten net cash flow
  • 60.0% maximum loan-to-value on a combined senior and mezzanine basis, based on MAI appraised value
  • Non-recourse financing with standard carve-outs, guaranteed by the sponsors subject to ongoing minimum net worth and liquidity requirements
  • A pre-closing lease condition: a named tenant had to execute a 10-year, triple-net industrial outdoor storage lease at $1.75 per square foot per month with no early-termination outs, be in occupancy, open for business, and paying full rent, with the lease payments personally guaranteed by the sponsors
  • Full CMBS-style cash management, with all tenant rents swept daily into a lender-controlled account, tax/insurance escrows, a $0.10/SF/year replacement reserve, and a $0.50/SF/year rollover reserve for future leasing costs
  • A 36-month prepayment lockout followed by defeasance, with a 6-month open window before maturity

The loan closed after the lease condition was satisfied, giving the ownership group a single, institutionally structured facility across two of their key Southern California industrial holdings.

Why We Won This One

Getting one lender to underwrite two non-adjacent industrial properties as a single credit, rather than requiring separate loans for Vernon and Perris, is what let the sponsors access institutional, non-recourse pricing at a combined $28 million scale that either property alone might not have supported on its own. Portfolio-level underwriting like this only works when the lender is comfortable with the combined cash flow and reserve structure across both assets.

Coordinating the lease condition against the closing timeline was the other piece that had to be managed carefully. With the loan expressly conditioned on a specific tenant being in occupancy and paying full rent before funding, sequencing the leasing milestone against the loan's underwriting and closing schedule required real attention to keep both tracks moving together rather than one holding up the other.
