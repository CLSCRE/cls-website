Commercial Bridge Loan Lenders: Types, Terms and How to Choose One

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

November 26, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial bridge loans come from debt funds, private and hard money lenders, banks and credit unions, insurance companies, and agency lender bridge programs. Debt funds generally fit larger transitional deals, private lenders fit smaller or faster ones, and banks fit borrowers who can accept recourse and a relationship. Commercial Lending Solutions does not publish a ranked list of lender names because lender appetite changes monthly. As a Los Angeles commercial mortgage broker, we match each deal to the right lender type and desk across 1,000+ lenders.

A commercial bridge loan is short-term financing that carries a property through a transition, and the lender you choose shapes the price, the leverage, the speed and the guarantees you give. This guide explains the main types of bridge lenders, what each one is built for, and how to compare quotes. For the full terms and costs, see our commercial bridge loans page.

Commercial Lending Solutions (CLS CRE) is a Los Angeles commercial mortgage broker. We do not lend. We arrange bridge loans from $2 million to $100 million and up through 1,000+ lenders, so the comparison below reflects how the market works across all of them rather than one lender's program.

The Main Types of Commercial Bridge Lenders

Five kinds of capital provide commercial bridge loans. Each is built for a different borrower and a different clock.

Lender typeBest fitLeverage and pricingSpeedRecourse
Debt fundsLarger transitional deals: value-add, lease-up, recapitalization. Often loans of $5 million and up.Highest leverage among institutional options. Floating rate. Future funding for renovation is common.Weeks, driven by third-party reports.Non-recourse available on institutional deals, with carve-out guarantees.
Private and hard money lendersSmaller, time-sensitive or unusual deals: auctions, light renovation, fast acquisitions.Lower leverage against value. Higher pricing and shorter terms.Fastest. Can close in days to a few weeks.Often recourse.
Banks and credit unionsExisting customers and near-stabilized properties with a clear path to a permanent loan.Lower pricing and more conservative leverage.Slower, because of committee approval.Recourse is common.
Insurance companiesStrong assets and experienced sponsors. More often the permanent takeout that repays the bridge than the bridge itself.Conservative leverage, long-term fixed pricing on the permanent loan.Slower than debt funds.Varies by program.
Agency lender bridge programsMultifamily value-add where the plan is to refinance into agency permanent financing.Sized to the expected agency takeout, sometimes with the bridge and permanent loan from the same lender.Weeks.Varies by program.

The same property can land with a different lender type depending on how fast the deal must close and how much leverage the borrower needs. For a side-by-side of the two most common choices, see hard money versus bridge loans and bridge debt fund versus bank balance sheet.

Why We Do Not Publish a "Best Bridge Lenders" Ranking

Ranked lists of bridge lenders are popular, but they age quickly. A lender that is aggressive on Phoenix multifamily this month may pass on a rent-controlled Los Angeles building next month. Appetite moves with the lender's capital, the rate environment, its portfolio and the sponsor on the other side of the table. When you read any ranked list, ask how it was built and whether lenders pay to be on it.

The more useful question is which lender type, and which desk within it, fits your deal right now. That is what we answer on every request. Lender names stay out of our public content, and a borrower's details stay private.

How to Compare Bridge Lenders and Quotes

Two quotes with the same rate can be very different loans. Put these questions to every lender and line the answers up side by side.

Question to askWhy it matters
Is the loan sized to as-is value, to cost, or to the exit?The tightest test sets your loan amount, so it tells you how much equity you really need.
Is future funding included, and how are draws released?It decides how much of the renovation budget the loan covers and how fast you get reimbursed.
What are the recourse terms, guarantees and carve-outs?Completion, interest and carry guarantees can matter as much as the headline recourse label.
What are the extension conditions?If the plan runs late, the extension terms are what protect you.
What are the origination, exit, extension and prepayment fees?Fees change the all-in cost. Some lenders reduce the exit fee in certain cases.
What reserves are held back at closing?Interest and carry reserves reduce the proceeds you receive on day one.
How certain is the close?Ask whether the lender has closed similar assets recently and how often it changes terms after the term sheet.
Can this lender or another provide the takeout?A planned exit shortens the path from bridge to permanent loan.

Matching the Lender Type to the Deal

SituationLender type that often fits
Value-add apartment building in Los Angeles with a renovation budgetA debt fund with a future funding holdback. See our California bridge loan guide.
Time-critical purchase or auction with light renovationA private or hard money lender.
Near-stabilized property and a borrower with a strong banking relationshipA bank or credit union.
Multifamily headed for an agency permanent loanAn agency lender bridge program or a debt fund.
Entitled land or a complex recapitalizationA debt fund or private lender, sometimes layered with mezzanine or junior capital.
A 1031 exchange deadline that permanent financing cannot meetA private lender as a backstop. Most exchanges close on time with permanent financing.
