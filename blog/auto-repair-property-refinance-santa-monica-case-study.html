Case Study: $5M Auto Repair Property Refinance in Santa Monica at 6.50% Fixed

By Trevor Damyan July 25, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions refinanced a multi-parcel automotive service property on a major commercial boulevard in Santa Monica, California with a $5,000,000 conventional credit union loan: 6.50% fixed for the full 7-year term, 30-year amortization at a maximum 65% loan-to-value, a 0.50% loan fee negotiated down from 0.75%, a 3-2-1 declining prepayment penalty, an optional 12 months of interest only, and a 1.25x minimum global DSCR. Five lenders competed, two banks and three credit unions. The letter of interest was signed March 20, 2024 and the loan closed through escrow in May 2024.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this one closed practically in our backyard. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in bridge loans, construction financing, and DSCR loans for investors and owner-operators across California and the rest of the country. The client: a multi-generational family ownership group based in the South Bay of Los Angeles, seven guarantors across two family trusts and an operating corporation, who own and operate an automotive service and repair property spanning multiple parcels on one of Santa Monica's busiest commercial boulevards. They had run the business on that corner for decades and needed to refinance the existing bank debt with a $5,000,000 credit union loan, ultimately placed with a Ventura County based credit union.

Santa Monica real estate is some of the most valuable dirt on the Westside, but the collateral here was not a glass office building. It was service bays, lifts, and a working repair operation. Automotive use is special-purpose collateral with an environmental file attached, and most lenders' first question is not about the rent roll, it is about what has soaked into the ground since the 1970s.

What Made It Hard

  • Special-purpose automotive collateral. Repair operations carry environmental stigma. This property had a diligence trail of Phase I environmental reports going back to 2006, another in 2014, another in 2017, and a subsurface investigation in the file. Every one of those documents had to tell a consistent, clean story.
  • Seven guarantors, one credit decision. Two family trusts, an operating corporation, and five individual family members all signed the guaranty. That meant seven personal financial statements, multiple entity tax returns, and a global cash flow analysis across everything the family owned, against a 1.25x minimum global DSCR test.
  • Leverage sensitivity. The lender's structure hinged on the new appraisal. At or under 65% loan-to-value the family got $5,000,000 on a 30-year amortization. Over 65%, the offer dropped to $3,875,000 on a 25-year schedule because of the DSCR constraint. The appraisal was not paperwork, it was the whole deal.
  • Participation risk. A $5,000,000 loan is large for a single credit union balance sheet, so the loan was subject to participation approval, meaning other institutions had to agree to buy pieces of it before funding was certain.
"Most brokers never call the credit unions on a deal like this. On special-purpose collateral in California, that is where the sharpest pricing actually lives."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), took the deal wide across the institutions that actually like owner-operated Southern California collateral: five lenders quoted it, two Los Angeles-area banks and three credit unions. In February 2024 we built a side-by-side comparison of all five quotes and sat down with the family to walk through the real differences, not just the headline rates. The credit unions beat the banks on this one, and one credit union based in Ventura County beat them all with a rate of 6.50% fixed for the full 7-year term:

  • $5,000,000 at a maximum 65% of appraised value, conventional, first lien, no SBA involvement
  • 6.50% fixed for the entire 7-year term, not a 5-year fix with a floating tail, on a full 30-year amortization
  • An optional 12 months of interest only at the borrower's election
  • 0.50% loan fee, negotiated down from the 0.75% in the first draft, and the 0.25% rate lock fee struck to zero, both changes initialed on the face of the letter of interest
  • 3-2-1 declining prepayment, waived entirely if the loan is refinanced with the same lender, open after year three
  • Relationship terms: a minimum $250,000 deposit held in a money market or term certificate

The letter of interest was signed March 20, 2024 with a $10,000 good faith deposit applied to loan fees. The new appraisal came in where it needed to, supporting the full $5,000,000 at the 30-year amortization. A fresh 2024 Phase I environmental confirmed what the 18-year diligence history already said. Escrow opened at a national title company, our broker demand went in on May 8, 2024, and the loan funded that month, paying off the incumbent bank.

Why We Won This One

The first lesson is that credit unions deserve a seat at the table on deals like this. Plenty of borrowers, and frankly plenty of brokers, never call them. On owner-operated, special-purpose Southern California real estate they are often the most aggressive money available: this one wrote a 7-year fixed rate at 6.50% with a 30-year amortization at a moment when banks were quoting the same deal shorter, tighter, and more expensive. Three of the five competing quotes here were credit unions, and that was not an accident.

The second lesson is that on automotive collateral, the environmental file is the deal. We did not wait for the lender to ask. We assembled every Phase I back to 2006, the 2014 report ordered by a prior bank, the 2017 update, and the subsurface work, organized them into a coherent narrative, and ordered the release of the prior reports so the new consultant could rely on them. When the 2024 Phase I landed clean, it was confirming a story the lender had already read, not opening a question.

And the negotiation showed up in ink. The first draft of the letter of interest carried a 0.75% fee and a 0.25% rate lock fee. The version the family signed says 0.50% and zero, with the changes initialed right on the page, and the balloon structure extended to match the full 7-year fixed period. That is what five competing quotes buys you: the winner still had to sharpen its pencil twice.
