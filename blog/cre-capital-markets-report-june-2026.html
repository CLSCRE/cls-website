Commercial Real Estate Capital Markets Report: June 2026 Treasury Yields and Deal Activity

By Trevor Damyan June 22, 2026

June 2026 Treasury Yield Curve: What Moved and Why It Matters

The US Treasury yield curve shifted meaningfully between May and June 2026, and for sponsors actively pursuing commercial real estate financing, that shift has a direct impact on execution. At CLS CRE, we track these movements monthly because the spread between benchmark Treasury yields and lender pricing is where deals get made or fall apart.

The month-over-month comparison shows a modest flattening at the intermediate part of the curve, with the 5-year and 7-year tenors showing the most notable movement. These are the benchmark tenors that life insurance companies and CMBS lenders most commonly use to price long-term fixed-rate permanent loans. When those tenors compress or expand, spreads that looked attractive two weeks ago can look very different at the closing table.

For borrowers considering permanent fixed-rate financing, the current yield curve environment rewards moving quickly. When the curve is flattening, lenders often tighten spreads on longer-duration paper as a hedge against future rate uncertainty. Sponsors who understand this dynamic can position their refinance or acquisition timelines to capture that window.

What Lenders Are Pricing in June 2026

Across the lender types we work with at CLS CRE, here is a practical read on where pricing landed in June 2026.

Life insurance companies remain the gold standard for stabilized assets with clean in-place cash flow. They are pricing conservatively on spreads but are actively competing for high-quality multifamily, industrial, and net-lease retail. A life insurance company closed a long-term fixed-rate loan for a stabilized industrial portfolio in the Southeast this quarter at a spread that reflected the competitive tone in that lending channel. These lenders are not chasing leverage, but if your debt service coverage is strong and the asset is institutional quality, this is the most cost-effective capital available.

Regional banks are still active on smaller-balance deals, particularly in the $3 million to $15 million range, but credit committees are focused on deposit relationships and recourse. A regional bank provided construction-to-permanent financing on a mixed-use project this quarter with a floating rate structure tied to a bank prime spread, reflecting the continued preference for variable-rate structures at the construction stage. For sponsors looking at transitional assets or ground-up projects, regional bank floating rate debt remains one of the more executable options in the current environment.

Private bridge lenders are extremely active right now. With agency lending still selective on workforce housing and value-add multifamily, private bridge capital has filled a meaningful gap in the market. A private bridge lender closed a short-term loan on a value-add multifamily property this month at a rate that reflects the risk premium for transitional business plans, but with flexible prepayment and a structure that allows for a clean agency or bank refinance once stabilization is achieved. For sponsors with a clear exit to permanent financing, bridge capital is not expensive relative to the opportunity cost of missing a deal.

Yield Curve Dynamics and Commercial Mortgage Spreads

One of the most practical questions we field from sponsors is how Treasury movements translate to the rates they actually see on term sheets. The answer depends on lender type, loan structure, and asset class, but the core mechanic is consistent: commercial mortgage rates are priced as a spread over a benchmark Treasury tenor that approximates the expected average life of the loan.

A 10-year fixed-rate loan from a life insurance company or CMBS lender will typically reference the 10-year Treasury. A 5-year bank term loan will often reference the 5-year Treasury or a swap rate. When the June 2026 yield curve shows movement at those key tenors, it flows directly into the all-in rate on a new loan origination or a refinance.

The practical implication for June 2026 is that sponsors who locked a rate quote or signed a rate lock agreement prior to the month-over-month yield shift are sitting in a better position than those still in the application pipeline. This is exactly why rate lock strategy is a core part of how we counsel clients through the financing process.

Deal Activity and Market Tone Heading Into Q3 2026

Transaction volume in the commercial real estate market has been building through the first half of 2026. Lender appetite is broadly constructive, with the clearest demand for stabilized income-producing properties in liquid markets. The assets generating the most competitive term sheet activity are industrial, multifamily with strong occupancy, and grocery-anchored retail.

Office remains a story-by-story credit decision, and lenders who are active in that sector are focusing on suburban Class A assets with long-term tenancy. The urban core office market continues to trade at significant discounts to replacement cost, and lenders are underwriting to current cash flow, not projections.

Self-storage, manufactured housing, and net-lease single-tenant assets continue to attract life insurance company and CMBS interest. These asset classes benefit from simplicity of operations and predictability of cash flow, both of which make credit underwriting cleaner and faster.

Heading into Q3 2026, the tone in the capital markets is cautiously optimistic. Lenders are open for business, but they are disciplined on structure and conservative on leverage assumptions. Sponsors who come to the table with clean financials, a clear business plan, and a realistic understanding of today's pricing environment will find execution relatively smooth. Sponsors expecting 2021-era terms will find the current market frustrating.
