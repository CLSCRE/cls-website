Commercial Real Estate Capital Markets Report: June 2026 Rate and Yield Update

By Trevor Damyan June 01, 2026

Every month, the CLS CRE team compiles what we are seeing across the commercial mortgage lending landscape, from Treasury movements to live deal flow across asset classes. The June 2026 edition reflects a market that continues to reward borrowers who are prepared, well-positioned, and working with brokers who have active lender relationships. Here is what the data is telling us and what it means for your next transaction.

US Treasury Yield Curve: Month-Over-Month Shift

The yield curve remained a central story heading into June 2026. Comparing May and June side by side, the curve continued its gradual re-steepening trend, with short-term rates holding relatively firm while the long end showed modest upward movement. The 2-year Treasury hovered in a range that reflects the Federal Reserve's cautious posture on rate cuts, while the 10-year Treasury, the benchmark most commercial real estate lenders price against, edged higher.

Why does this matter for deals? Lenders pricing fixed-rate commercial mortgages, including CMBS, life company loans, and agency executions, all reference the 10-year Treasury as their baseline. When the long end rises, all-in fixed rates move with it unless a borrower has locked a rate or is using a floating-rate bridge product. Borrowers who are mid-process on a refinance or acquisition should be watching this spread closely. Even a 15 to 20 basis point move in the 10-year Treasury over a 30-day period can shift a debt service coverage ratio calculation enough to affect sizing.

What Rate Volatility Means for Loan Sizing Right Now

Rate volatility is not just a pricing story. It is a proceeds story. As the 10-year Treasury moves, lenders stress-test debt service coverage at current rates, which directly affects how much they will lend. A deal that underwrote cleanly at a 6.75% fixed rate in April may look tighter at 7.00% in June, even if the property's income has not changed. This is the hidden cost of delay that many borrowers do not fully appreciate until they are deep in the process.

At CLS CRE, we are actively modeling rate sensitivity for every deal we submit to lenders. When the market is moving, getting to LOI quickly is not just good practice, it is a capital preservation strategy. The difference between a June rate lock and an August rate lock on a $5 million loan could represent tens of thousands of dollars in additional annual debt service.

Bridge Lending Activity: Demand Stays Elevated

Private bridge lenders remain highly active across the market. We are seeing strong deal flow from sponsors who need to move quickly on acquisitions, who are in the middle of a value-add execution and need time to season occupancy, or who are working through a lease-up before transitioning to permanent agency or bank financing.

A private bridge lender we work with recently closed a transaction in under three weeks from term sheet to funding on a transitional multifamily asset, demonstrating that the private lending space continues to offer execution speed that institutional capital simply cannot match. Rates on bridge loans in June 2026 are generally ranging from the mid-7% to low-9% range depending on leverage, asset type, sponsor track record, and market. Interest reserves and extension options remain standard features that sponsors should negotiate at the term sheet stage.

Bank and Credit Union Activity: Selective But Present

Regional banks and credit unions have not exited the market, but they are being highly selective. Credit committees are spending more time on stress testing, exit assumptions, and sponsor liquidity than they were 18 months ago. The banks that are actively quoting in June 2026 are generally focusing on relationship borrowers, strong primary markets, and asset classes with stable fundamentals like industrial, necessity retail, and workforce multifamily.

A regional bank we recently placed a loan with was willing to hold a five-year fixed rate on an industrial refinance, but required a personal guarantee and meaningful reserves at closing. That combination of terms would have been negotiable in a frothier market. Today, it is the cost of accessing lower cost of capital from a regulated lender. Borrowers who come to the table with clean financials, experienced operating histories, and realistic exit strategies are getting the best outcomes.

Life Company and CMBS Appetite

Life insurance companies remain a preferred execution for stabilized, lower-leverage commercial real estate loans. Their spreads over the 10-year Treasury have been relatively tight, and for the right deal, they continue to offer some of the most competitive all-in fixed rates available in the market. Typical life company deals in June 2026 are falling in the 55% to 65% loan-to-value range, with strong debt service coverage requirements and a preference for Class A or institutional-quality assets in primary and secondary markets.

CMBS volume has been building steadily through 2026. Conduit execution is particularly relevant for larger deals where borrowers are comfortable with the non-recourse structure and are willing to accept the servicing and reserve requirements that come with securitized debt. Spreads have tightened modestly as investor appetite for commercial real estate paper has improved. For deals that need higher proceeds or non-recourse carve-outs, CMBS deserves a place in the conversation.

What Borrowers Should Be Doing Right Now

If you have a maturity coming up in the next six to twelve months, now is the time to begin the process, not 60 days before the loan comes due. Lenders are taking longer to close, credit committees are asking more questions, and third-party reports including appraisals and environmental assessments are running on extended timelines. Waiting until the last minute puts you in a position where you are negotiating from weakness, not strength.

If you are looking at an acquisition, get your financing assumptions stress-tested against current rates before you put down earnest money. The deal that makes sense at a 7.00% all-in rate may not pencil at 7.50%, and the market can move that much between when you sign a purchase agreement and when you close.

The June 2026 market rewards preparation and speed. Lenders want to see sponsors who understand their numbers, have a clear business plan, and are working with advisors who know how to package and present a deal professionally.
