Commercial Mortgage Rates: Week of July 27, 2026

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial mortgage rates held in a narrow range during the week of July 27 to 31, 2026, as markets digested mixed labor data and awaited the Federal Reserve's next policy signal : here's what borrowers and investors need to know heading into August.

Rate Summary: Week of July 27 to 31, 2026

It was a week of controlled tension in the capital markets. Treasury yields edged modestly higher mid-week on stronger-than-expected job openings data before pulling back Friday as softer consumer spending figures reminded traders that the Fed's long-anticipated easing cycle is still very much in play : just not in a hurry. For commercial real estate borrowers, that translated to another week of rates that feel frustratingly "stuck," yet also increasingly predictable. And in today's CRE lending environment, predictability is something you work with.

At CLS CRE, we track rate movements weekly across the full spectrum of commercial mortgage products : from agency multifamily to bridge debt to CMBS conduit : and this week's read is a mixed bag with a few bright spots worth highlighting. The short version: if you've been waiting on the sidelines for a dramatic rate drop before pulling the trigger on a deal, the data is telling you to recalibrate that strategy. Rates are moving, but incrementally. The borrowers winning right now are the ones who are positioned and executing.

Treasury Yields: Where We Stood This Week

The benchmark 10-Year U.S. Treasury Note : the most widely referenced index for long-term commercial mortgage pricing : opened the week at approximately 4.38%, briefly spiked to 4.47% on Wednesday following the JOLTS report showing job openings holding above 8.1 million, and then settled back to close Friday around 4.33%. That 14-basis-point intraweek swing is meaningful context: it illustrates just how reactive the long end of the curve remains to any data that touches the Fed's dual mandate.

The 5-Year Treasury, which heavily influences shorter fixed-rate commercial loans and many agency products, followed a similar arc : opening near 4.05%, touching 4.14% mid-week, and closing Friday at roughly 4.02%. The 2-Year Treasury, most sensitive to near-term Fed rate expectations, remained relatively anchored between 4.18% and 4.25% throughout the week, reflecting a market that still prices in one or possibly two Fed cuts before year-end 2026, but is not betting aggressively on either.

The Fed Funds target rate currently sits at 4.25% to 4.50%, unchanged since the January 2026 adjustment. Fed Chair commentary in recent weeks has been deliberately balanced : acknowledging progress on inflation while flagging that the labor market remains "sufficiently resilient" to justify patience. The next FOMC meeting is scheduled for September 16 to 17, 2026, and is broadly viewed as a live meeting for a potential 25-basis-point cut, though futures markets as of Friday assigned only a 52% probability to that outcome.

What It Means for Borrowers

Here's the capital markets reality for CRE borrowers right now: the rate environment is better than it was twelve months ago, but the easy narrative of "rates are falling, wait it out" has grown stale and, frankly, costly for deals that pencil today. Let's break down what's actually happening at the borrower level.

  • Spread compression is doing real work. Even as benchmark Treasury yields have been sticky, lender spreads across several product categories have tightened meaningfully in 2026. Agency multifamily spreads, for example, are running 15 to 25 basis points tighter than the same time last year, partially offsetting benchmark movements. CMBS conduit spreads have also firmed on the back of stronger new issuance demand.
  • Floating-rate deals are getting more competitive. With SOFR hovering in the 4.30% to 4.40% range and bridge lenders hungry for quality collateral, floating-rate bridge and transitional loan pricing has become more aggressive. For value-add multifamily and light industrial plays, all-in rates in the low-to-mid 7% range are achievable on well-structured deals with strong sponsors.
  • Debt service coverage is the new underwriting battleground. With rates where they are, most lenders are scrutinizing DSCR at 1.20x to 1.25x minimum, and many permanent lenders are stress-testing at rates 50 to 100 basis points above today's note rate. If your deal barely clears DSCR today, expect significant lender pushback on proceeds.
  • Lender appetite is segmented, not uniform. Life insurance companies are actively competing on stabilized industrial, grocery-anchored retail, and Class A multifamily. Banks remain cautious on office and certain retail subtypes. Debt funds are filling the gap on transitional assets but pricing that flexibility into the rate.

Bottom line: the deals getting done this week are the ones where sponsors have done their homework, the basis is clean, and the capital structure makes sense at today's rates : not hypothetical future rates.

CRE Loan Rate Ranges by Loan Type

The following rate ranges reflect current market pricing as of the week of July 27 to 31, 2026. These are indicative ranges based on CLS CRE's active lender relationships and pipeline activity. Actual rates will vary based on property type, location, LTV, DSCR, sponsor strength, and loan term.

  • Agency Multifamily (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac), 10-Year Fixed: 5.55% to 6.10%
  • Agency Multifamily, 5-Year Fixed / Hybrid ARM: 5.25% to 5.75%
  • CMBS Conduit (Non-Recourse), 10-Year Fixed: 6.00% to 6.65%
  • Life Company (Stabilized CRE), 10-Year Fixed: 5.70% to 6.30%
  • Bank / Credit Union Portfolio Loans, 5-Year Fixed: 5.90% to 6.75%
  • SBA 504 (Owner-Occupied CRE), 25-Year Fixed Debenture: 5.80% to 6.20%
  • Bridge / Transitional (Floating, 12 to 36 Month): SOFR + 275 to 450 bps (all-in approx. 7.05% to 8.90%)
  • Construction / Mini-Perm (Floating): SOFR + 300 to 500 bps, depending on project type and sponsorship
  • Hard Money / Private (Short-Term): 9.00% to 11.50%+, with 1 to 3 points
  • Mezzanine / Preferred Equity: 11.00% to 14.00%, subject to deal structure and blended leverage

Industrial and multifamily continue to command the best pricing across most lender categories. Retail with strong grocery or necessity-based tenancy is finding good execution with life companies and select regional banks. Office : particularly suburban office below 85% occupancy : remains a challenging financing environment across nearly every lender type.

Market Outlook: What to Watch in August

August tends to be a quieter month for capital markets volume, but "quiet" does not mean "uneventful." Several data releases and macro developments will shape the rate narrative heading into the critical September FOMC meeting:

  • July Jobs Report (Released August 1): This will be the single most important data point of the early August period. A print above 180,000 nonfarm payrolls will likely push Treasury yields back toward the 4.45% to 4.50% range on the 10-Year. A miss below 130,000 could open the door to a modest rally (yields lower, bond prices higher), which would be modestly positive for fixed-rate CRE borrowers.
  • July CPI (Released August 13): Core inflation has been trending in the right direction, with core PCE running near 2.5% year-over-year. A continuation of that trend will reinforce the case for a September cut. An upside surprise would almost certainly delay any Fed action into Q4 or beyond.
  • CMBS New Issuance Pipeline: Several conduit deals are expected to price in early-to-mid August. Strong investor demand on these transactions would be a positive signal for spread stability and could create minor spread tightening opportunities for CMBS borrowers locking later in the month.
  • Agency MBS Flows: Prepayment speeds on agency MBS portfolios typically slow in summer, which can modestly widen agency spreads. This is a technical factor worth monitoring if you're comparing agency execution against life company or bank alternatives.

Our base case at CLS CRE: rates finish August within 15 to 20 basis points of where they closed this week, with directional bias slightly lower on any soft labor data. We do not anticipate a dramatic rate move between now and the September FOMC meeting unless a significant economic surprise forces the Fed's hand.

Action Items for Borrowers and Investors

If you're active in the CRE market right now : whether you're acquiring, refinancing, or recapitalizing : here's the practical playbook based on what we're seeing in the market this week:

  • Get your rate lock timing right. If you're closing a deal in late September or October, your lock window is approaching. Don't wait for the "perfect" rate moment : Treasury volatility can move rates 20 to 30 basis points in a matter of days around major data releases.
  • Run multiple capital stack scenarios. With the spread between agency, life company, CMBS, and bank debt unusually tight in certain property categories, it's worth letting lenders compete. We're seeing cases where a life company beats agency pricing by 15 basis points on a well-located multifamily asset : something that was rare 18 months ago.
  • Don't anchor to 2021 rates. We continue to see deals die because sponsors are mentally anchored to 3% rates. The market has repriced. The deals that get done are structured around today's economics, with realistic exit assumptions built on current cap rates and financing costs.
  • Reconsider floating-rate bridge for value-add plays. If your business plan is 18 to 36 months and the property has a legitimate execution path, floating bridge debt at SOFR + 300 to 375 gives you optionality without locking into a long-term rate at the top of what may prove to be a cycle.
  • Talk to your broker before you go to market on acquisition debt. Lender preferences by product type, geography, and sponsor profile have shifted materially in 2026. A 60-day head start on lender selection and pre-qualification can be the difference between closing on time and losing a deal to a financing contingency blowup.
