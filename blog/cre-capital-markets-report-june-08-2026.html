CRE Capital Markets Report: June 2026 US Treasury Yield Curve and Deal Activity

By Trevor Damyan June 08, 2026

The June 2026 capital markets landscape for commercial real estate continues to reward borrowers and sponsors who stay close to the data. Yield curve movements over the past 30 days have created meaningful shifts in pricing across property types and loan structures. Below is our read on what those movements mean for deals currently in the market and those in early-stage underwriting.

US Treasury Yield Curve: Month-Over-Month Comparison

The month-over-month comparison of the US Treasury yield curve heading into June 8, 2026 tells a nuanced story. The short end of the curve remains anchored as the Federal Reserve holds its policy rate steady while inflation data continues to print within a tolerable range for the committee. The longer end, however, has exhibited moderate upward pressure, reflecting persistent fiscal deficit concerns and continued foreign central bank repositioning away from longer-dated US paper.

For commercial mortgage borrowers, the steepening of the 5-to-10-year portion of the curve is the most actionable development. Most permanent financing in the CRE space prices off the 5-year or 10-year Treasury. When that segment of the curve moves by even 15 to 25 basis points over a 30-day window, it directly affects the all-in coupon a borrower receives at application versus at rate lock. Sponsors who are mid-process on stabilized acquisitions or refinancings should be working with their broker to understand where rate lock timing sits relative to their commitment window.

What a Steeper Long End Means for Permanent Financing

Life insurance companies and CMBS conduit lenders, the two execution channels that dominate longer-term permanent financing, both price off Treasury benchmarks with credit spreads layered on top. When Treasury yields rise on the long end, those lenders do not absorb the increase into their spreads. The coupon moves up with the index. A loan that penciled at a 6.40 percent all-in rate in early May could easily be quoting in the mid-to-upper 6.50s by the time a term sheet is issued in early June, depending on property type, market, and leverage level.

This is not a reason to rush into a deal that does not underwrite. It is, however, a reason to compress timelines on transactions that are already qualified and in motion. Borrowers sitting on executed purchase agreements who have not yet engaged a mortgage broker are at risk of watching their debt service coverage ratios compress as rates drift higher.

Bridge and Short-Term Capital: SOFR Stability Provides a Window

On the floating-rate side of the market, SOFR-based bridge lending has been comparatively stable. Private bridge lenders and debt fund capital remain active across transitional multifamily, mixed-use, and light industrial assets. Spreads over SOFR have compressed modestly from their 2024 peaks as more capital has returned to the space, increasing competition among lenders.

For value-add sponsors, this is a constructive window. A well-structured bridge loan at current SOFR levels with an interest rate cap in place provides operational flexibility during a lease-up or renovation period without locking into a permanent coupon that may look expensive in 18 to 24 months if the long end of the curve retraces. The cost of that optionality needs to be modeled carefully, specifically the cap premium, the exit fee structure, and the extension conditions, but for the right business plan the math often works.

Regional Bank Activity: Selective but Present

Regional bank lenders remain in the market but continue to operate with elevated selectivity. Construction and land lending are still largely off the table at most institutions, but stabilized income-producing assets in primary and strong secondary markets continue to clear regional bank credit committees. Relationship borrowers with deposits or other banking ties to a specific institution are seeing materially better execution than one-off applicants approaching the same bank cold.

Portfolio lenders at the regional bank level are pricing primarily off their internal cost of funds, which tracks shorter-term Treasuries and SOFR more closely than the 10-year. In an environment where the short end is anchored and the long end is drifting up, regional banks can sometimes offer competitive fixed-rate pricing on shorter-term paper, three to five year balloons, relative to what life companies and CMBS are quoting on 10-year fixed product. The tradeoff is prepayment flexibility and term length, not rate alone.

Deal Velocity and What We Are Seeing in the Market

Transaction volume across our pipeline in June reflects cautious optimism. Refinancing activity is the primary driver as sponsors who acquired assets in 2021 and 2022 on floating-rate bridge debt are now working through their exit options. Some of those assets have stabilized on the business plan and are qualifying for permanent takeout financing. Others are still in lease-up or dealing with occupancy softness and require an extended bridge or a structured recapitalization.

Acquisition financing for new purchases is more active than it was six months ago, driven primarily by buyers who have accepted the current rate environment as the new baseline rather than waiting for a Fed pivot that has not materialized on the timetable many expected. Well-priced assets in supply-constrained markets are trading, and debt is available for qualified sponsors at workable leverage levels.

The common thread across both refinancings and acquisitions is that lender scrutiny on cash flow quality has not relaxed. Rent rolls, lease terms, tenant credit, and expense histories are being reviewed with the same rigor we saw at the peak of the post-2023 credit tightening cycle. Sponsors who walk into a financing process with clean, well-organized due diligence packages are closing transactions. Those who do not are creating unnecessary friction and timeline risk.

Positioning Your Deal for the Current Environment

Whether you are refinancing an existing asset, acquiring a new one, or recapitalizing a joint venture, the June 2026 capital markets environment rewards preparation and speed. Lender appetite is present. Capital is available across the risk spectrum. The variable that separates executed transactions from stalled ones is almost always process quality and market access, not the absence of capital.

Working with a mortgage broker who maintains active relationships across the full lender universe, from regional banks and credit unions to life companies, CMBS conduits, debt funds, and private bridge capital, is the most direct path to identifying the right execution for your specific asset and business plan.
