Commercial Real Estate Capital Markets Report: May 4, 2026 Rate and Yield Update

By Trevor Damyan May 04, 2026

Every week in commercial real estate, the difference between a deal that closes and one that dies on the vine often comes down to timing and rate awareness. The May 4, 2026 edition of the CLS CRE Capital Markets Report captures where the US Treasury yield curve stands right now, how it shifted month over month, and what that movement means for borrowers actively seeking commercial mortgage financing.

US Treasury Yield Curve: Month Over Month Comparison

The yield curve is the backbone of commercial real estate pricing. Whether you are negotiating a 10-year fixed-rate permanent loan, a 5-year bank term loan, or a short-term bridge facility, lenders index to Treasuries. Understanding the curve is not academic for CRE investors. It is operational.

As of early May 2026, the yield curve continues to reflect a market that is recalibrating. After a prolonged period of inversion and volatility tied to Federal Reserve policy uncertainty, short-term yields have begun to drift lower while the long end of the curve has remained stubbornly elevated. This dynamic, sometimes called a bear steepener in reverse, is meaningful for commercial mortgage borrowers for several reasons.

First, spreads on 10-year CMBS and life company loans are priced off the 10-year Treasury. When the long end stays elevated, all-in rates on permanent financing remain elevated even if the Fed has begun cutting the overnight rate. Borrowers waiting for the Fed to move before locking long-term debt may be waiting for a signal that does not translate directly into lower permanent loan rates.

Second, floating-rate bridge loans are priced off SOFR, which follows the Fed funds rate more closely. As short-term rates ease, bridge loan costs drop, which is improving the math on value-add and transitional deals that were barely penciling 12 to 18 months ago.

What the Yield Curve Shift Means for Deal Structures Right Now

The month-over-month comparison in this report shows a moderate flattening at the short end and relative stability at the 7-year to 10-year range. For borrowers and their advisors, here is how to read that for execution purposes.

Permanent financing: Life insurance companies and CMBS conduits are pricing 10-year money in a range that rewards low-leverage, stabilized assets. Sponsors with properties at or above 90 percent occupancy, strong in-place cash flow, and modest LTV requests are seeing competitive execution. Debt service coverage requirements remain the primary governor on proceeds, not LTV alone.

Bridge and construction: A private bridge lender active in major metro markets recently repriced floating-rate construction and transitional bridge products lower in response to SOFR movement. That repricing has reopened conversations on deals that stalled in late 2025. If your project was underwritten 12 months ago and the rate environment felt prohibitive, it is worth a fresh look.

Regional bank portfolio loans: A regional bank with strong CRE concentration in the Southeast recently indicated increased appetite for 3-year to 5-year term loans on multifamily and industrial assets at 65 percent LTV or below. Community and regional banks remain an important source of relationship-based capital for mid-market sponsors, particularly in secondary and tertiary markets where CMBS and life company execution is less competitive.

Recent CLS CRE Closed Transactions

Deal activity at CLS CRE in the first weeks of May reflects the broader market dynamic: sponsors who are prepared with clean financials, realistic pricing expectations, and flexible structure are closing. Those anchored to 2021 and 2022 cost of capital expectations are still on the sideline.

Across recently closed transactions, the common thread is disciplined underwriting and lender relationships built over multiple cycles. A life insurance company provided long-term fixed-rate financing on a stabilized net lease industrial portfolio. A private bridge lender funded a ground-up multifamily construction loan in a high-barrier-to-entry coastal market. A regional bank closed a cash-out refinance on a grocery-anchored retail center with a long-term anchor lease providing certainty of income.

These are not outlier deals. They are representative of what is executable right now when a borrower works with an experienced intermediary who knows where capital is and where it is not.

Sector and Market Observations for May 2026

Multifamily: New supply deliveries in Sun Belt markets have compressed rent growth, and some lenders have pulled back on lease-up and new construction in overbuilt submarkets. Core coastal and gateway markets continue to attract deep lender interest. Workforce housing in supply-constrained markets remains a favored asset class across nearly every lender type.

Industrial: Demand from last-mile logistics, reshoring manufacturing, and data center adjacent uses continues to support strong lender appetite for well-located industrial. Cap rate compression has paused, but income growth from mark-to-market leasing continues to support valuations.

Office: Lenders remain highly selective. Class A trophy assets in proven live-work-play submarkets can find financing. Suburban Class B and C office continues to face a very narrow lender universe. If you are working through an office refinance or acquisition, early-stage lender outreach and realistic leverage expectations are critical.

Retail: Grocery-anchored and necessity-based retail is performing well from a lender appetite standpoint. Power centers and enclosed malls face continued structural headwinds with most mainstream lenders.

Working With CLS CRE in This Environment

The capital markets are not frozen. They are selective. Borrowers who treat lender outreach as a commodity process and blast applications to every lender they can find are not getting the best execution. The sponsors closing deals right now are working with advisors who know which lenders are active in which asset classes, which structures a given lender will consider, and how to present a deal so it gets a serious look.

At CLS CRE, every engagement starts with a capital markets assessment before an application goes anywhere. That means understanding your asset, your timeline, your exit, and the realistic universe of lenders who can deliver. It is a more deliberate process, and it produces better results.

If you have a deal in front of you, a refinance approaching maturity, or a value-add acquisition you are working to capitalize, now is the time to have that conversation.
