Case Study

$50,000,000 ED1 Large-Scale Affordable (140 Units) in South Los Angeles, CA

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Construction Loan

The Deal

Commercial Lending Solutions arranged $50 million in construction financing for a 140-unit affordable housing development in South Los Angeles, representing one of the largest Executive Directive 1 (ED1) projects approved in LA at the time. The seven-story Type III construction project leveraged California's ED1 streamlined approval process, which allows qualifying affordable housing developments to bypass traditional discretionary entitlements in favor of ministerial approval.

ED1, established under Executive Order N-06-19 and codified through various state legislation including AB 2011, enables affordable housing projects meeting specific criteria to receive expedited processing. Projects must achieve minimum density thresholds, include prevailing wage requirements, and meet affordability standards to qualify. This regulatory framework was designed to address California's housing crisis by reducing approval timelines from years to months.

The capital structure included seven distinct funding sources: tax-exempt bonds, 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC), Los Angeles Measure HHH funds, Affordable Housing and Sustainable Communities (AHSC) program financing, city soft money, general partner equity, and the conventional first mortgage construction loan. Construction timeline was projected at 28 months.

The Challenge

The primary challenge centered on coordinating seven different capital sources, each operating under distinct closing schedules, draw procedures, and compliance requirements. Traditional construction lenders typically manage two to three funding sources on affordable housing deals. This project's complexity required a lender with sophisticated operational infrastructure and experience managing multi-layered capital stacks.

Each funding source imposed unique requirements. The 4% LIHTC allocation required compliance with placed-in-service deadlines and cost certification procedures. Measure HHH funds operated under municipal oversight with specific reporting protocols. AHSC financing included sustainability benchmarks and transit-oriented development requirements. Tax-exempt bond proceeds required arbitrage compliance and qualified expenditure tracking.

The draw coordination presented the most significant operational challenge. Different funding sources required varying documentation standards, approval processes, and timing. Some sources required monthly draws while others operated on quarterly schedules. Misalignment between funding source requirements could create cash flow gaps during construction, potentially delaying the project or requiring expensive gap financing.

Additionally, while ED1 provided entitlement streamlining, the project's scale and complexity meant construction lenders remained cautious about execution risk on what was then among the largest ED1 projects approved.

The Solution

We identified a national Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI) with proven operational capacity for managing complex affordable housing capital stacks. The lender maintained dedicated affordable housing underwriting teams and possessed sophisticated project management systems capable of tracking multiple funding source requirements simultaneously.

The CDFI structured the construction loan to accommodate all seven funding sources through a master draw coordination system. This approach established standardized documentation requirements across funding sources where possible while maintaining separate compliance tracking for source-specific requirements. The lender designated a single project manager to interface with all capital providers, eliminating coordination confusion that typically emerges when multiple parties attempt to manage draw processes independently.

We negotiated flexible draw scheduling that accommodated the various funding source timelines while maintaining adequate cash flow for construction progress. The structure included provisions for temporary advances when funding source timing misalignments occurred, preventing construction delays.

The CDFI's experience with ED1 projects provided additional value. Their underwriting team understood the regulatory framework and associated risks, enabling faster loan approval compared to lenders unfamiliar with the streamlined approval process. This expertise proved crucial given the project's status as one of the largest ED1 developments at approval time.

The Outcome

The construction loan closed on schedule, with all seven funding sources successfully coordinated through the CDFI's project management system. The master draw coordination approach eliminated potential cash flow disruptions and maintained construction momentum throughout the 28-month build period.

The project achieved its ED1 timeline advantages, completing entitlement in approximately eight months compared to the typical three-plus years required for traditional discretionary approval processes. This timeline compression provided significant carrying cost savings and enabled the development team to commence construction while market conditions remained favorable.

The successful execution demonstrated that large-scale ED1 projects could achieve financial closing despite complex capital stack requirements. The deal established precedent for similar large-scale affordable developments utilizing streamlined approval processes, contributing to increased lender comfort with ED1 project structures.

Post-closing, the coordinated draw process functioned as designed, with no significant delays attributable to funding source misalignment. The project's success reinforced the viability of ED1 for substantial affordable housing developments and highlighted the importance of selecting construction lenders with appropriate operational sophistication for complex capital stack management.
