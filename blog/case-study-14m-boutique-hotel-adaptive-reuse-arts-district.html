$14,000,000 Hospitality / Boutique Hotel Conversion, Downtown Los Angeles / Arts District / Hollywood, CA

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan · April 21, 2026 · Construction Loan

Hospitality construction financing remains one of the most challenging asset classes in commercial real estate, particularly for boutique hotel concepts in urban markets. When a seasoned hospitality developer approached our team seeking $14 million in construction financing for an adaptive reuse project in downtown Los Angeles, the deal required navigating multiple layers of complexity that traditional construction lenders simply wouldn't touch in today's market.

The Deal

The borrower, an experienced hospitality developer with a proven track record in boutique hotel operations, identified an underutilized building in LA's Arts District for conversion into a 65-key micro-hotel. The project represented classic adaptive reuse: taking existing commercial space and transforming it into a hospitality asset designed for the modern traveler seeking authentic neighborhood experiences over standardized hotel chains.

The sponsor needed $14 million in construction financing to execute the conversion, with plans to refinance into permanent financing upon completion. The project timeline called for an 18-month construction period, with the borrower contributing 30% of total project costs as equity.

The Challenge

Three factors made this deal particularly difficult to finance through conventional channels. First, hospitality construction lending has contracted significantly since 2020, with most regional and community banks either avoiding the sector entirely or requiring guarantee structures that sophisticated developers won't accept.

Second, the micro-hotel concept, while increasingly popular with travelers, doesn't fit the underwriting templates that traditional construction lenders use for hospitality deals. Room counts, average daily rates, and revenue per available room metrics don't align with conventional hotel models, making it difficult for generalist lenders to properly evaluate the project's feasibility.

Third, despite the adaptive reuse approach reducing construction risk compared to ground-up development, lenders remained concerned about the Arts District's hospitality absorption rates and post-pandemic recovery trajectory. The location's transformation from industrial to mixed-use creative hub is relatively recent, leaving limited comparable data for underwriting purposes.

The Solution

Rather than approaching traditional construction lenders, we targeted specialty hospitality debt funds that maintain dedicated underwriting teams familiar with boutique hotel economics. These lenders understand that micro-hotels operate on different metrics than full-service properties and can properly evaluate location-specific factors that generic construction lenders miss.

The winning lender, a private debt fund specializing in hospitality construction, structured the deal as an 18-month construction facility at 70% loan-to-cost. The rate was set at prime plus 275 basis points with interest reserves built into the loan amount. The lender required a completion guarantee from the sponsor but avoided personal recourse provisions that had been deal-breakers with other potential financing sources.

Equally important, we secured a forward takeout commitment from the life insurance company arm of a major institutional investor. This hospitality-focused permanent lender agreed to a 10-year fixed-rate loan at 65% loan-to-value upon completion, assuming the project met agreed-upon performance thresholds during the initial operating period.

The Outcome

The borrower achieved several key objectives that weren't available through traditional financing channels. The construction lender's hospitality expertise meant faster underwriting and approval processes, with due diligence focused on relevant market factors rather than generic construction metrics that didn't apply to the project.

The forward takeout commitment eliminated refinancing risk, allowing the sponsor to proceed with construction knowing permanent financing was secured at predetermined terms. This structure also satisfied the construction lender's requirements for a clear exit strategy, facilitating approval of the initial facility.

Perhaps most importantly, the financing structure preserved the borrower's flexibility to optimize the hotel's operating profile during lease-up without pressure to conform to standard hospitality models. The lenders understood that boutique properties often require 12-18 months to establish market positioning and stabilize revenue, building appropriate expectations into their underwriting rather than treating extended lease-up periods as warning signs.

The deal closed in January 2026, with construction commencing immediately. For hospitality developers pursuing adaptive reuse projects in emerging urban submarkets, this transaction demonstrates that appropriate financing remains available, but requires lenders who understand both the asset class specifics and local market dynamics that make these projects viable.
