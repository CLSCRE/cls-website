Case Study: $2.4M Bank-Branch Net Lease Financing in Martinez, CA

By Trevor Damyan July 28, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $2,400,000 loan through Bank of Hope for an out-of-state family trust acquiring a net-leased bank branch property in Martinez, California. The loan closed at 3.375% fixed with a 10-year balloon, a 1.35x minimum debt service coverage requirement, and unlimited personal guaranties from both trustees.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal brought us an out-of-state family trust, based in Texas, looking to finance a single-tenant bank branch property in Martinez, California, in the East Bay. Financing an out-of-state buyer into a California net-lease asset meant building an underwriting story around the strength of the tenant and location rather than the trust's local footprint.

A net-leased bank branch is a straightforward asset on paper, but getting a regional bank like Bank of Hope to move quickly for a trust with no California presence still required careful packaging of the tenant credit, lease terms, and trustees' financial standing. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in net lease financing and out-of-state trust lending for investors acquiring commercial property in California.

What Made It Hard

  • An out-of-state borrowing trust. With the trustees based in Texas and no existing California banking relationship, we had to build a complete underwriting package from scratch rather than lean on an established local track record.
  • Balancing amortization against a 10-year balloon. Structuring 119 level payments against a full payoff at maturity meant the trust needed clear visibility into its refinance or sale exit well before the loan came due.
  • Meeting the lender's ongoing coverage covenant. A minimum 1.35x debt service coverage requirement meant the net lease's terms had to support that cushion cleanly, not just clear the bar at closing.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the loan with Bank of Hope, closing with a note dated September 17, 2021:

  • $2,400,000 secured by the bank-branch property in Martinez, California
  • 3.375% fixed for the full term, calculated on a 365/360 basis
  • 119 monthly payments of $10,675.22, followed by a final balloon payment of the remaining principal balance at the 10-year maturity date
  • A minimum 1.35x debt service coverage ratio maintained for the life of the loan
  • A declining prepayment penalty of 3% in years one through five, 2% in year six, and 1% in year seven, waived entirely in the event of a sale
  • Unlimited personal guaranties from both trustees of the borrowing family trust, with full payment and performance obligations rather than limited or capped exposure

The loan closed with the trust's California acquisition fully financed and its Texas-based trustees standing behind the loan as unlimited guarantors, giving the bank the coverage cushion and recourse it needed for an out-of-state borrower.

"An out-of-state trust with no California track record can still get a bank comfortable fast, as long as the tenant credit and lease terms do the talking instead of the borrower's local history."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

Building a clean underwriting package around the tenant credit and net lease terms, rather than the borrowing trust's local track record, is what got an out-of-state trust financed quickly on a California asset. A single-tenant bank branch on a long-term net lease gives a lender a straightforward income story to underwrite, and presenting that clearly kept the file moving despite the trust having no prior California banking relationship.

Locking in a 3.375% fixed rate with a declining prepayment schedule that steps down to zero on a sale also gave the trust real flexibility heading into the back half of the 10-year term, rather than boxing them into a rigid structure on an out-of-state hold.

A declining prepayment penalty schedule charges a percentage of the prepaid principal that steps down each year the loan is outstanding, giving the borrower increasing flexibility to refinance or sell over time. This loan carried a 3% penalty in years one through five, stepping down to 2% in year six and 1% in year seven, with the penalty waived entirely in the event of a sale.
A balloon structure amortizes the loan on a longer schedule for monthly payment purposes, but requires the full remaining principal balance to be paid off at a set maturity date well before the amortization period ends. This loan required 119 monthly payments before a final balloon payment of the remaining balance at year 10.
