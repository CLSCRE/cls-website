SB 79 Unlocks By-Right Multifamily Density Near Transit Across LA County

By Trevor Damyan May 29, 2026

On October 10, 2025, Governor Newsom signed SB 79 into law. Beginning July 1, 2026, every qualifying transit-adjacent parcel in Los Angeles County becomes eligible for ministerial multifamily approval at densities ranging from 80 to 120 units per acre, by right. No discretionary review. No CEQA. No public hearings. For developers who have spent years navigating local opposition and unpredictable entitlement timelines, that sentence changes the calculus on a lot of sites.

This article walks through what the law actually does, how to confirm whether a site qualifies, what a credible underwrite looks like before earnest money is wired, and how capital stacks are shaping up for the first wave of SB 79 projects.

What SB 79 Does

SB 79 (Wiener) overrides local zoning on parcels within one half mile of qualifying transit stations. The bill establishes statewide minimum density and height standards that cities must approve ministerially on residential or mixed-use projects meeting the stated criteria. The law eliminates the discretionary review, CEQA exposure, and public hearing process that has historically allowed local governments to stall or kill infill multifamily projects near transit.

The bill creates two development tiers based on transit service quality:

Tier 1 (heavy rail, BRT, ferry, or 72 or more trains per day) covers lines including the LA Metro B Line (Red), D Line (Purple), and A Line (Blue). Within the inner one quarter mile of a qualifying station: 120 dwelling units per acre, FAR 3.5, maximum height 75 feet. Within the outer one quarter to one half mile band: 100 dwelling units per acre, FAR 3.0, maximum height 65 feet.

Tier 2 (light rail and BRT) covers lines including the LA Metro C, E, and K Lines. Inner one quarter mile: 100 dwelling units per acre, FAR 3.0, maximum height 65 feet. Outer one quarter to one half mile band: 80 dwelling units per acre, FAR 2.5, maximum height 55 feet.

SB 79 also carries labor standards requiring prevailing wage on projects of 50 or more units, a lower-income housing set-aside requirement in Tier 1 zones, antidisplacement protections for existing tenants, and oversight authority for the California Department of Housing and Community Development. Local agencies must comply beginning July 1, 2026.

Does Your Site Qualify

Before running a proforma, confirm four threshold criteria:

1. Within one half mile of qualifying transit. Qualifying service includes heavy rail, light rail, BRT, ferry, and major commuter stations. Distance must be confirmed using the station map and a parcel-level calculation, not a rough estimate from a mapping tool.

2. Appropriate base zoning. Single-family-only zones are excluded. In Los Angeles City, R2 and above, CG, C2, C4, and mixed-use districts qualify. Verify the current zoning designation before proceeding, as overlay conditions or recent zone changes can affect eligibility.

3. Project geometry within statutory parameters. The project must include a minimum of 5 units, achieve a minimum density of 30 dwelling units per acre, and maintain an average unit size of 1,750 net habitable square feet or less. Critically, if any rent-controlled or deed-restricted affordable housing was demolished on the parcel within the prior 10 years, the site is disqualified.

4. Legally formed parcel. SB 79 operates ministerially on a legal parcel. It is not a substitute for a lot line adjustment or parcel map. Confirm all assessor parcel numbers, check for deleted prior APNs, unrecorded lot ties, and any outstanding parcel map conditions before committing capital.

How to Underwrite a Qualifying Site

The ministerial approval pathway removes entitlement risk from the equation, but it does not remove execution risk. A disciplined underwrite on an SB 79 site should address the following:

Density and massing confirmation. Model two scenarios: the maximum allowable density under the applicable tier and a conservative build program that optimizes unit mix for the submarket. Parking requirements under SB 79 are reduced near transit, which meaningfully improves buildable area on constrained sites.

Prevailing wage impact on construction cost. On projects of 50 or more units, prevailing wage applies. Budget accordingly. Developers who underwrite at non-union costs on SB 79 projects above the threshold are introducing a material budget gap before they break ground.

Affordable set-aside in Tier 1. Model the lower-income housing set-aside into revenue assumptions from day one. The exact percentage should be confirmed against the final HCD guidance, but underwriting to market-rate absorption on every unit in a Tier 1 location is an error.

Displacement and demolition history. Pull the rent roll and confirm no rent-controlled or deed-restricted units have been removed from the site in the prior 10 years. Title and assessor records alone are not sufficient. Order a dedicated tenant protection screening.

Timeline to building permit. Ministerial approval means the clock starts running differently. A well-prepared application package on a legally clean site can move from submittal to permit faster than any entitlement pathway that existed before this law. Build a realistic schedule and confirm it with the applicable building department before site acquisition.

Financing the Deal: Bridge, Construction, and Permanent

Capital markets are beginning to price SB 79 sites differently from speculative entitled land, and that distinction matters at every stage of the capital stack.

Acquisition and predevelopment. Sites that have confirmed ministerial eligibility but have not yet submitted for permit are attracting interest from private bridge lenders willing to lend against the land value with a path to construction. Loan-to-cost on these positions is conservative, typically in the 55 to 65 percent range, but the certainty of the approval pathway is being treated as a meaningful risk mitigant compared to discretionary entitled land.

Construction financing. Regional banks and debt funds are underwriting SB 79 construction loans on a project-by-project basis. Lenders are prioritizing sponsorship track record, market absorption data for the submarket, and confirmed prevailing wage compliance documentation. Interest reserves and contingency requirements remain elevated relative to pre-2024 construction lending benchmarks.

Permanent financing. Stabilized transit-oriented multifamily in Los Angeles has a strong permanent lending market. Life insurance companies and agency lenders are active on stabilized product at these density levels, particularly where the income profile includes a mix of affordable and market-rate units that supports agency qualification. Borrowers who structure the affordable set-aside correctly from the start are positioning themselves for the most competitive permanent debt available in the market.

Timeline: What Happens July 1, 2026

July 1, 2026 is the date local agencies are required to begin processing SB 79 applications on a ministerial basis. That does not mean every city will have its administrative procedures perfectly aligned on day one. Developers who want to be in the first wave of applicants should be preparing complete application packages now, confirming site eligibility with legal counsel, and opening conversations with building departments to understand local intake procedures before the effective date.

The developers who move in the first 90 days after July 1 will have the best selection of qualifying sites, the most negotiating leverage with land sellers who understand the law is now effective, and the earliest potential construction start windows in a submarket that will become increasingly competitive as the SB 79 pipeline builds.

CLS CRE Perspective

SB 79 is the most significant shift in multifamily entitlement risk in Los Angeles in a generation. Removing discretionary review and CEQA from qualifying transit-adjacent sites does not eliminate development complexity, but it relocates the primary risk from the entitlement process to execution: site selection, capital structure, construction management, and lease-up. Those are risks that experienced sponsors and their capital partners know how to underwrite.
