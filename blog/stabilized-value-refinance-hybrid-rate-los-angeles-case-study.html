Case Study: $13.8M Refinance Sized to Stabilized Value with a Hybrid Rate in Los Angeles

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $13,800,000 refinance through Ventura County Credit Union for a commercial property in Los Angeles, California, capped at 70% of the property's as-stabilized value. The loan closed at 4.50% fixed for the first seven years of a 10-year term, amortized over 25 years, with a capped rate reset for the remaining three years and guaranties from multiple individuals and their family trusts.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this refinance brought us a multi-guarantor ownership group refinancing a commercial property in Los Angeles, California held under a master lease structure. The ownership wanted a lender willing to size the loan against the property's stabilized upside rather than its current as-is performance, unlocking meaningfully more proceeds than a strictly as-is underwrite would support. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in permanent financing and complex multi-guarantor capital structures for commercial property owners across California.

Properties operating under a master lease structure, where an intermediate tenant re-leases space to end users, require a lender comfortable underwriting both layers of that arrangement, and finding a credit union willing to size to as-stabilized value on that structure gave this ownership group real leverage advantage.

What Made It Hard

  • Sizing to as-stabilized rather than as-is value. Capping the loan at 70% of the property's projected stabilized value, rather than its current performance, required the credit union's underwriting to have real confidence in the property's path to that stabilized income, adding scrutiny beyond a standard as-is refinance.
  • Structuring guaranties across multiple individuals and trusts. With personal guaranties required from more than one guarantor plus separate guaranties from each of their family trusts, and the new master lease itself required to be guaranteed by all parties, the documentation load across multiple guarantors and trust structures was substantial.
  • Meeting a tight acceptance deadline. With signed acceptance due within roughly nine days of the term sheet's date, the ownership group had to move through review, guaranty coordination, and signing on a compressed schedule.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the refinance with Ventura County Credit Union, with the signed term sheet dated March 21, 2022:

  • Up to $13,800,000, capped at 70% of the property's as-stabilized loan-to-value, secured by a first trust deed, assignment of rents, and hazardous substance filing on the property
  • 4.50% fixed for the first seven years of a 10-year term, with years eight through ten resetting to the Wall Street Journal Prime rate plus 1.00%, subject to a 4.50% floor and a 7.00% ceiling
  • A 25-year amortization schedule, with monthly principal and interest payments due on the 25th of each month and any outstanding balance due at maturity
  • A minimum 1.20x annual debt service coverage ratio
  • A 1.00% loan origination fee, plus a $2,000 documentation fee, a $550 environmental risk assessment fee, and out-of-pocket expenses capped at $2,500
  • A declining prepayment penalty of 3% in year one, 2% in year two, and 1% in year three
  • Personal guaranties from multiple individual guarantors, plus separate guaranties from each guarantor's family trust where assets were trust-held, with trust documents provided as part of underwriting
  • A guaranteed new master lease covering the property, required to be executed and guaranteed by all guarantors and their associated trusts
  • A $10,000 good faith deposit submitted with the signed term sheet, applied toward other loan fees, plus deposit relationships including a personal savings account and a business checking account opened and maintained at the credit union for the life of the loan
  • A signed acceptance deadline of March 30, 2022, requiring the ownership group to move quickly through internal review and guaranty coordination

The refinance closed with all guarantors and trusts executing their respective guaranties and the master lease in place, giving the ownership group proceeds sized to the property's stabilized upside rather than its current as-is performance.

"Sizing to as-stabilized value only works if you can show the lender a credible path to get there. We built that case around the master lease structure, and once the credit union had confidence in it, the extra proceeds followed."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

Getting the credit union comfortable sizing to as-stabilized value, rather than the property's current as-is performance, is what unlocked meaningfully more proceeds for this ownership group. That underwriting approach depends on the lender having real confidence in the property's path to stabilization, and building that case clearly through the master lease structure and supporting documentation made the difference in loan size.

Coordinating guaranties across multiple individuals and their family trusts, all inside a roughly nine-day acceptance window, also mattered here. With trust documents, personal financial statements, and tax returns required from every guarantor simultaneously, moving that documentation in parallel rather than sequentially kept the refinance from missing its signed-acceptance deadline.

Is This Structure Right for Your Deal?

If your property has real upside not yet reflected in its current performance, a lender willing to size a refinance against as-stabilized value can deliver meaningfully more proceeds than a strictly as-is underwrite.
