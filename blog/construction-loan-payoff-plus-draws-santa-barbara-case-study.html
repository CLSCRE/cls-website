Case Study: $10M Payoff-Plus-Construction Line for a Santa Barbara Property

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $10,000,000 non-revolving construction line of credit through Ventura County Credit Union for a property in Santa Barbara, California. The facility paid off the existing loan on the property with an initial $4,500,000 disbursement, then released the remaining balance through construction draws and an interest reserve, priced at 9.50% fixed interest-only over a 24-month term against a $17,875,000 as-proposed value.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, and this deal brought us a Santa Barbara, California (CA) estate property mid-transition: the ownership group had an existing loan on the property and a construction plan to complete, and needed one credit union lender willing to retire the old debt and fund the remaining construction budget under a single facility rather than juggling two separate loans. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in construction financing for high-end residential and estate properties for owners across California and nationwide.

Santa Barbara's high-end residential and estate market rewards patient, well-capitalized construction lending, and a credit union willing to size a facility against the property's finished, as-proposed value rather than its current condition was exactly what this project needed.

What Made It Hard

  • Structuring a payoff and a construction draw in one facility. The lender needed to release an initial $4,500,000 to retire the existing loan while simultaneously setting up the remaining roughly $5,500,000 for construction draws and an interest reserve, all under a single non-revolving line rather than two separate transactions.
  • Underwriting to a completed, as-proposed value. Sizing the loan against a $17,875,000 as-proposed valuation, rather than the property's current condition, required a lender comfortable underwriting the finished project rather than just the collateral sitting on the ground today.
  • Coordinating third-party construction monitoring. Every draw required updated site inspections verifying that construction had actually progressed in line with the budget and timeline before funds moved, adding a layer of process the borrower had to plan around.

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (CA DRE 02244836), placed the facility with Ventura County Credit Union, with the term sheet accepted in March 2024:

  • $10,000,000 maximum loan amount, structured as a non-revolving construction line of credit secured by a first lien position on the Santa Barbara property
  • $4,500,000 disbursed initially to pay off the existing loan on the property, with the remaining approximately $5,500,000 released per the construction schedule and third-party monitoring review, including an interest reserve
  • 9.50% fixed rate for the full 24-month term, interest-only, with the full balance due at the end of the term
  • 70% maximum loan-to-value and loan-to-cost, based on a $17,875,000 as-proposed appraised value once construction is complete
  • A $1,250,000 estimated interest reserve, giving the borrower a built-in source to cover monthly interest payments during the construction period
  • A minimum global debt service coverage ratio of 1.25x, evaluated across the guarantors' overall financial picture, not just the subject property
  • Third-party construction monitoring through a real estate project management company, with plan and specification review fees of roughly $5,000, funds control at 0.5% of hard costs, and $500 per site inspection
  • No prepayment penalty for the duration of the facility, and a 0.75% origination fee plus a 0.25% 45-day rate lock fee

The facility closed with the existing loan retired in the same transaction that established the construction line, giving the ownership group a single, coordinated source of capital to carry the project from payoff through completion.

"Anchoring the loan to the finished, as-proposed value instead of where the project sits today is what made the numbers work. One facility handled the payoff and the build instead of stacking two closings."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

Why We Won This One

Structuring the payoff and the construction draw schedule into one facility, rather than treating them as two separate financing events, is what let this deal close cleanly. A lender willing to fund both pieces under one non-revolving line meant the borrower did not have to coordinate a separate refinance closing before the construction budget could even begin funding.

Getting the as-proposed valuation and the loan-to-cost sizing right up front was equally important. Anchoring the loan to the property's completed value, rather than its current as-is condition, is what unlocked enough proceeds to both retire the existing debt and fund the construction budget within a single 70% loan-to-value ceiling.

Is This Structure Right for Your Deal?

If you have an existing loan on a property mid-construction and need a single lender to both retire that debt and fund the remaining build-out, this kind of combined facility can simplify your capital stack considerably.
