Hospitality RevPAR Recovery and Lender Commentary (Week of April 20, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·April 20, 2026

RevPAR Recovery Reflects Segment Stratification

Hospitality RevPAR recovery continues to exhibit stark segment-specific patterns that are reshaping how specialty lenders approach deal structures and pricing. Luxury properties are posting the most resilient performance metrics, with RevPAR levels in many markets reaching 95% to 105% of 2019 benchmarks. This strength reflects both ADR premiums and occupancy stability among high-net-worth leisure and corporate travelers who have largely resumed pre-pandemic booking behaviors.

Select-service and upscale segments are showing more varied recovery trajectories, typically ranging from 85% to 95% of historical RevPAR levels depending on market positioning and local demand drivers. Limited-service properties face the most challenging dynamics, with many assets still tracking 75% to 90% of pre-pandemic performance as cost-conscious travelers remain selective and corporate travel budgets stay compressed.

The divergence is particularly pronounced in gateway markets where luxury assets benefit from international travel normalization, while economy-focused properties contend with persistent rate compression and heightened competition from alternative lodging platforms.

Lender Appetite Mirrors Performance Tiers

Specialty hospitality lenders are calibrating their risk appetites and pricing structures to reflect these segment-specific recovery patterns. For luxury and upper-upscale assets with demonstrated cash flow stability, we're observing debt service coverage ratio requirements in the 1.25x to 1.35x range, with some flexibility for marquee properties in prime locations.

Life insurance companies and specialty debt funds are showing renewed interest in stabilized luxury assets, particularly those with strong brand affiliations and diversified revenue streams beyond rooms revenue. Loan-to-cost ratios for new development in the luxury segment are generally settling in the 65% to 75% range, assuming pre-leasing or management agreements with established operators.

Select-service and limited-service properties face more conservative underwriting standards. Lenders are typically requiring higher debt service coverage ratios, often 1.35x to 1.50x, and are placing greater emphasis on sponsor liquidity and operational track records. Interest rate spreads for these segments remain elevated compared to pre-pandemic levels, reflecting ongoing uncertainty about demand sustainability and margin pressure.

Market Dynamics Drive Underwriting Evolution

Beyond segment performance, lenders are increasingly focused on location-specific demand drivers and competitive positioning. Properties in leisure-oriented markets with strong demographic tailwinds are receiving more favorable treatment than those dependent solely on corporate travel recovery. Mixed-use developments that incorporate hospitality components alongside residential or retail elements are attracting interest from lenders seeking diversified cash flow profiles.

Environmental and social governance factors are also influencing lending decisions, with many institutional capital sources prioritizing energy-efficient properties and operators with demonstrated sustainability programs. This trend is particularly evident in new development financing, where green building certifications and renewable energy integration can meaningfully impact both pricing and loan proceeds.

Construction-to-permanent financing structures are gaining traction as developers seek certainty in an environment where exit financing terms remain somewhat unpredictable. Lenders are responding by offering more comprehensive deal structures, though typically at higher all-in costs compared to separate construction and permanent financing approaches.

Strategic Implications for Development Planning

For developers evaluating hospitality projects in the current environment, segment selection and market positioning are critical determinants of financing accessibility and cost. Luxury and upper-upscale projects in strong leisure markets offer the most straightforward path to competitive financing terms, while select-service development requires more careful underwriting of local demand dynamics and competitive positioning.

Timing considerations are equally important. Developers with flexibility in their project schedules may benefit from waiting for continued RevPAR recovery and potential improvements in construction costs, while those with firm site control and entitlements should engage with specialty lenders early to understand current appetite and structure preferences.

Sponsor strength and operational expertise carry heightened importance in current underwriting processes. Lenders are prioritizing developers and operators with demonstrated hospitality experience and strong balance sheet liquidity to navigate potential market volatility during lease-up and stabilization periods.
