1. Qwoted

Qwoted (app.qwoted.com) connects journalists to expert sources in real time. Journalists post queries and you receive email alerts when a query matches your expertise tags. Speed of response matters enormously; the first two or three replies typically get the quote.

Sign-Up Instructions

  1. Go to app.qwoted.com/sign-up
  2. Select Source (not journalist)
  3. Fill in profile fields with the content below
  4. Add expertise tags: Commercial Real Estate, Commercial Lending, Bridge Loans, Construction Finance, Expert Witness, CRE Capital Markets
  5. Enable email alerts for queries matching your expertise

Format: Structured fields plus a bio. Paste each section into the corresponding field on the Qwoted profile form. Bio limit is 250 words; the text below fits within that limit.

2. Muck Rack

Muck Rack (muckrack.com) is the primary platform used by PR professionals and sources to connect with journalists. It has a large directory of journalists organized by beat, and journalists actively search it for sources. The pitch bio format is shorter and more direct than Qwoted -- journalists see it in under five seconds and decide immediately whether to reach out.

Sign-Up Instructions

  1. Go to muckrack.com/public-relations/sign-up
  2. Select PR Professional / Source
  3. Fill in profile fields with the content below
  4. Claim your expert profile and add the beat keywords listed below

Muck Rack Source Profile

Format: Name, title, and a pitch bio of approximately 150 words. Muck Rack journalists see this bio when they search for sources by beat or topic. Write it as a direct pitch to a real journalist -- punchy, specific, immediately useful.

Founder of Commercial Lending Solutions (CLS CRE), he has arranged $1B+ in commercial real estate loans across all 50 states with access to 1,000+ lenders. Responds same day. Beat keywords to add: commercial real estate, CRE lending, bridge loans, construction finance, CMBS, multifamily finance, expert witness, capital markets
After creating your profile: Use Muck Rack's journalist search to find reporters who cover commercial real estate, CRE finance, multifamily, and capital markets. You can pitch them directly through the platform. Also set up a Muck Rack Alert for your own name and for "CLS CRE" so you are notified any time you or the firm are mentioned in media coverage.

After Signing Up -- 3 Tips to Actually Get Quoted

Creating a profile is step one. These three habits separate sources who get quoted regularly from those who sign up and never hear from a journalist.

  1. Respond to journalist queries within 2 hours. First responder usually wins the quote. Most journalists are on deadline and take the first two or three responses that directly answer their question. If you see a relevant query in your email and wait until tomorrow, the story is already filed.
  2. Keep responses under 100 words. Journalists quote the punchy version, not the full explanation. Give them one tight, quotable sentence with a specific data point or market observation. If they want more, they will follow up. The mistake most sources make is sending a five-paragraph response that is impossible to excerpt cleanly.
  3. After being quoted, send a thank-you and offer to be a regular source. That is how you get on their speed dial. A single follow-up email saying "happy to help on future stories covering CRE lending" takes thirty seconds and often turns a one-time quote into a repeat relationship with a journalist who covers your beat.
