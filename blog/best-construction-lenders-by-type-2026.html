Best Construction Lenders by Type: How to Choose in 2026

Trevor Damyan Founder & Principal Broker
CA DRE #02244836

Former CBRE and Marcus & Millichap Capital Corporation. $1B+ in closed commercial real estate financing across bridge, construction, permanent, and structured debt.

November 26, 2026
Quick answer: The best construction lender is the one whose credit box matches your project and sponsor. Banks and credit unions suit experienced sponsors who accept recourse and can wait 45 to 90 days. Debt funds suit speed and complexity. HUD-backed lenders suit ground-up multifamily and reach up to 85% of cost but take 12 to 24 months. Everyone else is capped at up to 75% of cost.

Construction lending is not one market. A bank that is the cheapest option for a seasoned developer will decline a first-time sponsor, a debt fund that closes in weeks charges for that speed, and a HUD-backed loan that offers the most leverage takes more than a year to close. Choosing well means matching your project and sponsor to the right type of lender before you ask for a term sheet. This guide compares each lender type on the points that decide a deal: leverage, recourse, speed and fit.

Match the lender to your project

  • Experienced multifamily or industrial developer with strong equity: Start with banks and credit unions for the best pricing. Add a debt fund to the field so the bank has competition and you have a fallback.
  • Ground-up apartments where leverage matters most: Compare a HUD-backed execution against a bank or debt fund loan. HUD gives the most leverage and a long fixed-rate permanent phase, in exchange for a 12 to 24 month process and prevailing wage requirements. Our HUD 221(d)(4) vs bank construction comparison walks through the decision.
  • Speculative project or a closing deadline: Debt funds and private lenders close fastest. Expect to pay for speed and to carry a higher spread.
  • Land or entitlement-stage project: Private lenders are usually the practical path until permits are issued, after which a bank or debt fund construction loan can take the project out. This is often a bridge loan versus construction loan question.
  • First-time developer: Banks will usually decline without an experienced co-sponsor. Bring one on, add equity and guarantees, or look at debt funds and private lenders that weigh the asset more than the resume.
  • You want one loan from construction to permanent: Compare construction-to-permanent loans with a separate takeout in our construction-to-perm comparison.

What to compare in a construction term sheet

The rate is the least reliable way to compare construction offers. Line them up on these items instead:

  • Proceeds: Loan-to-cost and the loan-to-value and debt yield tests that cap it. The lender lends the smallest of the three.
  • Recourse and completion guarantee: Who guarantees completion and repayment, and what falls away at completion or stabilization.
  • Interest reserve and contingency: Whether interest is funded from the loan and how much cost contingency is required, typically 5% to 10% of hard costs.
  • Draw mechanics: How fast draws fund (5 to 10 business days is typical), retainage held back (usually 5% to 10%), and who the inspector is.
  • Term and extensions: The initial term, extension tests and fees, and whether a conversion to permanent is built in.
  • Fees and prepayment: About 1 point is a typical lender fee, plus exit and extension fees and any prepayment terms.
  • Closing certainty: How many approvals remain, which third-party reports are required, and the lender's record of closing the construction loans it commits to.

How to read a "best construction lenders" list

Published rankings of construction lenders usually sort by balance sheet volume, or by how much land and construction lending a bank reports. Volume tells you who lends the most in total. It does not tell you who will lend on your property type, in your market, to your sponsor, at your leverage. A large national bank may be the biggest construction lender and still be the wrong desk for a $6 million infill project. Use a list to learn who is active, then test appetite with a specific package.

Construction lenders for Los Angeles projects

Los Angeles construction loans add entitlement and cost questions that most lenders will not fund through. Expect lenders to require permits issued or at final approval before the first draw, a fixed-price or guaranteed maximum price contract with a bonded contractor, and a budget that reflects local construction costs and fees. Projects using density programs such as Transit Oriented Communities or the City's ED 1 affordable housing process need lenders comfortable with that approval path, and they are often a different group from lenders for conventional market-rate projects.

How we choose lenders for a construction loan

  1. Underwrite the project first: Total cost, equity, sponsor experience, contractor, permits and exit.
  2. Target the right desks: We approach lenders whose current appetite fits the asset, sponsor and market, rather than sending the package to everyone.
  3. Create a comparable field: Proposals are compared on proceeds, recourse, reserves, fees and closing certainty, not only rate.
  4. Manage execution: We coordinate third-party reports, lender diligence and issue resolution until the loan funds.
