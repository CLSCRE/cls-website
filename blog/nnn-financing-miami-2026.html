NNN Financing in Miami: 2026 Guide for Net Lease Investors

Net Lease (NNN) Financing in Miami and South Florida: 2026 Market Guide

Miami and South Florida represent one of the most dynamic and competitive net lease markets in the Southeast. As a commercial mortgage broker, CLS CRE recognizes that investors seeking NNN properties in this region face unique opportunities and challenges. This guide explores the current Miami NNN landscape, lender programs, underwriting standards, and market dynamics that will shape investment decisions in 2026.

The Miami NNN Market in 2026

Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties collectively form a premier net lease market characterized by strong investor demand, limited available land, and consistent tenant quality. Cap rates in South Florida remain compressed relative to other Sun Belt markets, reflecting both foreign investor appetite and the region's demographic tailwinds.

Current cap rate ranges vary by property type and submarket:

  • Quick-Service Restaurants (QSR): 4.75 to 5.5%, with premium locations in Brickell and Miami Beach trading at 4.25 to 5.0%
  • Pharmacy: 5.0 to 5.75%, reflecting the stable cash flow and long lease terms typical of this sector
  • Auto Service: 5.25 to 6.25%, offering higher yields for investors accepting operational tenant risk

Foreign investor demand has been a primary driver of cap rate compression in Miami compared to comparable Sun Belt markets like Charlotte, Nashville, and Austin. Investors from Latin America, Canada, and Europe view Miami real estate as a stable, dollar-denominated asset class with strong demographic and economic fundamentals.

Active tenants in the Miami NNN market include McDonald's, Burger King, and Chick-fil-A for QSR; CVS and Walgreens for pharmacy; AutoZone for auto service; and Chase Bank and Regions Bank for financial services. The market also features a unique characteristic: significant participation from Spanish-language franchise operators and bilingual investors active in sale-leaseback transactions.

Land constraints present a structural advantage for existing NNN assets. Coastal development restrictions, urban density in Miami-Dade County, and limited suburban expansion opportunities limit new supply and support values of well-positioned net lease properties. This scarcity dynamic is expected to persist through 2026.

Lender Programs for Miami NNN

The Miami NNN market benefits from diverse lender participation across multiple loan size categories. Understanding which lenders serve different deal sizes is critical for structuring efficient financing.

Bank Programs (750K to 5M): National banks with dedicated net lease divisions offer 5-year fixed-rate terms with 25-year amortization schedules. These loans are fully recourse and typically close in 25 to 35 days. Banks in this category prefer investment-grade tenants and stabilized, single-tenant or dual-tenant properties.

CMBS Conduits (5M to 15M): Commercial mortgage-backed securities conduits are highly active in Miami, offering 10-year fixed-rate loans with non-recourse structures. CMBS lenders provide balance sheet efficiency for larger portfolios and typically accept both US and foreign borrower entities, though documentation requirements differ by conduit.

Life Insurance Companies (10M+): Life insurance companies provide long-term, fixed-rate financing for larger Miami NNN portfolios and are attracted to Florida's lender-friendly regulatory environment. These lenders often offer longer terms and flexibility on borrower structure.

Florida Regional Banks: Regional lenders active in Miami are competitive for deals between 1M and 5M, with a preference for recourse loans and local borrower relationships. These institutions often provide faster underwriting and approval for investors with existing deposits or business relationships.

Underwriting: What Miami Lenders Require

Miami NNN lenders employ consistent underwriting standards that reflect both tenant credit quality and regional risk factors. Borrowers should prepare comprehensive packages addressing the following criteria:

Loan-to-Value and Debt Service Coverage: Most Miami lenders target LTV ratios of 65 to 75%, with debt service coverage ratios (DSCR) of 1.25x to 1.35x. These ranges may tighten for secondary tenants or inland submarkets but remain stable for investment-grade anchors like national banks and major pharmacy chains.

Foreign National Borrowers: Foreign investors purchasing Miami NNN properties must typically establish a US-based LLC for lender purposes. Some CMBS conduits accept foreign entity ownership with enhanced documentation, but bank programs and life insurance companies universally require US entity structures. Personal guarantees from foreign principals are often required regardless of entity structure.

Flood Zone and Insurance Considerations: South Florida's FEMA flood map designations affect property-level risk assessment and insurance costs. Lenders require Phase I environmental reports and detailed flood risk analysis for any property in designated flood zones. This is critical in Miami-Dade and Broward counties, where coastal and low-lying properties face elevated flood exposure.

Insurance costs represent a material underwriting consideration in Miami. Windstorm and flood insurance premiums in South Florida are among the highest in the nation. Lenders will carefully evaluate the adequacy of tenant rent to cover increased insurance expense ratios, and borrowers should budget for these costs accordingly. A property with strong fundamentals in a high-risk flood zone may carry a slightly higher cap rate to reflect insurance burden.

Miami Market Dynamics and Tenant Mix

Several macro trends are reshaping Miami's NNN investment landscape in 2026.

Wealth Migration and 1031 Exchange Activity: Ongoing migration from the Northeast and California is driving substantial 1031 exchange demand in Miami. High-net-worth individuals replacing appreciated real estate in New York, New Jersey, and California are actively seeking NNN investments as diversification and income-producing assets.

Corporate Relocations: Finance and technology companies have established significant Miami presences, expanding the white-collar workforce and increasing demand for QSR, coffee, and service retail in Brickell, downtown Miami, and Miami Beach. This demographic shift supports premium cap rates for banking and professional services tenants.

Submarket Variation: Miami-Dade's most premium locations, including Brickell and Miami Beach, trade at ultra-low cap rates of 4.25 to 5.0% for bank and medical tenants. Inland markets such as Hialeah, Doral, and Kendall offer higher cap rates (5.5 to 6.0%) while maintaining comparable tenant credit quality. This geographic arbitrage appeals to value-oriented investors.

Palm Beach and Broward County: These counties represent strong alternatives to Miami-Dade, with cap rates typically 25 to 50 basis points higher. Properties in Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton, and West Palm Beach offer similar quality tenants at improved yields.

Foreign Investor Considerations

Foreign investor participation is a defining feature of the Miami NNN market. However, cross-border transactions involve tax, legal, and financing complexities that require careful planning.

Entity Structure and Lender Requirements: Most lenders require foreign investors to hold Miami NNN properties through US-based LLC structures. This requirement simplifies tax reporting, lender due diligence, and future disposition. Some CMBS conduits offer flexibility on this point, permitting foreign entity ownership with enhanced documentation and personal guarantees from foreign principals.

FIRPTA Withholding: Foreign investors selling appreciated NNN properties must comply with Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act (FIRPTA) requirements. A 15 percent withholding on the sales price is mandated at closing, with final tax liability determined at the investor's individual tax rate. This cost should be incorporated into after-tax return calculations.

Currency Considerations: Dollar-denominated NNN leases are particularly attractive to foreign investors seeking USD-denominated income streams. The stability of US tenant credit quality and currency diversification benefits position Miami NNN as a preferred asset class among international buyers.

Miami NNN Outlook 2026

Looking ahead to 2026, several trends will shape the Miami NNN market:

Continued Cap Rate Compression: Strong demand coupled with limited supply will likely compress cap rates further, particularly in premier submarkets and for investment-grade tenants. Investors should expect to accept lower yields in exchange for market-rate pricing and execution certainty.

Insurance Cost Headwind: Rising windstorm and flood insurance premiums represent a headwind for Miami NNN valuations. Borrowers should underwrite higher expense ratios and factor insurance inflation into pro forma assumptions. This dynamic may support cap rates in inland submarkets relative to coastal properties.

Best Performers in 2026: The highest-quality submarkets for NNN investment include Coconut Grove, Coral Gables, Brickell, Boca Raton, and Fort Lauderdale. These locations combine strong demographics, institutional tenant quality, and relative supply constraints.

The Miami and South Florida NNN market remains a premier investment destination for domestic and international investors. Understanding lender programs, underwriting standards, and submarket dynamics is essential for executing successful transactions.
