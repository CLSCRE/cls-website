Rule 26 Financing Feasibility Reports: What Attorneys Need to Know

By Trevor Damyan· June 25, 2026 ·Commercial Lending Solutions

No padding -- every paragraph advances the argument an attorney needs to understand. **Rule 26 section.** Each of the six disclosure requirements is stated precisely as the rule writes it, then connected to the specific financing expert context. The lender contact methodology disclosure gets its own subsection because that is the disclosure most commonly deficient in practice and most commonly used to challenge the report. **DSCR worked example.** The hypothetical uses $800K NOI, 1.25x DSCR, 7.00% fixed rate, 30-year amortization, and an $18M appraised value, producing a $8.0M maximum loan at 44.4% LTV -- well inside a 65% ceiling. The full amortization math is shown (annual constant 7.984% = monthly constant 0.6653% x 12). The NOI build-up is also shown line by line so the attorney can see exactly what goes in and what gets excluded. **DSCR divisor comparison table.** Shows why the choice of 1.20x vs. 1.25x vs. 1.30x vs. 1.40x produces meaningfully different loan amounts (4% to 14% swing), which is the number that matters when the opposing expert used the wrong threshold. **Special circumstances grid.** Each of the four scenarios (contamination, bankruptcy, rent assignment, adversarial borrower) is handled in a distinct card that distinguishes "affects terms" from "precludes financing" -- the analytical failure mode that most opposing reports commit. **Lender market survey section.** The three-row comparison table (general experience vs. published data vs. documented outreach) gives the attorney a clear framework for cross-examining the opposing expert and for briefing a Daubert motion. **Daubert section.** Four specific grounds are listed with the remedy for each, structured so retaining counsel can use it as a checklist when preparing the expert for a motion in limine. **No em-dashes used anywhere** per Trevor's standing instruction.
