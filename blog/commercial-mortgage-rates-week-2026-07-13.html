Commercial Mortgage Rates: Week of July 13, 2026

By Trevor Damyan July 13, 2026

Rate Summary: Week of July 13 to 17, 2026

It was a week defined more by what didn't happen than what did. No major policy surprises from the Federal Reserve, no dramatic Treasury selloff, no single data print that upended market consensus. Instead, commercial mortgage rates drifted sideways to marginally tighter across most product types, with the 10-year Treasury yield oscillating in a roughly 12-basis-point range before settling near the week's midpoint on Friday afternoon.

For borrowers and sponsors who have been watching the rate environment from the sidelines, the message this week is nuanced: conditions are neither obviously compelling nor obviously hostile. What they are, increasingly, is stable : and in today's CRE financing market, stability itself has value. Lenders are quoting, credit committees are moving, and deals that are priced correctly are getting done.

At CLS CRE, we track rates across the capital stack daily, sourcing from bank balance sheet lenders, life companies, debt funds, CMBS conduits, and agency platforms. What we're seeing this week reflects a market that is cautiously open for business : with meaningful differentiation by asset class, loan size, and sponsorship quality.

Treasury Yields: The Benchmark Picture

The 10-year U.S. Treasury note : the primary pricing benchmark for most fixed-rate commercial mortgages : opened the week at approximately 4.41% and closed Friday near 4.38%, representing a modest 3-basis-point tightening on the week. Intraday volatility was contained, with the high touching 4.47% on Tuesday following a slightly hotter-than-expected Producer Price Index (PPI) print before rallying back as equity markets absorbed the data without panic.

The 5-year Treasury, which underpins shorter-term fixed-rate structures and many bank portfolio loans, traded in a range of 4.18% to 4.29% before closing Friday at approximately 4.22%. The 2-year note, closely watched as a proxy for near-term Fed policy expectations, held near 4.55%, reflecting the market's continued pricing of a "higher for longer" posture through at least Q3 2026.

Key data points that moved markets this week included:

  • CPI (June 2026): Headline CPI came in at +2.7% year-over-year, slightly above the 2.5% consensus estimate, which briefly pushed yields higher mid-week before the move faded.
  • PPI (June 2026): Producer prices rose 0.3% month-over-month, modestly above expectations, reinforcing the view that the Fed's last mile on inflation remains unfinished.
  • Retail Sales (June 2026): Retail sales declined 0.2% month-over-month, a soft print that partially offset inflation concerns and reminded markets that consumer spending is showing signs of fatigue.
  • Fed Speak: Two Fed governors made public remarks this week, both reiterating data dependence and signaling no urgency to cut rates before seeing additional inflation progress. Fed funds futures currently price approximately a 28% probability of a 25-basis-point cut at the September 2026 FOMC meeting.

The net result: a yield curve that remains inverted in the short end but is gradually normalizing, with the 2s/10s spread narrowing to approximately -17 basis points : the tightest inversion reading in over a year. This trajectory is meaningful for commercial real estate debt pricing as it suggests that longer-term fixed-rate structures are becoming incrementally more competitive relative to floating-rate alternatives.

What It Means for Borrowers

The current rate environment requires borrowers to think strategically rather than reactively. Here's the ground-level translation of what the macro picture means for commercial mortgage decisions right now:

Floating-rate debt is still expensive in absolute terms. SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate) remains elevated at approximately 4.31%, which means bridge loans, construction facilities, and value-add debt fund structures are carrying all-in rates in the 7.25% to 9.50% range depending on leverage and asset type. Sponsors using short-term floating-rate debt are effectively betting on a meaningful rate drop within their hold period : a bet that is less certain today than it appeared six months ago.

Fixed-rate permanent financing is attractive on a relative basis. With 10-year Treasuries near 4.38% and life company spreads for core multifamily and industrial assets ranging from 130 to 165 basis points, best-in-class permanent financing is available in the low-to-mid 5% range. For stabilized assets with strong cash flow coverage, locking in fixed-rate debt today offers meaningful downside protection if rates stay higher for longer : while still leaving upside participation if refinancing opportunities improve.

CMBS execution has improved. Conduit spreads tightened approximately 8 to 10 basis points this week on the back of strong CMBS new issuance demand. For retail, office, and mixed-use assets that don't fit neatly into life company or agency boxes, CMBS is increasingly the right conversation to be having. Full-term interest-only is available for the right deals, and non-recourse structure remains a key advantage for many sponsors.

Agency platforms remain the gold standard for multifamily. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are actively quoting, with execution consistent and timely. The agencies continue to favor workforce housing and affordable components, with pricing incentives for green building certifications that can shave 15 to 25 basis points off the coupon for qualifying properties.

Market Outlook: The Second Half of Summer

Looking ahead to the remainder of July and into August, several dynamics are worth watching closely:

The September FOMC meeting looms larger each week. As we move through the summer data calendar : including two more CPI prints, July jobs numbers, and the PCE deflator : the September 17, 2026 FOMC decision will increasingly shape rate lock timing decisions. Borrowers with deals in process should be working closely with their mortgage brokers now to understand optimal lock windows and rate lock extension economics.

Cap rate compression has stalled : and that creates opportunity. With CRE values having reset meaningfully from their 2021 to 2022 peaks, the bid-ask gap between sellers and buyers has narrowed in industrial and multifamily sectors. Transaction volume in Southern California is showing early signs of recovery, particularly in the $5M to $25M deal range where CLS CRE is most active. More transactions means more financing activity, and lenders are competing more aggressively for quality deals.

Office remains a story of bifurcation. Trophy Class A office in major markets continues to attract life company and CMBS capital at workable spreads. Suburban and secondary office, however, remains effectively unfinanceable through traditional channels in most scenarios. Sponsors with office exposure should be engaging with their lenders proactively, especially on upcoming maturities.

Lender appetite is healthy but selective. Loan-to-value constraints have tightened modestly across bank platforms following recent regulatory guidance on CRE concentration limits. The most competitive capital : life company money, agency platforms, and top-tier CMBS conduits : continues to flow toward sponsors with track records, institutional-quality properties, and demonstrated cash flow. Newer sponsors or assets with hair on them are increasingly reliant on debt fund and private credit platforms.

Action Items for Borrowers This Week

  • Review upcoming maturities now. If you have commercial loans maturing in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, the refinancing process should already be underway. Extension options are not guaranteed, and lenders are less accommodating on troubled assets than they were 12 months ago.
  • Don't anchor to 2021 rate memories. The window for sub-4% financing has closed. Underwriting acquisitions and refinances to today's debt costs : not yesterday's : is the only way to stress-test deals reliably.
  • Get competitive quotes across multiple lender types. The variance between a bank balance sheet quote and a life company or CMBS execution on the same deal can be 40 to 75 basis points. That spread matters at scale.
  • Explore interest-only options aggressively. Full-term IO is available for the right deals across CMBS and some agency executions. For assets with strong in-place cash flow, IO periods materially improve cash-on-cash returns and create flexibility.
  • Understand your prepayment structure before you sign. Defeasance and yield maintenance provisions on fixed-rate loans can be expensive in a declining rate environment. Model exit scenarios before closing.

The week of July 13 to 17, 2026 won't be remembered as a turning point in the commercial mortgage rate cycle : but quiet weeks like this one are often the best windows to move deals forward. Lenders are engaged, spreads are competitive, and the absence of crisis is itself a favorable environment for disciplined borrowers.

Both CMBS (Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities) conduit loans and life company loans are popular fixed-rate, non-recourse financing options for stabilized commercial properties, but they differ in important ways. Life company loans typically offer lower interest rates and more flexible structures, but they have stricter underwriting criteria : they strongly prefer low-leverage, institutional-quality assets in primary markets with pristine sponsorship. CMBS conduit loans offer broader property type and market coverage, can accommodate higher leverage, and are often the right tool for retail, mixed-use, and secondary-market assets that don't meet life company criteria. CMBS loans are also sold into the bond market, which means they come with less flexibility post-closing around modifications or assumptions. Rate differences between the two can range from 25 to 75 basis points depending on the deal.
