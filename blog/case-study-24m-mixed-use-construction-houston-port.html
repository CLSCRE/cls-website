$24,000,000 Mixed-Use Construction, Near Port of Houston, TX

Deal arranged and written by Trevor Damyan, Commercial Mortgage Broker
By Trevor Damyan · April 21, 2026 · Construction Loan

Mixed-use construction deals test a lender's ability to underwrite multiple asset classes simultaneously. This $24 million ground-up project near the Port of Houston presented that exact challenge, combining 80-120 residential units with industrial-office flex space targeting port-adjacent logistics tenants. The deal required a lender comfortable evaluating both multifamily fundamentals and specialized industrial demand in one of the nation's busiest port markets.

The Deal

The sponsor needed construction financing for a mixed-use project strategically positioned to serve Houston's port ecosystem. The development plan called for residential units ranging from workforce housing to market-rate apartments, paired with industrial-flex space designed for logistics companies requiring proximity to port operations.

The borrower had solid Houston market experience but needed a lender who could underwrite the unique value proposition: residential income providing stable cash flow foundation while industrial-flex space captured premium rents from port-dependent tenants facing limited supply of quality space in the immediate port vicinity.

The Challenge

Most construction lenders approach mixed-use deals through a single asset class lens. Multifamily-focused lenders questioned the industrial component's lease-up risk and rent assumptions. Industrial lenders balked at underwriting residential units outside their expertise.

The port-adjacent location created additional complexity. While proximity to the Port of Houston justified premium industrial rents, some lenders viewed the location as too specialized, preferring broader industrial markets with diverse tenant demand. Others questioned whether residential tenants would accept the industrial activity and truck traffic inherent to port-adjacent locations.

The residential component's unit mix, spanning workforce to market-rate housing, required lenders to underwrite multiple rent levels and tenant profiles within the same building. Combined with industrial-flex space targeting a specialized tenant base, the deal demanded sophisticated market knowledge few lenders possessed.

The Solution

We identified a regional Texas bank with deep Houston market presence and experience financing both multifamily and industrial projects. Their local market knowledge proved crucial in understanding port-area dynamics and tenant demand patterns.

The structure addressed each component's risk profile appropriately. The lender underwrote the residential portion using established Houston multifamily metrics, crediting stabilized NOI at market cap rates for comparable workforce and market-rate properties. For the industrial-flex component, they relied on their direct experience with port-area logistics demand, recognizing the premium tenants pay for operational proximity to port facilities.

The construction loan totaled $24 million at 75% loan-to-cost, with an initial term of 24 months and two six-month extension options. Pricing floated at prime plus 100 basis points, reflecting the lender's comfort with both the sponsor's track record and Houston market fundamentals. The loan included standard construction administration with funds released based on completion milestones for each project component.

Critical to the approval was the lender's understanding that port-adjacent industrial space commands premium rents precisely because supply remains constrained. They credited the sponsor's pre-leasing discussions with logistics companies seeking quality space near port operations, viewing these conversations as validation of demand rather than requiring executed leases for approval.

The Outcome

The borrower secured construction financing allowing the project to proceed on schedule. The loan structure provided flexibility to adjust the final unit mix based on pre-leasing activity while maintaining consistent cost of capital across both property components.

The regional bank's local market expertise proved invaluable, as they understood Houston's port-area employment patterns supporting residential demand while recognizing logistics companies' willingness to pay premium rents for strategic location advantages. This dual market knowledge allowed them to underwrite both components at market terms rather than applying risk premiums for unfamiliar asset classes.

The deal highlighted the importance of matching borrowers with lenders possessing relevant market depth rather than simply seeking the lowest cost of capital. The successful financing enables the sponsor to deliver much-needed housing for port-area workers while providing quality space for logistics tenants facing limited alternatives in this specialized submarket.

For sponsors considering mixed-use construction projects, this deal demonstrates that the right lender will view complementary asset classes as diversification rather than added complexity, provided the underlying market fundamentals support each component's projected performance.
