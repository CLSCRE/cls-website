Case Study: $19.1M Multi-Property Portfolio Refinance with a Credit Union in Beverly Hills

By Trevor Damyan July 27, 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranged a $19,100,000 refinance through Ventura County Credit Union, structured as three separate loans across a commercial office property in Beverly Hills and a mixed-use property in Los Angeles, all closing within the same week for the same family ownership group. Each tranche carried its own five-year note, origination fee, and rate lock fee, secured by its own first-priority deed of trust.

The Deal That Walked in the Door

We are Commercial Lending Solutions, a commercial mortgage brokerage based in Los Angeles, California, and this refinance came to us from a family ownership group holding commercial real estate across Beverly Hills and the San Fernando Valley through several related entities and family trusts. Rather than refinancing one property at a time, the family wanted a single credit union lender relationship capable of underwriting and closing the entire portfolio together, on the same timeline, with consistent terms across every property. Commercial Lending Solutions specializes in permanent and portfolio refinancing for multi-property owners across California, including Beverly Hills, CA.

Coordinating three separate closings for the same guarantor group is a different challenge than a single-property refinance. Every entity needed its own complete underwriting package, and the lender needed comfort with the family's combined exposure across all three Beverly Hills and Los Angeles properties simultaneously, not just each deal in isolation.

What Made It Hard

  • Three separate closings on one deadline. Each loan required its own full underwriting package, entity documents, trust certificates, taxpayer consent forms, and title work, and all three had to close within days of each other to satisfy the family's broader refinancing timeline.
  • Two loans secured by affiliated entities at the same address. Two of the three tranches were tied to related ownership entities at the Beverly Hills property, each requiring its own first-priority title policy and deed of trust, a structure that demanded careful coordination with title and escrow to avoid any conflict in lien priority.
  • Consistent guaranty exposure across every entity. The same group of individual guarantors and family trusts stood behind all three loans, meaning the lender's underwriting had to account for the family's aggregate exposure across the full $19.1 million, not just each note individually.
"Treating this as one portfolio relationship, not three separate deals, is what let the family close on a single synchronized timeline instead of juggling three lenders."
, Trevor Damyan, Commercial Lending Solutions

How We Ran the Process

Trevor Damyan, a California-licensed real estate broker (DRE #02244836), placed all three loans with Ventura County Credit Union, with lender's closing instructions issued August 22-24, 2023 and closings completing by August 31, 2023:

  • $6,750,000 secured by a first-priority deed of trust and assignment of rents on the Beverly Hills office property, with a $40,500 origination fee (0.6%) and a $16,875 rate lock fee (0.25%)
  • $8,000,000 secured by a first-priority deed of trust and assignment of rents on the Los Angeles mixed-use property, with a $48,000 origination fee (0.6%) and a $20,000 rate lock fee (0.25%)
  • $4,350,000 secured by a separate first-priority deed of trust on the Beverly Hills office property through an affiliated ownership entity, with a $26,100 origination fee (0.6%) and a $10,875 rate lock fee (0.25%)
  • A consistent fee structure across all three tranches: a $1,300 underwriting fee, a $2,500 documentation preparation fee, a $2,500 property tax monitoring fee, a $100 credit union account opening fee, a $100 credit report fee, a $4,500 appraisal fee, a $500 appraisal review fee, a $900 environmental report fee, and a $400 environmental report review fee, per loan
  • Five-year notes maturing in August 2028 on each tranche, each supported by its own amortization schedule, business loan agreement, and promissory note
  • Joint and several guaranties from the same group of individual guarantors and family trusts across all three loans, with commercial guaranty agreements executed by each guarantor on every tranche
  • ALTA 2006 Lender's Extended title policies from Chicago Title Insurance Company on each property, insuring the credit union's first-priority position and confirming fee title vested in the correct borrowing entity for each loan
  • Full entity documentation per tranche: LLC resolutions, certificates of trust, trust certificates, taxpayer consent forms for every borrower and guarantor, and UCC-1 filings where applicable

All three loans closed within the lender's specified window, giving the family a consolidated $19.1 million refinance across their Beverly Hills and Los Angeles holdings with one lender relationship and one consistent set of terms.

Why We Won This One

Running three separate closings with the same lender on a synchronized timeline is what made this refinance work as a true portfolio transaction rather than three disconnected deals. Getting Ventura County Credit Union comfortable underwriting the family's full combined exposure across all three properties at once, rather than evaluating each loan in isolation, let the family consolidate its entire refinancing need into a single relationship instead of shopping three separate lenders.

Coordinating the two loans tied to affiliated entities at the same Beverly Hills address took particular care with title and escrow, making sure each tranche's first-priority deed of trust was properly sequenced and that the correct borrowing entity was reflected on each title policy. Getting that structure right the first time avoided any ambiguity in lien priority across the two loans secured at that property.

Is This Structure Right for Your Deal?

If your family or ownership group holds multiple commercial properties and wants to refinance them together under one lender relationship rather than juggling separate processes, a coordinated multi-loan portfolio closing can simplify both the underwriting and the timeline.
