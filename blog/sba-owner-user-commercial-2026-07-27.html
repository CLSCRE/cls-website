SBA Owner-User Commercial Deal Activity (Week of July 27, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·July 27, 2026

SBA Owner-User Volume Is Holding, But the Market Is Sorting Itself Out

As we move through the back half of summer 2026, SBA-financed owner-user commercial deals are still printing, but the environment has tightened in ways that matter to operators trying to time acquisitions or refinances into the fourth quarter cycle. Both the 504 and 7(a) programs remain active, and lender appetite for qualified owner-users has not collapsed. What has shifted is selectivity at the credit committee level, processing friction inside certain Certified Development Companies, and a narrowing of asset types that conventional lenders will pair with SBA financing without meaningful hair on the deal.

For owners and operators in office, medical office, specialty retail, and small hospitality, understanding where the soft spots are before you engage a lender saves weeks of lost time and, in some cases, prevents a deal from dying at the wrong moment in escrow.

504 Activity and CDC Processing Timelines

SBA 504 volume for owner-user commercial has remained reasonably constructive through mid-2026, particularly in medical office and single-tenant specialty uses where the owner-occupancy test is clean and the business cash flow underwrites without a lot of manual adjustment. Where things have slowed is on the CDC processing side. Turnaround times at several regional CDCs have stretched into ranges that deal teams were not budgeting for twelve months ago. Borrowers and their brokers are reporting processing windows that are running materially longer than historical norms, in some cases by several weeks, depending on which CDC is in the chain and how their current pipeline is loaded.

This has a direct structural consequence for deal timelines. If you are negotiating a purchase contract with a seller who has a hard close date, and your financing structure runs through a 504 debenture, you need to build in contingency or have an honest conversation with your CDC contact about their current bandwidth before you commit to a closing schedule. Sellers in competitive disposition situations are not extending for SBA friction without a concession, and that concession comes out of your economics.

On the conventional lender side of the 504 stack, first mortgage appetite from community banks and mission-focused CDFIs has remained reasonably available for strong credits, but underwriting standards have tightened on hospitality assets in secondary and tertiary markets. Operators in that segment should expect more documentation requests, more scrutiny on trailing revenue periods, and potentially more conservative loan-to-value posture from their first-lien partner than they saw in 2024 or early 2025.

7(a) Activity Across Office, Medical, and Specialty Uses

The 7(a) program continues to serve as the primary SBA vehicle for smaller acquisitions and owner-user transactions where the deal size or property type does not fit cleanly inside a 504 structure. Lender appetite under 7(a) has bifurcated noticeably by asset class. Medical office and certain allied health uses are drawing consistent interest from preferred lenders who understand the borrower profile and can move efficiently through credit approval. General office and specialty retail deals are requiring stronger business performance documentation and in some cases additional collateral support before lenders will engage at comfortable leverage levels.

Small hospitality, particularly limited-service properties under flag that are owner-operated, is the most complicated category right now. Lenders are available, but rate expectations and equity requirements have moved in a direction that makes some transactions harder to justify at current purchase prices. Borrowers in this segment who are working from pro forma assumptions based on deals that closed in a different rate environment need to rerun their numbers against current cost of capital before advancing into exclusivity on a target property.

One dynamic worth watching for the remainder of 2026 is how SBA lender participation levels respond to any shifts in secondary market pricing for guaranteed paper. Lender appetite is partly a function of what they can do with the guaranteed portion of a 7(a) loan in the secondary market, and that calculus affects how aggressively they pursue origination volume. Watch that indicator if you are planning a deal for the first quarter 2027 cycle.

Actionable Positioning for Deals in the Pipeline

If you are an owner-operator with a commercial acquisition or refinance in predevelopment or early site control, the practical steps are straightforward. First, identify your CDC early and have a direct conversation about their current pipeline load and realistic processing capacity before you structure your timeline. Second, pressure-test your business cash flow documentation against tighter underwriting standards, not the standards that closed your last deal. Third, if you are in hospitality or general office, run multiple capital structures in parallel so you have a conventional fallback if SBA execution develops friction.

The deals that are closing cleanly right now are the ones where the deal team did the structural and documentation work before engaging lenders, not during underwriting. Lender patience for incomplete submissions has shortened alongside processing bandwidth constraints.
