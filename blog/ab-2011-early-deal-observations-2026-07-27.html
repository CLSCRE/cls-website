AB 2011 Early Deal Observations (Week of July 27, 2026)

By Trevor Damyan·July 27, 2026

AB 2011 Hits Its Stride: Early Patterns Worth Watching

California's AB 2011 Affordable Housing and High Road Jobs Act has been on the books for a few years now, but the first meaningful wave of projects is only recently clearing entitlement and entering serious capital formation conversations. At CLS CRE, we are seeing enough deal flow across office-to-residential and retail-to-residential conversion structures to identify some durable early patterns. For developers actively working predevelopment on AB 2011-eligible sites, these observations carry real implications for how you size your stack, sequence your capital partners, and manage timeline risk heading into the next round of competitive financing programs.

The short version: the opportunity is real, the capital ecosystem is still assembling itself, and early movers who understand the structural nuances will have a meaningful advantage over the next several quarters.

Project Types Coming Through Entitlement

The projects we are tracking cluster into two broad categories. The first and more prevalent is underperforming suburban and urban office conversion, typically mid-rise concrete or steel frame product from the 1970s through the early 2000s. Floor plates in that vintage range present genuine structural challenges for residential conversion, and the deals that pencil most convincingly tend to involve buildings where existing core placement and window-line depth fall within workable tolerances for unit layouts. Developers are learning quickly that not every distressed office asset is a viable AB 2011 candidate, and the weeding-out process at feasibility is healthy for the market overall.

The second category involves ground-floor retail and light commercial corridors in infill locations zoned for mixed use. These sites tend to be smaller in scale, but they move through the streamlined AB 2011 ministerial review process with fewer complications because the existing structure is often fully demolished rather than adaptively reused. Workforce and moderate-income product at these scales is attracting interest from mission-driven CDFIs and smaller tax credit syndicators who can move quickly on sub-100-unit transactions.

A smaller but growing third subset involves hospitality-to-residential, where the physical conversion logic from AB 2011's commercial land use framework is being tested with some creative entitlement arguments. Those deals are further out and carry more legal uncertainty for now.

Capital Stack Configurations Taking Shape

The financing structures we are seeing reflect both the novelty of the conversion use case and the varying income profiles across projects. On the fully affordable side, the conventional LIHTC plus subordinate soft debt stack remains the anchor, but the conversion cost basis is forcing creative subordinate layering. Predevelopment and acquisition bridge facilities in the low-to-mid single digit rate range from mission CDFIs are playing an outsized role, particularly where developers need to move on an office acquisition before competitive TCAC rounds open.

For mixed-income structures relying on AB 2011's by-right pathway without tax credits, the capital stack looks meaningfully different. Permanent debt from specialty debt funds and select life insurance company programs is emerging as a viable takeout, particularly on projects with a percentage of market-rate units supporting debt service. These structures are interest rate sensitive, and deals underwritten at cap rates or yield assumptions from 18 to 24 months ago are getting re-penciled in the current environment.

One consistent theme across nearly every transaction we are tracking: the gap between conversion cost basis and supportable debt is wider than developers initially modeled. Land write-downs, seller carryback structures, and patient subordinate capital from public agency sources are showing up with greater frequency as the mechanism that bridges that gap to feasibility. Developers who do not have an existing public agency relationship are finding that originating one mid-deal is slower and more uncertain than anticipated.

The Evolving Specialty Lender Ecosystem

Perhaps the most important market development to understand right now is that the lender universe for AB 2011 conversion deals is narrowing from a field of expressed interest to a shorter list of capital sources with actual closed transactions and operational experience. Mission CDFIs with construction lending capacity are increasingly differentiated by whether they have worked through the draws, inspections, and title complexities specific to occupied or partially occupied commercial conversion. Developers who bring a lender to closing that has not navigated these mechanics before should budget for timeline friction.

On the permanent side, agency execution through preservation programs and Green and Resilient Retrofit pathways is applicable to some conversion deals, but the product type eligibility criteria reward early dialogue with agency lenders rather than assumptions built into a proforma. Specialty debt funds with affordable housing mandates are filling some of the permanent debt gap for mixed-income structures, and several are actively building conversion-specific underwriting criteria.

The actionable takeaway for developers: lender selection on an AB 2011 deal should be treated with the same rigor as site selection. The wrong capital partner at the wrong stage costs months and sometimes kills deals that were fundamentally sound. Build your capital advisory relationships now, before you are under timeline pressure from a purchase contract or a competitive round deadline.
