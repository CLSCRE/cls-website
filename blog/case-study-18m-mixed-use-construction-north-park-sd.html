Case Study

$18,000,000 Mixed-Use Construction (45 Units + Retail) in North Park, San Diego, CA

By Trevor Damyan · April 03, 2026 · Construction Loan

The Deal

Commercial Lending Solutions arranged $18 million in construction financing for a 45-unit mixed-use development in North Park, San Diego. The project combines four stories of residential units over 5,000 square feet of ground-floor retail space, targeting the neighborhood's growing young professional demographic.

The capital stack included a $18 million senior construction loan at 60% loan-to-cost, with the experienced developer contributing $12 million in equity. The 22-month construction timeline aligned with typical mixed-use delivery schedules, accounting for the sequential nature of retail tenant improvements following base building completion.

North Park's transit-oriented location and established walkable retail corridor made it an attractive site for mixed-use development, though the retail component required careful underwriting given longer lease-up timelines compared to residential.

The Challenge

Mixed-use construction financing presents unique underwriting complexities that many lenders struggle to evaluate effectively. Residential and commercial components require separate cash flow analyses, market studies, and risk assessments, often handled by different underwriting teams within the same institution.

The primary challenge centered on the retail component's pre-leasing status. While the developer had strong confidence in residential absorption based on comparable projects and demographic trends, no retail tenants had committed to leases. Most construction lenders require 50-70% retail pre-leasing before funding, creating a chicken-and-egg problem since tenants typically won't commit until construction is underway and completion is visible.

Additionally, retail lease-up traditionally extends 6-12 months beyond residential stabilization, creating a cash flow gap that concerned potential lenders. The developer's track record was strong in residential projects but limited in retail leasing and management.

The Solution

We identified a regional bank with significant Southern California mixed-use experience and comfort underwriting speculative retail construction. The key breakthrough came from the lender's willingness to underwrite the retail component at a conservative 75% stabilized occupancy assumption rather than requiring extensive pre-leasing.

This approach recognized North Park's strong retail fundamentals: average retail vacancy rates below 8%, growing foot traffic from nearby transit connections, and limited new retail supply in the immediate trade area. The lender's market knowledge allowed them to value the retail component based on comparable rents and realistic absorption timelines.

The loan structure included several protective features: interest reserves sized for 26 months to cover potential retail lease-up delays, step-down pricing tied to retail leasing milestones, and a experienced retail leasing consultant requirement. The developer agreed to engage a specialized retail broker with North Park market expertise within 60 days of loan closing.

We structured the construction draws to prioritize retail shell completion, allowing leasing activity to begin while residential units were still under construction. This sequencing proved critical to the project's ultimate success.

The Outcome

The project exceeded expectations across multiple metrics. Construction proceeded on schedule with no significant delays or cost overruns. More importantly, the retail space achieved full occupancy four months before residential completion, validating both the location choice and market analysis.

Early retail leasing success stemmed from North Park's continued gentrification and the developer's decision to target local businesses rather than national chains. The tenant mix included a specialty coffee roaster, fitness studio, and artisanal food market, all businesses that complemented the building's residential demographic.

Residential pre-leasing began six months before completion and reached 85% by the certificate of occupancy date. Average residential rents achieved $2.85 per square foot, slightly above initial projections, while retail rents averaged $28 per square foot triple net.

The developer refinanced into permanent financing two months after completion at attractive terms, benefiting from the property's strong occupancy and cash flow performance. The successful lease-up timeline allowed for early loan payoff, reducing overall project carrying costs.

This transaction demonstrates how experienced construction lenders can successfully underwrite mixed-use projects without requiring extensive retail pre-leasing, provided the market fundamentals support conservative occupancy assumptions and proper risk mitigation measures are implemented.
