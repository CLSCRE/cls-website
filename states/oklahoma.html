Commercial Real Estate Loans in Oklahoma

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan , Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated August 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Oklahoma from $1 million to over $100 million, spanning 42 loan programs and every major property type. We maintain dedicated market coverage for 2 Oklahoma metros, including Oklahoma City and Tulsa. Below: how Oklahoma's foreclosure process, recording taxes, and regulatory climate shape the loan terms lenders will offer here.

Oklahoma commercial real estate financing runs through two anchor metros, and Commercial Lending Solutions arranges loans in both: Oklahoma City and Tulsa. Oklahoma City is the larger and more diversified economy, built on state government, Tinker Air Force Base and its massive aircraft sustainment workforce, a growing biosciences corridor around the health center campus, and a downtown that two decades of MAPS public investment turned into a legitimate institutional submarket. Tulsa carries the state's energy heritage and has rebuilt around aerospace, with one of the largest airline maintenance bases in the world, a growing advanced manufacturing base, and the Tulsa Port of Catoosa giving the metro inland waterway access to the Gulf via the McClellan-Kerr navigation system.

For lenders, Oklahoma is a stable, low-cost, landlord-friendly market where local and regional banks have long dominated and national capital shows up selectively for scale. Deal flow concentrates in multifamily, which benefits from some of the most affordable rents in the country and steady absorption in both metros, and in industrial and aerospace-adjacent product around Tinker and the Tulsa airport corridor. Energy price cycles still ripple through the market, which is exactly why lender selection matters: CLS CRE matches Oklahoma deals to capital sources that understand basin cyclicality and secondary-market liquidity rather than lenders that headline-underwrite the state. With 1,000+ lender relationships, CLS CRE brings pricing tension to a market where borrowers too often accept the first local quote.

Foreclosure and Lender Appetite

Oklahoma technically permits a non-judicial power of sale, but borrowers can compel the judicial process, so lenders underwrite to a court-supervised foreclosure that can take a year or more. Some national lenders price Oklahoma collateral slightly wider or cap leverage to reflect the longer recovery, which is where local and regional bank relationships earn their value.

Recording Taxes and Closing Costs

Oklahoma charges a mortgage registration tax of up to $0.10 per $100 of debt (0.10%) scaled by loan term, so a $10 million mortgage can carry up to $10,000 in tax at recording, a real but rarely deal-changing closing cost.

Oklahoma is a bank-led market. Local and regional banks in Oklahoma City and Tulsa hold most of the commercial real estate paper in the state and compete hard for relationship borrowers, while national debt funds and life companies engage selectively on larger, newer assets. Underwriters watch energy exposure in tenant rosters and employment bases, and hail and tornado insurance costs are a standing line item on large-roof product. Agency lenders are reliable takeout capital for stabilized multifamily in both metros. Borrowers should also budget for the mortgage registration tax and, on judicial-state paper, slightly more conservative structure from out-of-state lenders.

Key Commercial Real Estate Sectors in Oklahoma

Aerospace and Defense Industrial

Tinker Air Force Base in Oklahoma City and the airline maintenance complex in Tulsa anchor one of the largest aerospace employment bases in the country, feeding demand for hangar, MRO, flex, and supplier industrial product that regional banks finance readily.

Multifamily

Oklahoma City and Tulsa offer some of the best affordability in the nation, and steady absorption keeps agency lenders and banks active on stabilized product while bridge capital funds workforce housing value-add plays in both metros.

Energy and Logistics

The state's energy economy still drives industrial and office demand, while the Tulsa Port of Catoosa and the I-35, I-40, and I-44 crossroads position both metros for distribution users serving the southern plains.

Retail and NNN

Net lease and neighborhood retail development follows suburban rooftop growth in Edmond, Moore, Broken Arrow, and Owasso, with credit-tenant deals placing well with banks and smaller strips routing to community lenders.

Market Intelligence

Regulatory Environment

Oklahoma is a light-touch, landlord-friendly state and that posture attracts steady private capital. There is no rent control, property taxes are low by national standards with assessment increases capped annually, and entitlement in both metros is fast and predictable. The two regulatory realities lenders actually price are the judicial foreclosure regime, which lengthens recovery timelines and makes some national lenders structure more conservatively, and the mortgage registration tax, a modest but real closing cost. Severe convective storm exposure, hail and tornado, has pushed insurance premiums and deductibles up on large-roof industrial and retail, and lenders increasingly stress those line items. Otherwise Oklahoma underwriting is refreshingly uncomplicated, which is part of the state's pitch to capital.

Which Lenders Are Active in Oklahoma

Local and regional banks are the backbone of Oklahoma commercial real estate lending and price their home market more aggressively than any outside capital source. Community banks and credit unions handle smaller balance-sheet deals across both metros, agency lenders are the standard execution for stabilized multifamily, and debt funds engage on value-add and construction deals of scale in Oklahoma City and Tulsa. Life companies and CMBS appear on larger, newer, well-located assets. The judicial foreclosure regime thins the out-of-state bridge lender pool slightly, so pairing the right local bank against a national quote is often the winning play.

Commercial Real Estate Lending in Oklahoma: FAQ

Oklahoma foreclosures generally run through the courts. A power of sale exists on paper, but borrowers can compel the judicial process, so lenders underwrite to a court-supervised timeline that can stretch a year or longer. That extended recovery makes some national lenders price Oklahoma collateral a bit wider or hold leverage a turn lower than they would in a trustee-sale state like Texas. Local and regional banks, which know the courts and the collateral, largely ignore the discount, which is why they routinely offer the best structure in the state and why CLS CRE runs them against national quotes.
Oklahoma charges a mortgage registration tax of up to $0.10 per $100 of secured debt, or 0.10%, with the rate scaled by the term of the loan. On a $10 million mortgage that is up to $10,000 due when the mortgage is recorded, alongside standard recording and title charges. It is a real closing cost but rarely changes a deal, and shorter-term instruments carry lower rates under the statute. Borrowers refinancing should budget for it on the new mortgage amount, and CLS CRE flags it in every Oklahoma closing cost estimate so there are no surprises at the table.
