Commercial Real Estate Loans in Alabama

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan , Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated July 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Alabama from $1 million to over $100 million, spanning 40 loan programs and every major property type. We maintain dedicated market coverage for 5 Alabama metros, including Birmingham and Dothan. Below: how Alabama's foreclosure process, recording taxes, and regulatory climate shape the loan terms lenders will offer here.

Alabama commercial real estate financing is riding one of the stronger industrial growth stories in the Southeast, and Commercial Lending Solutions arranges loans across the state's key metros: Birmingham, Huntsville, Montgomery, Dothan, and Florence. Huntsville is the headline market, one of the fastest-growing metros in the country, anchored by Redstone Arsenal, NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, a relocated federal law enforcement campus, and a defense and aerospace contractor base that keeps absorbing office, flex, and housing supply. Birmingham remains the state's financial and medical center, anchored by the University of Alabama at Birmingham, the state's largest employer, with a mature banking culture and steady infill redevelopment. Montgomery pairs state government with automotive assembly and Maxwell Air Force Base, while the state's broader auto corridor, with major assembly plants near Tuscaloosa, Lincoln, Montgomery, and Huntsville, feeds supplier industrial demand statewide. Dothan is the commercial and medical hub of the Wiregrass region near a major Army aviation training post, and Florence anchors the Shoals in the state's northwest corner.

Capital likes Alabama's combination of growth and low friction. Deal flow concentrates in Huntsville multifamily and industrial, Birmingham medical and infill mixed-use, and supplier industrial along the I-65 and I-20 corridors. Regional banks headquartered in-state compete hard for the market, agency lenders are deep in every metro, and national debt funds and life companies have followed the Huntsville story in force. CLS CRE runs that competition deliberately, because Alabama deals price best when local banks and national capital are bidding against each other.

Foreclosure and Lender Appetite

Alabama lenders foreclose non-judicially by publication under a power of sale, typically concluding in a few months. That efficient remedy keeps the full spectrum of lender types active in the state and supports normal bridge and construction leverage.

Recording Taxes and Closing Costs

Alabama charges a mortgage recording tax of $0.15 per $100 of debt, or 0.15%, so a $10 million mortgage carries $15,000 in tax at recording, a real closing cost borrowers should budget on both acquisitions and refinances.

Alabama is a competitive, bank-forward market. Regional banks headquartered in the state, Birmingham has long been a Southern banking center, defend their home turf aggressively on stabilized and construction deals, while community banks and credit unions cover smaller balance-sheet product statewide. Agency lenders are deep in Huntsville, Birmingham, and Montgomery multifamily, and debt funds and life companies actively chase Huntsville's growth pipeline. Underwriting friction is low: an efficient non-judicial foreclosure, low property taxes, and no rent control. The two line items to budget are the 0.15% mortgage recording tax and, in the southern tier near the coast, elevated wind insurance costs.

Key Commercial Real Estate Sectors in Alabama

Aerospace, Defense, and Advanced Industrial

Redstone Arsenal and NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center anchor Huntsville's contractor economy, while automotive assembly plants near Tuscaloosa, Lincoln, Montgomery, and Huntsville feed a statewide supplier industrial pipeline along I-65 and I-20 that banks, debt funds, and life companies all finance.

Multifamily

Huntsville is one of the strongest multifamily absorption stories in the Southeast, with agency lenders, banks, and bridge capital competing for both new deliveries and value-add product, while Birmingham, Montgomery, Dothan, and Florence offer steady workforce housing deal flow.

Medical and Office

The University of Alabama at Birmingham anchors one of the largest medical economies in the South, driving durable medical office demand, and Huntsville's defense contractor base keeps its office and flex market among the healthiest in the country.

Retail and NNN

Rooftop growth in Huntsville's suburbs, Birmingham's over-the-mountain corridor, and the Wiregrass region around Dothan keeps net lease and neighborhood center development active, with credit-tenant deals placing tightly among banks and net lease capital.

Market Intelligence

Regulatory Environment

Alabama is a light-touch state and underwrites like one. There is no rent control, and state law preempts local rent regulation; property taxes are among the lowest in the nation, which flatters operating expense loads; entitlement is fast in every metro; and the non-judicial foreclosure remedy keeps legal risk minimal. The state's aggressive industrial recruitment, with incentive packages that have landed automotive, aerospace, and shipbuilding investment for three decades, keeps the supplier industrial pipeline full and gives lenders a steady stream of build-to-suit and spec industrial requests. The costs to model are the 0.15% mortgage recording tax at closing and wind exposure in the state's coastal south, where insurance pricing follows Gulf dynamics. Otherwise Alabama adds little regulatory friction to a deal, which is central to its appeal.

Which Lenders Are Active in Alabama

Regional banks are the backbone of Alabama lending, with Birmingham's legacy as a Southern banking center still visible in how aggressively in-state banks compete for commercial real estate. Community banks and credit unions handle smaller deals statewide, agency lenders dominate stabilized multifamily in Huntsville, Birmingham, and Montgomery, and debt funds and life companies have moved decisively into Huntsville's industrial and multifamily pipeline. CMBS covers hospitality and single-tenant product. Huntsville deals of scale now draw genuinely national competition, and running that capital against motivated local banks is the winning execution.

Alabama lenders foreclose non-judicially by publication under a power of sale, without going to court, and the process typically concludes in a few months. That fast, predictable remedy reduces recovery risk, which is why the full spectrum of capital, from community banks to national debt funds, lends comfortably in Alabama at normal leverage. Borrowers see the benefit in structure: Alabama paper avoids the wider pricing and leverage caps that judicial states can trigger, so terms are set by the asset, the submarket, and the sponsor rather than by concerns over the legal regime.
Alabama charges a mortgage recording tax of $0.15 per $100 of secured debt, which is 0.15% of the loan amount. On a $10 million mortgage that is $15,000 due at recording, on top of standard recording and title charges. The tax applies when a mortgage is recorded, so refinances incur it on the new loan as well, and borrowers should budget for it in every Alabama closing cost estimate. It is a real but rarely deal-changing cost, and CLS CRE flags it up front so Alabama borrowers are never surprised at the closing table.
