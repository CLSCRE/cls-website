Commercial Real Estate Loans in South Carolina

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan , Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated July 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across South Carolina from $1 million to over $100 million, spanning 40 loan programs and every major property type. We maintain dedicated market coverage for 6 South Carolina metros, including Charleston and Columbia. Below: how South Carolina's foreclosure process, recording taxes, and regulatory climate shape the loan terms lenders will offer here.

South Carolina commercial real estate financing is carried by three engines that rarely slow at the same time: the port, the factories, and the coast. Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Charleston, Greenville, Columbia, Myrtle Beach, Hilton Head Island, Rock Hill, and Florence. Charleston pairs the deepest harbor on the East Coast with Boeing's North Charleston campus and a historic core that anchors one of the strongest hospitality markets in the South. The Upstate around Greenville is the state's manufacturing spine: BMW's Spartanburg plant, the largest BMW factory in the world, Michelin's North American headquarters, and a dense supplier network along I-85 have made Greenville a legitimate institutional market. Columbia combines state government, the University of South Carolina, Fort Jackson, the Army's largest basic training installation, and the Scout Motors plant rising in Blythewood. Myrtle Beach and Hilton Head Island run on tourism and in-migration, Rock Hill captures Charlotte's southern growth spillover, and Florence sits at the I-95 and I-20 junction as the Pee Dee region's medical and logistics hub.

Capital has followed the growth. Port-driven industrial along I-26, multifamily in Charleston, Greenville, and the coastal counties, and hospitality across the tourism markets are the dominant deal types. CLS CRE's work in South Carolina turns on knowing which lenders have already made peace with judicial foreclosure and point-of-sale property tax reassessment, because those two quirks decide who quotes and how tightly.

Foreclosure and Lender Appetite

South Carolina foreclosures run through the courts, typically before a master-in-equity, and while the process is more efficient than in many judicial states it still takes meaningfully longer than a trustee sale. Some national lenders price the longer recovery into spread or leverage, which keeps relationship-driven regional capital unusually competitive here.

Recording Taxes and Closing Costs

South Carolina charges standard recording fees but no mortgage recording tax, so financings and refinances carry no state tax cost at closing.

Two South Carolina quirks matter more than the foreclosure regime. First, commercial and investment property is assessed at a 6% ratio versus 4% for owner-occupied homes, and an assessable transfer of interest resets value at sale, so property taxes can jump sharply after a purchase. Sophisticated lenders underwrite the post-sale tax bill, not the seller's trailing expenses, and borrowers who model it up front avoid painful proceeds cuts in diligence. Second, coastal wind and flood insurance from Hilton Head through Myrtle Beach has repriced, and lenders stress those premiums in DSCR sizing. Regional and community banks remain deeply competitive statewide, particularly in Columbia, Florence, and the Upstate.

Key Commercial Real Estate Sectors in South Carolina

Industrial and Manufacturing

The Port of Charleston drives distribution demand up the I-26 corridor toward Columbia, while the I-85 Upstate corridor anchored by BMW in Spartanburg and Michelin in Greenville supports one of the Southeast's densest advanced manufacturing and supplier ecosystems.

Multifamily

Charleston and Greenville rank among the Southeast's stronger apartment growth stories, Rock Hill rides Charlotte's expansion, and coastal in-migration keeps Myrtle Beach area garden product leasing. Agency, bank, and bridge capital all compete for the state's multifamily deal flow.

Hospitality

Charleston is a top-tier leisure and group market, and Myrtle Beach and Hilton Head Island are among the highest-volume drive-to beach destinations on the East Coast, supporting steady hotel acquisition, renovation, and flag-change financings.

Retail

Tourism spending and population growth keep coastal and Upstate retail healthy, with grocery-anchored centers financing readily through banks and life insurance companies.

Market Intelligence

Regulatory Environment

South Carolina is a low-regulation, low-cost state with one significant underwriting wrinkle: the property tax system. Commercial and investment property carries a 6% assessment ratio, and an assessable transfer of interest at sale resets taxable value to the purchase price, so a buyer's tax bill can run well above the seller's. Lenders and appraisers who know the state underwrite the reset; out-of-state buyers are routinely surprised by it. Beyond that, there is no rent control, landlord-tenant law is owner-friendly, and entitlements move quickly outside historic Charleston, where design review is genuinely rigorous. Coastal wind and flood insurance cost is the other watch item, priced through the state wind pool and private markets.

Which Lenders Are Active in South Carolina

Regional and community banks are the backbone of South Carolina lending and remain aggressive on relationship deals across Columbia, Greenville, Florence, and the coast. Agency lenders are active on stabilized multifamily in Charleston, Greenville, and the larger coastal submarkets. Life insurance companies pursue Upstate industrial and credit-anchored retail, debt funds price Charleston and Greenville value-add and construction deals with growing confidence, and CMBS is a live option for hospitality, which is a larger share of the market here than in most states. The judicial foreclosure regime thins the most aggressive national bridge pricing slightly, which keeps local capital competitive.
