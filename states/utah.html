Commercial Real Estate Loans in Utah

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan , Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated July 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Utah from $1 million to over $100 million, spanning 40 loan programs and every major property type. We maintain dedicated market coverage for 3 Utah metros, including Ogden and Provo. Below: how Utah's foreclosure process, recording taxes, and regulatory climate shape the loan terms lenders will offer here.

Utah commercial real estate financing runs on one of the most consistently strong state economies in the country, concentrated along an 80-mile stretch of the Wasatch Front. Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Salt Lake City, Provo, and Ogden, the three metros that form that corridor. Salt Lake City is the institutional anchor: a diversified base of financial services, healthcare anchored by the University of Utah and Intermountain Health, and a logistics position at the crossroads of I-15 and I-80 that has made the Northwest Quadrant and Salt Lake International Airport area one of the mountain West's premier distribution markets. Provo-Orem, powered by Brigham Young University and the Silicon Slopes technology corridor through Lehi and the Point of the Mountain, has been among the fastest-growing tech employment centers in the country. Ogden pairs aerospace and defense employment around Hill Air Force Base with an industrial base along I-15 north and some of the corridor's most affordable commercial pricing.

The demographic story does the underwriting: Utah persistently posts among the highest birth rates, in-migration, and labor force growth in the nation, and lenders treat Wasatch Front collateral as conviction-market product. Agency lenders, life companies, debt funds, and a notably strong bench of regional banks and credit unions all compete here. CLS CRE places the full range, from Silicon Slopes flex and Salt Lake industrial to Provo student-adjacent multifamily and Ogden value-add, running lender competition that Utah's fundamentals make genuinely deep.

Foreclosure and Lender Appetite

Utah lenders foreclose through a trustee sale under a trust deed, typically completing in roughly four to five months without court involvement. That efficient remedy keeps the full lender spectrum active and supports competitive bridge and construction leverage.

Recording Taxes and Closing Costs

Utah charges no mortgage recording tax, so financing and refinancing carry only standard county recording fees.

Utah is one of the West's most competitive lending environments relative to its size. The state's regional banks and credit unions are unusually strong and fight for commercial balance-sheet deals well into the mid eight figures, often beating national quotes. Underwriting quirks are few: no rent control, owner-friendly landlord-tenant law, and predictable property taxes. Lenders do watch supply, because the Wasatch Front builds aggressively and multifamily lease-up assumptions in Lehi, Vineyard, and Salt Lake's fast-growing submarkets get real scrutiny after heavy delivery years. Water and geotechnical diligence matter on fringe development, particularly around the Great Salt Lake's evolving shoreline and Point of the Mountain grading. Execution costs are among the lowest in the country.

Where Deals Get Done

Key Commercial Real Estate Sectors in Utah

Industrial and Logistics

Salt Lake City's Northwest Quadrant and airport submarket anchor the mountain West's distribution network at the crossroads of I-15 and I-80, with an inland port designation accelerating rail-served development. Ogden and the Davis-Weber corridor add manufacturing depth tied to Hill Air Force Base's aerospace ecosystem.

Technology and Flex Office

Silicon Slopes, running from Lehi's Thanksgiving Point through the Point of the Mountain to Draper, hosts one of the country's densest concentrations of software and fintech employment outside the coasts, sustaining flex, office, and mixed-use demand that has held up better than most national office markets.

Multifamily

Utah's demographic engine, the youngest population and among the fastest household formation rates in the nation, keeps apartment demand ahead of even aggressive supply. Agency lenders are highly active across Salt Lake, Provo-Orem, and Ogden, with student-adjacent product around BYU and the University of Utah a persistent niche.

Aerospace and Defense

Hill Air Force Base and its contractor ecosystem in the Ogden-Clearfield area, plus composites and advanced manufacturing along the Wasatch Front, drive owner-occupied industrial and build-to-suit demand that community and regional banks finance readily.

Market Intelligence

Regulatory Environment

Utah is a deliberately light-touch, pro-development state and capital prices that in. There is no rent control, landlord-tenant law is owner-friendly, and the legislature has repeatedly acted to streamline housing entitlements and preempt local slow-growth measures. Property taxes are low and Truth-in-Taxation rules keep increases visible and gradual, making expense underwriting predictable. Entitlements along most of the Wasatch Front run fast, which cuts both ways: development is genuinely easier, but lenders scrutinize submarket supply pipelines because everyone else can build too. The state's active management of Great Salt Lake water policy and Point of the Mountain planning are worth watching on fringe land deals, and mountain resort jurisdictions like Park City and the Cottonwood canyons remain the exception, with tight entitlement and severe supply constraints.

Which Lenders Are Active in Utah

Utah's lender depth outruns its population. National banks, life insurance companies, and debt funds treat Salt Lake City as a core mountain West market, agency lenders dominate stabilized multifamily across all three Wasatch Front metros, and CMBS participates on stabilized retail, hospitality, and industrial. The distinguishing feature is the in-state bench: Utah's regional banks and credit unions are among the most aggressive commercial real estate lenders in the West, know the corridor block by block, and routinely win deals into the mid eight figures on pricing and speed. For borrowers, the winning strategy is almost always running national capital against strong local quotes.
