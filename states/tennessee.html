Commercial Real Estate Loans in Tennessee

Reviewed by Trevor Damyan , Commercial Mortgage Broker · Updated August 2026
Quick answer: Commercial Lending Solutions arranges commercial real estate loans across Tennessee from $1 million to over $100 million, spanning 42 loan programs and every major property type. We maintain dedicated market coverage for 7 Tennessee metros, including Chattanooga and Clarksville. Below: how Tennessee's foreclosure process, recording taxes, and regulatory climate shape the loan terms lenders will offer here.

Tennessee commercial real estate financing spans two of the South's most important markets and a bench of strong secondary metros, and Commercial Lending Solutions arranges loans across all of them: Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville, Chattanooga, Clarksville, and Johnson City. Nashville is the star, the healthcare industry's corporate capital with the largest concentration of hospital and health services headquarters in the country, layered over a tourism economy of national scale, major university anchors, and a decade of corporate relocations that filled downtown towers and suburban campuses alike. Memphis is the logistics counterweight: home to the global air cargo superhub that makes it one of the world's busiest cargo airports, America's third-largest rail intermodal network, and Mississippi River port access, with a massive electric vehicle assembly campus rising just east of the metro. Knoxville pairs the University of Tennessee with the Oak Ridge national laboratory corridor, Chattanooga combines automotive assembly with a freight and logistics heritage, Clarksville rides Fort Campbell and Korean manufacturing investment, and Johnson City anchors the Tri-Cities medical and university economy.

Capital is deep everywhere in the state. No state income tax, landlord-friendly law, and population inflows keep every lender category allocating to Tennessee. Deal flow concentrates in Nashville multifamily, hospitality, and mixed-use, Memphis industrial, and healthcare-related product statewide. CLS CRE's job is matching each deal to the capital that wants it most, because a Nashville tower, a Memphis distribution box, and a Johnson City medical office building each have a different natural lender, and all three price best under competition.

Foreclosure and Lender Appetite

Tennessee lenders foreclose non-judicially through a trustee sale under a deed of trust, typically concluding in a few months. That efficient, predictable remedy keeps every lender type active in the state and supports aggressive bridge and construction leverage.

Recording Taxes and Closing Costs

Tennessee charges a recordation tax on indebtedness of 11.5 cents per $100, or 0.115%, so a $10 million mortgage carries roughly $11,500 in tax at recording, a modest but real closing cost to budget on acquisitions and refinances.

Tennessee offers one of the deepest lender pools in the Southeast. Regional banks, several headquartered in-state, compete hard across every metro, community banks and credit unions cover smaller balance-sheet deals, agency lenders are aggressive on multifamily from Nashville to Johnson City, and debt funds, life companies, and CMBS desks all chase Nashville and Memphis product at institutional scale. Underwriting friction is low: an efficient trustee-sale remedy, low property taxes, no rent control, and manageable insurance costs. Budget the 0.115% recordation tax at closing, and expect Nashville construction lenders to focus on submarket supply pipelines, since deliveries there run heavy in cycles.

Market Intelligence

Regulatory Environment

Tennessee is structurally landlord-friendly and lenders price that in. There is no state income tax on wages, no rent control, and state law preempts local rent regulation; property taxes are low by national standards; and entitlement is fast outside Nashville's most contested urban submarkets, where downtown density and short-term rental rules get local attention. Memphis and Nashville both use long-standing PILOT programs to incentivize industrial and redevelopment projects, and lenders underwrite those tax abatements as part of the cash flow. The recordation tax on indebtedness, 0.115%, is the one statewide closing cost quirk. Insurance costs are manageable relative to Gulf states, with severe storm exposure the main consideration. The overall posture is low-friction growth, which is exactly why national capital keeps deepening its Tennessee allocations.

Which Lenders Are Active in Tennessee

Every capital source is active in Tennessee. Regional banks, including several headquartered in the state, anchor the market and compete aggressively on construction and stabilized deals, community banks and credit unions cover smaller product statewide, agency lenders dominate stabilized multifamily in every metro, life companies pursue Nashville and Memphis industrial and multifamily at the tightest spreads, and debt funds price bridge, lease-up, and construction risk deep into the secondary markets. CMBS is a natural fit for Nashville hospitality and single-tenant assets. Well-packaged Tennessee deals reliably draw multiple quotes, and CLS CRE runs that competition to the borrower's advantage.

Commercial Real Estate Lending in Tennessee: FAQ

Tennessee lenders foreclose through a trustee sale under a deed of trust without court involvement, typically completing the process in a few months. That certainty of remedy reduces recovery risk, which is a structural reason Tennessee supports such a deep bench of bridge lenders, debt funds, and construction capital at meaningful leverage. Borrowers benefit indirectly but materially: more lender types compete with confidence in their downside, so pricing is tighter and structures more flexible than judicial-only states can support. Terms in Tennessee are set by the deal and the sponsor, not by legal-regime risk.
Tennessee charges a recordation tax on indebtedness of 11.5 cents per $100 of secured debt, which works out to 0.115% of the loan amount. On a $10 million mortgage that is roughly $11,500 due when the deed of trust is recorded, in addition to standard recording and title charges. The tax applies to new recordings, so refinances incur it on the new loan amount as well. It is a modest but real closing cost, smaller than the mortgage taxes in states like New York or Florida, and CLS CRE includes it in every Tennessee closing cost estimate up front.
